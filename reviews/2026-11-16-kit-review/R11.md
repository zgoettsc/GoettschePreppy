### 02-17 CL-17 Opioids (SAMHSA timeline and naloxone verbatim)
- **The withdrawal table L2 wrote from memory was wrong** (it said long-acting 12-48 h onset,
  10-20 days; short-acting 4-10 days). Replaced with TIP 63 (2021) Exhibit 2.10 as published:
  early withdrawal 8–24 h (short-acting) / up to 36 h (long-acting); fully developed 1–3 days /
  72–96 h; total 7–10 days / 14 days or more. MedlinePlus's 12 h heroin / 30 h methadone onset
  added as a second source. Example drug names removed from the table (not in the exhibit).
- Naloxone steps now follow the OTC Narcan 4 mg Drug Facts (DailyMed): check, give (either
  nostril, one spray per device), call 911, wait 2-3 minutes and keep giving every 2-3 minutes
  until awake ("safe to keep giving"), stay, re-dose if very sleepy again. "Other nostril" and
  "rub the breastbone" removed (not on the label). CDC: stay until help arrives or at least four
  hours; more than one dose may be needed with fentanyl; on the side.
- Overdose signs re-cited to CDC What to Do If You Think Someone Is Overdosing (CDC Lifesaving
  Naloxone does not list signs). "Does no harm to someone who has not taken opioids" corrected to
  what CDC says (won't harm someone overdosing on other drugs).
- Naloxone storage printed from the Narcan label (36-77 °F, do not freeze, avoid above 104 °F,
  protect from light), labelled as that product's.
- FDA 2019 tapering DSC (404 everywhere) and FDA loperamide DSC (404) replaced by MedlinePlus
  Oxycodone and MedlinePlus Loperamide; DEA One Pill (403) replaced by CDC Fentanyl Facts. CDC
  2022 guideline wording corrected to "non-opioid treatments are preferred" for long-term pain.
- Withdrawal dangers now MedlinePlus as written (aspiration, most overdose deaths after detox).
