# R13 review: Section 08 (heating and cold-weather shelter) and Section 16 (tools and repair)

Pages reviewed: 22 (08-01 to 08-10, 16-01 to 16-12). All 22 now carry `review_pass`. 21 changed and
went up one version; 16-04 was unchanged. The validator gives 0 errors on both sections. The only
warnings are related pages outside the folder checked. I rendered every page against the three test
profiles (66 renders) with `render` and `buildContext` against the existing `app/build/kit.json`.
None had leftover `{{`, a broken table, `undefined` or `null`. The remaining blanks are the
household's own fill-ins (fuel counts, gas utility line, heater model) and read correctly.

Sources I read for this review: the Buddy MH9BX manual, the Dyna-Glo WK11C8 manual and the
YSN/Flame King training PDF (archive copies); the First Alert CO alarm manual, Matheson propane data
and NIST J034 (fetched); CPSC CO Q&A and CPSC CO fact sheet (WebFetch); the CDC, EPA, AAP, Red
Cross, AFP, NWS, USFA and CSIA archives; EPA Burn Wise; MU G5450, G1954 and G1959; AFH 10-644
section 15.4 and its knot plates; and the Dexter manual LIT-001-00. 11 sources are new, in
`sources/additions/R13.json`.

- 08-01: "a turned-down wick is the setting that makes the most CO" → "makes smell and CO, not savings". The manual says too low a flame makes odour and CO; it does not say "the most". Source: Dyna-Glo WK11C8.
- 08-01: "two unvented burners double the CO" → "add their CO and oxygen use together". "Double" was unsourced.
- 08-01: the NFPA 58 1-lb line now cites the Minnesota State Fire Marshal (R3's key). The NFPA 58 archive is empty.
- 08-01: Buddy 9 sq in high and low, and Dyna-Glo 200 cu ft per 1,000 BTU/h and 1 inch, re-read. The 10,500 → 2,100 cu ft example checked. The AAP baby-sleep and CDC "babies in cold bedrooms" lines re-read.
- 08-02 (wave 2): the UL 2034 alarm points were attributed to CPSC. They now cite the First Alert manual that prints them, as on 29-03: 30 ppm steady no alarm after 30 days; 70 ppm 1-4 h; 150 ppm 10-50 min; 400 ppm 4-15 min.
- 08-02 (wave 2): "EPA 9 ppm 8-hour" is not on the EPA page cited. I replaced it with EPA's home levels: 0.5-5 ppm; 5-15 near a gas stove; 30 or more near a badly adjusted one.
- 08-02: CPSC symptom bands are now stated as CPSC gives them: 1-70 ppm usually no symptoms (heart patients may get chest pain); above 70 noticeable; 150-200 disorientation, unconsciousness, death. The action table keeps its cautious advice but no longer invents bands. Source: CPSC CO Q&A.
- 08-02: placement details are now traced to sources: not within 4 in of the ceiling corner; not near fans, vents or open windows; 10 ft from a bath; one manual's 20 ft from fuel-burning heat sources (First Alert); never in kitchens or above fuel-burning appliances (CPSC).
