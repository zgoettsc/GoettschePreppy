## Formats

I followed A3's spec (notes/workers/A3.md 1.1, 1.2, 1.5) exactly; county keys are Census full names
("Minnehaha County", "Orleans Parish", "Capitol Planning Region"). Each county object has: `fips`,
`centroid` [lat, lon], `latitude` (one decimal; for C08's `region.home.latitude`), `bbox`
[w, s, e, n], `coastal`, `elevation_ft`, `zone`, `jan_low_f`, `jul_high_f`, `record_low_f`,
`record_high_f`, `precip_in`, `snow_in`, `last_frost`, `first_frost` (print-ready, "May 1", or
"None in most years"), `growing_days`, `ecoregion`, `ecoregion_code`, `nws_office` ("Sioux Falls, SD",
no "NWS" prefix, as in A3's example), `nws_cwa`, `nwr` [{call, freq, same}], `state_em`,
`hazards` {13 snake_case keys: 1..3}, `sun_hours_dec`, `sun_hours_jun`. State file top level adds
`state_fips`, `nws_offices` {code: name}, `ecoregions` [names], `sources` [keys].

## Methods (also in data/regions/national.json and on page 32-00)

- **Point:** Census 2024 Gazetteer internal point (inside the county, near the centre), not the
  county seat (no federal county-seat table was at hand). Aleutian longitudes are kept continuous (-180.4).
- **Elevation:** USGS 3DEP Elevation Point Query Service at the point, all 3,144 answered;
  ACIS PRISM 4-km cell elevation kept as fallback and as the second source.
- **Climate:** NCEI 1991-2020 normals (annual/seasonal + monthly archives, 15,616 stations).
  Per value, the station with the value and the lowest score = km + |elevation difference m| / 10.
  The temperature station's own frost/precip/snow are used when its score is within 25 of the best.
  CoCoRaHS gauges (US1...) carry a 30 km penalty for precip/snow (short records). Frost: 32 F at
  50 % probability (PRBLST/PRBFST/PRBGSL-T32FP50); -9999 with a growing season = "None in most years".
- **Records:** ACIS period-of-record min of daily low / max of daily high, from the first of the
  four best temperature stations with >= 30 yr record within 80 km (fallback >= 20 yr within 250 km:
  only remote Alaska). Dates in detail. SD state records come out right (-58 F Corson, 120 F Buffalo).
- **Zone:** official PRISM 2023 PHZM grid at the point (CONUS, AK, HI grids); zone = 10 F bands from
  -60 F, a/b halves. `zone_range` in detail samples the whole AWIPS county polygon.
- **NWS office:** AWIPS county shapefile c_16ap26 (county -> CWA); the CWA containing the point first.
  Kalawao HI (absent from AWIPS) gets the nearest AWIPS county's office (Honolulu).
- **NWR:** the data file behind NWS's "County Coverage By State" pages (CCL.js). One row per
  transmitter; `same` is the whole-county code (0 + FIPS). Partial-county codes (first digit 1-9)
  are kept in detail and explained on the state page. Connecticut planning regions use the legacy
  county codes (009001..009015) of the counties they overlap (Census crosswalk), because NWR still
  alerts by those. 29 counties have no transmitter listed by NWS (mostly AK, mountain CO/CA/NV);
  their `nwr` is [] and the page says "No transmitter listed".
- **Hazards:** see next section.
- **Ecoregion:** EPA Level III (CONUS 2013 revision; Alaska) at the point, Albers projection done in
  code; whole-county shares in detail. **Hawaii has no EPA Level III layer**; its counties carry
  "Hawaiian Islands (not mapped at EPA Level III)", code `HI` (an entry exists in ecoregions.json for D2).
- **Sun hours:** NASA POWER climatology 2001-2025, latitude-tilt south-facing panel, 1-degree cell
  (823 cells). Flat values in detail. NREL is the second source (below).
- **Coastal:** NRI Coastal Flooding applicable, minus Great Lakes and Great Salt Lake shores
  (IL, IN, MI, MN, OH, WI, UT, Lake Erie/Ontario counties of NY and Erie PA). Tidal estuary counties
  (DC, Philadelphia) count as coastal.

## Hazard ratings (answers L4's question)

FEMA National Risk Index, December 2025 release, hazard-type **Risk Index rating** (`*_RISKR`):
Very High, Relatively High -> **3 (High)**; Relatively Moderate -> **2 (Moderate)**; Relatively Low,
Very Low, No Rating, Not Applicable, Insufficient Data -> **1 (Low)**. Kit hazard -> NRI hazard:
tornado TRND; blizzard WNTW (Winter Weather); ice storm ISTM; flood = max(IFLD Inland Flooding,
CFLD Coastal Flooding); wildfire WFIR; earthquake ERQK; hurricane HRCN; extreme heat HWAV;
extreme cold CWAV; drought DRGT; volcano VLCN; tsunami TSUN; landslide LNDS. So 1 = lower, 3 = higher,
and A3's `rating_text` High/Moderate/Low matches; "promote at 2 or 3" is right.

Caveat to know: NRI risk weighs exposure (what there is to damage) and community resilience, so big
metros rate high for rare hazards (Cook IL earthquake = 3: NRI Relatively High risk, but its
historic loss ratio is Relatively Low) and thin rural counties rate low for real hazards. Pages say
this in plain words. EAL, historic-loss-ratio classes and annual frequency are in detail if the
coordinator wants a different rule (it is one dict in build.py).

## Owner-token conflict (needs a coordinator decision)

`scripts/owner-tokens.json` lists public place names that a national appendix must print: the NWS
office "Sioux Falls", "Minnehaha" County SD, "Kimball" County NE, "Goshen" County WY, "Ozark" County MO
and the EPA ecoregion "Ozark Highlands", NWR sites "Dalhart" TX and "Tonopah" NV. Validation fails on
them in any page. `build-region-pages.mjs` therefore writes the first letter of such a word as an
HTML character reference (`&#83;ioux Falls`): it renders normally and `validate.mjs 32-` passes. This
is a workaround, not a fix, and it is visible: 12 state pages carry 38 such references. Options:
(a) exempt `layer: generated` Section 32 pages in validate.mjs (as Section 31 is) and in A3's
rendered-binder owner-token check, then run `node scripts/build-region-pages.mjs --owner-tokens=plain`;
(b) keep the encoding, but A3's post-render check will still see the rendered words. I recommend (a).
The data JSON files carry the names in plain text (A3 checks them only for URLs).
