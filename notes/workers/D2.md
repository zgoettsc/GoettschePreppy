## species.json

- 321 species; deadly 138, edible 125, firewood 79; fungi 13.
- 285 species with plates (599 plates): 106 with 3, 102 with 2, 77 with 1, 36 with none.
- 112 look-alike pairs (edible ↔ deadly and deadly ↔ deadly), each with a how-to-tell sentence.
- Pilot-region species (recorded in SD or IL): 220 of the 308 PLANTS-backed species; all 220 were reviewed first.

Per species: `key`, `group` (primary list) and `groups` (every list; e.g. black locust is deadly + firewood, oaks/hickories/walnut/mesquite/pinyon are edible + firewood), `kind` (plant/fungus), `scientific` (modern name), `plants_name` + `symbol` + `family` (USDA PLANTS accepted, checked against the PLANTS checklist; death camases are still filed under *Zigadenus* there), `common_names`, `risk`, `id_features` (plain words), `harm` / `use` / `burn {heat, season_months, notes}`, `habitat`, `mistaken_for` (free text, may name things not in the dataset, e.g. edible mushrooms), `lookalikes [{key, how_to_tell}]` (**two-way**: every link exists on both species), `distribution {basis, states, county_records, state_coarse, planted_states, range_note}`, `ecoregions`, `plates` (count), `sources` (keys).

- **Risk scale.** deadly: `lethal` (deaths recorded) / `serious` / `contact` (rash, burns, blindness; includes "never burn"); edible: `starter` / `caution` (dangerous double or a preparation rule) / `do-not-eat` (camas and wild carrot: listed only so the deadly pair can be shown; the binder's rule is not to eat them).
- **Fungi** (13: death cap, both destroying angels, fly agaric, panther cap, deadly galerina, deadly dapperling, deadly conocybe, false morel, green-spored parasol, both jack-o'-lanterns, sweating mushroom) are deadly-only. No edible mushroom is listed (floor: no wild mushroom on a test; household rule: no mushrooms); their edible doubles (true morel, chanterelle, honey mushroom, puffball, meadow mushroom, fairy-ring mushroom) appear only as text in `mistaken_for`.
- **Firewood heat class** is the Extension heat-per-cord grouping (Missouri G5450) in three bands, consistent with Wood Handbook densities. No BTU numbers were introduced. `season_months` is rough Extension guidance.
- **Numbers**: only ones already verified in the household binder (water hemlock 2–3 cm of root, CDC MMWR 1994; seizures 15–60 min) or standard published statements; I removed a "walnut-sized root" draft and a sap:syrup ratio I could not source.

## Ecoregion mapping (coarse, and it says so)

1. A species is listed for every EPA Level III ecoregion that covers at least 10 percent of a county where USDA PLANTS records it (D1's whole-county `ecoregions_present` shares in `counties-detail.json`).
2. Where PLANTS records the species in a state but gives no county, it is listed for every ecoregion in that state (`state_coarse`).
3. Fungi use hand-set state lists from the literature (`basis: literature-coarse`). Planted killers (yews, oleander, sago palm, nandina, lantana, castor bean, foxglove, lily of the valley, daffodil, horse chestnut, etc.) also get `planted_states` (where they are planted in yards), mapped as in 2.
4. Hawaii: D1's placeholder "Hawaiian Islands (not mapped at EPA Level III)". PR/VI have no region (ackee, physic nut etc. still carry PR/VI in `states`).

A PLANTS record means "documented here", not "common here". Lists are therefore generous (an Eastern ecoregion has 80–100 deadly entries). A3 may want to show a short "learn these first" set: the deadly list is already sorted lethal → serious → contact.

## Plates: licence and caption checks

- **USDA PLANTS** images are kept only if the live PLANTS record says `Copyright=false`, re-checked on every build.
- **Watermarks override the flag.** Every sampled image credited to Patrick J. Alexander, the Smithsonian R.A. Howard collection, or Norman Melvin carries a "©" notice in the frame although PLANTS says Copyright=false; all images with those credits are refused by rule (`EXCLUDED_CREDITS` in `build_plates.py`).
- **Finding for the household binder (read-only, not changed):** six of its plates carry "© Smithsonian Institution" in the frame: bittersweet nightshade `sodu_004/005/006_lhp.jpg` and dandelion `taof_014/016/018_lhp.jpg` (ImageIDs 38852–38854, 40430/40432/40434). The household's `usfs-wildflowers` water hemlock photos were not reused: their licence note ("archived for personal offline reference") is not clearly PD.
- **Wikimedia Commons** was used only to fill gaps, and only for files whose Commons licence tag is public domain or CC0 (tag and author recorded per plate; re-checked at build). Commons tags are the uploader's statement; federal photos (BLM, NPS, USDA FS) are marked as such in `credit`.
- **Visual check.** Every plate was looked at on a contact sheet (`build/plants-raw/sheets*/`) before selection; `shows` describes what is actually in the frame, `feature` says which feature it shows (whole, leaf, flower, fruit, seed, stem, bark, root, drawing). Seed-laboratory photos, seed cross-section diagrams, tree-ring sections, people/machinery scenes and anything I could not confirm as the species were refused.
- **No AI imagery.** Only photographs and published botanical line drawings (mostly Britton & Brown 1913 via PLANTS).
- **Fungi: no photographs at all.** Being conservative: no fungus photo was accepted. Text only (features, toxin, delayed symptoms, edible doubles).
- **Commons credits** have URLs stripped (the binder renders credits; `url` holds the Commons file page for provenance only).
- **Compression.** Longest side 900 px (grainy film scans step down, never below 560 px), JPEG q45–82, near-greyscale images stored as one-channel; average 68 KB, max 80 KB, 52 small originals under 40 KB, total 40.8 MB. A3 recompresses to 720 px q62 anyway.
