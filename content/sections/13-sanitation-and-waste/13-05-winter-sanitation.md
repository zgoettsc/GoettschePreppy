# Sanitation when water lines are frozen

**What this page is for.** Keeping toilets, drains and washing going when the pipes freeze or
the house goes unheated, and planning for the spring thaw.
{{#if is.cold_winters}}Your January nights average about {{ region.home.jan_low_f }} °F: plan
for months of this, not a two-week outage.{{/if}}

> **CAUTION. Never thaw a frozen pipe with an open flame:** no torch, no propane heater, no
> blowtorch. It is a leading cause of house fires during cold snaps (American Red Cross,
> Preventing and Thawing Frozen Pipes). Use a hair dryer, a heating pad, warm towels, or an
> electric space heater kept at a safe distance, working **from the tap end back toward the
> frozen part, with the tap open.**
>
> **A frozen supply pipe and a frozen drain pipe are different problems.** Pouring water into a
> frozen **drain** just backs sewage into the house. **Find out which is frozen before you pour
> anything.**

## Do this now

1. **Winterize before the first hard freeze** (→ 08-03): insulate exposed pipes, seal the
   crawlspace and the gaps where the walls meet the foundation, keep cabinet doors open on
   outside-wall plumbing, and know where the main shutoff is.
2. **Decide now whether you will drain the plumbing** if the house goes unheated. It is the
   final answer to frozen pipes, and a decision better made calmly.
3. **Set up an indoor bucket toilet now** (→ 13-01). In hard cold, outdoor options are often not
   usable.
4. **Stock RV antifreeze**, the non-toxic pink kind (propylene glycol), for drain traps and
   toilets. Cheap, and it prevents a specific, annoying failure.

## Why winter is the hardest case

Everything gets harder at once:

- **Water freezes:** the tap, the well pump, stored water, the rain barrel (→ 03-06).
- **Drain pipes freeze**, especially in cold or unheated parts of the house.
- **Outdoor options become impractical.** You cannot dig a latrine in frozen ground, and going
  outside in deep cold with a small child is a serious undertaking.
- **A septic drain field can freeze** (EPA) (→ 13-12).
- **Handwashing water is harder to get** (→ 13-02) exactly when the household is packed into
  one heated room and illness spreads most easily (→ 13-11).

**One large mercy: cold kills smell and flies.** Frozen waste does not smell and does not draw
insects. **Winter is easier for *storing* waste even as it is harder for *handling* it.**

## Which pipe is frozen, and what to do

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| **No water at the tap, house heated** | Probably a frozen supply pipe. Find it and thaw it, **never with flame**. Open the tap first so melt water can escape |
| **Water runs in but the drain will not take it** | **Frozen drain. Stop adding water.** It will back up. Warm the drain pipe if you can reach it; otherwise use the bucket toilet (→ 13-01) |
| **House going unheated** | **Drain the system, after every container is filled:** main water valve off, all taps open, toilets flushed, RV antifreeze in traps and toilet bowls, and **drain the water heater by the six-step procedure on → 03-02** (heat off first; the water can be scalding). Never from memory, never with the burner or elements on |
| **Pipe already burst** | Shut off the main at once. Deal with the water; repair is on → 16-05 |
| **Septic system** | Keep normal use if you can: regular flow helps stop freezing (EPA). Add an insulating cover of snow, straw or mulch over the drain field (→ 13-12) |

**Prevention beats all of it:** insulate, seal drafts near outside-wall plumbing, keep a
**slow drip running** through pipes at risk during extreme cold, and keep cabinet doors open so
warm air reaches the pipes.

## The winter toilet

**Indoor bucket toilet, in or near the heated room, urine separated.** This is the answer, and
it works.

- **Indoors.** Nobody, adult or child, should be going outside to a toilet at 3 a.m. in deep
  cold.
- **In or near the one heated room** (→ 08-01), screened for privacy. Months in one room make
  privacy a real morale issue (→ 21-02).
- **Separate urine.** Even more important in winter: you cannot easily pour liquid outdoors
  without making ice on the paths and a smell in spring.
- **Keep the cover material indoors too.** Sawdust left outside freezes into a useless block.
- **Bagged waste goes to a sealed outdoor container.** It freezes, stops smelling, and waits.
- **Handwashing station indoors with warm water** (→ 13-02). Cold hands do not get washed
  properly, and cracked winter skin gets infected (→ 12-04).

## Where frozen waste goes in spring

**Plan for the thaw.** A winter of frozen bagged waste becomes an urgent problem the week it
warms up.

- **Have the disposal plan before spring:** sewer service back, a properly placed pit (→ 13-07),
  or a compost system that can take it (→ 13-06). **Make the plan by late winter.**
- **Do not let it thaw in the yard.** It will smell, draw flies, and contaminate the ground and
  runoff.
- **Snowmelt moves everything downhill.** Anything buried or piled over winter ends up in the
  runoff. **Place it accordingly** (→ 13-07).

## Water for sanitation in winter

- **Snow is free water** (→ 03-05, → 03-06). **Melting it costs fuel** (→ 06-04). Melted snow
  is ideal for handwashing and laundry (soft water), and is treated before drinking (→ 03-04).
- **Reuse water hard** (→ 13-03). Every drop melted with hard-won fuel should do two or three
  jobs.
- **Keep water containers inside**, above freezing, or they burst.
- **Warm washing water matters.** Nobody washes properly in ice water, and hygiene that does not
  happen does not prevent disease. **Budget a little fuel for warm washing water.**

## Safety around all of this

- **No open flame for thawing pipes.** Repeated because it is the fire that burns houses down
  (→ 12-03).
- **No fuel-burning heater in a closed space without carbon monoxide precautions** (→ 08-02;
  carbon monoxide explained on → 29-03).
- **Ice on paths** from water poured outside: choose a spot away from walkways (→ 11-06).
- **Cold injury during outdoor chores:** hats, mittens, don't linger (→ 11-04, → 12-10; NWS,
  During Extremely Cold Weather).
- **Keep children away from the waste system** (→ 20-02), which is harder when everyone lives in
  one room.

| Item | Why | Notes |

| **Urine container** | Matters more in winter than any other season | A jug or a second bucket |
| Privacy screen or curtain | Months in one room | Cheap, and it matters |
| **RV antifreeze (propylene glycol, pink)** | Traps and toilets once the system is drained | Non-toxic type only |
| Pipe insulation, heat tape, foam sleeves | Prevention beats thawing | → 08-03 |
| Hair dryer, heating pad, electric space heater | **Thawing without flame** | Red Cross guidance |
| Sealed outdoor waste container | Freezes and waits for spring | Lidded, animal-proof |
| Indoor handwashing station and a way to warm water | Cold hands don't get washed | → 13-02 |
| A large pot for melting snow | Water from snow | → 03-06 |
| The main water shutoff and its tool | Burst-pipe response | Water heater draining: → 03-02 |
| Headlamps at the toilet area | It is dark by 5 p.m. | → 10-01 |

**If you don't have it:**

- **Cover material:** sawdust from firewood cutting (→ 06-04); keep a supply **indoors** so it
  does not freeze solid.
- **Pipe insulation:** foam, towels, blankets, straw around exposed pipes.
- **Thawing:** warm towels applied again and again, a heating pad, a hair dryer, a lamp near
  (not touching) the pipe. **Never flame.**
- **Privacy:** a blanket on a rope, a folding screen, a corner behind furniture.
- **Snow melting:** any big pot on a stove that is already running for cooking (→ 07-01), not
  a separate fire.

## When it runs out

- **Cover material and bags** run out as on → 13-01, and you cannot easily gather frozen
  material outdoors. **Keep an indoor supply.**
- **Fuel is the winter multiplier.** Melting snow for washing competes with heating and cooking
  (→ 06-04). **Reuse water, melt on a fire that is already burning, and use snow directly where
  you can** (scrubbing, rinsing) instead of melting it first.
- **Runway:** in winter, storage gets easier (frozen waste waits) and water and fuel get
  tighter. **Plan the winter sanitation budget in fuel, not bags.**
- **The thing that does not wait is spring.** Have the thaw plan made by late winter (→ 13-06,
  → 13-07).

## Phase notes

- **A (sheltering at home):** the whole system indoors, waste frozen outdoors in sealed
  containers, water from snow and storage.
- **B (moving):** **avoid moving in deep winter unless you must** (→ 01-02). Sanitation on the
  move in hard cold means ground too frozen to dig, worse hygiene, and cold injury while doing
  it. Carry hand sanitizer and deal with waste well away from water and camp.
- **C (rural or at a destination):** you cannot dig a latrine in frozen ground, so **dig it in
  autumn** (→ 13-07). A septic system benefits from regular use and an insulating cover of snow
  or straw over the drain field (EPA). Plan the whole winter system before the first freeze.
