# Siting a latrine away from water

**What this page is for.** Where to put a pit toilet (latrine) so it does not poison the water,
how to build one, and how to deal with waste while traveling.

> **SAFETY. This is a decision you make once and cannot undo.** A latrine placed too close to,
> or uphill of, a water source contaminates that water, possibly for years, and possibly your
> neighbors' water too. **Groundwater contamination cannot be seen, and by the time people are
> sick the damage is done.**
>
> **Get it right on the first day at any new site.** It is one of the most important decisions
> in setting up at a new place (→ 01-04), and far easier to get right at the start than to fix
> later.
>
> **Safe distances depend on soil, slope and how deep the groundwater is.** The numbers here are
> cautious planning minimums. When in doubt, go further.

## Do this now

1. **If a place has a working septic system, use it first** (→ 13-12): a gravity septic system
   needs no power, only water and a working drain field (EPA). A latrine is the fallback for
   when that system fails or is overloaded, which is exactly when you will need these numbers.
   {{#if has.septic}}Your home is on septic.{{/if}}
2. **When choosing any destination, look at where waste could safely go** (→ 01-05), alongside
   water, wood and access. **A site with nowhere safe to put waste is a worse site than it
   looks.**
3. **In town, a pit latrine is usually not possible** on a house lot: the distances cannot be
   met and neighbors are close. **Bucket and bag** (→ 13-01) is the answer at home in town.
4. **Learn the rules of thumb below** so you can apply them fast while traveling or at a new
   site.
5. **If you will need a pit in winter, dig it in autumn.** Frozen ground cannot be dug
   (→ 13-05).

> **Before the collapse:** state and county rules govern pits, privies and setback distances.
> Check them. They never decide whether you keep waste away from drinking water.

## The core rule

**Waste goes downhill from, and far away from, anything anyone drinks.** Water moves through
soil, and it moves downhill. Everything else is detail on that one rule.

## Cautious planning distances

{{figure fig-latrine-setbacks}}

**These are minimums to plan around. Further is better.**

| From | Minimum distance | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **A well or spring** | **At least 100 ft, and further in sandy or cracked rock ground** | **Downhill of the well, never uphill** |
| **Any surface water** (creek, pond, river, ditch) | **At least 100 ft, more on a slope** | Includes drainage channels that only run in spring |
| The house or living space | 50 ft or more | Far enough for smell and flies, close enough to actually use in winter |
| The edible garden | Well apart | And downhill |
| Food storage, kitchen | Well apart | |
| Property line, neighbors | As far as possible | Their water matters too |
| **The groundwater (water table)** | **At least 5 ft (1.5 m) of dry soil under the bottom of the pit.** Dig a test hole and wait a day to see if water seeps in | **The one people miss** |

**The water table matters most.** A pit dug into or near groundwater empties straight into the
water people pump from wells. **Near rivers and in low ground the water table can be very
shallow.** Dig a test hole first and look. If you hit water or wet, soggy soil, **that site is
wrong**: go higher, or use a system that does not soak into the soil (→ 13-06, or bag and
store, → 13-01).

## Reading the site

- **Slope and drainage.** Stand there in a rainstorm, or imagine snowmelt. **Where does the
  water go?** Everything you put in the ground goes with it. **Never uphill of a well, never in
  a drainage path, never where snowmelt sheets across.**
- **Soil.** Sandy and gravelly soils drain fast, so contamination travels far and quickly:
  **increase the distances a lot.** Heavy clay holds contamination closer but drains badly and
  can pool. Cracked bedrock is the worst: contamination travels a long way through the cracks
  with almost no filtering.
- **Flooding.** Never in a floodplain, a low spot, or anywhere a river can reach (→ 17-03).
  {{#if is.flood_region}}Your home area has a real flood risk.{{/if}}
- **Wind:** downwind of the living space if you can manage it.
- **The winter path.** A latrine you cannot safely reach in a blizzard is one people stop using
  (→ 13-05, → 11-06). **Close enough to reach, far enough to be safe**, with a shovelled,
  sanded path.
- **Existing systems.** If the site has a septic system, **use it** (→ 13-12). Find the tank and
  drain field before digging anything, to protect them, and because **a latrine dug through a
  drain field is dug into sewage.**
- **Your own well:** → 03-10.

## Building a pit latrine

1. **Dig a test hole first** to check for groundwater and rock.
2. **Dig deep and narrow rather than wide and shallow:** more room, less surface, less smell,
   easier to cover.
3. **Put a slab or platform over the pit.** This is what makes it an "improved" latrine in CDC
   and WHO terms: it keeps people from touching the waste and keeps rain out. **A wooden
   platform with a seat and a lid.**
4. **A lid that closes.** Flies breeding in a latrine and then landing on food is a textbook
   way disease spreads.
5. **Cover material beside it:** soil, ash, lime, sawdust. **A scoop after each use** controls
   smell and flies.
6. **A handwashing station at the latrine** (→ 13-02). A tippy-tap costs nothing, and it is the
   whole point.
7. **When it is about two-thirds full, close it:** fill with soil, mound it so water runs off,
   mark it, and **do not plant food crops there.** Dig the next one somewhere else.
8. **Keep a record of where old pits are** (→ 25-04).

## Waste while traveling

- **200 feet (about 70 paces) from any water, camp or trail.**
- **A "cathole" 6 to 8 inches deep** in the dark top layer of soil, filled in and covered.
- **Pack out toilet paper**, or bury it deep with the waste; **never leave it on the surface.**
- **Never near a water source anyone might drink from** (→ 03-04).
- **In frozen ground you often cannot dig:** pack it out. Carry bags for exactly this.
- **Wash hands:** carry sanitizer for when there is no water (→ 13-02).

The 100-foot pit distance and the 200-foot cathole distance do not conflict: a shallow,
unlined scrape on the move gets more distance.

## What goes wrong

- **Uphill of the well.** The most serious mistake.
- **Too shallow, or into groundwater.**
- **In a drainage path:** fine in August, a disaster in the spring melt.
- **Too far to use in winter**, so people start going closer to the house.
- **No lid, no cover material:** flies, smell and disease.
- **No handwashing station**, which cancels most of the benefit.
- **Digging where you will later want a well or a garden.**
- **Ignoring the neighbors' water.** In a community, sanitation is a shared problem (→ 18-01):
  one bad latrine uphill affects everyone.

| Lumber for a platform and seat | Makes it an improved latrine | Solid, with a lid that closes |
| **Lime or wood ash** | Smell and germ control | Ash is free (→ 06-02) |
| Cover material and a scoop | Every use | Soil, sawdust, ash |
| **Handwashing station (tippy-tap)** | The point of the whole thing | → 13-02 |
| Screen or netting for the shelter | Flies spread disease | |
| A shovel kept only for the latrine | Touches nothing else | Label it |
| Marker stakes, a record of old pits | So nobody digs or plants there later | → 25-04 |
| Trowel and bags for traveling | Catholes, or packing out when frozen | → 22-03 |
| A site map with distances marked | Get the decision right once | → 25-04 |

**If you don't have it:**

- **Platform:** pallets, planks, plywood, a seat taken from a house toilet.
- **Shelter:** tarps on a frame, salvaged siding, pallets. Privacy matters over months
  (→ 21-02), and a windbreak matters in cold.
- **Cover material:** soil from the digging (keep the dug-out pile beside the pit: it is your
  cover supply), ash, sawdust, leaves.
- **Digging hard ground:** soak it the day before to soften it; a mattock and pry bar. A fire
  built on the spot slowly thaws frozen ground (it works, slowly).
- **Handwashing:** a jug with a nail hole; a tippy-tap from sticks and cord.

## When it runs out

- **A pit fills.** Plan for **more than one pit** over a long event. Pick the second and third
  sites when you dig the first, so you are not choosing under pressure.
- **Capacity:** a household fills a pit faster than expected, **especially if urine goes in.**
  Separating urine (→ 13-01, → 13-06) makes a pit last several times longer (roughly three times
  is a planning figure, not a published one).
- **Runway:** estimate pit volume against your household's use and put it in → 23-02. A slow
  clock, but a clock.
- **Substitute:** compost system (→ 13-06), bag and store (→ 13-01), or the sewer or septic if it
  works.
- **What never runs out is the siting rule:** downhill, far from water, above the water table,
  out of the drainage path, with a lid and a handwashing station.
- **The community side matters over time** (→ 18-01). If neighbors are also improvising, **where
  they put waste affects your water.** Agreeing where waste goes is one of the most useful early
  conversations in a group, and one of the least contentious, because everyone benefits.

## Phase notes

- **A (sheltering at home):** {{#if is.urban}}generally not possible on a town lot: the
  distances cannot be met. **Bucket and bag** (→ 13-01), and treat sanitation as one of the real
  limits of staying put in a long event.{{else}}on a rural lot, do the site work now: test hole,
  drainage, distances from your well, and mark the spot.{{/if}}
- **B (moving):** catholes, 200 feet from water, buried and covered; pack out in frozen ground.
  **Never contaminate a water source you or others will use.**
- **C (rural or at a destination):** if there is a septic system, **operating it is the first
  move** (→ 13-12), not digging. The site work is still first-week work (→ 01-04): test-dig for
  groundwater, walk the drainage, **choose and mark the latrine site downhill and far from the
  well, and clear of the septic tank and drain field, before it is ever needed**, pick the next
  two pit sites, and dig a winter pit in autumn.
