# Preventing a disease outbreak in the household

**What this page is for.** Stopping an illness (especially a stomach bug) from spreading through
the whole household when you are living close together with no clinic: routes, the sick corner,
the cleanup, and the bleach strengths to use.

> **SAFETY. The whole household falling ill at once is one of the worst situations in this
> binder**, worse than most single injuries, because it takes away every adult's ability to haul
> water, tend the fire and care for the sick at the moment care is most needed.
>
> **For some people a routine stomach bug is not routine:** babies and young children dehydrate
> dangerously fast (→ 12-05), and vomiting and diarrhoea are a dangerous sick day for anyone with
> type 1 diabetes{{#if has.condition.CL-01}} (→ 02-01){{/if}}.
>
> **The structural problem: you may be living in one heated room** (→ 08-01). That is right for
> heat, fuel and morale, and **the worst possible arrangement for containing an illness.** This
> page manages that tension on purpose.
>
> **Norovirus:** alcohol hand sanitiser is **not** good enough against it (CDC, Preventing
> Norovirus). **Soap and water.**
>
> **Floor:** bleach is never mixed with ammonia, acids or other cleaners. The mix gives off a
> gas that can kill. (→ 30-01)

## Do this now

1. **Decide now where a sick person would go:** a second room you would heat, a corner you
   would screen off, or a marked "sick zone" in the warm room. **Deciding while everyone is well
   is far easier than at 2 a.m. with a vomiting child.**
2. **Stock unscented bleach (5 to 9 percent sodium hypochlorite) and learn the cleanup**: the
   surface table is below. Rotate bleach; it weakens in storage (→ 29-05).
3. **Stock oral rehydration salts (ORS) heavily** (→ 12-05). They matter most when this happens.
4. **Give everyone their own items now:** cup, plate, utensils, towel, marked with their name.
   Costs nothing, stops a lot of spread.
5. **Build the handwashing stations** (→ 13-02). The single control that does the most.

## How illness spreads, and what stops it

| Route | What spreads | The control |
|---|---|---|
| **Faecal-oral** (from stool to mouth) | Norovirus, diarrhoeal illness, hepatitis A | **Handwashing with soap** (→ 13-02), waste handling (→ 13-01), dishwashing (→ 13-10) |
| **Coughs, sneezes, breath** | Colds, flu, most chest illness | Distance, fresh air, covering coughs, separation |
| **Surfaces** | Norovirus survives a long time on surfaces | Clean, **then** disinfect (CDC) |
| **Food and water** | Food poisoning, waterborne illness | → 04-17, → 03-04, → 13-10 |
| **Shared items** | Everything | **Personal cups, plates, towels, bedding** |
| **Cleaning up vomit and stool** | Norovirus especially | **Gloves, bleach, careful cleanup, thorough handwashing** (CDC) |

## The one-room problem, handled honestly

You cannot fully isolate someone in a household living in one heated room. What you can do:

- **A sick corner:** the far end of the room, screened with a blanket, with the sick person's own
  bedding, cup, plate and bucket.
- **Air the room on purpose:** brief, wide-open airing several times a day. It costs less heat
  than people fear (→ 11-03), and it really does cut spread through the air.
- **One caregiver** where possible: ideally **not** someone with diabetes or another serious
  condition, and **not** the person who cooks. One person in and out, washing hands thoroughly
  every time.
- **Heat a second room if you possibly can** during an illness. **This is a good use of
  firewood** (→ 06-04): extra wood for a week to stop an outbreak taking the whole household is a
  good trade.
- **Children are the hardest part.** A sick child wants comfort, and a baby cannot be separated
  from its parents at all. **Accept that, and put your effort into the controls that work:**
  handwashing, surfaces, personal items, and containing vomit and stool.

## The sick kit

Put this together in advance, in one labelled container:

- **A bucket with a lid kept only for vomiting and waste** (→ 13-01), kept with the sick person
  and emptied carefully with gloves.
- **Gloves, and masks if you have them.**
- **Unscented bleach and a spray bottle**, for the table below.
- **Rags and paper towels kept for cleanup**, thrown away or washed separately (→ 13-04).
- **The sick person's own cup, plate, utensils, towel and bedding**, marked.
- **ORS packets, a thermometer, a symptom log** (→ 12-05, → 25-05).
- **A basin, a washcloth and a way to warm water.** Comfort matters, and it helps.

## The surface bleach table

**These are SURFACE strengths (CDC, Cleaning and Disinfecting with Bleach; CDC, Preventing
Norovirus). They are hundreds of times stronger than the DRINKING-water amounts on → 03-04 (a few
drops per gallon).** Never confuse the two tables. Bleach at surface strength in drinking water is
poisoning; bleach at drinking strength on a norovirus spill does nothing. **If a mixed jug has no
label, it is surface solution: pour it away. It is never drinkable.**

Published amounts, printed as published:

| Job | Recipe (per gallon of room-temperature water) | Contact time |
|---|---|---|
| **General surface disinfection** (counters, door handles, the sick corner) | **5 tablespoons (1/3 cup)** of household bleach, or 4 teaspoons per quart | **At least 1 minute, visibly wet** |
| **Norovirus: vomit and stool cleanup** | **5 to 25 tablespoons** (1,000 to 5,000 ppm). **Use the strong end (up to 25 tablespoons, about 1½ cups plus 1 tablespoon) on the spill itself and on porous or heavily soiled surfaces**; the low end on the hard surfaces around it | **At least 5 minutes** |

**Bleach strength:** these recipes are for **plain unscented household bleach at 5 to 9 percent
sodium hypochlorite.** The common US strengths (6 percent and 8.25 percent "concentrated") both fall
in that range, so the same spoon counts apply to either. If the label says anything outside 5 to 9
percent, or the product is splash-less, scented or a gel, do not use it for disinfecting.

**Rules that make the table work (CDC):**

- **Clean first, then disinfect.** Bleach on a visibly dirty surface does not work.
- **Mix it fresh every day.** Diluted bleach loses strength after about 24 hours. Date the jug.
- **NEVER mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaner.** The fumes can kill. Open windows while
  using it indoors.
- **Food-contact surfaces and dishes:** after the contact time, **rinse with clean, safe water**
  before food touches them. The dish-sanitising tub on → 13-10 uses a much weaker solution, not
  this table.
- **Wear gloves; keep it off skin and out of children's reach** (→ 20-02).

## Cleaning up vomit and diarrhoea: the riskiest moment

Norovirus spreads very easily during cleanup (CDC).

- **Gloves, always.** A mask if you have one.
- **Contain it:** cover it with paper towels or disposable cloth first, then lift it away.
- **Clean, then disinfect** with the **norovirus-strength** solution from the table: 5 to 25
  tablespoons per gallon, at least 5 minutes wet. **Cleaning first is required.**
- **Handle soiled laundry carefully:** gloves, as little shaking as possible (shaking throws
  germs into the air), wash hot and separately, dry completely (→ 13-04; CDC).
- **Wash your hands with soap and water afterwards**, **not sanitiser alone** (CDC).
- **Air the room** afterwards.

## Watching the whole household

- **Log symptoms and times for everyone** (→ 25-05). Knowing who fell ill when tells you whether
  it is spreading and whether the controls work.
- **Watch babies and young children closest:** fewer wet diapers, floppiness or unusual
  sleepiness, no tears when crying (→ 12-05).
{{#if has.condition.CL-01}}- **Watch anyone with type 1 diabetes for ketones.** Illness plus
  vomiting is the road to diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA). Ketone strips out and in use at the first
  sign of illness (→ 02-01).
{{else}}- **Anyone with diabetes:** illness plus vomiting is dangerous; follow their condition
  page's sick-day guidance (→ 02-01, → 02-05).
{{/if}}
- **Watch for signs it is beyond home care:** blood in stool, high fever, dehydration that does
  not improve with ORS, confusion, or signs of sepsis (a body-wide reaction to infection; → 12-04;
  CDC, About Sepsis).

## Prevention: where the real leverage is

This page's controls come after the ones that stop an outbreak starting:

1. **Handwashing at the key moments** (→ 13-02): **the single most effective thing.**
2. **Safe water** (→ 03-04).
3. **Keeping waste separate** (→ 13-01, → 13-07).
4. **Food safety:** cooking to temperature, raw and cooked kept apart (→ 13-10, → 04-17).
5. **Proper dishwashing** (→ 13-10).
6. **Personal items, not shared.**
7. **Managing outside contact.** Anyone who comes in (a neighbour, a trader, → 18-02) can bring
   illness. **Handwashing on arrival is a reasonable, friendly household rule.** Start it early.
8. **Keeping everyone fed, warm and rested.** A cold, hungry, exhausted household gets sicker and
   stays sicker (→ 08-01, → 21-07).

## If the whole household goes down anyway

It happens. Plan for it:

- **Put water and ORS where a sick person can reach them without going outside.** **The most
  important preparation:** everything else can wait; drinking cannot.
- **Simple food that needs no cooking** for the days nobody can cook (→ 04-18).
- **Keep the fire and the house going.** Even a barely functioning adult can keep one stove
  going. **Have enough wood indoors that nobody has to go out** for several days (→ 06-04).
- **Medical routines do not pause.** Insulin and other daily medicines continue through illness,
  and matter **more**, not less.
- **Neighbours** (→ 18-01). A household on good terms with its neighbours has somewhere to turn
  when everyone is ill at once.

| Symptom log sheets | Who, when, what | → 25-05 |

**If you don't have it:**

- **Disinfectant:** bleach by the CDC table; boiling for anything that can be boiled; **soap and
  water with scrubbing does most of the work** even without a disinfectant.
- **ORS:** the WHO home recipe from salt, sugar and safe water, **read from → 12-05, never from
  memory.**
- **Separation:** a blanket on a rope, furniture, a screened corner, a second room heated for a
  while.
- **Personal items:** mark existing cups and plates with tape and marker.
- **Masks:** cloth is partial protection; better than nothing.
- **What not to substitute:** **alcohol sanitiser for handwashing during a norovirus outbreak**
  (CDC). Soap and water.

## When it runs out

- **Bleach is the consumable you cannot make**, and it weakens in storage. Rotate it; **boiling
  and thorough soap-and-water cleaning** replace most of what it does.
- **Soap can be made** (→ 13-09), and matters more anyway.
- **ORS is the stock that matters most** in a real outbreak (→ 12-05): count it in litres and in
  days of a sick child.
- **Water use jumps in an outbreak:** sick people drink more, and laundry, cleaning and washing all
  increase, while the people who haul water are ill. **That is a specific reason to store more
  water than the daily maths suggests** (→ 03-02, → 23-02).
- **When supplies run out, behaviour substitutes:** thorough handwashing, personal items,
  separation, fresh air and careful cleanup need almost no supplies. **When the disinfectant runs
  out, be more careful, not less.**
- **The big point:** an outbreak is one of the few household emergencies that is **almost entirely
  preventable with cheap, boring, repeatable habits:** handwashing, safe water, keeping waste
  separate, and careful food handling. **The water, sanitation and food sections are the outbreak
  plan.** This page is what you do when they have been breached.

## Phase notes

- **A (sheltering at home):** crowding into one room is the risk. Build the stations, start the
  personal-item rule, decide the sick-corner plan, and **be willing to burn extra wood to heat a
  second room during an illness.**
- **B (moving):** hygiene slips and everyone is exhausted, which is exactly when illness spreads.
  **Hand sanitiser and soap for each person**, strict discipline before eating and after any waste
  handling, and treat all water (→ 03-04). **An outbreak while moving is a reason to stop and
  shelter** (→ 01-02).
- **C (rural or at a destination):** more room to isolate properly. But further from any care
  (→ 12-16), animals adding illnesses that pass from animals to people (→ 05-04), and a new water
  and sanitation setup that has to be right from the first day (→ 13-07). **Placing the latrine
  correctly at a new site is the best outbreak prevention there.**
