# CL-09. High blood pressure and heart disease

**What this page is for.** High blood pressure and heart disease are among the most common
conditions in any household with adults over 40. Most are managed with daily pills that keep
well, which makes them easier to stock than insulin. Two things make them dangerous in a long
emergency: **some heart and blood pressure pills are dangerous to stop suddenly**, and **heart
attacks and strokes need care fast**. This module covers both, plus the daily habits that matter
more when the pills are short.

{{#if has.condition.CL-09}}**Someone in your household has this condition.** This module is at the front of the medical section for you.{{else}}**Nobody in your household is marked with this condition.** The module stays in the binder because anyone can have a heart attack or stroke; the warning signs below are for everyone.{{/if}}

> **SAFETY**
> - **Some of these medicines must never be stopped suddenly.** Stopping a beta-blocker such as
>   metoprolol suddenly can cause chest pain or a heart attack; stopping clonidine suddenly can
>   cause a rapid rise in blood pressure (MedlinePlus, Metoprolol; MedlinePlus, Clonidine). Ask
>   the prescriber now which of the person's medicines are like this (part 12).
> - **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products.** Some
>   heart and blood pressure pills are dangerous to a small child in very small amounts (floor,
>   → 30-01).
> - **This page never calculates, adjusts or suggests a dose.**

## Do this now

**Possible heart attack** (American Heart Association, Warning Signs of a Heart Attack):
chest discomfort (pressure, squeezing, fullness or pain) that lasts more than a few minutes or
goes away and comes back; discomfort in one or both arms, the back, neck, jaw or stomach;
shortness of breath; cold sweat, nausea or light-headedness. Women more often have breathlessness,
nausea or vomiting, and back or jaw pain.
1. **Call for help** if any exists: 911.
2. **Stop all effort.** Sit them down, half-upright, loosen clothing, keep them calm and warm.
3. **Their own nitroglycerin**, if prescribed, exactly as its label says. MedlinePlus: if chest
   pain is not better or is worse 5 minutes after one dose, call for emergency help at once
   (MedlinePlus, Nitroglycerin Sublingual).
4. **Aspirin:** AHA says call 911 first; the dispatcher may tell you to chew aspirin (AHA,
   Aspirin and Heart Disease). With no 911, follow what the prescriber wrote in part 7. The
   household's general line on aspirin in emergencies is on → 12-16.
5. **If they collapse and are not breathing normally: start CPR** (→ 12-18).

**Possible stroke: F.A.S.T.** (American Stroke Association): **F**ace drooping, **A**rm
weakness, **S**peech difficulty, **T**ime to call 911. Note the time it started. Lay them down
with head and shoulders slightly raised, on their side if vomiting. Nothing by mouth. **Do not
give aspirin for a possible stroke unless a clinician says so**: some strokes are bleeding, and
aspirin makes bleeding worse. Low blood sugar can look like a stroke; check it if a meter is at
hand.

## 1. What it is

**High blood pressure (hypertension)** means blood pushes too hard against the artery walls. It
usually causes no symptoms while it slowly damages the heart, brain, kidneys and eyes (NHLBI,
High Blood Pressure). **Heart disease** covers narrowed heart arteries (which cause chest pain
and heart attacks), heart failure (a weak or stiff heart that lets fluid build up), and
irregular heartbeats.

## 2. What runs out, and how fast

| What | Clock | When it is gone |
|---|---|---|
| Daily pills | Days on hand from the bottle (part 7). Most keep well, cool and dry. | Blood pressure rises over days to weeks; for some pills, stopping suddenly is itself dangerous. |
| Nitroglycerin (if prescribed) | Expiry, and it weakens once the bottle is opened; check the label. | No relief for chest pain. |
| Blood pressure cuff batteries | Months. | No readings; learn a manual cuff or count the pulse. |
| Low-salt food | Most stored and canned food is salty. | Blood pressure and swelling rise. |

## 3. Storing the medication without power

- **Tablets:** original bottles, tightly closed, cool and dry, out of sun and away from
  children. Not in a car, a bathroom or a hot attic.
- **Nitroglycerin tablets:** keep them in their **original glass bottle, tightly closed**, at
  room temperature, away from light, heat and moisture (MedlinePlus, Nitroglycerin Sublingual).
  Never move them into a pill organiser.
- Few heart medicines need a fridge. If one does, see → 29-07 and write its rule here: ________

## 4. Day by day without it

- **Most blood pressure pills, stopped:** pressure creeps back up over days to weeks. Usually no
  symptoms, sometimes headache. The risk of stroke and heart attack rises over time.
- **Beta-blockers or clonidine, stopped suddenly:** a dangerous rebound within days (part
  "SAFETY"). **If supply is running out, the prescriber's taper plan matters more than anything
  else on this page.**
- **Heart failure pills (often a "water pill"), stopped:** fluid builds up over days: swollen
  ankles, weight gain, breathlessness lying flat.

**What helps, and is in your control** (NHLBI): less salt, more vegetables and fruit, regular
activity, no smoking, little alcohol, sleep, and managing stress. Off-grid:
- **Most canned and stored food is salty.** Rinse canned beans and vegetables, cook from dried
  beans and grains, and save salty foods for when they are needed.
- **Hard physical work** is good for blood pressure, but a heavy, sudden effort in the cold
  (shovelling snow, hauling wood) strains the heart. Pace it.
- **Stress raises pressure** (→ 21-01). Sleep and routine count.

## 5. Warning signs that mean an emergency

- **Heart attack and stroke:** see "Do this now".
- **Very high blood pressure with symptoms:** severe headache, chest pain, breathlessness,
  confusion, vision changes, weakness. Rest, the prescriber's plan, any care that exists.
- **Heart failure getting worse:** breathless lying flat or waking breathless, fast weight gain,
  swelling spreading up the legs.
- **Fainting, a racing or very slow pulse,** or a new irregular beat.
- **Heat:** some heart and blood pressure medicines make heat illness more likely (CDC, About
  Heat and Your Health). In a heatwave, watch for dizziness and dehydration (→ 17-04).

## 6. Sick-day basics

- **Vomiting, diarrhoea and heat dehydrate**, and some blood pressure pills and water pills
  make that worse or drop the pressure too far: dizziness, fainting on standing.
- **Fluids and rest**; oral rehydration for diarrhoea (→ 12-05). Stand up slowly.
- **Decongestants and some pain relievers can raise blood pressure** or strain the heart; ask the
  pharmacist which over-the-counter medicines are safe for this person, and write them in
  part 7.

## 8. Sourcing when the pharmacy is closed

- **Pharmacies, clinics and disaster medical points** while they run, with the printed
  prescription.
- **Many of these medicines are common generics** that other households hold. Someone else's
  prescription is a card: {{choice DC-15}} (→ 30-16). Same drug and same strength only; the
  prescriber's agreement if any can be reached.
- **Expired tablets:** {{choice DC-04}} (→ 30-05).
- **There is no safe herbal or over-the-counter substitute**, and supplements sold "for blood
  pressure" are not tested replacements.

## 9. Power and devices

An electronic cuff runs for months on batteries; keep spares. A manual cuff with a stethoscope
needs no power and survives an EMP (→ 27-03) but takes practice. A pacemaker or implanted
defibrillator: write its type and the clinic's number on → 25-09; ask the clinic now what
the device needs and what to avoid.

## 10. Travel and evacuation

- Pills and nitroglycerin on the person, out of heat (→ 01-07, → 22-06).
- **Pace the walk and the load.** Rest before breathlessness.
- A copy of the medicine list and any heart tracing (ECG) the person has, on paper (→ 25-09).
