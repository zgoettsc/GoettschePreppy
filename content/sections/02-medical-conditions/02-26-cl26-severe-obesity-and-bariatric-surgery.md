# CL-26. Severe obesity and bariatric surgery

**What this page is for.** This module covers two related situations. **Severe obesity** brings
practical problems in a long emergency: moving and evacuating, heat, skin care, equipment that
does not fit, and the conditions that often come with it (diabetes, high blood pressure, sleep
apnoea). **Bariatric (weight-loss) surgery** changes how the body takes in food and medicine for
life: the person needs vitamins every day, cannot eat large or sugary meals, may absorb some
medicines poorly, and can become seriously ill from vomiting. Emergency food is often exactly
the wrong food. This module covers both, without judgement.

> **SAFETY**
> - **After bariatric surgery, vomiting that will not stop is an emergency.** A lack of vitamin
>   B1 (thiamine), which can follow weight-loss surgery, damages the brain (MedlinePlus,
>   Wernicke-Korsakoff Syndrome; part 5).
> - **After gastric bypass, do not take aspirin, ibuprofen or similar pain relievers (NSAIDs)
>   unless the surgeon says so.** MedlinePlus says they may harm the stomach lining or cause
>   ulcers (MedlinePlus, Gastric Bypass Surgery: Discharge; → 12-13).
> - **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products.** (floor,
>   → 30-01).
> - **This page never calculates, adjusts or suggests a dose.**

## Do this now

1. **Bariatric surgery: keep taking the daily vitamins and minerals.** Count them; write days on
   hand in part 12.
2. **Vomiting for more than a day, or any vomiting with confusion, unsteady walking or eye
   problems:** get care (part 5).
3. **Severe belly pain after bariatric surgery,** especially with a fast heartbeat or fever: get
   care.
4. **Heat:** shade, water, rest, cool cloths (→ 17-04).
5. **Evacuation:** plan the transport and helpers now (part 10).

## 1. What it is

**Severe obesity** is a level of body weight high enough to affect health and daily function. It
often comes with health problems such as type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and sleep apnoea
(NIDDK, Bariatric Surgery). **Bariatric surgery** makes the stomach smaller and, in some operations,
bypasses part of the small intestine. The common operations are the **sleeve gastrectomy**, the
**gastric bypass**, the **adjustable band**, and the **duodenal switch** (NIDDK, Bariatric
Surgery). After surgery a person eats small amounts, needs protein first, and takes vitamins and
minerals for the rest of their life. Some people instead take weekly or daily weight-loss
injections.

## 4. Day by day without it

**After bariatric surgery, without the vitamins.** After gastric bypass the body may not absorb
some vitamins well from food. MedlinePlus says the usual supplements are a multivitamin, vitamin
B12 and vitamin D, sometimes with calcium and iron, with blood tests for life to check them
(MedlinePlus, Gastric Bypass Surgery: Discharge). Shortages build slowly; the surgeon or
dietitian can say which matter most for this person (part 7). Thiamine (vitamin B1) shortage is
the dangerous one when the person is vomiting or barely eating (part 5).

**The food plan after surgery:**
- **Small meals, eaten slowly, protein first.** Canned meat and fish, beans, eggs, powdered milk,
  peanut butter and protein powder belong in the stockpile (→ 04-18).
- **Little sugar and refined starch.** Many emergency rations are sweet or starchy. After
  gastric bypass or sleeve surgery, food can move too fast into the gut and cause **dumping
  syndrome** (NIDDK, Dumping Syndrome). Early dumping comes within 30 minutes of a meal: diarrhoea,
  bloating, nausea, cramps, light-headedness, flushing, a fast heartbeat, sweating. Late dumping
  comes 1 to 3 hours after a meal, from low blood sugar: light-headedness or fainting,
  shakiness, sweating, weakness, trouble concentrating (NIDDK, Symptoms & Causes of Dumping
  Syndrome). NIDDK's eating advice: smaller, more frequent meals, more protein, fibre and fat,
  and fewer simple sugars such as sweets and sugary drinks (NIDDK, Eating, Diet, & Nutrition for
  Dumping Syndrome).
- **Do not eat and drink at the same time:** MedlinePlus says to drink fluids at least 30
  minutes after eating, slowly, in sips (MedlinePlus, Gastric Bypass Surgery: Discharge). Sip
  all day to avoid dehydration.
- **The household's calorie plan** assumes ordinary eating (→ 04-01). For this person, plan by
  protein and small portions instead, and ask the dietitian now how much.

**Medicine after surgery.** Ask the prescriber or pharmacist now whether any of the person's
medicines should be in a different form because of the surgery (part 7). Do not crush or break
tablets unless the pharmacist says that form allows it.

**Severe obesity, day to day, off-grid:**
- **Heat:** CDC warns that heat can make heart conditions and other chronic health conditions
  worse (CDC, About Heat and Your Health). Shade, water, rest in the heat of the day, cool cloths
  (→ 17-04).
- **Skin:** skin folds stay damp and break down; wash, dry fully, and keep them aired (→ 13-02).
  Long sitting or lying causes pressure sores (MedlinePlus, Preventing Pressure Ulcers; → 02-22).
- **Sleep apnoea** is common; see → 02-24.
- **Physical work:** build up gradually; joints and heart need pacing.

**Weight-loss injections stopped:** appetite and weight return over weeks to months. There is no
known immediate danger in stopping. **Do not restart at the old dose after a gap without asking**:
the label has restart rules, and the prescriber decides.

## 5. Warning signs that mean an emergency

- **Vomiting that does not stop,** or **confusion, loss of coordination, double vision, jerky
  eye movements or a drooping eyelid**: possible thiamine (B1) shortage (Wernicke
  encephalopathy), which MedlinePlus says can progress to coma and death; get care urgently
  (MedlinePlus, Wernicke-Korsakoff Syndrome). MedlinePlus also says to contact the provider for
  vomiting after eating or for trouble drinking enough (MedlinePlus, Gastric Bypass Surgery:
  Discharge).
- **Severe belly pain after bariatric surgery,** especially with fever, a racing heart or
  vomiting: a leak, an ulcer or a twisted bowel; get care.
- **Black or bloody stool, or vomiting blood:** a bleeding ulcer.
- **Food that will not go down,** with pain and drooling: something is stuck.
- **Heat stroke:** hot skin, confusion, collapse (→ 17-04).
- **A swollen, painful calf, or sudden breathlessness and chest pain:** a blood clot; more likely
  after long sitting on a trip.

## 6. Sick-day basics

- **Dehydration comes fast** after bariatric surgery because the stomach holds little. Sip fluids
  all day; oral rehydration solution for vomiting or diarrhoea (the WHO home recipe: 1 litre safe
  water, 6 level teaspoons sugar, half a level teaspoon salt; WHO, Cholera Q&A; safety notes on
  → 12-05). Sip it slowly; the sugar can trigger dumping in some people.
- **Pain relievers:** after bypass, acetaminophen as its label says, not NSAIDs, unless the
  surgeon has said otherwise (→ 12-13).
- **Weight-loss injections** commonly cause nausea and vomiting; with vomiting or diarrhoea from
  any cause, ask the prescriber now whether to hold the next dose.

## 8. Sourcing when the pharmacy is closed

- **Vitamins and minerals** are sold over the counter everywhere and are easy to scavenge; match
  the names and forms the surgeon wrote. Chewable or liquid forms may be needed in the first
  months after surgery.
- **Someone else's prescription** is a card: {{choice DC-15}} (→ 30-16).
- **Protein:** canned meat and fish, beans, eggs, milk powder; traded food rich in protein is
  worth more to this person than bread.
- **Equipment:** medical supply stores and hospitals stock larger cuffs, chairs and commodes.

## 9. Power and devices

None for the vitamins. A refrigerated injection needs the cold ladder (→ 29-07). A CPAP, if used,
needs power (→ 02-24). An electric blood pressure cuff runs on batteries.

## 10. Travel and evacuation

- **Plan transport now:** the vehicle seat and belt, a seat-belt extender if needed, helpers, and
  for someone who cannot walk far, the carrying plan (→ 02-22, → 01-07).
- Carry vitamins, protein snacks, water and the medical summary (→ 22-06, → 25-09).
- On long rides: stand and walk at every stop, flex the calves, drink water (blood clots).
- Shelter cots may not be rated for the person's weight; bring a pad and ask for a sturdy bed.
