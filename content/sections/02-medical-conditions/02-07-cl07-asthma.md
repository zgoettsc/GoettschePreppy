# CL-07. Asthma

**What this page is for.** Asthma is common, usually well controlled, and turns dangerous fast
when the inhaler is empty, the house is full of smoke, or the air is bitter cold. Off-grid life
brings all three: wood stoves, candles, fires, dust and hard work in winter air. This module is
the attack response, how to keep inhalers working, the triggers to control, and what to settle
with the prescriber now.

{{#if has.condition.CL-07}}**Someone in your household has this condition.** This module is at the front of the medical section for you.{{else}}**Nobody in your household is marked with this condition.** The module stays in the binder so you can help someone else, or add the condition later in settings.{{/if}}

> **SAFETY**
> - **Trouble walking and talking because of breathlessness, or lips or fingernails turning blue,
>   are danger signs.** NHLBI's action plan says go to the hospital or call an ambulance now
>   (NHLBI, Asthma Action Plan, 2020). NHLBI also says call 911 if the medicines are not
>   relieving an attack or breathing is still very hard (NHLBI, Asthma Attack).
> - **Wheeze or cough after eating, in someone with a food allergy: epinephrine first, inhaler
>   after** (→ 02-03). A food reaction can look exactly like an asthma attack, and asthma makes
>   it worse.
> - **This page never suggests how many puffs.** That is the prescriber's action plan, written in
>   part 7.

## Do this now: an asthma attack

1. **Sit them upright**, leaning slightly forward. Loosen tight clothing. Stay calm and talk
   calmly.
2. **Rescue (quick-relief) inhaler now, with a spacer if there is one**, exactly as the person's
   written action plan says.
3. **Get them away from the trigger**: smoke, cold air, dust, animals.
4. **Follow the plan's next step** if they are not better in the time it gives.
5. **Danger signs** (trouble walking or talking from breathlessness, blue lips or nails, rescue
   inhaler not helping): **call for any help that exists**, keep following the plan, and be
   ready to start CPR if they stop breathing (→ 12-18).

## 1. What it is

Asthma is a long-term condition in which the airways swell, tighten and fill with mucus when
something sets them off. An attack makes breathing hard and noisy. Two kinds of medicine control
it: **quick-relief** (rescue) inhalers that open the airways within minutes, and **long-term
control** medicines, often a steroid inhaler taken every day, that calm the swelling over time
(NHLBI).

## 2. What runs out, and how fast

| What | Clock | When it is gone |
|---|---|---|
| **Rescue inhaler** | The dose counter on the inhaler. Use rises sharply in smoke, cold and illness. | No way to stop an attack. |
| **Controller inhaler or tablets** | Dose counter or days on hand. | Asthma gets worse over days to weeks; attacks become more frequent and severe. |
| Steroid tablets for flares (if prescribed) | Count them. | Bad flares last longer. |
| Spacer | Lasts years; easy to lose. | Each puff works less well. |
| Nebulizer and its power (if used) | Power every use (→ 09-06). | Back to inhaler and spacer. |

**Write the clock:** rescue inhaler doses left ________ (counter); controller doses left
________; days at the person's usual use ________. Copy it to → 23-02.

## 3. Storing the medication without power

- **Room temperature, away from heat, flame and moisture.** Never puncture an inhaler canister
  or put it in a fire (MedlinePlus, Albuterol Oral Inhalation). Do not leave inhalers in a hot
  car or near a stove.
- **Keep it at room temperature**, as its label says: in winter, the rescue inhaler rides in an
  inside pocket, not in a pack or a cold car.
- **Prime it** (spray into the air) as its leaflet says when new, or when it has not been used
  for a while.
- **Trust the dose counter.** Throw the canister away after the labelled number of puffs even if
  it still sprays (MedlinePlus, Albuterol Oral Inhalation): the last sprays carry little or no
  medicine. Read your inhaler's leaflet (→ 29-14) and write its rules here:
  ______________________________________________
- **Clean the mouthpiece exactly as its leaflet says.** Some inhalers are washed; powder
  inhalers must never get wet (MedlinePlus, Albuterol Oral Inhalation). A blocked mouthpiece
  gives no dose.

## 4. Day by day without it

- **Without the controller:** over days to weeks, the airways become more swollen and twitchy.
  More coughing at night, more wheeze, more rescue puffs, and attacks from smaller triggers.
- **Without the rescue inhaler:** an attack has to pass on its own, or not. Severe attacks can
  kill.
- **What helps:** controlling triggers (part 5), staying warm, avoiding smoke, resting during
  bad-air days, and treating colds early with rest and fluids. These do not replace medicine,
  but they reduce how much is needed.

## 5. Warning signs that mean an emergency

**NHLBI's action plan zones** (NHLBI, Asthma Action Plan, 2020), in plain words:
- **Green (doing well):** no cough, wheeze, chest tightness or shortness of breath, day or
  night; can do usual activities. Keep taking the daily medicine.
- **Yellow (getting worse):** cough, wheeze, chest tightness or shortness of breath, or waking at
  night because of asthma, or can do some but not all usual activities. Add the quick-relief
  medicine and follow the plan's yellow steps.
- **Red (medical alert):** very short of breath, quick-relief medicines have not helped, cannot
  do usual activities, or symptoms the same or worse after 24 hours in the yellow zone.
  **Danger signs:** trouble walking and talking because of shortness of breath; lips or
  fingernails blue. Hospital or ambulance now.

**Triggers that get worse off-grid**, and what to do:

| Trigger | Off-grid source | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| **Smoke** | Wood stoves, fireplaces, candles, oil lamps, cooking fires, wildfire | Keep indoor smoke to the minimum; check the stove draws properly; no smoking indoors. Indoor open flame is a household card: {{choice DC-20}} (→ 30-21). |
| **Fumes from unvented heaters** | Propane and kerosene space heaters | Ventilation as the heater's label says; the overnight trade-off is a card: {{choice DC-01}} (→ 30-02). |
| **Cold, dry air** | Unheated rooms, outdoor work in winter | Breathe through a scarf or mask; warm room for sleep (→ 08-01). |
| **Wildfire smoke** | Grass and forest fires | EPA: stay indoors with doors and windows shut, make one "clean-air room", run a portable air cleaner if there is power; a well-fitted N95 respirator reduces what you breathe outdoors (EPA, Wildfire Smoke Guide). → 17-05. |
| **Dust and mould** | Sweeping, cleanup after floods, stored hay and wood | Damp-wipe instead of sweeping; mask during cleanup; dry the house out. |
| **Colds and flu** | Crowding, poor hygiene | Handwashing; follow the plan's yellow zone early. |
| **Exertion** | Hauling water and wood | Pace the work; NHLBI says a provider may recommend the reliever medicine before exercise (NHLBI, Asthma Treatment and Action Plan); ask. |

## 6. Sick-day basics

- **Colds and chest infections are the most common trigger for bad attacks.** Start the
  action plan's yellow-zone steps at the first sign, not when breathing is already hard.
- **Fluids help keep mucus thin.** Rest. Sleep propped up if lying flat is hard.
- **Fever, green or brown mucus, chest pain, or breathlessness at rest** may mean pneumonia
  (→ 12-04).
- If the person also has a food allergy, a "cold" with hives or swelling is not a cold (→ 02-03).

## 8. Sourcing when the pharmacy is closed

- **Pharmacies, clinics, disaster medical points** while they run, with the printed
  prescription.
- **Other households' inhalers:** someone else's prescription is a card: {{choice DC-15}}
  (→ 30-16). Check the drug name: a **steroid controller is not a rescue inhaler** and will not
  stop an attack.
- **Expired inhalers:** {{choice DC-04}} (→ 30-05).
- **There is no herbal or household substitute** for a rescue inhaler. Caffeine and steam are
  sometimes suggested; they are not treatments.

## 9. Power and devices

- **Nebulizers need power.** Know how many treatments the household's power can run (→ 09-06),
  and keep inhaler and spacer as the no-power fallback.
- **Portable air cleaners need power**; a clean-air room is still worth making without one: keep
  it closed, damp-dust it, no flame in it.
- Inhalers need no power and survive an EMP.

## 10. Travel and evacuation

- Rescue inhaler **in a pocket**, never only in a pack; spare in the go-bag (→ 22-06).
- Cold air and exertion on the move: scarf over the mouth, pace the walk.
- Smoke on the route: wear the N95, keep windows shut, recirculate the vehicle's air (→ 01-07).
