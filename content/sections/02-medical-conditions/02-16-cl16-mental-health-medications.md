# CL-16. Mental-health medicines

**What this page is for.** Many people take daily medicine for depression, anxiety, bipolar
disorder, psychosis, ADHD or sleep. In a long emergency two things make these medicines a medical
issue, not just a comfort: **several of them are dangerous to stop suddenly**, and a crisis
raises stress for everyone at the moment the supply gets shaky. This module names the medicine
groups that must not be stopped abruptly, the warning signs of trouble, and the plan to get from
the prescriber now. The acute crisis (someone in danger) is on → 12-15.

{{#if has.condition.CL-16}}**Someone in your household has this condition.** This module is at the front of the medical section for you.{{else}}**Nobody in your household is marked with this condition.** The module stays in the binder so you can help someone else, or add the condition later in settings.{{/if}}

> **If anyone is in crisis and any phone or internet works: call or text 988** (988 Suicide and
> Crisis Lifeline). In a disaster, SAMHSA's **Disaster Distress Helpline is 1-800-985-5990 (call
> or text, 24/7)**. What to do while you wait, or when no one answers, is on → 12-15.

> **SAFETY**
> - **Some of these medicines must never be stopped suddenly** (part 4). Stopping them can cause
>   seizures, a dangerous rebound, or a fast return of severe illness. **The taper plan comes
>   from the prescriber while one exists** (part 7). When there is no prescriber, part 7 prints
>   each label's own stopping instruction as published; the binder never works out the steps
>   for a person.
> - **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products.** Several
>   of these medicines are dangerous to a child in small amounts (floor, → 30-01).
> - **In a crisis, medicines are lethal means too.** Lock them up and hand out one day at a time
>   if anyone is at risk (→ 12-15).
> - **This page never calculates, adjusts or suggests a dose.**

## Do this now

1. **Know which medicine group each person takes** (the table in part 4) and circle the ones
   marked "do not stop suddenly" in part 12.
2. **Count the supply** and write days on hand in part 12 and on → 23-02.
3. **If supply will run out:** follow the prescriber's written taper in part 7, or with no
   prescriber the label's stopping instruction printed there. **Do not simply stop.**
4. **Seizure** in someone who has run out of a benzodiazepine, a seizure medicine, or alcohol:
   seizure first aid (→ 02-11) and get help.
5. **Thoughts of suicide, losing touch with reality, or a person who cannot be kept safe:**
   → 12-15 now.
6. **Sudden change in behaviour:** rule out a physical cause first: low blood sugar, infection,
   dehydration, head injury, cold, or a medicine that has run out (→ 12-15).

## 1. What it is

Mental-health medicines change brain chemistry to treat illnesses such as depression, anxiety
disorders, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia and ADHD. The main groups are antidepressants,
anti-anxiety medicines, mood stabilisers, antipsychotics and stimulants (NIMH, Mental Health
Medications). Antidepressants usually take 4 to 8 weeks to work, and no prescribed medicine
should be stopped without a provider's help (NIMH, Mental Health Medications).

## 2. What runs out, and how fast

| What | Clock | When it is gone |
|---|---|---|
| Daily tablets or capsules | Days on hand from the bottle (part 7). Most keep well cool and dry. | Depends on the group (part 4): from hours (some) to weeks (most). |
| Long-acting injections (some antipsychotics) | The date of the next injection, given by a clinic. | The illness can return over weeks after a missed injection. |
| Lithium and clozapine blood tests | The clinic schedule. | No way to check levels or blood counts; watch for the signs in part 5. |
| Sleep, routine, daylight, people | Under your control. | Every mental-health condition gets harder (→ 21-04, → 21-07). |

## 3. Storing the medication without power

- **Tablets and capsules:** original bottles, tightly closed, cool and dry, out of sun, **locked**
  and out of children's reach. Not in a bathroom, a car or a hot attic.
- **Liquid forms and dissolving tablets:** the label's rules; keep dissolving tablets in their
  blister until use, away from moisture (→ 29-14).
- **Long-acting injections** are usually kept by the clinic; if one is kept at home, the label's
  storage rule applies (→ 29-07).
- **Expired medicine** when nothing else exists is a household choice: {{choice DC-04}}
  (→ 30-05).

## 4. Day by day without it

**Medicines that are dangerous to stop suddenly.** These groups need the prescriber's taper plan
(part 7). The table names each group, examples of names you will see on bottles, and what FDA or
MedlinePlus (the National Library of Medicine's drug pages) says happens when it is stopped
suddenly. The leaflet for each medicine says the same in its own words; read it.

| Group | Examples on the label | What sudden stopping can cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Benzodiazepines** (for anxiety, panic, sleep, seizures) | alprazolam, clonazepam, diazepam, lorazepam | FDA: stopping suddenly or reducing the dose too quickly can cause serious withdrawal reactions, **including seizures, which can be life-threatening** (FDA, Benzodiazepine Boxed Warning, 2020). |
| **Seizure medicines used for mood, pain or anxiety** | valproate (valproic acid, divalproex) | MedlinePlus: stopping suddenly may cause a **severe, long-lasting and possibly life-threatening seizure** (MedlinePlus, Valproic Acid). |
| | carbamazepine | In someone with a seizure disorder, seizures may get worse (MedlinePlus, Carbamazepine). |
| | lamotrigine | Seizures. If it is stopped for any reason, **do not restart it without the prescriber**: a starting dose that is too high raises the risk of a serious rash (MedlinePlus, Lamotrigine). See → 02-11. |
| | gabapentin, pregabalin | Withdrawal: trouble sleeping, nausea, anxiety, pain, sweating, headache; seizures (MedlinePlus, Gabapentin; MedlinePlus, Pregabalin). |
| **Beta-blockers** (for anxiety, tremor, heart) | propranolol, metoprolol | Serious heart problems (MedlinePlus, Propranolol; MedlinePlus, Metoprolol). See → 02-09. |
| **Clonidine and guanfacine** (for ADHD, sleep, blood pressure) | clonidine, guanfacine | Clonidine: a rapid rise in blood pressure, with fast heartbeat, nervousness, headache, chest tightness and shaking (MedlinePlus, Clonidine). Guanfacine: blood pressure may rise (MedlinePlus, Guanfacine). See → 02-09. |
| **SSRI and SNRI antidepressants** | sertraline, paroxetine, fluoxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, venlafaxine, duloxetine | For sertraline, MedlinePlus lists withdrawal symptoms: nausea, sweating, depression, mood changes, abnormally excited mood, irritability, anxiety, confusion, dizziness, headache, tiredness, **seizures**, ringing in the ears, numbness or tingling, trouble sleeping (MedlinePlus, Sertraline). Check the leaflet for the others. |
| **Lithium** (for bipolar disorder) | lithium carbonate, lithium citrate | Lithium treats and prevents mania. MedlinePlus: do not stop it without talking to the doctor (MedlinePlus, Lithium). |
| **Clozapine** (for schizophrenia) | clozapine | MedlinePlus: do not stop it without the doctor, who will probably lower the dose gradually. **If more than 2 days are missed, call the doctor before taking any more** (MedlinePlus, Clozapine). |

**Every other psychiatric medicine:** NIMH says no prescribed medicine should be stopped without
a health care provider's help, because stopping too soon may cause unpleasant or harmful effects
(NIMH, Mental Health Medications). Read each leaflet and ask the prescriber.

**Alcohol** is not a medicine, but sudden withdrawal in a heavy drinker can kill. MedlinePlus
says symptoms tend to occur within 8 hours after the last drink but can start days later, tend to
peak by 24 to 72 hours, and may go on for weeks. The most severe form (delirium tremens) brings
sudden severe confusion, fever, hallucinations and seizures, and death is possible; seizures,
fever, severe confusion, hallucinations or an irregular heartbeat need emergency care
(MedlinePlus, Alcohol Withdrawal). If someone in the house depends on alcohol, plan for it now
(→ 12-15, → 12-16).

**What helps, and is in your control** (and helps everyone, medicine or not): a daily routine,
sleep protected like a supply (→ 21-07), daylight and physical work, regular meals, contact with
people, a job to do, and less alcohol (→ 21-01, → 21-02, → 21-04).

## 5. Warning signs that mean an emergency

- **Suicidal thoughts, talk of being a burden, a sudden calm after despair:** → 12-15 now.
- **Mania:** days of little sleep without tiredness, racing speech, risky behaviour, grand ideas.
- **Psychosis:** hearing or seeing things others do not, fixed false beliefs, severe confusion.
  Keep them and others safe, keep calm, reduce noise and demands, and get help.
- **Withdrawal seizure or delirium** after running out of a benzodiazepine or alcohol: seizure
  first aid (→ 02-11), on their side, nothing by mouth, get care.
- **Lithium toxicity:** MedlinePlus lists drowsiness, shaking that cannot be controlled, muscle
  weakness, stiffness or twitching, loss of coordination, diarrhoea, vomiting, slurred speech,
  giddiness, ringing in the ears and blurred vision, and says that with any of these, **stop
  taking lithium and call the doctor immediately** (MedlinePlus, Lithium). With no doctor
  reachable, the first half still applies: stop the lithium, give fluids by sips if they can
  swallow, and get any care that exists.
- **Clozapine danger signs:** fever, sore throat, chills or other signs of infection (clozapine
  can lower the white blood count); constipation, a swollen or painful belly, or vomiting; chest
  pain or a fast, irregular or pounding heartbeat; a seizure (MedlinePlus, Clozapine). Get care.
- **Overdose,** accidental or not: → 12-17 and poison control if any phone works.

## 6. Sick-day basics

- **Vomiting and diarrhoea** can mean doses are lost. For lithium, MedlinePlus says to tell the
  doctor about severe diarrhoea, heavy sweating or fever, and to keep to the right amounts of
  fluid and salt (MedlinePlus, Lithium). Fluids by small sips (→ 12-05) and ask the prescriber
  now what to do. With no doctor: keep up fluids and normal salt, and watch for the toxicity
  signs in part 5; no source we read gives a household a lithium plan for illness.
- **Heat and heavy sweating:** CDC says many medicines can make people dehydrated or overheated
  on hot days, and not to stop or change medicines without talking to the doctor (CDC, About
  Heat and Your Health). For lithium, heavy sweating is one of the warnings above. Shade, water,
  rest (→ 17-04).
- **New medicines:** many common pain relievers, cold medicines and antibiotics interact with
  psychiatric medicines. Ask the pharmacist now which over-the-counter medicines are safe for
  this person, and write them in part 7.
- **Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)**, an older antidepressant group (NIMH, Mental Health
  Medications), react with foods high in tyramine, a substance that builds up in aged and
  fermented food. For phenelzine, MedlinePlus names meat,
  poultry, fish or cheese that has been smoked, aged, badly stored or spoiled, some fruits,
  vegetables and beans, alcohol, and fermented yeast products (MedlinePlus, Phenelzine). Check
  the leaflet before eating home-cured, smoked or fermented food (→ 04-07, → 04-08).

## 7. What to stock, and how much

**Ask your prescriber now, while you can, and write it here.** Why it matters: a prescriber's
taper lets the body adjust step by step. Without it, a household that runs out stops at full
strength all at once, which is exactly the situation that causes withdrawal seizures,
rebound blood pressure, or a fast return of mania or psychosis. If supply is going to run out
anyway, a planned step-down is safer than a cliff, **but only the prescriber can write the
steps.**

**When there is no prescriber.** No source gives a household a taper schedule. Each label says
how a doctor stops the medicine, and that is the published guidance, as published:
- **Alprazolam** (Xanax label): "When tapering, decrease dosage by no more than 0.5 mg every 3
  days. Some patients may require an even slower dosage reduction." The label adds that if
  withdrawal reactions develop, the taper can be paused or the dose raised back to the previous
  step, then lowered more slowly.
- **Clonazepam** (Klonopin label, written for panic disorder): "Treatment should be discontinued
  gradually, with a decrease of 0.125 mg twice daily every 3 days, until the drug is completely
  withdrawn."
- **Other benzodiazepines** (lorazepam, diazepam and others): the Xanax and Klonopin boxed
  warnings say "use a gradual taper", and FDA required that boxed warning on the whole group in
  2020 (FDA, Benzodiazepine Boxed Warning); read the label in the box for its own line.
- **Sertraline** (Zoloft label): "Gradually reduce the dosage rather than stopping ZOLOFT
  abruptly whenever possible."
- **Venlafaxine** (Effexor XR label): "A gradual reduction in the dose, rather than abrupt
  cessation, is recommended. If intolerable symptoms occur following a decrease in the dose or
  upon discontinuation of treatment, then resuming the previously prescribed dose may be
  considered." In its studies, "tapering was achieved by reducing the daily dose by 75 mg at
  one-week intervals."
- **Other SSRIs and SNRIs:** the label or Medication Guide in the box.
- **Gabapentin, pregabalin, lamotrigine:** the label lines on → 02-11 part 7.
  **Beta-blockers and clonidine:** the label lines on → 02-09 part 4.
- **Lithium, clozapine, antipsychotics and anything else:** no label we read gives a stopping
  schedule a household could follow. Take the full dose while it lasts, watch the person
  closely for the relapse signs written above and for the danger signs in part 5, and plan
  for the crisis page (→ 12-15). **Clozapine:** MedlinePlus says to call the doctor before
  taking any more after more than 2 days missed; with no doctor, do not restart it on your own.

**What this binder recommends when no clinician exists:** if the supply will certainly run out,
do not take full doses to the last day and then stop. Spread the reduction over at least the
period the label gives, so it ends when the supply does. The binder does not work out the steps
for a person. **A missed dose:** follow the leaflet in the box; where it says "ask your doctor"
and there is none, take the next dose at the usual time and do not double it.

## 8. Sourcing when the pharmacy is closed

- **Pharmacies, clinics and disaster medical points** while they run, with the printed
  prescription.
- **Someone else's prescription** is a card: {{choice DC-15}} (→ 30-16). Many of these medicines
  are common, but switching between them, or mixing them, can cause serious reactions. Same drug,
  same strength only, and the prescriber's agreement if any can be reached.
- **Controlled medicines** (benzodiazepines, stimulants) are high-value for theft and barter.
  Keep them locked and do not advertise them.
- **There is no safe over-the-counter or herbal substitute.** NIMH warns that St. John's wort,
  sold for mood, can cause a rare but life-threatening reaction (serotonin syndrome) when
  combined with antidepressants (NIMH, Mental Health Medications).

## 9. Power and devices

None for the medicines. A light box (if the person uses one for winter depression) needs power;
daylight outdoors is the free version (→ 21-02).

## 10. Travel and evacuation

- Medicines on the person, locked if possible, out of heat (→ 01-07, → 22-06).
- The written step-down plan and the crisis numbers on paper (→ 25-03, → 25-09).
- Travel breaks routine and sleep. Keep medicine times as regular as the road allows.
