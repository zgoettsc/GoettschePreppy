# CL-16. Mental-health medicines

**What this page is for.** Many people take daily medicine for depression, anxiety, bipolar
disorder, psychosis, ADHD or sleep. In a long emergency two things make these medicines a medical
issue, not just a comfort: **several of them are dangerous to stop suddenly**, and a crisis
raises stress for everyone at the moment the supply gets shaky. This module names the medicine
groups that must not be stopped abruptly, the warning signs of trouble, and the plan to get from
the prescriber now. The acute crisis (someone in danger) is on → 12-15.

{{#if has.condition.CL-16}}**Someone in your household has this condition.** This module is at the front of the medical section for you.{{else}}**Nobody in your household is marked with this condition.** The module stays in the binder so you can help someone else, or add the condition later in settings.{{/if}}

> **If anyone is in crisis and any phone or internet works: call or text 988** (988 Suicide and
> Crisis Lifeline). In a disaster, SAMHSA's **Disaster Distress Helpline is 1-800-985-5990 (call
> or text, 24/7)**. What to do while you wait, or when no one answers, is on → 12-15.

> **SAFETY**
> - **Some of these medicines must never be stopped suddenly** (part 4). Stopping them can cause
>   seizures, a dangerous rebound, or a fast return of severe illness. **The taper plan must come
>   from the prescriber** (part 7). This binder never writes one.
> - **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products.** Several
>   of these medicines are dangerous to a child in small amounts (floor, → 30-01).
> - **In a crisis, medicines are lethal means too.** Lock them up and hand out one day at a time
>   if anyone is at risk (→ 12-15).
> - **This page never calculates, adjusts or suggests a dose.**

## Do this now

1. **Know which medicine group each person takes** (the table in part 4) and circle the ones
   marked "do not stop suddenly" in part 12.
2. **Count the supply** and write days on hand in part 12 and on → 23-02.
3. **If supply will run out:** follow the prescriber's written taper in part 7. **Do not simply
   stop**, and do not invent a taper.
4. **Seizure** in someone who has run out of a benzodiazepine, a seizure medicine, or alcohol:
   seizure first aid (→ 02-11) and get help.
5. **Thoughts of suicide, losing touch with reality, or a person who cannot be kept safe:**
   → 12-15 now.
6. **Sudden change in behaviour:** rule out a physical cause first: low blood sugar, infection,
   dehydration, head injury, cold, or a medicine that has run out (→ 12-15).

## 1. What it is

Mental-health medicines change brain chemistry to treat illnesses such as depression, anxiety
disorders, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia and ADHD. The main groups are antidepressants,
anti-anxiety medicines, mood stabilisers, antipsychotics and stimulants (NIMH, Mental Health
Medications). Most take weeks to work, and most should be stopped only with a prescriber's plan.

## 2. What runs out, and how fast

| What | Clock | When it is gone |
|---|---|---|
| Daily tablets or capsules | Days on hand from the bottle (part 7). Most keep well cool and dry. | Depends on the group (part 4): from hours (some) to weeks (most). |
| Long-acting injections (some antipsychotics) | The date of the next injection, given by a clinic. | The illness can return over weeks after a missed injection. |
| Lithium and clozapine blood tests | The clinic schedule. | No way to check levels or blood counts; watch for the signs in part 5. |
| Sleep, routine, daylight, people | Under your control. | Every mental-health condition gets harder (→ 21-04, → 21-07). |

## 3. Storing the medication without power

- **Tablets and capsules:** original bottles, tightly closed, cool and dry, out of sun, **locked**
  and out of children's reach. Not in a bathroom, a car or a hot attic.
- **Liquid forms and dissolving tablets:** the label's rules; keep dissolving tablets in their
  blister until use, away from moisture (→ 29-14).
- **Long-acting injections** are usually kept by the clinic; if one is kept at home, the label's
  storage rule applies (→ 29-07).
- **Expired medicine** when nothing else exists is a household choice: {{choice DC-04}}
  (→ 30-05).

## 4. Day by day without it

**Medicines that are dangerous to stop suddenly.** These groups need the prescriber's taper plan
(part 7). The table names each group, examples of names you will see on bottles, and what public
sources say happens when it is stopped abruptly.

| Group | Examples on the label | What sudden stopping can cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Benzodiazepines** (for anxiety, panic, sleep, seizures) | alprazolam, clonazepam, diazepam, lorazepam | FDA requires a boxed warning: stopping suddenly or cutting the dose quickly can cause **life-threatening withdrawal, including seizures** (FDA, Benzodiazepine Boxed Warning, 2020). |
| **Seizure medicines used for mood, pain or anxiety** | lamotrigine, valproate (divalproex), carbamazepine, gabapentin, pregabalin | Seizures, even in people who never had one; mood relapse. **Lamotrigine** must not be restarted at the old dose after a gap without the prescriber, because of a serious rash risk (MedlinePlus, Lamotrigine). See → 02-11. |
| **Beta-blockers** (for anxiety, tremor, heart) | propranolol, metoprolol | Chest pain or a heart attack in people with heart disease (MedlinePlus, Metoprolol). See → 02-09. |
| **Clonidine and guanfacine** (for ADHD, sleep, blood pressure) | clonidine, guanfacine | A rapid rise in blood pressure (MedlinePlus, Clonidine). See → 02-09. |
| **SSRI and SNRI antidepressants** | sertraline, paroxetine, fluoxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, venlafaxine, duloxetine | A discontinuation reaction: dizziness, irritability, agitation, anxiety, confusion, headache, tiredness, trouble sleeping, numbness or tingling (MedlinePlus, Sertraline); some people describe brief electric-shock feelings. Usually not dangerous in itself, but miserable, and depression can return. |
| **Lithium** (for bipolar disorder) | lithium carbonate, lithium citrate | Mania or depression can return; do not stop without the prescriber (MedlinePlus, Lithium). |
| **Clozapine** (for schizophrenia) | clozapine | Psychosis can return quickly. After a gap of 2 days or more, it must not be restarted at the old dose without the prescriber, who will restart it slowly (MedlinePlus, Clozapine). |

**Medicines where stopping is less dangerous in itself** (but the illness can return): other
antidepressants, most antipsychotics, ADHD stimulants (tiredness, low mood and hunger for some
days), and sleep aids (poor sleep for some nights). Even these: ask the prescriber.

**Alcohol** is not a medicine, but sudden withdrawal in a heavy drinker can kill. MedlinePlus
says symptoms usually begin within 8 hours of the last drink, can peak at 24 to 72 hours, and
the most severe form (delirium tremens) brings confusion, fever, hallucinations and seizures
(MedlinePlus, Alcohol Withdrawal). If someone in the house depends on alcohol, plan for it now
(→ 12-15, → 12-16).

**What helps, and is in your control** (and helps everyone, medicine or not): a daily routine,
sleep protected like a supply (→ 21-07), daylight and physical work, regular meals, contact with
people, a job to do, and less alcohol (→ 21-01, → 21-02, → 21-04).

## 5. Warning signs that mean an emergency

- **Suicidal thoughts, talk of being a burden, a sudden calm after despair:** → 12-15 now.
- **Mania:** days of little sleep without tiredness, racing speech, risky behaviour, grand ideas.
- **Psychosis:** hearing or seeing things others do not, fixed false beliefs, severe confusion.
  Keep them and others safe, keep calm, reduce noise and demands, and get help.
- **Withdrawal seizure or delirium** after running out of a benzodiazepine or alcohol: seizure
  first aid (→ 02-11), on their side, nothing by mouth, get care.
- **Lithium toxicity:** MedlinePlus lists diarrhoea, vomiting, drowsiness, muscle weakness,
  shakiness and loss of coordination as signs to act on (MedlinePlus, Lithium). Stop no
  medicine on your own; get care, and give fluids if the prescriber's plan says so.
- **Clozapine danger signs:** fever, sore throat, chills or other signs of infection (a low white
  blood count); severe constipation or belly pain; chest pain or a racing heart (MedlinePlus,
  Clozapine). Get care.
- **Overdose,** accidental or not: → 12-17 and poison control if any phone works.

## 6. Sick-day basics

- **Vomiting and diarrhoea** can mean doses are lost, and for some medicines (lithium above all)
  dehydration raises the level in the blood to a dangerous one (MedlinePlus, Lithium). Fluids by
  small sips (→ 12-05) and ask the prescriber now what to do.
- **Heat and heavy sweating:** some psychiatric medicines make heat illness more likely (CDC,
  About Heat and Your Health); lithium levels rise with sweating and dehydration. Shade, water,
  rest (→ 17-04).
- **New medicines:** many common pain relievers, cold medicines and antibiotics interact with
  psychiatric medicines. Ask the pharmacist now which over-the-counter medicines are safe for
  this person, and write them in part 7.
- **Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)**, an older antidepressant group, react with some foods
  that are aged, fermented or cured (NIMH, Mental Health Medications). Check before eating
  home-cured, smoked or fermented food (→ 04-07, → 04-08).

## 7. What to stock, and how much

**Ask your prescriber now, while you can, and write it here.** Why it matters: a prescriber's
taper lets the body adjust step by step. Without it, a household that runs out stops at full
strength all at once, which is exactly the situation that causes withdrawal seizures,
rebound blood pressure, or a fast return of mania or psychosis. If supply is going to run out
anyway, a planned step-down is safer than a cliff, **but only the prescriber can write the
steps.**

## 8. Sourcing when the pharmacy is closed

- **Pharmacies, clinics and disaster medical points** while they run, with the printed
  prescription.
- **Someone else's prescription** is a card: {{choice DC-15}} (→ 30-16). Many of these medicines
  are common, but switching between them, or mixing them, can cause serious reactions. Same drug,
  same strength only, and the prescriber's agreement if any can be reached.
- **Controlled medicines** (benzodiazepines, stimulants) are high-value for theft and barter.
  Keep them locked and do not advertise them.
- **There is no safe over-the-counter or herbal substitute.** Herbal products sold for mood can
  interact dangerously with prescribed antidepressants.

## 9. Power and devices

None for the medicines. A light box (if the person uses one for winter depression) needs power;
daylight outdoors is the free version (→ 21-02).

## 10. Travel and evacuation

- Medicines on the person, locked if possible, out of heat (→ 01-07, → 22-06).
- The written step-down plan and the crisis numbers on paper (→ 25-03, → 25-09).
- Travel breaks routine and sleep. Keep medicine times as regular as the road allows.
