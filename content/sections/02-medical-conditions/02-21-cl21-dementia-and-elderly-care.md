# CL-21. Dementia and elderly care

**What this page is for.** Older people, and anyone with dementia (memory and judgement failing
because of a brain disease such as Alzheimer's), are the most likely to be hurt by an emergency
that goes on for weeks. Not because of one medicine running out, but because of what changes
around them: the house is cold and dark, routines break, someone else must manage their
medicines, and a fall or a walk out of the door in winter can kill. This module covers wandering,
falls, warmth, medicine management, sudden confusion, and the caregiver who has to keep going.

{{#if has.condition.CL-21}}**Someone in your household has this condition.** This module is at the front of the medical section for you.{{else}}{{#if has.elder}}**Your household includes an older member.** Parts on falls, warmth, sudden confusion and medicines apply to them even without dementia.{{else}}**Nobody in your household is marked with this condition.** The module stays in the binder so you can help an older neighbour or relative, or add the condition later in settings.{{/if}}{{/if}}

> **SAFETY**
> - **A person with dementia who walks out may not know where they are or that they are in
>   danger, and in cold or heat that can kill.** Plan for wandering before it happens (part 4;
>   NIA, Wandering and Getting Lost).
> - **Sudden worse confusion is a medical sign, not "just the dementia"** (part 5).
> - **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products.** The same
>   locks protect a confused adult: firearms, fuel, medicines, chlorine and car keys (floor,
>   → 30-01).
> - **This page never calculates, adjusts or suggests a dose.**

## Do this now

**They are missing:**
1. **Search at once,** starting close: the house, the yard, sheds, cars, the route they used to
   walk, places from their past.
2. **Call 911** if any phone works; give the photo (part 4; NIA keeps a recent photo to help the
   police) and what they are wearing. Ask neighbours to look.
3. **Cold or heat:** treat finding them as urgent; warm or cool them once found (→ 08-05,
   → 17-04).

**Sudden worse confusion, drowsiness or agitation (hours to a day or two):**
1. **Look for the cause:** fever or infection (often the bladder or chest), dehydration, low
   blood sugar, pain, constipation, a fall or head injury, a new or missed medicine, cold (part 5).
2. **Fluids, warmth, light by day, quiet by night, a familiar face.**
3. **Get care** if any exists.

**A fall:** do not pull them up straight away. Check for pain, a head injury and a broken hip
(cannot stand, leg turned out or shorter) (→ 12-09, → 12-19). Keep them warm on the floor while
you decide.

## 1. What it is

**Dementia** is a loss of memory, thinking and judgement that gets worse over time and makes
daily life harder. Alzheimer's disease is the most common cause. **Frailty** in old age means
less strength, balance and reserve: an illness, a cold night or a missed meal hits harder and
recovery is slower. Older adults also lose body heat faster and may not notice they are cold
(NIA, Cold Weather Safety for Older Adults), and they are at higher risk in heat (CDC, About Heat
and Your Health).

## 2. What runs out, and how fast

| What | Clock | When it is gone |

## 3. Storing the medication without power

- **One adult manages all of the person's medicines.** Original bottles, **locked**, cool and dry.
- **A weekly pill organiser** filled by that adult, with the bottles kept locked. A confused
  person may take a day's tablets twice, or none.
- **Write each dose on a chart as it is given** (→ 25-05). It prevents double doses when
  caregivers change.
- **Expired medicine** when nothing else exists: {{choice DC-04}} (→ 30-05).

## 4. Day by day without it

**Wandering:** many people with Alzheimer's disease wander away from home or their caregiver
(NIA, Wandering and Getting Lost). A crisis brings strange surroundings, noise, darkness and
people coming and going. To prepare (NIA):
- **Secure the doors:** keep them locked, with an extra lock placed up high or down low; STOP or
  CLOSED signs on doors; a chime or alarm that sounds when a door opens (with no power, a bell or
  a tin can on the handle does the same job). Keep the key nearby for emergencies.
- **ID on the person:** an ID or medical bracelet with name, address and your phone number; if
  they might take it off, label their clothes.
- **A recent photo** in the binder, below, to help searchers.
- **Tell neighbours** that the person tends to wander.
- **Know their patterns:** time of day, places they head for.

{{photo recent-photo-for-search}}

**Falls** (CDC, Check for Safety, 2017): clear floors and stairs of clutter; coil or tape cords
against the wall; remove loose rugs or fix them with non-slip backing; light at the top and
bottom of the stairs and a night-light on the path from bed to bathroom; handrails on both sides
of the stairs; grab bars by the toilet and tub and a non-slip mat in the tub; often-used things
on low shelves; a sturdy step stool with a bar, never a chair. **Off-grid adds new hazards:** stacked supplies
in hallways, dark nights, wet and icy steps, a bucket toilet that is hard to rise from (→ 13-01).
A battery night-light or a headlamp by the bed matters.

**Warmth.** NIA advises setting the heat to at least 68 °F, and warns that even mildly cool homes
of 60 to 65 °F can lead to hypothermia in older adults (NIA, Cold Weather Safety for Older
Adults). With no heat, keep the person in the one warm room (→ 08-01),
in layers, a hat indoors, warm drinks, and blankets in bed. Check their hands and face often: they
may not say they are cold.

**Fire.** A confused person and an open flame are a dangerous pair. US Fire Administration
advises flashlights rather than candles in an outage (USFA, Candle Fire Safety). Open flame
indoors is a card: {{choice DC-20}} (→ 30-21). Keep heaters, stoves and lighters out of reach.

**Routine and calm** (→ 21-04): the same times for waking, meals, medicine and bed; daylight in
the morning; a familiar task (folding, sorting, shelling beans); one person speaking at a time.

**Eating and drinking:** thirst fades with age. Offer drinks on a schedule. Soft food if chewing
or swallowing is hard; sit them upright to eat (→ 12-18 for choking).

## 5. Warning signs that mean an emergency

- **Delirium:** confusion that comes on over hours or days, with drowsiness or agitation, trouble
  paying attention, seeing things. MedlinePlus lists causes including infections (such as a
  urine infection or pneumonia), medicines such as pain medicines and sedatives, alcohol or
  medicine withdrawal, upset body salts, and severe lack of sleep (MedlinePlus, Delirium). Find
  and treat the cause.
- **Hypothermia:** NIA's early signs are cold feet and hands, a puffy or swollen face, pale skin,
  shivering, slow or slurred speech, and feeling sleepy, angry or confused; later, stiff jerky
  movements, slow heartbeat or breathing and loss of consciousness (NIA, Cold Weather Safety for
  Older Adults; CDC, Preventing Hypothermia). Move them somewhere warmer, warm drink if awake,
  dry blankets (NIA); → 08-05.
- **Heat illness:** hot dry or damp skin, confusion, fast pulse, fainting (→ 17-04).
- **A fall with a head strike**, especially on a blood thinner (→ 12-19, → 02-10).
- **Not eating or drinking for a day**, few or no wet pads.
- **Choking, coughing at every meal, chest infection** (→ 12-18).

## 6. Sick-day basics

- **Infections often show as confusion,** not fever. A bladder infection may show only as new
  incontinence or agitation (→ 12-04).
- **Dehydration** comes fast and worsens confusion; fluids by small sips (→ 12-05).
- **Constipation** causes pain and agitation; water, fibre, movement.
- **Antibiotics without a prescription** are a card: {{choice DC-16}} (→ 30-17).
- **Cold medicines, sleep aids and allergy pills that cause drowsiness** can make confusion and
  falls worse in older people. Ask the pharmacist which are safe.

  short? __________________________________________________________________________________
- Which medicines must never stop suddenly? __________________________________________
- What to do about agitation or sleeplessness if it gets severe: _______________________
- Safe over-the-counter medicines for pain, sleep and colds: _____________________________
Prescriber: ____________________ Date: __________

**When there is no prescriber.** No source we read tells a household which of an older
person's medicines can be stopped safely, so the binder does not choose: keep giving each one
as labelled while it lasts, and use the condition page for each medicine group (for example
→ 02-09, → 02-10, → 02-16) for what its label says about stopping. For over-the-counter
products, read the Drug Facts label and leave out any whose "Ask a doctor before use" or "When
using this product" lines name drowsiness, confusion or the person's conditions (→ 29-14);
with nobody to ask, those lines are the answer. Severe agitation: first look for a physical cause (part 5), then
"Routine and calm" in part 4 and → 12-15, not a sleep aid.

## 8. Sourcing when the pharmacy is closed

- **Pharmacies, clinics and disaster medical points** while they run.
- **Someone else's prescription** is a card: {{choice DC-15}} (→ 30-16). Older people are more
  sensitive to medicines; be more cautious, not less.
- **Help:** neighbours, church groups, home-care workers who live nearby. One extra pair of hands
  for a few hours a day keeps the caregiver going.

## 9. Power and devices

Battery lights, hearing aids and any powered bed or lift chair need charging or batteries
(→ 09-06, → 02-23). A powered bed or lift without power: know how to lower it by hand. Oxygen:
→ 02-08.

## 10. Travel and evacuation

- **Plan the transport and the helpers now.** Ready.gov advises older adults to plan
  transportation if they need help evacuating, and to build a support network that practises
  the plan together (Ready.gov, Older Adults). Leave early and by day if you can: a rushed night
  move is harder on a person with dementia.
- Medicines, the chart, glasses, hearing aids, pads, a change of clothes, a familiar object, and
  the medical summary (→ 01-07, → 22-06, → 25-09).
- **Never leave them alone** in a car, a shelter or a crowd; ID on them at all times.
