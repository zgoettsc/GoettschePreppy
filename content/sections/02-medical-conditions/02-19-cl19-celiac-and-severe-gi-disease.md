# CL-19. Celiac and severe gut disease

**What this page is for.** This module covers two kinds of gut disease that make an emergency
harder. **Celiac disease** is controlled by food alone: no gluten, ever. In a crisis the food
that arrives (relief rations, traded bread, scavenged boxes, a neighbour's stew) is mostly wheat.
**Inflammatory bowel disease** (Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis) and living with an
ostomy depend on medicines and supplies, and flares bring diarrhoea and dehydration. Both need a
food plan made now and a clear eye for the signs of trouble.

> **SAFETY**
> - **Dehydration is the danger that kills** in severe diarrhoea. Start oral rehydration early
>   (part 6).
> - **Long-term steroids for gut disease must not be stopped suddenly** (MedlinePlus,
>   Prednisone; → 02-20).
> - **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products.** (floor,
>   → 30-01).
> - **This page never calculates, adjusts or suggests a dose.**

## Do this now

1. **Severe diarrhoea or vomiting:** start oral rehydration solution at once, small sips often
   (part 6, → 12-05).
2. **Blood in the stool with fever, severe belly pain, a swollen hard belly, or vomiting with no
   stool or gas passing:** get care if any exists (part 5).
3. **Celiac:** keep a sealed, labelled gluten-free shelf and separate cooking tools (part 4).
   When food is shared, ask what is in it every time.
4. **Count medicines and ostomy supplies**; write days on hand in part 12 and on → 23-02.

## 1. What it is

**Celiac disease** is an immune disease in which eating **gluten**, a protein in wheat, barley and
rye, damages the lining of the small intestine. The only treatment is a gluten-free diet for life
(NIDDK, Celiac Disease). **Crohn's disease** and **ulcerative colitis** are long-term
inflammation of the gut that comes and goes in flares: diarrhoea, pain, bleeding, weight loss
(NIDDK, Crohn's Disease; NIDDK, Ulcerative Colitis). They are treated with medicines that calm
the inflammation, sometimes steroids or immune-suppressing medicines, and sometimes surgery that
leaves an **ostomy** (an opening on the belly where stool leaves into a bag).

## 3. Storing the medication without power

- **Tablets:** original bottles, cool and dry, out of sun.
- **Refrigerated injections:** each label's rule; the cold ladder is on → 29-07. After a heat
  excursion: {{choice DC-08}} (→ 30-09).
- **Ostomy supplies:** dry, out of heat and direct sun; adhesive wafers fail when stored hot.
- **Gluten-free food:** sealed, labelled, on a shelf above wheat foods so flour cannot fall into
  it (→ 04-03).

## 4. Day by day without it

**Celiac with gluten getting into the food.** Symptoms differ widely: diarrhoea, bloating, gas,
belly pain, nausea or vomiting, tiredness, joint or bone pain, an itchy blistering rash
(dermatitis herpetiformis), headaches; in children, weight loss and slow growth. Some people have
no symptoms at all (NIDDK, Symptoms & Causes of Celiac Disease). Gluten damages the small
intestine even so, and over time celiac disease puts people at risk of nutritional deficiencies,
weak bones (osteoporosis) and slow growth (FDA, Gluten-Free Labeling of Foods). It is rarely an
emergency on the day; it is a slow drain on someone who needs every calorie.

**Keeping the food gluten-free off-grid:**
- **Naturally gluten-free staples to stock:** NIDDK names meat, fish, fruits, vegetables, rice
  and potatoes without additives, and flours made from potatoes, rice, corn, soy, nuts, cassava,
  amaranth, quinoa, buckwheat or beans (NIDDK, Eating, Diet, & Nutrition for Celiac Disease).
  Plain beans and lentils keep for years (→ 04-02).
- **Read every label.** NIDDK says foods labelled "gluten-free" in the US must contain less than
  20 parts per million of gluten (NIDDK, Eating, Diet, & Nutrition for Celiac Disease). Oats:
  ask the doctor or dietitian whether to eat them at all, and if so use oats labelled gluten-free
  only, because ordinary oats pick up gluten from other grains (NIDDK).
- **Hidden gluten:** NIDDK lists malt, malt extract, malt vinegar, brewer's yeast and beer, and
  gluten in baked goods, baking mixes, breads, cereals and pastas (NIDDK). Many sauces, soup
  mixes, seasonings and ration meals contain wheat too. If the label is gone, treat it as unsafe.
- **Cross-contact:** NIDDK says gluten-free food is unsafe once it touches food or products that
  contain gluten, and advises storing and preparing it separately (NIDDK). A shared toaster,
  flour sifter, grain mill, wooden board, colander or pot of pasta water is enough. Keep a
  separate set (→ 13-10). Flour dust settles on everything nearby.
- **Shared and traded food:** ask what is in it and how it was cooked. The allergy card
  (→ 30-19) sets out the same kind of trade-off for allergies, and its reasoning applies here.
  Questionable food more generally is a card: {{choice DC-14}} (→ 30-15).

**Crohn's or colitis without medicine.** The medicines aim to calm the inflammation and prevent
flares (NIDDK, Crohn's Disease; NIDDK, Ulcerative Colitis). Without them, flares become more
likely: diarrhoea, cramping and belly pain, blood in the stool (colitis), weight loss (NIDDK).
Some people find certain foods worse during a flare and keep a list.

**Ostomy without supplies.** Leaks and sore skin first; then broken skin that can become
infected. Ask the ostomy nurse now how to make a bag last and how to improvise in an emergency,
and write it in part 7.

## 5. Warning signs that mean an emergency

- **Dehydration:** very little urine, dark urine, dizziness on standing, dry mouth, confusion,
  fast heartbeat; in a child, no tears and few wet diapers (→ 12-05).
- **Blood in the stool** that is heavy, or with fever and fast heartbeat.
- **Severe belly pain, a swollen hard belly, vomiting, and no stool or gas passing:** a blocked
  bowel. Nothing by mouth; get care.
- **High fever with a painful, swollen belly** in someone with colitis.
- **A stoma that turns purple, black or white, bleeds more than usual, swells or pulls in, or
  output that is more watery than usual with signs of dehydration** (MedlinePlus, Ileostomy:
  Caring for Your Stoma). MedlinePlus says people with an ileostomy get dehydrated more easily
  and should use oral rehydration solution when output is high. **No output for many hours
  with cramps, swelling or vomiting** can be a blockage: get care.

## 6. Sick-day basics

- **Oral rehydration solution** (ORS) replaces water and salts lost in diarrhoea. The WHO home
  recipe: **1 litre of safe water, 6 level teaspoons of sugar, half a level teaspoon of salt**
  (WHO, Cholera Q&A). Measure carefully; safety notes on → 12-05. Packaged ORS sachets are better
  when you have them (CDC Yellow Book, Travelers' Diarrhea).
- **Keep eating** simple food as soon as it stays down.
- **Diarrhoea medicines:** only as their labels say, and **not** during a colitis flare with fever
  or blood unless the prescriber says so. Ask now and write it in part 7.
- **Antibiotics** for a gut infection without a prescription are a card: {{choice DC-16}}
  (→ 30-17).
- **Infection in someone on immune-suppressing medicine** is more serious: see → 02-18.

## 8. Sourcing when the pharmacy is closed

- **Pharmacies, clinics and disaster medical points** while they run. Medical supply companies
  for ostomy supplies.
- **Someone else's prescription** is a card: {{choice DC-15}} (→ 30-16).
- **Relief food** is mostly wheat; say "celiac, no gluten" clearly and ask for rice, beans,
  potatoes, canned meat or fruit. Trade wheat foods away for safe ones (→ 18-01).
- **Scavenging:** sealed, labelled foods only for the celiac person. Unlabelled grain or flour is
  not safe.
- **Ostomy:** other ostomates, home health stores, hospitals. Sizes and systems differ; know the
  person's wafer size and system.

## 9. Power and devices

None, unless a medicine needs a fridge (→ 09-10).

## 10. Travel and evacuation

- Medicines, ORS and a day's ostomy supplies on the person (→ 01-07, → 22-06).
- **Gluten-free food for the whole trip plus a spare day.** Road food and shelter food are mostly
  wheat.
- Toilets will be scarce: plan stops, carry toilet paper and a bag kit (→ 13-01).
