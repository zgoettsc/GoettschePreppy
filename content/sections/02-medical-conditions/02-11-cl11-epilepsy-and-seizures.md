# CL-11. Epilepsy and seizure disorders

**What this page is for.** Most people with epilepsy control their seizures with daily medicine.
Missed doses, lost sleep, illness and stress, all common in a crisis, make seizures more likely,
and an off-grid household is full of open fires, stoves, wells, deep water and sharp tools where
a seizure becomes an injury. This module is seizure first aid for everyone in the house, the
supply clock, and how to make the home safer.

{{#if has.condition.CL-11}}**Someone in your household has this condition.** This module is at the front of the medical section for you.{{else}}**Nobody in your household is marked with this condition.** The module stays in the binder because anyone can have a seizure (from fever, low blood sugar, a head injury or poisoning); the first aid below is for everyone.{{/if}}

> **SAFETY**
> - **Never put anything in a person's mouth during a seizure, and never hold them down**
>   (CDC, First Aid for Seizures).
> - **A seizure that lasts 5 minutes or longer, or seizures one after another without waking
>   between, is an emergency** (CDC; NINDS). Use the prescriber's rescue plan if there is one.
> - **Do not stop or cut antiseizure medicine on your own.** Stopping suddenly can bring on
>   seizures, including long ones (NINDS, Epilepsy and Seizures).
> - **This page never calculates, adjusts or suggests a dose.** Rescue medicine is given only as
>   the prescriber wrote it, in part 7.

## Do this now: a seizure with jerking and loss of awareness

CDC's first aid (CDC, First Aid for Seizures):
1. **Ease them to the floor.** Move hard and sharp things away; away from fire, stove, water and
   stairs.
2. **Turn them gently onto one side** to keep the airway clear. Something soft under the head.
   Remove glasses; loosen anything tight at the neck.
3. **Time it.** Look at a watch now.
4. **Do not hold them down. Nothing in the mouth.** No food or drink until fully awake.
5. **Stay with them** until they are awake and know where they are. Talk calmly.
6. **Emergency** (call for any help that exists, and give the prescriber's rescue medicine if
   one is written in part 7) if: the seizure lasts **5 minutes or longer**; another follows
   soon after; they have trouble breathing or do not wake; it is their first seizure; it
   happened in water; they are injured, pregnant, or have diabetes or heart disease (CDC).
7. **Not breathing normally once the jerking stops: start CPR** (→ 12-18).

**Seizure without jerking** (staring, chewing, wandering, picking at clothes): stay with them,
guide them gently away from danger, do not restrain them, talk calmly until they are aware.

**Low blood sugar can cause a seizure.** In anyone on diabetes medicine, think of it first
(→ 02-01; CDC, Low Blood Sugar).

## 1. What it is

A seizure is a burst of abnormal electrical activity in the brain. It can cause jerking,
stiffening, staring, odd movements or loss of awareness. **Epilepsy** means a tendency to have
repeated seizures; daily antiseizure medicine prevents them in most people (NINDS).

## 2. What runs out, and how fast

| What | Clock | When it is gone |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily antiseizure medicine** | Days on hand (part 7). Most tablets keep well, cool and dry. | Seizures return, sometimes within days; stopping suddenly can bring prolonged seizures (NINDS). |
| Rescue medicine (nasal spray, mouth or rectal form), if prescribed | Expiry on the label. | No way to stop a long seizure. |
| Sleep | Crisis life steals it. | Seizures more likely (NINDS). |

## 3. Storing the medication without power

Original bottles, tightly closed, cool and dry, out of sunlight and **out of children's reach**.
Rescue medicines have their own labels; read them (→ 29-14) and write the storage band here:
________. Keep rescue medicine where the person spends time and in the go-bag, not in a car.

## 4. Day by day without it

- **Missed doses:** seizure risk rises within days for many people; the prescriber knows this
  person's pattern.
- **Running out:** seizures may become frequent and long. **A slow taper written by the
  prescriber is much safer than a sudden stop.**
- **What helps, and is in your control** (NINDS lists these as common triggers): **sleep** (the
  biggest one; → 21-07), avoiding alcohol, treating fever early (→ 12-13), regular meals,
  managing stress, and, for some people, avoiding flashing lights.

**Make the home safer, especially off-grid:**
- **Fire:** guard open fires and stoves; the person does not cook alone over an open flame if
  seizures are not controlled (→ 12-03). Back burners and short handles turned inward.
- **Water:** no bathing alone in a tub; shower or sponge-wash instead. No swimming alone. Covers
  on wells, cisterns and rain barrels (→ 03-10).
- **Heights and tools:** no ladders or roofs alone; no chainsaw or axe work alone (→ 16-06).
- **A buddy for risky jobs.** Who does what is the household's call: {{choice DC-24}} (→ 30-25)
  covers children; write the rule for this adult too: _________________________________

## 5. Warning signs that mean an emergency

- Seizure **5 minutes or longer**, or one after another without waking (CDC, NINDS).
- **Breathing trouble** or not waking after the seizure.
- **Injury**, especially a head injury (→ 12-19), or a seizure in water.
- **First seizure ever**, or a seizure with fever, stiff neck, severe headache, or after a head
  injury: think of other causes.
- In pregnancy or in someone with diabetes (CDC).

## 6. Sick-day basics

- **Fever and illness lower the seizure threshold.** Treat fever (→ 12-13), keep fluids up.
- **Vomiting a dose:** follow the prescriber's missed-dose words in part 12.
- **Some medicines interact** with antiseizure drugs (some antibiotics and others). Ask the
  pharmacist now which over-the-counter medicines are safe.

## 8. Sourcing when the pharmacy is closed

- **Pharmacies, clinics, disaster medical points**, with the printed prescription.
- **Antiseizure medicines are not interchangeable.** A different drug, or even a different
  strength, is a prescriber's decision. Someone else's prescription is a card: {{choice DC-15}}
  (→ 30-16).
- **Expired tablets:** {{choice DC-04}} (→ 30-05).
- **No herbal or over-the-counter substitute.**

## 9. Power and devices

No power needed for pills. Some people use seizure alarms or watches; they need charging and
should not be the only safeguard. A light at night helps a carer reach someone quickly.

## 10. Travel and evacuation

- Medicine and rescue medicine on the person (→ 01-07, → 22-06), with the written plan.
- **Moves break sleep and routines**: guard sleep, keep dose times, eat regularly.
- Do not let the person drive while seizures are uncontrolled. Nobody walks near roads, water or
  drops alone.
