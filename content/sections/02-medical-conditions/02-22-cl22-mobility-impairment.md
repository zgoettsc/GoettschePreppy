# CL-22. Mobility impairment

**What this page is for.** For someone who uses a wheelchair, walker or crutches, or who needs
help to stand, move or turn in bed, an emergency changes three things at once: **getting out**
(stairs, rubble, no lifts, a car that will not take the chair), **daily care without power or
plumbing** (charging a power chair, using a toilet that is now a bucket), and **skin**, because
sitting or lying in one position for too long causes pressure sores that can become deadly
infections. This module covers all three, and the medicines that cannot be stopped suddenly.

> **SAFETY**
> - **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open
>   window.** Carbon monoxide kills in minutes with no warning, and charging a power chair is a
>   common reason people run one too close (floor, → 30-01).
> - **Some muscle-relaxing medicines must not be stopped suddenly** (part 4).
> - **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products** (floor,
>   → 30-01).
> - **This page never calculates, adjusts or suggests a dose.**

## Do this now

1. **Plan the way out today:** which door, which helpers, what carries the person if the chair
   cannot go (part 10; Ready.gov, Individuals with Disabilities).
2. **Change position often** and **look at the skin every day** (part 4).
3. **Charge the chair whenever power exists**, and keep the manual backup ready (part 9).
4. **A red, hot, swollen or smelly sore, or fever:** treat as infection (→ 12-04) and get care.
5. **Pounding headache, flushing or sweating above the injury level in someone with a spinal cord
   injury:** sit them up, loosen tight clothing, check the bladder and bowel (part 5).

## 1. What it is

Mobility impairment means difficulty walking, standing or moving, from injury, stroke, spinal cord
injury, multiple sclerosis, arthritis, amputation, frailty or many other causes. People manage it
with aids (cane, walker, manual or power wheelchair, scooter, lifts), with help from others, and
sometimes with medicines for pain, muscle spasm or the bladder.

## 2. What runs out, and how fast

| What | Clock | When it is gone |
|---|---|---|
| Power chair or scooter battery | Range on one charge (the manual says); recharge needs power. | A manual chair or carrying. |
| Tyres, tubes, brakes, cushions | Spares on hand (part 7). | A chair that cannot roll, a cushion that no longer protects. |
| Catheters, bags, gloves, lubricant | Count them; days each lasts. | Infection risk rises with reuse. |
| Incontinence pads, wipes, barrier cream | Count them. | Sore skin and pressure sores. |
| Medicines (pain, spasm, bladder) | Days on hand (part 7). | Pain and spasm return; some stops are dangerous. |
| Helpers | People in or near the house. | The person cannot move, turn or leave. |

## 3. Storing the medication without power

- **Tablets:** original bottles, cool and dry, out of sun, locked from children.
- **Catheters and sterile supplies:** sealed, dry, off the floor, out of heat.
- **Lithium-ion chair batteries:** store and charge as the maker says; do not charge a damaged,
  swollen or very cold battery; charge where you can see it and away from exits (NFPA,
  Lithium-Ion Batteries).
- **Expired medicine** when nothing else exists: {{choice DC-04}} (→ 30-05).

## 4. Day by day without it

**Pressure sores** (also called pressure injuries or bedsores) form where bone presses skin
against a seat or mattress for too long: tailbone, hips, heels, shoulder blades, back of the head,
elbows (MedlinePlus, Preventing Pressure Ulcers). They can start within hours and become deep,
infected wounds over days. To prevent them (MedlinePlus):
- **Change position often.** In bed, turn at least every 2 hours. In a wheelchair, shift weight
  every 15 minutes, or have someone help. Write a turning chart (→ 25-05).
- **Check the skin every day,** with a mirror or a helper. Redness that does not fade after
  pressure is taken off is the first sign.
- **Keep skin clean and dry,** especially after incontinence; barrier cream.
- **Cushions and padding:** use the person's pressure cushion; pillows between knees and under
  calves to keep heels off the bed. Do not use donut-shaped cushions.
- **Eat and drink enough**; skin heals badly in someone short of protein and water.

{{figure fig-pressure-points}}

**Toileting without plumbing** (→ 13-01): a commode chair or a bucket toilet with a proper seat
and a sturdy frame or grab handle; a urinal or bedpan for night; a slide board if the person
transfers. Plan the transfer before it is needed and practise it. Hygiene and handwashing for
the helper (→ 13-02).

**Bladder care:** someone who uses catheters needs clean hands, clean water and clean supplies
every time. Reuse raises the infection risk; ask the clinic now how to clean and reuse safely if
supplies run out, and write it in part 7.

**Medicines for spasm:** MedlinePlus says baclofen must not be stopped suddenly, because stopping
can cause hallucinations and seizures (MedlinePlus, Baclofen). A baclofen pump (implanted) needs
refills on a schedule; ask the clinic now what happens if a refill is missed and what the warning
signs are.

**Falls** for people who still walk: clear paths, light at night, handrails, non-slip mats (CDC,
Check for Safety). Ice and rubble outside: stay in, or go with help.

**Warmth:** people who sit still all day, or who have reduced feeling, get cold faster and may
not notice cold feet. Layers, blankets, checks of hands and feet (→ 08-01).

## 5. Warning signs that mean an emergency

- **An infected pressure sore:** spreading redness, heat, swelling, pus, a bad smell, fever. Get
  care (→ 12-04). Sepsis signs (CDC, About Sepsis): confusion, fast breathing, fast heart rate,
  fever or feeling very cold, extreme pain, clammy skin.
- **Urine infection:** fever, cloudy or smelly urine, new spasms, confusion.
- **Autonomic dysreflexia** in people with a spinal cord injury at mid-back level or above: a
  sudden pounding headache, very high blood pressure, flushing and sweating above the injury.
  Usually set off by a full bladder, a blocked catheter, constipation or a pressure point. Sit up,
  loosen clothing, check and empty the bladder, find the cause. Ask the spinal cord clinic now for
  the person's written plan.
- **Baclofen withdrawal:** confusion, hallucinations, high fever, muscle stiffness, seizure.
- **A fall with a possible broken bone or head strike** (→ 12-09).
- **A swollen, painful, warm calf or sudden breathlessness** (a blood clot) in someone who sits a
  lot.

## 6. Sick-day basics

- **Illness keeps people in bed longer:** turn more often, check skin twice a day.
- **Fever and diarrhoea** soak skin and dehydrate; fluids (→ 12-05), clean and dry skin.
- **Antibiotics** for an infected sore or urine infection without a prescription are a card:
  {{choice DC-16}} (→ 30-17).
- **Pain medicines:** → 02-17 and → 12-13.

## 8. Sourcing when the pharmacy is closed

- **Pharmacies, medical supply stores, home health stores** while they run; churches and loan
  closets often lend wheelchairs, walkers and commodes.
- **Someone else's prescription** is a card: {{choice DC-15}} (→ 30-16).
- **Scavenging:** hospitals, care homes and supply stores hold chairs, cushions, catheters,
  commodes. Bicycle tubes and tyres sometimes fit chair wheels; check the size printed on the
  tyre (→ 16-09).

## 9. Power and devices

- **Power chairs and scooters** need a charge every day or two of use. Charge from a power
  station, solar or a generator (→ 09-06, → 09-10, → 29-02). The charger's label gives the watts
  it draws; write it here: ________. The generator stays outdoors, at least 20 feet from windows
  and doors (CDC, Carbon Monoxide Poisoning: Basics; floor, → 30-01, → 09-04).
- **Save charge:** use the manual chair indoors, the power chair for distance.
- **Powered beds and lifts:** learn the manual lowering method now.
- **EMP and storms:** a spare charger in a metal box (→ 27-03).

## 10. Travel and evacuation

- **Leave early.** A person who needs help to move cannot leave fast at the last minute
  (Ready.gov, Individuals with Disabilities).
- **The way out:** the route without steps, or the carry down the stairs, with named helpers.
- **The vehicle:** does the chair fit? Can it be folded? Is there a ramp? If not, what goes
  instead?
- Carry: medicines, catheters and pads for the trip plus extra days, the cushion, a tyre kit,
  the charger and cable, the medical summary (→ 01-07, → 22-06, → 25-09).
- Label the chair and parts with the person's name.
