# Dental emergencies

**What this page is for.** Keeping teeth healthy when there is no dentist, and buying time
when something goes wrong. The first-aid steps for a knocked-out tooth, a lost filling and a
dental abscess are on → 12-20.

> **Dental problems are one of the most underestimated risks in a long emergency, and one of
> the most miserable.** A toothache that lasts weeks stops eating and sleeping and wrecks the
> judgement and morale of the person who has it.
>
> **A dental abscess (a pocket of infection at a tooth) can spread and become
> life-threatening.** Facial swelling, swelling that closes the eye or spreads to the neck,
> trouble swallowing or breathing, fever, and feeling unwell all over are **emergency signs**
> (→ 12-04; CDC, About Sepsis). Before antibiotics, dental infections killed people regularly.
>
> **This page has no tooth-pulling procedure.** Pulling a tooth is a real procedure that can go
> badly wrong. Get *Where There Is No Dentist* (Hesperian), a widely used field book, and read
> it before you need it.

## Do this now

- **Get every outstanding dental problem fixed now.** Cavities, cracked fillings, the wisdom
  tooth someone keeps putting off, a loose crown. **This is the single most valuable step on
  this page, and its deadline is invisible.** Everyone, children included.
- **Ask your dentist directly:** "If I could not see you for a year, what would you fix
  first?" It is a normal question and you will get a useful answer.
- **Buy temporary filling material, dental wax and a dental first-aid kit.** Cheap, they keep
  for years, and they turn a crisis into an inconvenience.
- **Stock toothbrushes, floss and fluoride toothpaste for years**, for all {{ household.size }}
  of you{{#if has.child}}, including children's sizes and children's toothpaste{{/if}}. Tiny,
  cheap, and the actual prevention (CDC, Oral Health).
- **Get *Where There Is No Dentist* on paper.**

## Why it gets worse when the grid goes down

Three things change at once, all in the wrong direction:
1. **The diet shifts to stored carbohydrates**: rice, flour, pasta, sugar, honey, dried fruit.
   **Sugars and starches feed the bacteria that cause decay** (NIH, Tooth Decay). A
   food-storage diet is heavy in exactly those.
2. **Hygiene slips.** Water is rationed, routines lapse, toothpaste runs out.
3. **There is no dentist.** A cavity that was a twenty-minute appointment becomes a
   months-long slide toward an abscess.

{{#if has.child}}**Children are especially exposed.** A young child on a stored-food diet,
brushing unevenly, is a cavity waiting to happen, and a child in dental pain is hard on the
whole household.{{else}}**Children are especially exposed**, if you take any in: a stored-food
diet and uneven brushing is a cavity waiting to happen.{{/if}}

## Prevention: most of this page

- **Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste** (CDC, Oral Health). Fluoride is why modern
  teeth do better than historical ones. A tube lasts a long time, so a couple of years' supply
  is a small box.
- **Floss.** Decay between teeth is the kind you do not see coming.
- **Rinse with salt water.** Cheap, useful for sore gums and after injuries, and you already
  stock salt (→ 04-13).
- **Limit how often teeth meet sugar, not just how much.** Constant sipping and snacking is
  worse than the same sugar at one meal. Sweet things are morale (→ 21-06); have the treat,
  then brush.
- **Do not use teeth as tools.** Opening packages, cutting cord, holding nails. Cracked teeth
  from this are a classic and avoidable injury.
- **Watch for grinding.** Stress and cold both cause jaw clenching, which cracks teeth
  (→ 21-01).
{{#if has.young_child}}- **Keep brushing young children's teeth for them.** A preschooler's
  own brushing is not enough. Protect this routine even when everything else slips (→ 20-03).
{{/if}}

## Common problems and what a household can do

**This is buying time and easing pain, not treatment.**

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Lost filling or broken tooth** | Temporary filling material from a kit (adults; see → 12-20 for children); dental wax over a sharp edge; chew on the other side; keep it clean |
| **Toothache without swelling** | Salt-water rinses; keep it clean; pain relief (→ 12-13); clove oil (eugenol) is a traditional numbing remedy for adults; avoid very hot and very cold |
| **Abscess (swelling, throbbing, pressure)** | **This is an infection** (→ 12-04). Warm salt-water rinses inside the mouth; **no heat on the outside of the face if swelling is spreading**; watch for the emergency signs below; → 12-20 |
| **Knocked-out adult tooth** | Handle by the crown, never the root; put it back in the socket if you can, or keep it in milk or the person's own saliva (**not water**). **The clock: best within 30 minutes; a tooth can sometimes still be saved after an hour or more out of the mouth** (American Association of Endodontists, Knocked Out Teeth). Whoever is closest acts. Full steps on → 12-20 |
| **Knocked-out baby tooth** | **Not** put back: that can damage the adult tooth growing underneath. Stop the bleeding with pressure on gauze; see a dentist if one exists |
| **Chipped tooth** | Dental wax over sharp edges; pain on hot, cold or air can mean the nerve is exposed |
| **Broken jaw or face injury** | Support it and keep it still; beyond household care (→ 12-16) |
| **Sore or bleeding gums** | Better brushing and flossing, salt-water rinses; usually reversible early (NIH, Gum Disease) |

## When a tooth problem becomes a medical emergency

- **Swelling of the face, jaw or neck**, especially spreading
- **Swelling that closes the eye**
- **Trouble swallowing, opening the mouth or breathing: an airway emergency**
- **Fever and feeling unwell all over** with a tooth infection
- **Signs of sepsis** (→ 12-04)

**Any of these means the infection has moved beyond the tooth.** If care exists, go now.
Without it, this is one of the hardest situations on → 12-16, and the clearest argument for
getting dental work done **now**.

## About pulling teeth

People plan to "just pull it." Pulling a tooth needs the right instruments, real technique and
numbing medicine you likely will not have. It can go wrong in ways that make things much worse:
a root snapping off, damage to neighbouring teeth, a broken jaw, bleeding that will not stop, or
a worse infection. **Sometimes removing a tooth is the right answer, and it is not something to
learn from a paragraph.** The far better plan is not needing it. If your community has anyone
with dental training, they are an extraordinarily valuable person (→ 18-03, → 18-08).

## Scavenge or substitute

- **Toothbrush:** a twig chewed into a brush at one end (a traditional method), a clean cloth on
  a finger, a strip of cloth between teeth.
- **Toothpaste:** baking soda (mildly gritty). **It has no fluoride**, and fluoride is what
  prevents decay. Salt water works as a rinse.
- **Floss:** strong thread, fishing line used carefully, fine cord.
- **Rinse:** salt water.
- **Pain:** cold on the outside of the cheek (**not heat if swelling is spreading**); clove oil
  for adults; pain relief per the label.
- **What you cannot improvise:** fluoride, dental instruments, numbing medicine and a dentist.
  That is why the top item is "get the work done now."

## When it runs out

- **Toothpaste and brushes are cheap and keep for years.** Buy a box now.
- **Fluoride has no household substitute.** When it is gone, decay rises. Make up for it with
  brushing, flossing, salt rinses and above all **less frequent sugar in the stored-food diet**
  (→ 04-18).
- **Dental problems build slowly and then arrive all at once, often in the second year.** That
  is predictable, so plan: fix everything now, stock prevention supplies deep, protect the
  brushing routine, keep the temporary filling kits in reserve.
- **Ration:** brushing children's teeth comes first (growing teeth, worst consequences), then
  anyone with existing dental work.
- **Substitute:** the twig brush and salt rinse work as a minimum. They just work less well than
  fluoride toothpaste, and the difference shows over years.
- **Do not ban treats; brush after them.** Denying children every sweet thing for years has its
  own costs.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** dentists may still work for a while. **Use that window.** Then prevention
  and routine.
- **B (moving):** a toothbrush and paste per person weigh nothing; so does a temporary filling
  kit. **A dental emergency on the move is serious**, and prevention is your only tool.
- **C (resettled):** further from care, a harder diet, more years adding up. Keep the brushing
  routine formal; it quietly lapses over a hard year and then costs someone a tooth.
