# Cold injuries quick reference

**What this page is for.** A one-screen card to find the essentials on hypothermia, frostbite
and trench foot fast. The full guidance is on → 08-05; read it before you need this card.

> **SAFETY**
> - **Confusion in someone who is cold means hypothermia is serious.** They will not recognise
>   it themselves.
> - **Shivering that stops while the person is still cold is a severe warning sign, not
>   improvement.**
> - **Never rub frostbite, never use direct heat on it, and never thaw a part that may refreeze.**
> - Export this card to the vehicle kit and go-bag copies (→ 25-08).

## The three cold injuries

| | **Hypothermia** | **Frostbite** | **Trench foot (non-freezing)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| What | **Core** body temperature falling | Tissue actually **freezing** | Long hours **wet and cold**, above freezing |
| Where | Whole person | Fingers, toes, ears, nose, cheeks | Feet |
| Needs freezing temperatures? | **No**: happens above freezing, especially when wet | **Yes** | **No** |
| Key sign | **Shivering, then confusion, then shivering stops** | **Numb, white or waxy, hard** | Painful, pale, wet, wrinkled, numb |
| Emergency? | **Yes** | **Yes** | Serious, less sudden |

## Hypothermia: the one that kills

**Recognise** (CDC, Preventing Hypothermia):
- **Early:** shivering, cold, clumsy hands, tiredness
- **Worse:** **confusion, slurred speech, poor judgement, stumbling**, fumbling
- **Severe:** **shivering stops** while still cold, drowsiness, unconsciousness
- **In a baby:** bright red **or** pale cold skin, very low energy, weak cry, hard to wake.
  **Feel the belly and the back of the neck, not the hands** (→ 11-02).

> **The most important sign is confusion.** Remember "the umbles": stumbles, mumbles, fumbles,
> grumbles. Judge people by how they act, not by what they say.

**Respond:**
1. **Get out of the cold, wind and wet.**
2. **Remove all wet clothing.** Wet clothing is how heat is lost (→ 11-03).
3. **Dry layers; cover the head; insulate from the ground**, which steals heat fast.
4. **Warm the core gently**: blankets, dry clothes, skin-to-skin with a warm person, warm (not
   hot) drinks **only if fully awake and swallowing normally.**
5. **Handle gently.** Rough handling of a severely hypothermic person is dangerous.
6. **No alcohol. No rubbing. No hot-water bath. No direct high heat.**
7. **Keep watching.** People can get worse after being brought inside.

## Frostbite: where cold freezes tissue

**Recognise** (CDC, Preventing Frostbite):
- **Numbness**: the tissue stops hurting, which is the trap
- White, greyish-yellow or **waxy** skin
- Skin that feels **unusually firm or hard**
- Clumsiness in the affected part
- On rewarming: pain, tingling, redness, blisters

**Respond:**
1. **Get inside.** Off with wet gloves, socks and boots.
2. **Do not rub or massage** the area. It damages frozen tissue.
3. **No direct heat**: stove, fire, heater, hot water. **Numb skin burns without feeling it.**
4. **Rewarm in water at 98.6 to 102 °F (37 to 39 °C).** Numb skin cannot judge "warm", so
   **use a thermometer, or test with your own uninjured elbow** (comfortably warm, never hot).
   **15 to 30 minutes, until the part is soft and pink or red-purple**, topping up with warm
   water to hold the temperature. **Hotter burns the numb tissue; cooler does not thaw it**
   (Wilderness Medical Society frostbite guidelines, 2019 update; CDC). Body heat (armpits, a
   companion's belly) is the fallback when no warm water can be made. **Only if it will not
   refreeze** (next step).
5. **Do not thaw a part that might refreeze before you reach safety.** Refreezing thawed tissue
   is much worse. **This is a real decision on the move** (→ 01-03).
6. **Do not walk on thawed frostbitten feet.**
7. **Do not break blisters.** Cover, protect, watch for infection (→ 12-04).

**How fast skin freezes:** read the National Weather Service wind chill chart at today's actual
temperature and wind (NWS, Wind Chill Chart; → 29-10). In severe cold with wind, exposed skin
can freeze in **minutes**.{{#if is.extreme_cold}} Your home's record low is

## Trench foot (non-freezing cold injury)

**Happens above freezing**: wet socks and cold, for hours. **Likely in a grid-down winter when
drying things is hard** (→ 11-03).
- **Recognise:** feet painful, numb, pale or blotchy, wrinkled, swollen; may turn red and very
  painful on warming.
- **Respond:** **dry the feet**, warm them gradually, raise them, put on dry socks, keep them dry,
  and walk on them as little as possible. **Prevention is dry socks, changed often** (→ 11-04).

## Prevention card

- **Wet plus wind are the killers**, not just temperature.
- **Do not sweat.** Open layers before you overheat (→ 11-01).
- **No cotton** (→ 11-05).
- **Change into dry base layers before bed, every night.**
- **Hat on**: the fastest thermostat you have.
- **Mittens over gloves.**
- **Boots loose enough to wiggle your toes** (→ 11-06).
- **Eat and drink.** Staying warm burns calories, and dehydration in cold is easy.
- **No alcohol.**
{{#if has.child}}- **Check children by touch, not by asking** (→ 11-02): belly and back of the neck.
{{/if}}- **Check each other's faces** for white patches.
- **Check the wind chill chart** before going out. Set a time limit and keep it.

## When it is beyond you

- Severe hypothermia (unconscious, not shivering, very cold)
- Deep frostbite with blackened or hard tissue
- Any cold injury in a baby or toddler that does not improve quickly
- Cold injury together with another serious injury, or in someone with a condition from → 12-11
  (diabetes especially)

**Get professional care wherever it exists.** If it does not, → 12-16.
