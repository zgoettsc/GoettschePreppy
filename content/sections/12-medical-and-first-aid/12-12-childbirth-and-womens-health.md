# Pregnancy, childbirth and women's health

**What this page is for.** Planning for pregnancy in a long emergency, the warning signs that
must never be dismissed, contraception and menstrual supplies as preparedness items, and
everyday women's health. The full pregnancy, birth and after-birth module is → 02-12; infant
feeding is → 02-13; contraception and menstrual health is → 02-25.

> **SAFETY**
> - **Childbirth without a skilled attendant is one of the highest-risk situations in this
>   binder.** Deaths of mothers and babies in childbirth fell because of skilled attendants,
>   clean technique, the ability to stop heavy bleeding, and surgery when needed. **None of
>   those can be improvised from a book.**
> - **This page is planning, prevention and warning signs. It is deliberately not a delivery
>   manual.** If pregnancy is possible or present, the right preparation is **a trained
>   attendant identified in advance**, a real midwifery reference, and **being near working
>   care when the time comes** (→ 01-02, → 01-05).
> - **Learn the urgent maternal warning signs** (CDC, Hear Her; below) and post them. Many
>   mothers' deaths follow warning signs that were missed or dismissed.

{{#if has.pregnant}}> **Someone in your household is pregnant.** Read → 02-12 today. Write the due date
> on → 25-09 and make the decision on → 01-02 early, well before that date.{{/if}}

## Urgent maternal warning signs (CDC, Hear Her)

During pregnancy and in the year after delivery, CDC says to seek care immediately for any of
these:
- A headache that will not go away or gets worse over time
- Dizziness or fainting
- Changes in vision
- Fever of 100.4 °F (38 °C) or higher
- Extreme swelling of the hands or face
- Thoughts about harming yourself or your baby
- Trouble breathing
- Chest pain or a fast-beating heart
- Severe nausea and throwing up
- Severe belly pain that does not go away
- Baby's movement stopping or slowing during pregnancy
- Vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking during pregnancy
- Vaginal bleeding or discharge after pregnancy (heavy bleeding means soaking through one or more
  pads in an hour)
- Severe swelling, redness or pain of a leg or arm
- Overwhelming tiredness
- **If something just isn't right**, CDC says to talk to a health care provider. **Take it
  seriously when she says it.**

If any care exists, go. If not, → 12-16.

## Do this now

- **Decide, as a household, your position on pregnancy during a long crisis: explicitly, all
  the adults concerned, in advance.** It is a hard conversation and far better had calmly than
  after the fact. Whatever you decide, decide deliberately.
- **If the answer is "not now", stock contraception in depth**, watching expiry dates. It is
  cheap, light, and **removes the highest-risk medical scenario from the plan.**
- **Stock menstrual supplies deeply, and get reusable ones** (→ 13-08). The need goes on for
  years; menstrual cups and cloth pads end the dependency.
- **Find out now whether anyone in your circle has midwifery, nursing or obstetric training**
  (→ 18-01, → 18-03). That person is worth more than any supply on this page.

> **Before the collapse:** if long-acting contraception (an implant or IUD) fits your plans, ask
> a clinician now; it lasts for years with nothing further needed. Ask about extended fills for
> any prescription method (→ 12-07). If anyone is or may become pregnant, ask about prenatal
> care, prenatal vitamins and what their own risks are.

## Why this page matters even if nobody is pregnant

This binder plans for **months to years.** Over that time, in a household with adults of
reproductive age, pregnancy is a realistic possibility, planned or not, and it is **the single
highest-risk medical event that could plausibly happen.** Planning ahead is far better than
facing it unprepared.

**Contraception and menstrual supplies are consumables with a supply clock**, like food, and
they belong on → 23-02.

## Contraception as a preparedness item

Bluntly: **when childbirth is dangerous and there is no obstetric care, reliable contraception
is a life-safety item** (CDC, Contraception and Birth Control Methods).
- **Long-acting methods** (implants, IUDs) are placed by a clinician and then need nothing for
  years. If they fit your plans, doing it before a crisis removes the problem for the duration.
- **Prescription methods** need ongoing supply: ask about extended fills (→ 12-07).
- **Barrier methods** (condoms) keep for years in cool, dry storage, need no prescription, and
  **also reduce the spread of infections.** Stock them.
- **Fertility-awareness methods** need training and consistency and are less reliable than the
  above. They need no supplies, which is their advantage.
- **Whatever you use, stock extra and check expiry dates.**

## If pregnancy happens

**The goal becomes reaching skilled care by the time of delivery.** That is the plan; everything
else is backup.
- **Use any prenatal care that exists.**
- **Nutrition matters enormously**, and a stored-food diet has gaps (→ 04-18). **Prenatal
  vitamins, folic acid and iron** are cheap, keep well, and are worth stocking even with no
  current pregnancy.
- **Higher-risk pregnancies need care even more:** a first pregnancy, past complications,
  twins, and existing long-term conditions, **diabetes** especially, which greatly complicates
  pregnancy (→ 12-11).
- **The weeks after birth are dangerous too.** Heavy bleeding, infection, high blood pressure
  and blood clots can happen days or weeks later. Keep watching for the warning signs above.

## The realistic risks of an unattended birth

Stated plainly so the planning is honest (WHO, Maternal mortality fact sheet; WHO, Maternal
Health):
- **Heavy bleeding after birth (postpartum haemorrhage)**: WHO lists severe bleeding, mostly
  after childbirth, first among the complications behind about three quarters of mothers'
  deaths, and says it "can kill a healthy woman within hours if she is unattended." Managing it
  takes medicines and skills a household does not have.
- **Labour that cannot progress (obstructed labour)**: needs surgery. There is no home answer.
- **Infection** of the mother or the newborn (→ 12-04).
- **Newborn problems**: trouble breathing at birth, being born early, getting cold. A newborn
  in a cold house is at serious risk.
- **Dangerously high blood pressure in pregnancy (pre-eclampsia and eclampsia)**: can kill, and
  is why blood pressure is checked in pregnancy.

**This list is the argument for → 01-02 and → 01-05.** A known pregnancy with a due date is one
of the strongest reasons to be near working medical care, and it should outweigh most other
considerations when deciding where to be.

## Newborns and infant feeding

- **Breastfeeding is the most robust way to feed a baby in a crisis**: no supply chain, no water
  treatment, no sterilising bottles, the right temperature, and it passes on protection against
  infection (CDC, Breastfeeding). Its advantages in a grid-down situation are enormous.
- **It needs the mother to be well fed and well watered.** A nursing mother needs much more food
  and fluid, and that goes on the food and water plan (→ 04-18, → 03-01).{{#if has.nursing}}
  Your household's daily water figure already includes extra for nursing
  ({{ derived.water.floor_gal_day }} gallons a day minimum).{{/if}}
- **If formula is needed**, it requires **safe water and clean preparation** (→ 03-04, → 20-05).
  Stock formula and safe water if there is any chance of needing it, and learn the preparation
  in advance.
- **Newborns lose heat very fast.** Skin-to-skin with a parent, a hat and a warm room (→ 08-01)
  matter more for a newborn than for anyone else (→ 11-02).
- Full detail: → 20-05 and → 02-13.

## Everyday women's health

- **Menstrual supplies**: a need for years. **Reusable cups and cloth pads end the dependency**,
  need only water and soap, and are the best purchase in this category (→ 13-08).
- **Urinary tract infections** are common, become serious if untreated (→ 12-04), and are more
  likely with less washing and less water. **Drinking enough and hygiene are the prevention**
  (→ 13-02).
- **Yeast and other infections** are more common with less washing; over-the-counter treatments
  are worth stocking (→ 12-07).
- **Long-term conditions and prescriptions**: same framework as → 12-11.
- **Menopause and other changes**: discuss supply and management with a clinician now.

## Scavenge or substitute

- **Menstrual:** clean cotton cloth, folded and washed. Historically standard and it works
  (→ 13-08). It needs water and soap, which is why → 13-09 matters.
- **Clean birth supplies:** boiled cloth, boiled string for tying the cord, a clean surface,
  clean hands. **Cleanliness is the one thing improvised supplies can truly deliver**, and it
  matters more than any equipment.
- **Newborn warmth:** skin-to-skin with a parent is the best method there is and needs nothing.
- **What you cannot improvise:** a skilled attendant, a way to stop heavy bleeding, surgery and
  blood. **That is why the plan is to be near care.**

## When it runs out

- **Contraception and menstrual supplies are the consumables here.** Put both on → 23-02.
  **Reusable menstrual supplies end one of those lines for good.**
- **If contraception is finite and the decision is "not now", its supply clock is a planning
  limit.** Settle it with a clinician before it becomes urgent.
- **Substitute for menstrual supplies:** clean cloth, washed and reused. Adequate, and it makes
  water and soap a women's health need (→ 13-02, → 13-09).
- **There is no substitute for obstetric care.** The strategy is: **avoid needing it, or be
  near it.**
- **Community is the real answer.** A midwife, nurse or doctor in your circle changes this more
  than any stockpile (→ 18-01, → 18-03). Find out now who is out there.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** clinics and pharmacies may work for a while. **Use that window** for
  contraception decisions, extended prescriptions and any delayed care. Being near a hospital is
  an asset worth weighing.
- **B (moving):** **do not move a woman late in pregnancy or soon after birth unless there is no
  alternative.** If there is a pregnancy, make the decision to move (→ 01-02) **early**, well
  before the due date, and move *toward* care.
- **C (resettled):** furthest from care. **If pregnancy is possible or present, closeness to
  working obstetric care should be a main reason for choosing a destination** (→ 01-05), above
  land, water and defensibility.
