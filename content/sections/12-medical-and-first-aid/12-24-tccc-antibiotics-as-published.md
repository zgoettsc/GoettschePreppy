# Military battlefield antibiotics (TCCC 2026), as published

> **Written for trained military medics treating battlefield wounds.** This is the antibiotic
> section of the *Tactical Combat Casualty Care (TCCC) Guidelines*, 01 May 2026, from the
> Committee on Tactical Combat Casualty Care of the US Department of Defense's Joint Trauma
> System. It is printed word for word, as the owner of this kit asked. It is not written for
> households, for children, or for illness. It gives no child dose and no length of treatment.

**What this page is for.** Some prepping books quote the military's battlefield antibiotic list.
This page prints the current one exactly, so you can read what it actually says and who it is
for. What it means for a household is in the notes at the end. Household infection guidance is
on → 12-22 (by condition) and → 12-23 (by drug).

> **SAFETY**
> - **This is a military protocol for healthy adult soldiers, given by trained medics who can
>   give injections and evacuate the casualty.** A household has none of that.
> - **Allergy:** all three drugs below are cephalosporins (→ 12-23). Check allergies first
>   (→ 25-09).
> - **This binder never works out or adjusts a dose.** Nothing below has been changed.
> - **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products** (floor,
>   → 30-01).

## The text, as published

The 2026 guidelines say: "RED text indicates new text in this year's update to the TCCC
Guidelines". In the published file the following are in red, so new in 2026: the words "invasive
procedures" in item 12a; "Cefadroxil 1 gram PO once a day"; "OR Cephalexin 500mg PO every 6 hours
(Alternative)"; "Ceftriaxone 2 gm" in item 12c; item 8e step 4; and the eye-injury antibiotic
line in item 9. The burns line and the rest are unchanged (black).

Layout note: the published lists are shown as indented lists here; every word, number, unit
and capital letter is as published.

**From "Basic Management Plan for Tactical Field Care", item 12:**

> **12\. Antibiotics**
>
> - a. Antibiotics recommended for all open combat wounds and invasive procedures.
> - b. If able to take PO meds:
>   - Cefadroxil 1 gram PO once a day.
>   - OR Cephalexin 500mg PO every 6 hours (Alternative)
> - c. If unable to take PO meds (shock, unconsciousness):
>   - Ceftriaxone 2 gm IV/IO/IM once a day.

**Item 8 (Traumatic Brain Injury), part e, which sends the medic to item 12:**

> e. Penetrating TBI or open skull fractures are not automatically expectant casualties:
>
> - 1\. Apply a surface dressing which prevents contaminants from entering the wound.
> - 2\. If there is active bleeding from the wound or wound edges, apply hemostatic gauze to the
>   surface and hold gentle pressure.
>   - DO NOT pack any material into the wound cavity.
>   - DO NOT attempt to close the wound with staples or sutures.
> - 3\. Gentle low-pressure irrigation of gross contamination with saline or potable water is
>   acceptable.
> - 4\. Administer antibiotics per TCCC Guidelines in Section (12).

**Item 9:**

> **9\. Penetrating Eye Trauma**
>
> - If a penetrating eye injury is noted or suspected:
>   - Perform a rapid field test of visual acuity and document findings.
>   - Cover the eye with a rigid eye shield (NOT a pressure patch).
>   - Administer ceftriaxone 2g IV or IM, or cefadroxil 1g orally as soon as possible.

**Item 15 (Burns), part g:**

> g. Prehospital antibiotic therapy is not indicated solely for burns, but antibiotics should be
> given per the TCCC guidelines in Section (12) if indicated to prevent infection in penetrating
> wounds.

**From "Basic Management Plan for Tactical Evacuation Care":**

> 11\. Antibiotics (same as Tactical Field Care)

(Committee on Tactical Combat Casualty Care, Tactical Combat Casualty Care (TCCC) Guidelines,
01 May 2026.)

## Notes for a household (not part of the TCCC text)

- **Words.** *PO*: by mouth. *IV*: into a vein. *IO*: into the bone marrow with a special
  needle. *IM*: into a muscle. *Open combat wound*: a wound that breaks the skin, from a bullet,
  blast or fragment. *Invasive procedure*: a medical procedure that cuts or punctures the body.
  *TBI*: traumatic brain injury (→ 12-19). *Expectant*: in military triage, a casualty not
  expected to survive.
- **Who it is for.** Adult soldiers, usually young and healthy, wounded in combat, treated by a
  trained medic until evacuation to surgical care. The text prints no child dose, no allergy
  alternative, no length of treatment, and nothing about kidney disease or pregnancy.
- **What it treats.** Prevention of infection in open wounds from combat. It is not a guide for
  coughs, sore throats, urinary infections or other illness.
- **The drugs.** Cefadroxil and cephalexin are cephalosporin tablets or capsules; ceftriaxone
  is an injection. Dangers, allergy cross-reactions and storage: → 12-23. A household without
  injection training cannot use the "unable to take PO meds" line.
- **Cleaning comes first.** The same text tells the medic to dress, cover and irrigate the wound;
  WHO likewise calls cleaning and removing dead tissue "the cornerstone of initial treatment"
  of wounds (→ 12-02).
- **Burns alone do not get antibiotics** in this text (part g above). Household burn care:
  → 12-03.
- **Whether a household uses any antibiotic without a prescriber is a decision card:**
  {{choice DC-16}} (→ 30-17). For civilian wounds, bites and infections, WHO and US guideline
  doses are on → 12-22, sections 7 to 9.
