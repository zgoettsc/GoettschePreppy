# Dental first aid: knocked-out teeth, lost fillings, abscess

**What this page is for.** The dental first aid anyone can give with no dentist: saving a
knocked-out tooth, covering a lost filling or crown, recognising a dry socket, and knowing
when a tooth infection has become an emergency. Prevention and the everyday problems are on
→ 12-06. This page has no tooth-pulling or injection steps; those need a trained person.

> **SAFETY**
> - **The dangerous one is the abscess.** Swelling spreading toward the eye, down into the neck,
>   or under the tongue, with fever, trouble swallowing or trouble breathing, is a **whole-body
>   emergency** (CDC, About Sepsis; → 12-04, → 12-16). Everything else here is saving teeth and
>   easing pain.
> - **A knocked-out adult tooth has a clock: best within 30 minutes, real chances within 60**
>   (American Association of Endodontists, Knocked Out Teeth). Whoever is closest acts.
> - **Most temporary filling products contain clove oil (eugenol), which is seriously toxic to
>   small children if swallowed.** Children get dental wax only.
> - **Never inject or improvise numbing medicine.** Overdose and injection into a blood vessel
>   can kill.

## Do this now: knocked-out adult tooth

1. **Pick it up by the crown** (the white chewing part). **Never touch, scrub or dry the root.**
2. **Dirty?** Rinse a few seconds in milk or clean water. No scrubbing, no soap.
3. **Put it back in the socket now** if the person is awake and old enough not to swallow it.
   Push it gently into place and have them bite on gauze or cloth to hold it.
4. **Cannot put it back?** Keep it wet: **cold milk**, or the person's own saliva (tucked in the
   cheek, only for a calm adult), or a tooth-saving kit. **Not plain water. Not dry in a
   tissue.**
5. **Get to any dentist that exists**, with the tooth in place or in milk.

**Baby teeth are never put back.** Doing so can damage the adult tooth growing underneath.
Press gauze on the gap to stop bleeding and comfort the child (→ 12-06).

## Lost filling or crown: cover it and buy time

From the American Dental Association (Dental Emergencies):
- **A crown that came off and still fits:** clean it out, then put it back with **temporary
  dental cement** from a pharmacy kit (a dab inside, seat it, bite gently). Dental wax will hold
  it in a pinch. Do not use household glue.
- **A lost filling:** press temporary filling material or dental wax into the hole.
- **These are placeholders that last days to weeks.** Keep the area clean, chew on the other
  side, and get it fixed properly as soon as anyone can.

**Children: read this before opening the kit.** Most over-the-counter temporary filling
materials and cements are made with zinc oxide and **eugenol (clove oil)**. Swallowed eugenol is
seriously toxic to a small child (→ 12-17). **For a child, use dental wax only**, keep the tooth
clean, and get them to a dentist when one exists.{{#if has.child}} Your household has children:
store the kit locked with the medicines (→ 12-07).{{/if}}

## Dry socket: after a tooth comes out

After a tooth is pulled, a blood clot fills the hole and the pain fades day by day. **A dry
socket** happens when the clot is lost. It shows on day 2 to 4: throbbing pain that **gets
worse** instead of better, spreads toward the ear, a bad taste, and a hole that looks empty or
shows white bone.

It is miserable but not dangerous in itself. Gently rinse with warm salt water after meals, take
pain relief (→ 12-13), and keep food out of the hole. A
dentist can pack it if one exists. Fever, spreading swelling or pus means infection: the next
section.

## Abscess: the one that kills

An abscess is a pocket of infection at the root of a tooth or in the gum: swelling, throbbing
pain, often a bad taste, sometimes fever.

- **Rinse with warm salt water** inside the mouth several times a day. Pain relief (→ 12-13).
- **No heat on the outside of the face** if swelling is spreading.
- **The emergency line is spreading:**
  - swelling moving **up** toward or closing the eye,
  - swelling moving **down** into the neck,
  - swelling **under the tongue** (tongue pushed up, drooling, trouble swallowing or opening the
    mouth),
  - **trouble breathing**, fever, feeling unwell all over, confusion.
- **Any of those means the infection is spreading through the body.** It is an airway and sepsis
  emergency. If any care exists, go now. If not, → 12-16 and → 12-04, including your household's
  antibiotics decision: {{choice DC-16}} (→ 30-17)

## Why this page does not teach pulling teeth or numbing

Pulling a tooth needs instruments, technique and numbing medicine given by injection. Numbing
medicine has a maximum safe amount that depends on the person and the product, and injected into
a blood vessel or given in too large an amount it causes seizures and heart rhythm problems that
kill. **No household should inject or improvise it.** If your community has anyone with dental
training, they are an extraordinarily valuable person: support them and learn from them
(→ 18-03, → 18-08).
