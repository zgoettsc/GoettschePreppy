# First response to a mental-health crisis

**What this page is for.** The sharp end of mental health: someone in danger, someone who
cannot function, and keeping psychiatric medicines going. Ordinary stress, fear, grief and the
long grind of a crisis are in Section 21 (→ 21-01, → 21-02, → 21-03), which is where most of the
real work happens.

> **If anyone is in crisis and any phone or internet works: call or text 988** (988 Suicide and
> Crisis Lifeline). In a disaster, SAMHSA's **Disaster Distress Helpline is 1-800-985-5990 (call
> or text, 24/7)**. The **National Domestic Violence Hotline is 1-800-799-7233 (text START to
> 88788).** All three are printed here so they can be read with no internet; copy them onto the
> wall. **These may keep working when much else does not.** Communications fail unevenly, and a
> call is worth trying before assuming it is impossible.

## Do this now: someone may be thinking of suicide

1. **Ask directly and calmly:** "Are you thinking about killing yourself?" **Asking does not put
   the idea in their head.**
2. **Listen.** Do not argue, minimise or rush to fix.
3. **Do not leave them alone.**
4. **Put distance between them and lethal means**: firearms, medicines, vehicles, alcohol. Lock
   them away or have someone else hold them.
5. **Call or text 988** if any communication exists.
6. **Rule out a physical cause** (low blood sugar, head injury, infection, dehydration, cold) if
   behaviour changed suddenly.
7. **Stay with it.** One conversation is not the end of it.

## Do this now: preparation

- **Post the three numbers above on the wall and on → 25-03**, with any local crisis line and
  your own clinicians' numbers.
- **If anyone takes psychiatric medicine, plan its continuity now** (→ 12-07, → 02-16). **Stopping
  some psychiatric medicines suddenly is dangerous.** Ask the prescriber what happens if supply
  is interrupted and what to do, and write it in the "my meds" table.
- **Have the conversation between adults now**, while things are calm: "If one of us is not
  coping, how do we tell each other, and what do we do?" Agreeing that in advance is worth a
  great deal (→ 21-05).
- **Plan how you would secure lethal means in a crisis.** **Access to lethal means is one of the
  strongest risk factors for suicide that a household can change** (CDC, Risk and Protective
  Factors for Suicide).{{#if has.firearms}} Your household owns firearms: decide now that if
  anyone is in crisis, guns will be locked, kept apart from ammunition, or held by someone else
  (→ 14-14). Deciding in the moment is much harder.{{/if}} Medicines stay locked (→ 12-07).

## Why this belongs in the medical section

Mental-health crises are medical emergencies. In a long disaster the risk factors rise together:
loss, fear, isolation, broken sleep, money gone, exhaustion, constant low-level threat, loss of
purpose and routine{{#if is.cold_winters}}, and in your winters, months of darkness and
confinement (→ 21-02){{/if}}. Existing conditions get worse; new problems appear in people who
never had them.

**Planning for this is not pessimism. It is the same logic as stocking bandages.**

## Suicide: the warning signs

From the CDC (Risk and Protective Factors for Suicide) and the 988 Lifeline:
- Talking about wanting to die, being a burden, having no reason to live, or feeling trapped
- Looking for ways to do it
- Pulling away from family and activities
- Extreme mood swings, or a **sudden calm after a time of despair**
- Drinking or using drugs more
- Giving things away, putting affairs in order
- Sleeping far too much or too little
- **Hopelessness**, the strongest single sign

**What to do:** the "Do this now" list above. Ask directly, listen, stay, remove lethal means,
call 988 if you can, and keep checking in.

## Panic attacks and acute stress reactions

Common, frightening and usually manageable (→ 21-01):
- **Panic attack:** racing heart, short of breath, tight chest, terror, a certainty of dying.
  **It feels like a medical emergency and usually is not, but chest pain and breathlessness can
  also be a real one.** If there is any doubt, treat it as medical (→ 12-16).
- **Response:** calm presence, slow breathing together, grounding (name five things you can see),
  reassurance that it will pass, space away from the trigger. **Do not shout, do not minimise,
  do not leave them alone.**
- **Acute stress reaction:** someone frozen, wandering, hysterical or blank after a terrible
  event. **Short, concrete instructions given calmly** work better than explanations. Give them a
  small task.
- **Check for physical causes first.** **Low blood sugar can look exactly like anxiety, panic,
  confusion, irritability or aggression** in anyone with diabetes (→ 02-01, → 02-05). When
  someone with diabetes suddenly acts differently, **check their sugar first.**

## Keeping psychiatric medicines going

- **Some psychiatric medicines cause serious problems if stopped suddenly**; others are safer to
  taper. **The prescriber knows which. Ask now** and write the answer down (→ 02-16, → 12-11).
- **Build the buffer legitimately**: extended fills, early refills (→ 12-07).
- **Store properly**: cool, dark, dry, above freezing, locked away from children.
- **Write it in the family medical summary** (→ 25-09).
- **If supply is interrupted, tell someone.** Every adult should know what the others take and
  what an interruption might look like.

## When someone cannot function

One adult unable to function is a capability crisis as much as a medical one, the same problem as
→ 12-09.
- **Lighten their load. Do not demand performance.** Recovery is faster than forcing.
- **Protect sleep** (→ 21-07). Lack of sleep causes and worsens nearly every mental-health
  problem, and a cold, dark house wrecks sleep.
- **Give simple, concrete, achievable tasks.** Purpose and routine protect (→ 21-04).
- **Body first**: food, water, warmth, sleep. A cold, hungry, exhausted person will not feel
  better through willpower.
- **Rule out the physical:** low blood sugar (above), dehydration (→ 12-05), infection (→ 12-04),
  hypothermia (→ 08-05), head injury (→ 12-19) and medicine side effects **all cause confusion
  and changes in behaviour.** Rule them out before deciding it is psychological.

{{#if has.child}}## Children{{else}}## Children in your care{{/if}}

- Young children show distress **through behaviour**: acting younger than their age, clinging,
  sleep problems, tantrums, new fears, changes in eating (→ 20-08). **Treat sleep problems with
  routine, never with medicine: never an antihistamine to make a child sleep** (→ 12-13).
- **Children take their cues from the adults.** Calm adults produce calmer children, which is
  another reason to take adult mental health seriously.
- **Routine protects children** more than almost anything else (→ 20-03, → 21-04).
- **Honesty suited to their age** beats false reassurance; children know when something is
  wrong (→ 20-04).

## Alcohol and other substances

- **Alcohol is a common way of coping and it makes everything worse**: sleep, judgement,
  depression, hypothermia risk (→ 08-05), conflict (→ 21-05) and firearm safety.
- **If someone depends on alcohol or another substance, suddenly stopping can be medically
  dangerous.** Alcohol withdrawal in particular can kill. This is a real medical issue (→ 12-16,
  → 02-17). Think about it in advance if it applies.

## What helps, mostly free

- **Routine, purpose, physical work, daylight, sleep, other people, being listened to, honest
  conversation** (→ 21-02, → 21-04).
- **Community** protects; isolation is a risk factor (→ 18-01). A real argument for not going it
  alone.
- **Physical activity and daylight outdoors** are both evidence-supported and cost nothing.
- **What cannot be substituted:** a professional, and psychiatric medicine for someone who needs
  it. That is why continuity planning comes first.

## When it runs out

- **Psychiatric medicine supply clocks** belong on → 23-02 alongside every other life-critical
  medicine (→ 12-11). Discuss interruption with the prescriber **in advance**.
- **The non-medicine supports do not run out, but they take deliberate effort**, and they are
  exactly what erodes in month six when everyone is tired. Build the routines early and defend
  them (→ 21-04).
- **The long picture:** a crisis lasting years takes a real toll on everyone, children included.
  **Section 21 is load-bearing.** A household that is fed and warm but coming apart is not
  surviving well; collapsing morale leads to bad decisions, conflict, accidents and giving up.
- **Substitute for professional care:** each other, honestly and patiently; other people
  (→ 18-01); and structure. It is not the same, and pretending otherwise does not help. Where
  care exists, use it.
