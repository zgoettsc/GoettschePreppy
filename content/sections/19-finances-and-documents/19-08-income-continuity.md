# Income continuity for the self-employed and small business

**What this page is for.** Keeping a household's income alive when the adult who earns it cannot work,
or when a disaster shuts the business. It is written for any adult who is self-employed, runs a small
business or a practice, or depends on their own hands for income: a tradesperson, a clinician, a
shop owner, a farmer, a freelancer. A binder that plans food and medicine to the day and skips the
thing that pays for them has a hole in it.

**For an employed adult**, most of this is covered by the employer's benefits; read the "employed"
box near the end and the playbook on → 19-07.

> **CAUTION. The scenario that matters most is usually not the storm. It is the owner unable to
> work** (an injury, an illness, a complication of a chronic condition; → 12-09, → 12-11) **while
> rent, staff, loans and the household's own bills keep running.** Insurance for exactly that exists,
> and it can only be bought **before** it is needed. **Pull the actual policies and fill in the
> worksheets from the paper.** "I think we have that" is the failure.
>
> **Write worksheets by hand on paper.** No policy numbers, account numbers or passwords in this app
> (→ 19-07).

## Do this now

1. **Pull the actual policies** and fill in every blank below by hand: company, benefit, waiting
   period.
2. **Disability insurance first** (worksheet 1). If it does not exist, or is not the right kind, that
   call goes ahead of any purchase in the rest of this binder.
3. **Name who runs the business, or closes it, if you cannot**, and put their phone numbers on your
   contact list (→ 25-03).
4. **If you hold a license and keep client, patient or customer records, find your licensing board's
   rules** on how long records must be kept and what to do if you close. Write them in worksheet 5.
5. **Write a one-page business continuity plan** (Ready.gov, Business Continuity Planning): what
   stops, what it costs per week, who does what.

## Three kinds of interruption

| Scenario | What stops | What bridges it |
|---|---|---|
| **Short outage or storm** (days) (→ 17-08) | Appointments, sales, perhaps spoiled stock | Cash buffer (→ 19-02); rescheduling; property insurance for losses (→ 19-04) |
| **Regional disaster** (weeks to months) | The business **and** your customers' ability to pay | **Business interruption and overhead insurance** (below); US Small Business Administration disaster loans (SBA, Disaster Assistance) |
| **The owner unable to work** | Income stops completely while every cost keeps running | **Disability insurance, overhead insurance, and a named person to run or close it**: the reason this page exists |

## Worksheets: fill in by hand from the documents

**1. Disability insurance: the critical one.**
**"Own-occupation"** disability pays if you cannot do **your own** occupation, even if you could do
some other work. **"Any-occupation"** pays only if you cannot do any reasonable job. For someone whose
income depends on a specific physical skill (a surgeon's hands, a carpenter's back, a driver's
eyes), the difference is everything.

```
ON PAPER
Company: ____________________
Own-occupation? (true own-occ / modified / any-occ): ____________
Monthly benefit: $________   Waiting ("elimination") period: ___ days
Pays until (age __ / __ years): ______
Extras (inflation increase, partial disability, option to
  increase later): _______________________________________
Any exclusions for existing conditions: _________________________
Premium paid and current, paid from: ____________________________
```

**2. Business overhead expense insurance.** Pays the business's fixed costs (rent, staff, loan
payments) while the owner is disabled, so there is a business to return to or sell.

```
Exists? ____  Company: ______________________
Monthly benefit: $________  Waiting period: ____ days  Pays for: ______
```

**3. Business interruption (on the business property policy).** What events it covers, the waiting
period, the limit, and whether it pays when the government orders an area closed.

```
Company: ____________________  Covers: ________________________
Waiting period: ______  Limit: $________  Agent: ______________
```

**4. Who runs, or closes, the business if you cannot.**

```
Partner, associate or employee who can do the work: ____________
Temporary replacement (agency, colleague) and phone: ____________
Person who carries out this page: ______________________________
Checklist, in order:
 [ ] contact clients and reschedule or refer
 [ ] secure anything dangerous or controlled (chemicals, medicines,
     tools, firearms, client records)
 [ ] payroll decision (line 6)
 [ ] notify insurers (1 to 3 above)
 [ ] a weekly go / no-go decision
Close the business if out of work for more than ____ weeks
Who values or sells it (broker, accountant): ____________________
```

**5. Client, patient or customer records.** An obligation that can outlast the business.

```
Records kept in (system, where hosted): _________________________
Who besides the owner can open and export them TODAY (19-07 test):
  ______________________
Required keeping period (licensing board rule, checked on ___):
  ______________________
Who will hold the records if the business closes: _______________
```

**6. Staff and payroll.** How long can the business pay staff with no income, and from what?

```
Payroll is run by / through: ____________________
Reserve: covers ____ weeks
Line of credit (bank, limit): ____________________
```

**7. What else leans on the business.** Relationships, a building, equipment, a vehicle, health
insurance for the family: ______________________________________

## If you are employed

- **Know your benefits:** short- and long-term disability, life insurance, and how long health
  insurance continues if you cannot work.
- **Write the HR and benefits phone number** on the contact list (→ 25-03, → 19-07).
- **Unemployment insurance** is run by each state; know how to apply.
- **A side skill** that earns in any economy is real insurance (→ 18-03).

## The two honest framings

- **Insurance solves the bounded scenarios**, which are by far the likely ones (→ 19-04). Disability,
  overhead and interruption coverage are a business's version of the grab folder: boring, decisive,
  and buyable today.
- **In a long collapse, most businesses stop with the payment system**, and the owner's skills become
  something to trade and share in the community instead (→ 18-03; for dental skills, → 18-08). Until
  then, the business funds everything else in this binder.

## Gear

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Disability insurance, the right kind** | The most important policy for anyone who lives by their own work | Worksheet 1 |
| Business overhead expense insurance | Keeps the business alive while you are down | Worksheet 2 |
| Business interruption coverage | The regional-disaster bridge | Worksheet 3 |
| **Named people and phone numbers** | A plan is a person with a phone | Worksheet 4 |
| Records access for a second person, tested | The obligation and the asset | Worksheet 5, → 19-07 |
| Copies of all business policies in the grab folder | Claims start from paper | → 19-01 |
| A one-page continuity plan | What stops, what it costs, who does what | Ready.gov |

**Substitutes:** SBA disaster loans and insurer or lender accommodations (→ 19-04) help partly, slowly,
and usually only in declared disasters. Another adult's income is often the real household backstop.
**Nothing substitutes for coverage bought before the disability.**

## When it runs out

- **Benefits have a waiting period at the front and a limit at the back.** The cash buffer (→ 19-02)
  covers the waiting period; the benefit period in worksheet 1 is your household's runway for a long
  disability. Know both numbers.
- **Review every year** (→ 25-10): premiums paid, benefit still matching costs, the named people still
  willing, licensing rules re-checked.
- **If one person is both the household's income and its biggest health risk**, insuring their
  ability to work is as central to this binder as any stockpile.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** everything on this page happens now, while insurers answer phones. The worksheet
  blanks are this month's homework.
- **B, on the move:** the business stays behind; policies, records-access notes and the closure plan
  travel in the grab folder (→ 19-01). Insurance and the business's future get sorted from wherever
  you land, with paper in hand.
