# Bank access in a crisis

**What this page is for.** Understanding which parts of banking stop working in an emergency, and
arranging your money and papers so that does not hurt. Card networks, ATMs, online banking, branches
and safe-deposit boxes all depend on power, staff and connections (→ 17-08). In most of this binder's
scenarios, some or all of them stop. **The rule this page exists to print: nothing you need in the
first 72 hours depends on a bank being open.**

> **CAUTION. A safe-deposit box is a vault with banker's hours.** It is excellent at one thing,
> surviving a fire, flood or burglary at your house, and useless at another: being reachable on a bad
> day. Nights, weekends, holidays, outages, evacuations: the box might as well be on the moon. **So
> nothing crisis-needed lives only in the box.**
>
> **This page never asks for account numbers, PINs, passwords or combinations, and you should never
> type them into this app.** Anything like that is written by hand on paper kept separately (→ 19-07).

> **Before the collapse:** the FDIC insures bank **deposits**, not the contents of a safe-deposit box
> (FDIC, Understanding Deposit Insurance). A box rented in one person's name can be sealed for a time when that
> person dies, until the estate is authorized to open it. Rules differ by state and bank; ask yours.

## Do this now

1. **Count how many ways your household can reach money:** institutions, cards, cash. If the answer
   is one, add a second institution (→ 19-02).
2. **Keep cash at home, split** (→ 19-02), because the box, the ATM and the card reader fail on the
   same days.
3. **On paper, list each institution's name, its phone number and the nearest branch address**, and put
   the list in the grab folder (→ 19-01). No account numbers on that list.
4. **If you rent a safe-deposit box:** list what is in it, check each item against the rule below, and
   ask the bank two questions (below).
5. **Make sure more than one adult can reach each account and the box** (→ 19-07).

## What stops working, and when

| Service | Fails when | What covers it |
|---|---|---|
| Card payments | Power or network out at the shop | Cash in small bills (→ 19-02) |
| ATMs | Power out, network down, or emptied | Cash at home |
| Online and app banking | Your phone or the network is down; a sign-in code goes to a dead phone | Paper backup codes (→ 19-03); phone number on paper |
| Branches | Closed for the storm, staff cannot get in, power out | A second institution; cash |
| Safe-deposit box | Nights, weekends, holidays, any closure | Home copies of anything needed soon |
| Transfers and direct deposit | Bank or payment system disruption | Cash; a buffer in more than one account |

## Safe-deposit box rules

| The box is | The box is not |
|---|---|
| The most burglar-, fire- and flood-resistant storage you can rent | **Reachable outside branch hours** |
| A copy that survives your house burning (→ 19-03) | **Reachable in an outage, evacuation or bank disruption** |
| The right home for originals you need only after a crisis | **Insured**: the FDIC covers deposits, not box contents |
| Cheap | **Guaranteed reachable after the renter dies** |

1. **Nothing crisis-needed lives only in the box.** If losing access to the box for a month would
   hurt in that month, a copy lives at home (→ 19-01).
2. **Originals needed for rebuilding, not surviving, belong in the box:** deeds, vehicle titles, extra
   certified birth and marriage certificates, a copy of the will.
3. **The grab folder must work without the box.** Test it against "the bank never reopens".
4. **More than one adult can open it**: joint rental, or the bank's authorized-deputy arrangement,
   confirmed in writing, not assumed (→ 19-07).
5. **The original will does not live only in a box that might be sealed.** The lawyer keeps a copy,
   and its location is written on → 19-07.

## A home safe has the same problem

A fire-rated safe at home covers part of the box's job without the hours problem. But **to anyone who
does not know how to open it, it has exactly the box's problem.** If only one adult knows the
combination or where the key is, the papers inside are out of reach the day that adult is away, hurt
or dead. Every adult who may need it can open it without help; check it at the yearly review
(→ 19-07, → 25-10). **The combination itself is never written in this app.**

## Cash caches: names only

Write **where**, never **how much**. A written amount turns a lost or stolen page into a shopping list
(→ 14-03). Amounts live in the adults' heads.

## Gear

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Home copies of everything crisis-needed** | Rule 1 | → 19-01 |
| A second institution | No single point of failure | → 19-02 |
| **Cash at home, split, small bills** | Box and ATM fail together | → 19-02 |
| Institution names, phone numbers and branch addresses on paper | Reach the bank without the internet | In the grab folder |
| Joint or deputy access on any box | The death-sealing fix | Confirm with the bank |
| A box contents list | Know what is there without going | Update yearly |

**No box?** A relative's house out of the area is the traditional away-from-home copy (→ 19-03): less
burglar-proof, more reachable, free.

## When it runs out

- **What runs out first is access**, before cash or supplies. Plan as if the box and the bank do not
  exist until the bounded disaster is over. That is when they matter most: rebuilding, claims
  (→ 19-04), settling an estate.
- **Check the box list and access arrangements every year** (→ 25-10).
- **The box is the deep archive; the grab folder is working memory; cash at home is the reflex.**
  Mixing the layers is the whole failure this page describes.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** the bank works as designed for bounded disasters. Do the access audit and ask the
  two questions this month.
- **B, on the move:** the box's contents are gone for the duration, which is fine if rule 1 held. The
  grab folder and cash are the bank now. Banks in other states may not serve you in person; a national
  bank or credit union network may help (CFPB, Disasters and Emergencies).
- **C, settled:** a box far away is a once-a-season errand at best. Plan one deliberate trip for deeds
  and originals when conditions allow, not before (→ 01-05).
