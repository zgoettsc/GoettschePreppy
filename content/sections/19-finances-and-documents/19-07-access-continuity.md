# Access continuity: if one adult is gone

**What this page is for.** Making sure that if one adult is in hospital, unable to act, or dead, the
others can still pay the bills, reach the insurance, keep income coming, and get into the accounts,
**this week**, not after months of court. In most households, money and digital life run
disproportionately through one person's logins. If the honest answer to "could the other adult do it
without them?" is "probably not without their thumb on the phone", that is this page's finding.

**Adults in your household:** {{#each adults as a}}{{a.label}}; {{/each}}
If you are the only adult, the "other adult" on this page is the person you trust most to act for
you, and the estate documents below matter even more.

> **CAUTION. Never write passwords, PINs, unlock codes, safe combinations or account numbers in this
> app.** This page records **where and how**, never **what**. The secrets themselves live in a password
> manager and on paper kept separately, written by hand (→ 19-03).
>
> **The failure is quiet.** Everything works until the day it is needed, because the everyday
> arrangement ("she just handles that") hides the missing access. **The fix is the three tests below,
> actually done and dated.** An access plan never tested is a hope.

## Do this now

1. **Each adult runs the three tests below this month, cold**, with no coaching from the other. Date
   the lines.
2. **Fill in the access map in pencil**: locations and methods only.
3. **Share phone unlock codes between the adults today**, if you have not. It costs nothing and is the
   most common gap. (Tell each other in person; do not write it here.)
4. **Check the estate documents exist** (table below). A power of attorney cannot be signed by someone
   who is already unconscious (CFPB, Managing Someone Else's Money).

## The access map

Locations and methods only. Anyone who reads this binder may read this page (→ 14-03).

```

  Its emergency or recovery kit,
  printed and kept where:        _________________    _________________

  TODAY (not "could get")?       YES / NO             YES / NO

  everything else:               _________________    _________________
  Its backup codes printed?
  Kept where?                    _________________    _________________

Employer and benefits systems:   _________________    _________________
Where the paper password sheet
  is kept (19-03):               _________________    _________________
```

A password manager's "emergency kit" or "recovery kit" is a printable sheet that lets you get back
into the account. Many managers also offer a way to grant another person emergency access. Strong,
unique passwords in a manager are the right everyday answer (CISA, Use Strong Passwords).

## The three tests: each adult, dated, no coaching

**A test is passed only when it was done cold**, from memory or from the binder alone.

```
                                        ADULT 1      ADULT 2
TEST 1: Open the OTHER adult's password
  manager (their vault, your hands,
  no help):                             date: _____  date: _____
TEST 2: Reach the critical accounts:
  a bank, the reset email, an insurance
  portal, one employer or business
  system. Sign in, look, sign out:      date: _____  date: _____
TEST 3: Physically find the paper
  password sheet, the originals box,
  and any safe or box key (19-01,
  19-06); open each and close it:       date: _____  date: _____
```

**A failed test is a good result.** It found the gap on a calm afternoon instead of in a hospital
waiting room. Fix it, test again, date it.

## If one adult is gone: the playbook

1. **Medical care and children first** (→ 12-16). This page is for day two onward.
2. **Their phone** is the practical master key, because sign-in codes go to it. **Keep it charged.** A
   dead or wiped phone makes everything below harder.
3. **Open their password manager** (test 1), then **the reset email**, then banks, bills and insurance
   (→ 19-04).
4. **Income:** if they were self-employed or ran a business, start → 19-08. If employed, call their
   employer's HR and benefits line: ______________________. Health insurance through that employer
   may be what covers the family.
5. **Money:** automatic payments keep running, which is good; know what they are (→ 19-02). **A
   financial power of attorney** is what lets you act on accounts in their name alone (CFPB).
6. **Write down what you did and when** (→ 25-05). You will need it for the bank, the insurer and any
   court.

## Estate documents: the paper under all of it

Access gets you through the month; **legal authority gets you through the year** (CFPB, Managing
Someone Else's Money). Fill in honestly:

```
                          EXISTS?  REVIEWED IN LAST 3 YRS?  ORIGINAL KEPT WHERE?
Will (each adult)         ______   ______                   _______________
Financial power of
  attorney (each)         ______   ______                   _______________
Health care power of
  attorney / advance
  directive (each)        ______   ______                   _______________
Guardian named for
  children (in the wills) ______   ______                   _______________
Beneficiaries checked on
  every policy and account ______  ______                   (19-04 worksheet)
```

**Blanks in that table are the real emergency on this page.** A missing power of attorney means courts
and months. {{#if has.child}}**You have children under 13: a missing guardianship nomination means a
judge chooses who raises them.** {{/if}}**The original will should not live only in a box that may be
sealed on death** (→ 19-06).

> **Before the collapse:** wills, powers of attorney and guardianship nominations follow state law,
> and the forms that work differ by state. A lawyer, once, is the usual route; some states offer
> official statutory forms for powers of attorney and advance directives.

## The yearly re-test

**All three tests, every adult, every year** at the review (→ 25-10). Passwords change, phones are
replaced, sign-in methods are re-enrolled, and last year's dated line is this year's hope.

## Gear

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Each password manager's emergency kit, printed and stored** | The cold-start sheet | Kept with the paper password sheet; not in a wallet |
| **Backup codes for the reset email, printed** | The account that resets everything must itself be recoverable | → 19-03 |
| Phone unlock codes shared between adults | The most common gap | In person, not written here |
| **Wills, powers of attorney, guardianship, signed** | Authority, not just access | A lawyer, once |
| This page's map and tests, filled in and dated | The record | Pencil |

**Nothing substitutes for authority arranged in advance.** Afterward there is only the court. The
bank's own procedures after a death (death certificate, court letters) are slow and cover only the
legal side, never this month's bills (→ 19-06).

## When it runs out

- **Access decays silently.** Every password change, new phone or new account un-passes a test.
  That is why the tests are dated and yearly.
- **The gap this page cannot close: every adult gone at once.** The guardianship nomination, the
  estate documents, and a sealed letter to whoever will handle the estate saying where things are
  is the honest best available. Letter kept where: ______________________
- **Access is the cheap half of having a backup adult.** Buy it with an afternoon and three tests.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** do the map, the tests and the lawyer visit now. Every item on this page needs the
  working world.
- **B, on the move:** phones and the grab folder (→ 19-01) carry the access layer. Each adult's
  password-manager emergency kit travels sealed, apart from the devices.
- **C, settled:** connection may be the problem, not knowledge. The printed layer (emergency kits,
  backup codes, this map) is what still works. Re-test whenever you can reach the network.
