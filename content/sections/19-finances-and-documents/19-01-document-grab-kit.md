# The document grab kit

**What this page is for.** Making sure your household's papers leave with you. The scenario: you have
ten minutes to leave and will not come back, because of a fire, a flood, a storm's aftermath or a
fast evacuation (→ 17-03, → 01-03). **Documents are what people forget and most regret.** Replacing
vital records is possible but slow (USA.gov, Replace Your Vital Documents), and much harder when the
offices that issue them were hit too. Without them you may struggle to prove who you are, get money,
file an insurance claim (→ 19-04), get medical care, or enroll a child in school.

> **CAUTION. Never write safe combinations, passwords, PINs or full account numbers into this app.**
> Write them **by hand on paper**, and keep that paper **separately** from the grab folder (→ 19-07).
> A lost phone or a lost folder should never hand someone both your identity and your keys.
>
> This is a one-afternoon project that costs almost nothing. Do it this month.

## Do this now

1. **Decide where the originals live** (birth certificates, Social Security cards, passports, titles,
   wills): one fire-resistant, waterproof place, **off the floor**, that every adult can open.
2. **Assemble the grab folder**: a waterproof zip pouch with a handle, kept **with the go-bags**
   (→ 22-03), holding **copies** of the list below. This is the under-a-minute layer.
3. **Scan everything** and keep the digital copies in two places, one at home and one away from home
   (→ 19-03).
4. **Give a copy of the critical few to someone out of the area**: a relative or friend (→ 15-03). A
   house fire takes the originals and the home backup at once.
5. **Put the medical papers in** (→ 25-09). For many households they matter as much as the identity
   papers.
6. **Test the grab:** can one adult, carrying a child or a bag, pick up the folder and leave in under a
   minute?
7. **Write both locations** on the family plan's "documents are at" line (→ 00-04).

## What goes in

Originals or certified copies where noted; good photocopies otherwise.

| Category | Documents |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Birth certificates (every member), Social Security cards, passports, driver's licenses or state IDs, marriage certificate, adoption or custody papers, immigration documents |
| **Medical** | Family medical summary (→ 25-09), insurance cards, prescription lists, allergy action plans, vaccination records, clinicians' contacts, and for anyone on a life-critical medicine: the medicine names and strengths, any device models and serial numbers, the prescriber, and a recent pharmacy printout (→ 01-07) |
| **Property** | Deed or mortgage, lease, vehicle titles and registration, home inventory with photos (→ 19-04, → 22-04) |
| **Insurance** | Policies (home or renters, auto, life, health, flood); policy numbers and agent contacts (→ 19-04) |
| **Financial** | Names of your banks and card issuers and their phone numbers; recent statements; recent tax returns; safe-deposit box details; some cash (→ 19-02). **Full account numbers: handwritten, on the separate paper** |
| **Legal** | Wills, powers of attorney, advance directives, trusts, divorce decrees |
| **Contacts** | Printed contact list: family, out-of-area contact, clinicians, insurers, employer (→ 15-03, → 25-03) |
| **Pets** | Vaccination and ownership records; shelters ask for them (→ 20-09) |
| **Children** | School and immunization records, medical cards |

## Two layers: originals and the grab folder

Originals belong somewhere secure and fire-resistant. That often means a place that takes a key,
a dial or a combination and a few minutes, which is fine for originals and useless for a
one-minute grab. So the kit has two layers.

| Layer | Where | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Originals** | A fire-resistant, waterproof box or safe, **off the floor**, that every adult can open | Birth certificates, Social Security cards, passports, titles, deed, wills, powers of attorney, an encrypted backup drive (→ 19-03) |
| **2. Grab folder** | **With the go-bags** (→ 22-03), in a waterproof pouch with a handle, known to everyone and findable in the dark | The laminated medical summary, allergy plans, prescription and device sheet, insurance cards and numbers, printed contacts, **photocopies** of every ID, birth certificate and Social Security card, some cash in small bills (→ 19-02) |

- **One handle, one item.** You should be able to pick it up while carrying a child.
- **Never on a basement floor.** Basements flood first (→ 16-12).
- **"Fire-resistant" ratings have limits.** A serious house fire may still destroy a box's contents,
  which is why the copy kept away from home exists (→ 19-03).
- **Every adult can open the originals box.** If one cannot, fix that this week (→ 19-07). Where the
  key or combination is kept is written on paper, not in this app.
- **Write both locations here:** originals: ______________ grab folder: ______________

## Three copies, three ways to fail

1. **Originals** at home, with the grab folder's copies.
2. **Digital scans**, encrypted, stored at home **and** away from home (→ 19-03).
3. **Paper copies with a trusted person out of the area.**

A fire takes the first and maybe the second; a network failure takes the second; **the third
survives both.**

## Rebuilding what you lost

If documents are destroyed, they can be recovered, slowly:
- **Birth, marriage and death records** come from the vital records office of the state or county
  that issued them (USA.gov). For {{ home.state_name }}, write the office and how to request
  here: ______________________ (→ 25-03).
- **Social Security cards** from the Social Security Administration; **driver's licenses** from the
  state; **passports** from the State Department.
- **Tax records:** the IRS has guidance on rebuilding records after a disaster, including getting
  transcripts of past returns (IRS, Reconstructing Records After a Disaster).
- **Bank and card records** from the institutions (CFPB, Disasters and Emergencies).
- **Property records** from the county recorder or register of deeds. Usually recoverable, since the
  county holds them.
- **Medical records** from clinicians and pharmacies: another reason the contact list is in the
  folder.

**All of it is much easier if you know names, dates and which institutions you use**, which is the
argument for the digital and away-from-home copies.

## The photo inventory: one hour

For insurance and for rebuilding (→ 19-04, → 22-04):
- **Walk through the house filming or photographing every room**, opening closets and drawers, saying
  what things are.
- **Photograph serial numbers** on major items.
- **Photograph storage areas**: food, tools, equipment.
- **Store it away from home** (→ 19-03). **Update it every year** (→ 25-10).

This hour is what makes an insurance claim work.

**Substitutes:** any waterproof container: a dry bag, a sealed metal ammunition can, doubled zip bags
in a hard case. Something today beats something perfect never. **Phone photos of documents** are
better than nothing and worse than paper, since the phone dies. **A relative's house** is a good place
for the third copy.

## When it runs out

- **Documents do not run out; they go out of date.** Review every year (→ 25-10): expired IDs and
  passports, changed policies, changed medicines, new clinicians, children's records as they grow.
- **Renew IDs before they lapse.** Replacement offices may be closed exactly when you need them.
- **Check the digital copies still exist and still open** (→ 19-03). A backup nobody has checked is a
  hope, not a backup.
- **Most documents can be rebuilt; the cost is time**, and time is what you will be short of.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** assemble the grab folder this week, back up three ways, test the grab, do the photo
  inventory.
- **B, on the move:** both layers go: the grab folder on a person, the originals box in the vehicle if
  it is portable. After medicines, papers are the highest-priority cargo that is not used up.
- **C, settled:** same kit, kept the same way. Add the new place's papers, and set up a new
  away-from-home copy with someone you trust. It matters more far from everything, not less.
