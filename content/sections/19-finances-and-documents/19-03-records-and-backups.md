# Records and backups

**What this page is for.** Making sure your household's records survive a fire, a flood, a dead phone
and a failed drive. **The rule: three copies, two formats, one away from home.** Three copies, so one
failure does not lose everything. Two formats (paper and digital), because they fail differently. One
away from home, because fire and flood take everything in the house at once (→ 17-03). Most
households have one copy, in one format, in one place.

> **Digital-only records depend on a device, a battery, a file format and a network**, and in this
> binder's scenarios all four are unreliable (→ 09-06, → 17-08). **Paper is the format that
> survives.**
>
> **Passwords, PINs, safe combinations and account numbers are never typed into this app.** The few
> you need in a crisis are written by hand on paper and kept separately (below, and → 19-07).

## Do this now

1. **Print what matters:** the key pages of this binder (use the app's PDF export, → 00-10), the
   medical summary (→ 25-09), the contact list (→ 25-03), the family plan (→ 15-03) and the document
   list (→ 19-01). A file on a dead phone is not a record you can use.
2. **Do the photo inventory of the house**, an hour with a phone (→ 19-01, → 22-04).
3. **Set up the away-from-home copy**: a relative, a friend, a safe-deposit box, or encrypted online
   storage. This month.
4. **Check that a backup actually opens.** An unchecked backup is a hope, not a backup.

## The three copies

| Copy | Format | Where | What kills it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Originals** | Paper | Your originals box, off the floor; grab-folder copies with the go-bags (→ 19-01) | Fire, flood, leaving in a hurry (the grab folder is the fix) |
| **2. Digital scans** | Encrypted files | A drive at home **and** a second drive or online storage away from home | Device failure, ransomware, no power, no network |
| **3. Paper copies** | Photocopies | **Out of the area**, with a relative or friend | Nothing local. This is the one that survives the house burning |

Each layer covers the others' failures. That is the design.

## What to back up

- **Documents** (→ 19-01): identity, medical, property, insurance, financial, legal.
- **Medical records** (→ 25-09): prescriptions, device details, allergy plans, clinicians' contacts,
  vaccination records. For many households, as important as identity papers.
- **The photo inventory** (→ 22-04): every room, closets open, serial numbers, storage areas. It is
  what makes an insurance claim work (→ 19-04).
- **Family photographs.** What people most regret losing, and almost always digital-only. **Print the
  important ones** (→ 21-03).
- **This binder.** On more than one device, with the key pages printed (→ 18-07).
- **Your own household records** (→ 25-05): consumption logs, garden notes, weather, medical logs. Over
  years they become more useful than any published reference.

## Digital, done sensibly

- **Encrypt anything with identity or financial information on it.** A lost unencrypted drive is an
  identity-theft event (FTC, Identity Theft: Report and Recover; → 19-05).
- **Two digital places, not one:** two drives kept in different places, or a drive plus online
  storage. **One drive travels** (with the grab folder or a go-bag) and **one stays home**, never both
  in the same place.
- **Check that it opens.** Restore one file as a test, every year (→ 25-10).
- **Use formats anyone can open:** PDF and plain text outlast formats that need special software.
- **Keep the encryption password where you will still have it**: on the separate paper (below). A
  backup you cannot unlock is not a backup, and this is a very common failure.
- **Online storage works until the internet does not**, which is the scenario this binder plans for.
  It is a layer, not the plan.
- **Protect drives and spare devices from a power surge or worse** (→ 27-03).

## Passwords: the awkward one

- **A password manager is the right answer in normal times.** It depends on a device and possibly a
  network.
- **For a crisis, you need the critical few on paper:** bank access, insurance and medical portals,
  and **the email account that resets everything else** (→ 19-07). Every adult must be able to use
  them without help.
- **Write them by hand. Never type them into this app.** Keep the sheet **in your most secure place
  at home, separate from the grab folder** (→ 19-01). A locked box in the house is reasonable; a pouch
  by the door or a note in a wallet is not. That is a security trade-off; make it knowingly.
- **Do not keep a password on the same paper or drive as the thing it unlocks.** A lost bag should
  never carry both the encrypted drive and its key.
- **Two-step sign-in** (a code sent to your phone) protects you normally and **can lock you out when
  the phone is dead or lost** (→ 09-06). **Print the backup codes** each service offers and keep them
  with the password sheet.

## Paper: the format that survives

- **No power, device, network or format needed; anyone can read it** (→ 18-07).
- **Dry and off the floor.** Basements flood (→ 17-03).
- **Fire is paper's weakness**, which the away-from-home copy solves.
- **Pencil or waterproof ink** for anything handwritten. **Inkjet printing runs when wet.**
- **Laminate the critical cards:** medical summary, family plan, emergency contacts.

## Rebuilding what is lost

Possible, and slow (→ 19-01): vital records from the issuing state or county (USA.gov); tax records
using the IRS's disaster guidance, including transcripts (IRS, Reconstructing Records After a
Disaster); bank records from the institutions (CFPB); property records from the county, usually
intact; medical records from clinicians and pharmacies; insurance from your company (→ 19-04).
**All of it goes faster if you know the names, institutions and dates.**

## The yearly review (→ 25-10)

- Documents current? Expired IDs, changed policies, changed medicines.
- Backups checked? Do they open?
- Away-from-home copy updated?
- Photo inventory refreshed?
- Medical summary current, especially as children grow?
- Contact list current?
- Printed binder pages current?

**Substitutes:** a relative's house is a perfect away-from-home copy and costs nothing. Phone photos
of documents are better than nothing and worse than paper. Handwritten copies of contacts and medical
details. **What you cannot substitute is doing it before the event.**

## When it runs out

- **Records do not run out; they go stale and get lost.** The upkeep is the yearly review.
- **Digital storage fails silently:** drives die, accounts lapse, formats age, passwords are lost.
  Check yearly, or assume it is gone.
- **Paper ages slowly and predictably**, which is why it is the main format here.
- **Your own records grow more valuable every year**, and exist only if you write them down.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** do all of it now. Every part of this page needs working infrastructure, which you
  have today.
- **B, on the move:** the grab folder and one drive travel; the away-from-home copy protects you if
  the house is lost while you are gone.
- **C, settled:** set up a new away-from-home copy with someone you trust. Keep the household records
  going; at a new place, your own observations become the reference nobody else has.
