# DC-10. Pets when food, water or movement makes keeping them impossible

**What this page is for.** Nobody wants to think about this. In a long emergency a household
may not be able to feed its animals, or may have to leave in a way that cannot include them.
Deciding now, calmly, is kinder to the animals and to the people than deciding at the door.

> **Children.** Explain whatever happens to pets honestly and simply. Do not let children
> watch a humane end. → 20-08, → 21-03.

## 1. The thing, in plain words

This card covers dogs, cats and other house pets (livestock is part of food production).
"Cannot keep them" means one of three things: there is not enough food or water for people and
pets; the household must move and the pets cannot come (on foot, no room, a destination that
will not take them); or a pet is sick or injured with no vet.

## 2. The rule, and why it was set

- FEMA's advice is to include pets in the family plan: "if local officials ask you to
  evacuate, that means your pet should evacuate too", and "if you leave your pets behind, they
  may end up lost, injured or worse" (FEMA/Ready.gov, Pets and Animals).
- The American Veterinary Medical Association says a humane death minimises or eliminates
  pain and distress, admits that a complete absence of pain and distress cannot always be
  achieved, and describes acceptable methods for each species; it lists drowning, cold,
  household chemicals and several improvised routes as unacceptable (AVMA, Guidelines for the
  Euthanasia of Animals, 2020).

**Why:** FEMA's rule assumes shelters, vets and transport exist. The AVMA's rule exists because
a failed or panicked attempt causes great suffering to the animal and lasting harm to the
person who did it.

## 3. How it actually hurts people (and animals)

- **Keeping them when there is not enough:** pets drink water the family needs (CDC says to
  store more water when there are pets) and eat food that could feed a child. Hungry or frightened
  animals may bite, and a barking dog draws attention to a stocked house.
- **Releasing them:** most house pets have never had to find their own food or shelter, and
  FEMA warns that pets left behind may end up lost, injured or worse (FEMA/Ready.gov). Cold,
  heat, traffic, predators and hunger are the obvious dangers, and loose dogs can become a
  danger to livestock and people.
- **A humane end done badly:** an improvised method that fails causes pain and fear, and the
  memory stays with the adult and any child who sees or hears it.
- **Not deciding:** the decision gets made in panic, at the door, by whoever is there.

## 4. What makes it safer

**Removes most of the risk**
- **Plan for them now:** several days of food and water stored for the pets, a carrier and a
  collar with ID tag and leash, extra medicine, copies of records, a microchip, and a safe place
  you can take them, since many public shelters and hotels do not allow pets (FEMA/Ready.gov).
  Count them in the water plan (→ 03-01).
- **Find a person** who would take them in, and agree it in advance.

**Reduces it a lot**
- **Decide in advance**, as adults, with this card. Write the order you would try things.
- For release: leave as much dry food and water as can be spared, a way in and out of shelter,
  and a collar with an ID tag; release near people who may take them in.
- For a humane end: a veterinarian first, whenever one can be reached; otherwise **only** the
  method on the pets page (→ 20-09), done by an adult with the skill it requires, away from the
  children.

**Reduces it a little**
- Keep pets healthy now (vaccines, weight, teeth) so they need less later.
- Talk to the children before it happens, in plain words (→ 20-08).

## 5. The options

**`keep-at-all-costs`: Keep them at all costs.**
You give up: food, water, vehicle space and sometimes quiet. You accept: that people may go
shorter so the animals can stay.

**`release-with-food-out`: Release with food and water left out.**
You give up: control over what happens to them. You accept: that many released pets will not
survive, and that some may become a problem for others.

**`humane-end`: A humane end, by the method page.**
You give up: the pet. You accept: the grief, and the responsibility of doing it properly.

You may answer differently for different animals. Write it beside the box.
