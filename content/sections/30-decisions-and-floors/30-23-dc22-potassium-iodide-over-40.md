# DC-22. Potassium iodide for adults over 40

**What this page is for.** Potassium iodide is often sold as an "anti-radiation pill". It is not.
This card explains what it does, why FDA treats adults over 40 differently, and what the household
decides for its older adults. The full FDA dose table and the children's measuring recipe are on
→ 27-06.

> **Caution.** Take potassium iodide only when public health officials say to. FDA: it should not
> be taken as a preventive before exposure, and taking more than directed does not give more
> protection and can cause severe illness or death (FDA, Frequently Asked Questions on Potassium
> Iodide). Sheltering protects far more than any pill (→ 27-05).

## 1. The thing, in plain words

The **thyroid** is a gland in the neck that takes up iodine from the blood. A nuclear accident or
bomb can release **radioactive iodine**, which the thyroid absorbs and which can cause thyroid
cancer years later, mostly in people exposed as children. **Potassium iodide (KI)** is a salt of
ordinary iodine. Taken at the right time, it fills the thyroid so it absorbs less of the
radioactive kind. FDA: it "works only to prevent the uptake of radioactive iodine into the
thyroid gland. It is not a general radioprotective agent" (FDA, Frequently Asked Questions on
Potassium Iodide).

## 2. The rule, and why it was set

- FDA's guidance sets different exposure levels at which KI is recommended by age (the full table
  is on → 27-06). FDA says **anyone over 40 should take KI only if the predicted exposure is high
  enough to destroy the thyroid and cause lifelong hypothyroidism** (the 500 cGy row) (FDA,
  Frequently Asked Questions on Potassium Iodide; FDA, Potassium Iodide as a Thyroid Blocking
  Agent in Radiation Emergencies, 2001).
- FDA lists people who should not take KI, including those with known iodine sensitivity, the skin
  disease dermatitis herpetiformis, the blood-vessel disease hypocomplementemic vasculitis, and
  nodular thyroid disease with heart disease (FDA FAQ).

**Why:** HHS REMM gives the reason: adults over 40 have the lowest risk of thyroid cancer from
radioactive iodine and a greater risk of an allergic reaction to KI than younger people (HHS
REMM, Potassium Iodide). FDA's 2001 guidance adds that iodine-induced overactive thyroid
(thyrotoxicosis) is more common in older people, though it usually takes repeated doses, and
that people with multinodular goitre, Graves' disease or autoimmune thyroiditis need caution,
"the vast majority" of them adults.

## 3. How it actually hurts people

- **Taking it when not needed or not directed**: skin rash, swelling of the salivary glands,
  "iodism" (metallic taste, burning mouth and throat, sore teeth and gums, cold symptoms, sometimes
  upset stomach and diarrhoea), and in some people an allergic reaction with fever, joint pain,
  swelling or trouble breathing (FDA FAQ). FDA says people with nodular thyroid and heart disease
  should not take it.
- **Taking extra**: more does not protect more, and overdose can cause severe illness (FDA FAQ).
- **Relying on it**: a household that takes KI and then goes outside, instead of sheltering, takes
  the full exposure to everything else in the fallout.

## 4. What makes it safer

**Removes most of the risk**
- **Shelter first**, in the most protected place: Ready.gov says remain in the most protective
  location for the first 24 hours unless there is an immediate hazard or officials say it is safe
  to leave (Ready.gov, Nuclear Explosion; → 27-05).
- **Take KI only on official direction** (→ 15-01 for how you will hear it).

**Reduces it a lot**
- **Decide now with a clinician** for each adult over 40, and write it on the family medical
  summary (→ 25-09), including whether anyone has thyroid or heart disease.
- **Know FDA's do-not-take list** before the bottle is opened.

**Reduces it a little**
- Keep the FDA table and recipe from → 27-06 with the bottle.
- Record who took what and when; do not repeat doses early.

## 5. The options

**`follow-fda-table`: Follow FDA: over 40 only at very large exposure.**
You give up: KI for the older adults in all but the largest releases. You accept: a small
remaining thyroid risk for them in a moderate release.

**`take-anyway`: Adults over 40 take it whenever others do.**
You give up: FDA's age guidance. You accept: a higher chance of side effects, especially in anyone
with thyroid or heart disease, for a small added protection.
