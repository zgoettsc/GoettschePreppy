# DC-14. Food past its date, dented or rusted cans, or food from a failed freezer

**What this page is for.** Throwing out food that might be fine is a real loss when no more is
coming. Eating food that is not fine can put a child or an elder in serious danger with no
hospital. This card sets out what the published rules say, which parts are about quality and
which about safety, and what the household decides.

> **SAFETY.** Low-acid foods are pressure-canned to the published time and pressure, or not
> canned (floor, → 30-01). **Any can or jar that is bulging, leaking, spurting liquid or foam
> when opened, or smells bad, is thrown away without tasting.** Never taste food to find out if
> it is safe.

## 1. The thing, in plain words

- **Date labels** ("best by", "use by", "sell by") are set by the maker for best quality, not
  safety. Infant formula is the exception: its use-by date matters (USDA FSIS, Shelf-Stable Food
  Safety; → 29-14).
- **Shelf-stable food** is food sealed so it keeps without a fridge: cans, jars, dry goods.
- **Perishable food** is meat, fish, eggs, dairy, cooked leftovers: food that needs the cold.
- **Botulism** is poisoning by a toxin that bacteria make in sealed, airless, low-acid food. It
  paralyses muscles, including the breathing muscles (→ 29-08).

## 2. The rule, and why it was set

- USDA: dating is for quality, not safety, except for infant formula. Store cans cool and dry:
  below 85 °F is best, and above 100 °F harms them. **Discard cans that are bulging, leaking,
  heavily rusted or deeply dented** (a dent you can lay a finger into), and any can with a deep
  dent on a seam; a can with a small dent that is otherwise in good shape is safe. Discard frozen
  cans that are swollen or that thawed at 40 °F or higher. USDA's chart gives low-acid canned
  goods 2 to 5 years on the shelf and high-acid ones (tomatoes, fruit, juice) 12 to 18 months
  (USDA FSIS, Shelf-Stable Food Safety, 2014).
- In a power outage: a closed fridge keeps food safe about **4 hours**; a full freezer about
  **48 hours**, a half-full one about **24 hours**; **discard perishable food above 40 °F for 2
  hours or more**; and **never taste food to decide** (FoodSafety.gov, Food Safety During a Power
  Outage).
- CDC: "when in doubt, throw it out": a container that is leaking, bulging or swollen; looks
  damaged, cracked or abnormal; spurts liquid or foam when opened; or food that is discoloured,
  mouldy or smells bad (CDC, Home-Canned Foods).

**Why:** a sharp dent on a seam can break the seal invisibly and let bacteria in; deep rust can
eat through the metal (USDA FSIS). Between 40 and 140 °F, bacteria that cause illness grow
quickly (FDA, What You Need to Know About Egg Safety). The agencies draw simple lines because no one can see
or smell most of these dangers.

## 3. How it actually hurts people

- **Botulism from a failed can**: difficulty swallowing, double or blurry vision, drooping
  eyelids, slurred speech, muscle weakness and difficulty breathing (CDC, Botulism: Signs and
  Symptoms). Without a hospital and a breathing machine, it can kill.
- **Spoiled perishables**: vomiting and diarrhoea within hours. In a baby, an elder or someone
  with diabetes, the dehydration that follows is a medical emergency (→ 12-05).
- **Old but sealed dry goods**: usually just stale taste, rancid fat or lost vitamins. Rarely
  dangerous.

## 4. What makes it safer

**Removes much of the risk**
- **Rotate**: eat oldest first so little ever goes past date (→ 04-10).
- **Thermometers in the fridge and freezer**, so you know what actually happened during an outage
  rather than guessing. Keep doors shut (→ 04-17).
- Cook and eat perishables first in an outage, then freezer food as it thaws, then the pantry.

**Reduces it a lot**
- **Inspect every can**: discard if bulging, leaking, heavily rusted, deeply dented, or dented
  on the top or side seam. Surface rust that rubs off and a small dent on an otherwise sound can
  are not a safety problem (USDA FSIS).
- **Inspect every package**: discard if torn, wet, mouldy, infested or smelling wrong.
- **Thawed freezer food** that still has ice crystals or was kept at 40 °F or below can be cooked
  or refrozen; fully warm is a discard (FoodSafety.gov).
- **Cook to safe internal temperatures** with a thermometer (FoodSafety.gov, Cook to a Safe
  Minimum Internal Temperature).

- **Boil home-canned low-acid food before eating it**, as CDC advises: 10 minutes below 1,000
  feet, plus 1 minute for each additional 1,000 feet (CDC, Home-Canned Foods). This does not make
  a bulging, leaking or spurting jar safe: those are thrown out.

**Reduces it a little**
- Feed questionable food to adults before children, never to infants.

## 5. The options

**`label-strict`: Follow the label and the published rules strictly.**
You give up: food that is probably fine. You accept: a smaller food supply and running out
sooner.

**`inspect-and-eat`: Inspect and eat, with the rules for each case.**
You give up: the simple bright lines. You accept: that a judgement call will sometimes be wrong
and someone may get sick, and that bulging, leaking or spurting cans and jars stay on the
discard side regardless.
