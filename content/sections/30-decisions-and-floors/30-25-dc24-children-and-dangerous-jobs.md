# DC-24. Children doing dangerous jobs because hands are short

**What this page is for.** This card is a worksheet. Children with real jobs are calmer, braver and
safer, and in a long emergency their help matters (→ 20-07). Some jobs can kill or maim a child.
The household writes, in advance, which jobs each child may do, at what age, with what training
and supervision.

> **SAFETY.** Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products
> (floor, → 30-01). That stays true whatever this table says: storage is locked, and any work a
> child does with these is done beside an adult, never alone and never from unlocked supply.
> Infants and toddlers have no dangerous jobs; they have supervision.

## 1. The thing, in plain words

A **dangerous job** here means fire (lighting, tending, cooking), fuel (carrying, pouring),
cutting tools (knives, axes, saws, chainsaws), power tools, large animals and butchering, water
(buckets, barrels, ponds, pools) and anything with chemicals. **Supervision** means an adult
watching the child, within reach, not doing something else. **Demonstrated habit** means the child
has done it right, every time, for weeks, with an adult watching.

## 2. The rule as safety agencies state it, and why

- The guidelines for children's farm work developed with NIOSH support (the North American
  Guidelines for Children's Agricultural Tasks, now the Agricultural Youth Work Guidelines) match
  a child's physical, mental and social abilities to the demands of each specific job, so parents
  can assign work a child is ready for; NIOSH reports they work (CDC/NIOSH, Guidelines for
  Children's Agricultural Tasks Demonstrate Effectiveness, 2011).
- For adult workers, OSHA says chainsaw operators must wear hand, foot, leg, eye, face, hearing
  and head protection (OSHA, Chainsaw Safety QuickCard), and that workers should be trained in
  the proper use of all hand and power tools (OSHA, Hand and Power Tools). A child deserves at
  least the same.
- CDC's parenting tips say preschoolers can help with simple chores, and that a child should be
  watched at all times around water (CDC, Positive Parenting Tips: Preschoolers).

**Why:** a child's reach, grip, weight, reaction time and judgement of risk all grow with age, but
unevenly. The dangerous moment is a job slightly too big for the child, done when the adult is
busy elsewhere.

## 3. How it actually hurts people

- **Fire and hot liquid**: a child's sleeve catches, or a pot tips; scald burns cover a large part
  of a small body fast.
- **Fuel**: a child pours gasoline near a running engine, or swallows fuel siphoned by mouth.
- **Blades**: an axe glances off a knot into a shin; a chainsaw kicks back.
- **Animals**: a cow, horse or pig knocks a child down; a dog bites a child at its face height.
- **Water**: a young child falls head-first into a bucket, barrel or pond. "Drowning happens in
  seconds and is often silent", and more children aged 1 to 4 die from drowning than from any
  other cause (CDC, Preventing Drowning).
- **Chemicals**: bleach or lye splashes into eyes, or is swallowed (AAP, Poison Prevention).

## 4. What makes it safer

**Removes most of the risk**
- **Locked storage** for firearms, fuel, medicines and chlorine (floor). Covers on water
  containers.
- **The job matched to the child**, not to the household's need that day.

**Reduces it a lot**
- **Teach with an adult every time**; unlock solo work only after weeks of demonstrated habit, not
  on a birthday.
- **Protective gear sized for the child**: eye protection, gloves, boots, hearing protection.
- **One adult supervises and does nothing else** while a child does a dangerous job.

**Reduces it a little**
- Child-sized tools in good repair.
- Short sessions; stop when the child is tired, cold or upset.
- Praise the safe way of doing it, specifically.
