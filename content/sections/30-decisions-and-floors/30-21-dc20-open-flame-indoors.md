# DC-20. Open-flame cooking, candles or oil lamps indoors

**What this page is for.** When the power goes out, the house gets dark and the electric stove
stops. Candles, oil lamps and a gas burner lit with a match are the obvious answers. This card
explains what fire agencies say, how flames indoors actually hurt people, and what the household
decides.

> **SAFETY.** Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an
> open window (floor, → 30-01). **Camp stoves, grills and charcoal are not part of this card**:
> CDC says never use them indoors, and this binder treats them under that floor. Children do not
> have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products (floor): that includes lamp oil
> and kerosene.

## 1. The thing, in plain words

This card covers:
- **Candles.**
- **Oil lamps and kerosene lamps**: a wick in a glass chimney burning liquid fuel (→ 29-01).
- **Open-flame cooking indoors**: a gas range lit with a match when the electric igniter is dead,
  or cooking on an indoor fireplace or wood stove.

It does not cover heaters (→ 30-02) or camp stoves and grills (floor).

## 2. The rule, and why it was set

- The US Fire Administration says **use flashlights, not candles, during a power outage**; keep
  candles at least 12 inches from anything that can burn; blow them out when you leave the room or
  go to sleep; and avoid using them in bedrooms and sleeping areas (USFA, Candle Fire Safety).
- CDC says **never heat a home with a gas oven** (CDC, Carbon Monoxide Poisoning: Basics).
- The same USFA page counts about 20 home candle fires reported each day, and suggests
  battery-operated flameless candles instead (USFA, Candle Fire Safety).

**Why:** USFA: open flames make candles dangerous "if they tip over or when they are placed too
close to anything that can burn". A flame can also be forgotten or reached by a child. In an
outage the risks pile up: more candles than usual, people tired and distracted, dark rooms, and
often no fire department coming.

## 3. How it actually hurts people

- **Fire**: a candle left burning when people fall asleep, a curtain or paper too close, a cat's
  tail, a toddler reaching up. The fire spreads before anyone wakes. With smoke alarm batteries
  removed or dead, there is no warning.
- **Carbon monoxide**: one candle makes little, but a gas burner left on for warmth, many lamps
  in a closed room, or a smoky lamp adds CO and uses oxygen (→ 29-03).
- **Poisoning**: a child drinks lamp oil or kerosene from a lamp or a bottle that looks like juice.
  A small amount breathed into the lungs causes a severe chemical pneumonia; do **not** make the
  child vomit, call Poison Control (CDC MMWR, Near Fatal Ingestion of Household Lamp Oil, 1997;
  Poison Control, 1-800-222-1222; → 12-17).
- **Burns**: hot wax, a glass chimney, a pan on an unsteady flame.

## 4. What makes it safer

**Removes the risk**
- **Battery light**: flashlights, headlamps and battery lanterns, with spare batteries (→ 10-01).
- **Cook outdoors** in a sheltered spot (→ 07-01), or on electric appliances run from stored power
  (→ 07-06).

**Reduces it a lot**
- **Working smoke and CO alarms**, tested, with fresh batteries.
- **A fire extinguisher within reach** and an adult who knows how to use it (→ 29-12).
- **12 inches clear all round**, on a stable, non-flammable surface, out of reach of children and
  pets.
- **An adult in the room** the whole time; **out when you leave the room or sleep**; never in
  bedrooms or sleeping areas.
- For a gas range: cooking only, never for heat; a window cracked; burners off when the pan comes
  off.

**Reduces it a little**
- Candles in deep jars or holders; lamps with a glass chimney and a stable base.
- Lamp fuel stored locked, in its original labelled container, never in a drink bottle.
- One flame at a time.

## 5. The options

**`never-indoors`: No open flame indoors.**
You give up: candle and lamp light and match-lit stove cooking. You accept: needing enough
batteries or stored power for light, and cooking outside in all weather.

**`with-extinguisher-and-alarm`: Indoors with extinguisher, alarms and rules.**
You give up: the USFA's flashlights-only advice. You accept: a fire and CO risk controlled by
alarms, an extinguisher, clearance and an adult present at all times.
