# DC-11. Firing weapons on your own property for practice, pests or zeroing

**What this page is for.** When ranges are closed and gunsmiths are gone, a household may want
to fire on its own land: to practise, to "zero" (sight in) a rifle, or to kill pests. This card
explains what is beyond the target, why every safety rule is built around it, and what the
household decides.

> **SAFETY.** Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products
> (floor, → 30-01). New to firearms? Read → 29-11 before this card.

## 1. The thing, in plain words

A **backstop** is whatever stops the bullet after it passes the target: a thick earth bank, a
hillside, a purpose-built berm. A fence, a wooden shed, a tree or a snowbank is not a backstop.
**Downrange** means the direction the gun points, all the way to where the bullet could land.
A **ricochet** is a bullet bouncing off a hard or flat surface (water, rock, frozen ground,
metal) in a direction nobody planned. **Zeroing** means adjusting a sight so the bullet hits
where you aim.

## 2. The rule, and why it was set

- The universal firearms safety rules include **be sure of your target and what is beyond
  it** (NSSF, Firearm safety: rules for safe gun handling).
- Shooting ranges are built with berms and baffles and set distances from houses for this
  reason. Many towns ban discharge inside limits.

**Why:** a bullet does not stop because it missed. NSSF: "Once a gun fires, you have given up all
control over where the shot will go or what it will strike." It gives the distances: even a .22
short bullet can travel over 1 1/4 miles, a high-velocity rifle cartridge such as a .30-06 more
than 3 miles, shotgun pellets 500 yards and shotgun slugs over half a mile (NSSF, Firearm safety:
rules for safe gun handling). SAAMI prints the same rule (SAAMI, Small Arms Ammunition). A bullet
can also pass through a target, an animal or a wall and keep going. The shooter rarely sees where
it lands.

> **Before the collapse:** your town and state set rules on where firearms may be discharged.
> Look them up while they are enforced. The backstop physics below does not change with the

## 3. How it actually hurts people

- **A miss or a pass-through** travels into a neighbour's wall, a car on the road or someone
  walking a field you cannot see.
- **A shot at a target above the horizon** (a squirrel in a tree, a bird on a roof) has no
  backstop at all; the bullet comes down somewhere far away.
- **A ricochet** off a frozen yard, a pond or a rock goes sideways or back toward people.
- **Noise**: "Exposure to shooting noise can damage hearing" (SAAMI), and hearing damaged by
  loud noise does not come back (CDC/NIOSH, About Noise and Hearing Loss).
- **Signalling**: in a tense neighbourhood, a shot says "this house is armed and occupied". It
  can start fear, rumours, or a response you did not want.

## 4. What makes it safer

**Removes the risk**
- **No live fire at home.** Practise dry, with an unloaded gun and no ammunition in the room
  (→ 14-15). Trap pests instead (→ 05-15). Zero and train at a real range or on open land
  with a proper backstop, away from houses.

**Reduces it a lot**
- **A known, solid earth backstop** that will stop the ammunition you use, high enough that a
  miss still hits it.
- **Never toward a house, road, path or livestock**, whatever the backstop.
- **Never at a target above the horizon**, and never over water, rock or frozen ground.
- **Nobody downrange.** Check before every string of shots.
- **Daylight only.**
- **Tell the neighbours beforehand** when and where you will shoot.

**Reduces it a little**
- Low-power, small-calibre ammunition for pests at short range.
- Hearing and eye protection for everyone nearby.
- Children kept well back and supervised by an adult who is not shooting.

## 5. The options

**`never`: Never on our property.**
You give up: practice, zeroing and pest control by firearm at home. You accept: less live-fire
practice and more reliance on traps and dry practice.

**`with-backstop-rule`: Only into a known backstop, nobody downrange, neighbours told.**
You give up: freedom to shoot where it is convenient. You accept: that the backstop and the
rules above are all that stand between a missed shot and a neighbour, and that every shot is
heard.
