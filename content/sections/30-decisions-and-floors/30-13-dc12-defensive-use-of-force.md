# DC-12. Defensive use of force: our household rule

**What this page is for.** This card is a worksheet, not a multiple choice. It gives the adults
the facts and the questions; they write the household's own rule, in their own words, now,
while they are calm. A rule written in advance is worth more than any judgement made at 3 a.m.

> **SAFETY.** Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products
> (floor, → 30-01). This page is not legal advice.

> **Before the collapse:** {{ home.state_name }} has its own self-defence laws, and they differ
> from state to state. Read them while they are available and enforced, and write the section
> numbers here: ______________. Nothing in this binder summarises them.

## 1. The thing, in plain words

**Use of force** is any physical action to stop another person: a shove, a locked door held
shut, pepper spray, a weapon. **Deadly force** is force likely to kill or cause serious injury,
which includes firing a gun at someone. **De-escalation** means calming or ending a
confrontation without force: a locked door, distance, light, a calm voice (→ 14-13).

## 2. The rule as lawful self-defence has always stated it, and why

Across traditions of lawful self-defence the test has four parts:
- **Imminence**: the threat is happening now, not feared, threatened or over.
- **Necessity**: nothing less would stop it (a door, a light, a warning, leaving).
- **Proportionality**: the force matches the threat. Deadly force answers only a threat of
  death or serious injury **to a person**.
- **Reasonableness**: a calm adult in the same place would have believed the same.

**Why these tests persist without anyone enforcing them:** a shot cannot be taken back, and
the people who live around you will still be there tomorrow.

## 3. How it actually hurts people

- **Misidentification.** A family member or neighbour coming in at night is shot because nobody
  identified them first.
- **Through the wall.** Bullets from ordinary rifles, shotguns and handguns can pass through
  interior and exterior walls into other rooms and other houses (NSSF: know what is beyond your
  target).
- **No surgeon.** With no hospital, a gunshot wound, to the other person or to you if it goes
  wrong, is likely fatal. Bleeding control buys time, not a cure (→ 12-02).
- **Over property.** A household that shoots someone over a fuel can or a generator becomes the
  household the neighbourhood fears and turns against (→ 18-01).
- **Children watching.** What children see at the door stays with them (CDC, Helping Children
  Cope During and After a Disaster; → 21-03).

## 4. What makes it safer

**Removes most confrontations**
- Locked, hardened doors and lights (→ 14-02). Not looking like the best-stocked house on the
  street (→ 14-03). Good relations with neighbours (→ 18-01).

**Reduces harm a lot**
- **Avoid, then de-escalate**: stay behind a locked door, use a light and a loud, calm voice,
  let a thief leave (→ 14-13).
- **Identify before anything else.** A light and a challenge word the family knows.
- **Know the safe directions** in your house: where a shot would go toward the fewest people.
- **Less-lethal tools** for lesser threats; pepper spray stops many people, though not all
  (National Institute of Justice, The Effectiveness and Safety of Pepper Spray).

**Reduces harm a little**
- Rehearse the rule out loud with every adult who may hold a weapon.
- A trauma kit and the training to use it, for anyone hurt (→ 12-02).
