# Using winter as a freezer, and where it does not work

**What this page is for.** In a cold winter, the outdoors, an unheated garage or the unheated
rooms of the house can keep food cold or frozen with no power at all. This page says how to do
that safely, and says plainly where and when it does **not** work.

> **CAUTION.** Outdoor temperature is not a thermostat. A sunny afternoon on a south wall can
> push food into the danger zone (40 to 140 °F) while the air is still below freezing.
> **Measure, do not assume.**
>
> **Floor:** **A frozen-then-thawed insulin vial or pen is discarded.** Loss of potency with no
> way to tell (→ 30-01). The cold that keeps food also destroys insulin and some other
> refrigerated medicines (→ 29-07).

## Do this now

1. **Put a thermometer wherever you store food in the cold.** A min/max thermometer (one that
   remembers the highest and lowest reading) tells you what actually happened at midday and
   overnight.
2. **Keep food at 40 °F or below at all times,** and at 0 °F or below if you are treating it as
   frozen (FoodSafety.gov; NCHFP, Freezing Foods).
3. **Store it in the shade, on the north side,** off the ground and out of the sun.
4. **Protect it from animals:** dogs, cats, raccoons, bears and rodents will find it.
5. **Throw out perishable food that has been above 40 °F for 2 hours or more** (→ 04-17).
6. **Keep insulin and other refrigerated medicines in a separate, warmer place,** with their own
   thermometer (→ 02-01, → 29-07).

## Does this work where you live?

**Winter cold storage only works while the food stays at 40 °F or below, every hour of every
day.** That means sustained cold, not the odd frost.

{{#if is.cold_winters}}**Your home has cold winters** (January average low below 20 °F). In
the coldest months, outdoor and unheated-garage storage can work as a freezer. The months either
side are unreliable: measure every day.{{else}}**Your home does not have reliably cold winters.**
Do not plan on winter as a freezer. Use outdoor cold only as a short-term refrigerator on
measured cold nights, and rely on shelf-stable methods (→ 04-05 to → 04-08) and any powered
cold you have (→ 04-17).{{/if}}

**Where it does not work:**

- **Anywhere daytime highs regularly climb above 40 °F in winter.** That covers most of the
  South, the Gulf and Atlantic coastal plains, the Pacific coast, the low deserts of the
  Southwest, and Hawaii. Food stored outside there spends hours in the danger zone most days.
- **Places with repeated freeze-thaw swings.** A winter that crosses freezing week after week is
  the dangerous case: food thaws above 40 °F, refreezes overnight, and **looks fine.** You will
  not know it happened unless a min/max thermometer tells you.
- **Any spot in direct sun,** even in a cold place.
- **Warm spells.** Sudden thaws and warm dry winds (chinooks east of the Rockies) can raise the
  temperature 40 °F or more in hours. Have a plan to cook or preserve fast.
- **Spring, everywhere.** It ends, often abruptly.

**In humid places the other problem is moisture.** Opened dried food picks up water fast in a
humid summer, and mould follows. Favour **sealed original packs and Mylar with oxygen
absorbers** (→ 04-03), reseal opened containers at once, and use opened packs sooner (→ 04-19).

## Where it works: what it gives you

In a place with reliable hard freezes, cold storage with no power lets you:

- **Freeze a whole deer** (→ 05-09) instead of racing to can it.
- **Stretch the contents of a fridge and freezer** when the grid fails (→ 04-17).
- **Keep cooked food, dairy and produce** cold with no fuel cost.

## The traps

| Trap | Why it bites | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Sun** | Direct sun on a container can lift it above freezing even at 20 °F air temperature | North side, shade, insulated container |
| **Thaw and refreeze** | Repeated cycles ruin quality and are a safety risk | An insulated container damps the swings; a min/max thermometer catches them |
| **Warm spells** | Swings of 40 to 50 °F in a day happen in some regions | Min/max thermometer; a plan to process food fast |
| **Animals** | Everything wants your meat | Secure, raised, hard-sided container |
| **"Cold enough"** | Air temperature is not food temperature | Measure the **food,** not the air |
| **Spring** | It ends, often suddenly | Have the food preserved (→ 04-05, → 04-06) first |

## Doing it properly

- **Use insulated containers (coolers) outside.** It sounds backwards, but insulation buffers
  the swings in both directions.
- **North side of the house, shaded, raised** off the ground, secured against animals.
- **An unheated garage or porch** is often better than fully outdoors: darker, steadier, and
  harder for animals.
- **The unheated part of the house** works as a refrigerator once you heat only one room
  (→ 08-01).
- **Freeze in meal-sized portions** so you never thaw more than you will eat.
- **Wrap well.** Dry winter air causes freezer burn (a quality problem, not a safety one).

## Food cold, medicine not frozen

{{#if has.refrigerated_meds}}**Someone in your household takes a refrigerated medicine.** {{/if}}This
page and the medicine pages pull in opposite directions. **Food wants to be frozen. Insulin must
never freeze;** freezing destroys it and it can look normal afterwards (FDA, Information
Regarding Insulin Storage). Keep food and medicine in **completely different places,** each with
its own thermometer. Do not let "the cold room" become one shared storage idea. → 02-01, → 29-07.

## When it thaws

- **At 40 °F or below the whole time:** still safe; use it or re-preserve it.
- **Above 40 °F for 2 hours or more:** throw out perishables (meat, poultry, fish, eggs,
  leftovers). Do not taste to check (→ 04-17).
- **Partly thawed but still with ice crystals and at 40 °F or below:** generally safe to
  refreeze, with some loss of quality (FoodSafety.gov).
- **When in doubt, throw it out.** Food poisoning without a clinic is a medical emergency
  (→ 12-05). How strictly your household applies the published rules when food is short is a
  decision card: {{choice DC-14}} → 30-15.

## The end of the season

**Winter storage has a hard deadline.** Before the thaw, convert anything you want to keep into a
shelf-stable form: **pressure canned** (→ 04-05), **dried** (→ 04-06), **cured** (→ 04-07) or
**fermented** (→ 04-08). Plan that conversion **before** the thaw, not during it: a warm week
with a deer in the garage is a crisis.

## Gear and supplies

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Min/max thermometers** (several) | The whole safety case rests on measuring | One per storage spot |
| Coolers or insulated containers | Buffer swings outdoors | Hard-sided, animal-resistant |
| Hard-sided secure bins or a chest | Animal protection | Latching |
| Heavy freezer wrap, bags, butcher paper | Prevent freezer burn | In bulk |
| Labels and marker | Contents, date, portion | Not optional |
| A shaded north-side spot, cleared | Site preparation | Free |
| A plan and gear to make food shelf-stable | Spring is a deadline | → 04-05 to → 04-08 |

**If you don't have it:**

- **Snow** packed around a container is good insulation and cooling.
- A buried or snow-banked cooler stays very steady.
- An unheated garage, porch, shed or the north side of the house.
- Any hard container with a weight on the lid keeps most animals out.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** this is **seasonal, not used up.** {{#if is.cold_winters}}At your home, roughly the
  coldest months; the shoulder months are unreliable, so measure.{{else}}At your home, little or
  none: plan on other methods.{{/if}}
- **Ration:** none, but do not let free cold make you lazy about preserving. It ends.
- **Resupply:** the shelf-stable methods (→ 04-05 to → 04-08) carry food through the rest of the
  year.
- **The yearly rhythm (cold climates):** hunt and slaughter in autumn and early winter, hold in
  winter cold, convert to shelf-stable before the thaw, live on stores through summer, grow and
  preserve through the season (→ 25-01).

## Phase notes

- **A (sheltering at home):** garage, north side, and the unheated part of the house. Watch for
  animals and for neighbours noticing (→ 14-03).
- **B (moving):** frozen food does not travel; dried and canned food does.
- **C (rural or at a destination):** often more space, outbuildings and a real cellar (→ 04-08).
  Check the destination's climate against the list above before counting on it.
