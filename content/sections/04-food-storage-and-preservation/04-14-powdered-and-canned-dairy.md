# Dairy: powdered, canned and shelf-stable

**What this page is for.** Which kinds of milk and dairy keep without a fridge, how long each
keeps, which ones young children need, and why none of them replaces infant formula.

> **CAUTION.** **Powdered or canned milk is not infant formula.** Giving a baby under 12 months
> improvised milk instead of breast milk or formula is dangerous. The infant feeding plan is on
> → 02-13 and → 20-05. **Children 12 to 23 months drink plain whole milk, not reduced-fat** (CDC,
> Foods and Drinks for 12 to 24 Month Olds), so their stored milk is **whole-milk powder or UHT
> (long-life boxed) whole milk, not the nonfat powder** that keeps longest. **Never home-can any
> dairy** (below).

## Do this now

1. **Stock powdered milk.** It is the most storable dairy, and it makes stored grain far more
   appealing to children.
2. **If you have a child 12 to 23 months old,** stock **whole-milk powder and/or UHT whole milk**
   for them, label it, and rotate it. The nonfat powder is for everyone else.
   {{#if has.toddler}}**You have a child in this age range.**{{/if}}
3. **If you have a baby under 12 months,** stock **infant formula,** not milk, even if the baby
   is breastfed (as a backup), and follow → 02-13 and → 20-05. Formula's use-by date matters
   (→ 04-10). {{#if has.infant}}**You have a child under 2 in the house.**{{/if}}
4. **Check labels for allergens.** Milk is one of the nine major allergens; dairy blends and
   "non-dairy" creamers often contain soy or tree nuts (→ 02-04).
5. **Store cool, dark and sealed.** Fat content limits shelf life.
6. **Rotate.** Dairy is not a decades item, apart from nonfat powder (→ 04-10).

## The options

Heat is what shortens every one of these: keep them as cool as you can (→ 04-03). Rotate by the
date on the package; the order below is longest-keeping first.

| Product | How it keeps (cool, sealed) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Nonfat dry milk** | The longest: years, when sealed, dry and cool | The storage champion: no fat to go rancid. Not for children 12 to 23 months as their main milk |
| **Whole dry milk** | Shorter (the fat goes rancid) | Better taste. The toddler's powder; rotate it |
| **Evaporated milk (canned)** | Use the can's date | Ready to use; heavy |
| **Sweetened condensed milk** | Use the can's date | Sugar preserves it; morale food |
| **UHT boxed milk** (long-life) | Months: the shortest | Tastes closest to fresh. Whole UHT is the toddler's other option |
| **Commercially canned or powdered butter, ghee** | Use the maker's date | → 04-12 |
| **Hard cheese, wax-sealed** | Months | Keeps without a fridge surprisingly well |
| **Powdered cheese, powdered buttermilk** | Years | Flavour for plain food |

**Never home-can butter, milk or cheese.** There is no tested process for it (NCHFP); the fat
shields botulism spores from the heat. **Floor:** **Low-acid foods are pressure-canned to the
published time and pressure, or not canned.** Botulism has no field treatment and the food looks
and smells normal (→ 30-01).

**Nonfat dry milk is the backbone:** sealed, it keeps for decades, and it reconstitutes for
drinking, baking and cooking.

## Why it matters with children

- **Calcium and protein** for growth.
- **Fat for toddlers.** From 12 to 23 months, whole milk, not reduced-fat, is the rule (CDC).
  Log the toddler's whole-milk stock as theirs on → 22-04.
- **Familiarity.** Small children under stress eat familiar things. Milk on oatmeal turns stored
  grain into breakfast rather than a fight (→ 20-03).
- **Calories** in a form children accept.
- **Morale.** Hot chocolate in a cold house is worth far more than its calories (→ 21-02).

## The allergen check

If anyone in the house has a food allergy (→ 02-04):

- **Milk itself** is a major allergen. A milk-allergic person needs a different calcium and
  protein plan; ask their doctor while you can.
- **Blends and mixes** (hot chocolate, creamers, "milk alternatives") often contain **soy** or
  **tree nuts.**
- **Plain nonfat dry milk** is usually a single ingredient: the easiest to check.
- **Keep the label** when you repackage (→ 04-03).

## Using it

- **Mix it ahead and chill it.** Powdered milk tastes much better cold and after a few hours'
  rest.
- **Cook with it** if people will not drink it: oatmeal, gravy, baking, soup.
- **Use it to enrich plain food:** a scoop of dry milk in bread, porridge or soup adds protein and
  calories.
- **Reconstituted milk is perishable.** Treat it exactly like fresh milk: the 40 °F and 2-hour
  rule applies (→ 04-17). Whether to use dairy that has been warm, or past its date, when food is
  short is a decision card: {{choice DC-14}} → 30-15.

## Is dairy renewable?

- **Not realistically in town.** A dairy animal does not work on a small lot.
- **Goats** are the small-scale option at a rural site (Section 05): a real long-term dairy source,
  and a serious daily commitment.
- **So at home, dairy is a stored, finite resource.** Plan it that way and favour the long-life
  forms.

## Gear and supplies

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Nonfat dry milk (bulk)** | Decades of storage | Repackage as on → 04-03 |
| **Whole-milk powder and/or UHT whole milk** | For children 12 to 23 months | Rotate by the package date; label it |
| **Infant formula** (if there is a baby under 12 months) | The only safe substitute for breast milk | → 02-13, → 20-05; mind the use-by date |
| Evaporated and condensed milk (cans) | Ready to use; morale | Rotate |
| Shelf-stable boxed milk | Closest to fresh | Short life: rotate hard |
| Powdered cheese, buttermilk | Flavour for plain food | Small, big effect |
| Ghee or **commercially** canned butter | Fat and flavour | → 04-12; never home-canned dairy |
| Airtight containers and oxygen absorbers | Dry milk keeps far longer sealed | → 04-03 |
| Whisk or shaker jar | Mixing without lumps | Small but useful |

**If you don't have it:**

- **Hard, waxed cheeses** keep for months without a fridge.
- Canned milk is common in pantries and stores.
- **For cooking,** dry milk replaces fresh milk in nearly every recipe.
- **Do not improvise infant nutrition.** → 02-13 and → 20-05. For a toddler, the shelf substitute
  is whole-milk powder or UHT whole milk, not nonfat.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** pounds of dry milk ÷ use. It multiplies comfort and nutrition more than calories,
  but it is what makes the calorie base edible for children.
- **Ration:** many households keep it for the children and for cooking, not for adults' drinks.
- **Resupply:** trade (→ 18-02); goats at a rural site; otherwise accept that it is
  finite.
- **The gap it leaves:** calcium and vitamin D. Sprouts (→ 04-16), greens (→ 05-01), canned
  fish with soft bones (if no one is allergic to fish), and bone broth from game (→ 05-09) help.

## Phase notes

- **A (sheltering at home):** stock deep now; it is cheap and keeps well.
- **B (moving):** dry milk is light and dense; worth carrying for children.
- **C (rural or at a destination):** goats become possible; until then, the same stored supply.
