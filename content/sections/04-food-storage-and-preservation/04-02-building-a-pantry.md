# Building a shelf-stable pantry

**What this page is for.** How to build a store of food that keeps without a fridge, in the
right order, on a budget, and in a way that it actually gets eaten and replaced. "Shelf-stable"
means sealed food that keeps at room temperature: cans, jars, boxes and dry goods.

> **CAUTION.** The pantry that saves you is the one you **actually eat from**. A wall of
> unfamiliar buckets you never open is a museum, not a food supply: you will not know how to
> cook it, whether your family will eat it, or whether **everyone in the house can safely eat
> it** (→ 02-04). Build a deep version of your normal diet, and rotate through it.

## Do this now

1. **Buy what you already eat,** in the forms you already cook. Then buy more of it.
2. **Check every label against every allergy in the house** before you buy in bulk (below).
3. **Store the deep stock where you can see it** and use it. Out of sight is out of rotation.
4. **Date everything on purchase,** in marker, on the item.
5. **Add a little each shopping trip** rather than one big buy. It is cheaper and builds the
   habit.
6. **Buy two manual can openers.** It is the single most forgotten item.

## Build it in layers

| Layer | Target | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Working pantry** | 1 to 2 weeks | Normal food, just more of it. Deep and familiar |
| **2. Extended** | 1 month | Canned goods, pasta, rice, oil, shelf-stable milk |
| **3. Bulk staples** | 3 months or more | Grains, beans, flour, sugar, salt in proper storage (→ 04-03) |
| **4. Preserving equipment** | Ongoing | Jars, lids, salt, a canner, a dehydrator (→ 04-04 to → 04-08) |
| **5. Production** | Years | Seeds, garden, animals (Section 05) |

**Most people jump to layer 3 and skip layers 1 and 2.** That is backwards. The first two
weeks are the emergency you are most likely to face, and they cost the least to prepare for.
Your household's two-week calorie number is on → 04-01.

## The allergen check: do this before you buy in bulk

US food labels must name nine major allergens: **milk, egg, fish, shellfish, tree nuts,
peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame** (FDA, Food Allergies). Sesame was added most recently.
How to read the ingredient list and the "Contains" line: → 29-14.

Traps that catch people:

- **"Vegetable oil"** in the US is usually **soybean oil.** Highly refined oils are treated
  differently under the labelling law, so ask the allergist whether a refined oil is safe for
  your allergic person before you stock it (→ 04-12).
- **Soy lecithin** is in a huge range of packaged food, especially anything with chocolate.
- **Sesame** turns up in breads, buns, crackers, seasoning blends and hummus.
- **Egg** is in pasta, baked goods, mayonnaise and many prepared mixes.
- **An allergen that is not one of the nine never appears in a "Contains" line.** Read the
  whole ingredient list for it.
- **Most freeze-dried "bucket" systems and ration bars contain soy, and many contain egg.** Do
  not buy a year of food an allergic person cannot eat. → 04-19 has a product-by-product check.
- **Recipes change without notice.** A product that was safe last year is not checked this
  year. Read the label on the package in your hand.

**Keep a photo or the cut-out label** of anything you repackage (→ 04-03). Feeding an allergic
person unlabelled, traded or scavenged food is a decision card: {{choice DC-18}} → 30-19.

## The rotation habit

**First in, first out** (FIFO): the oldest food gets eaten first. The mechanics matter more
than the intention:

- **Date everything** on purchase, in large marker, where you can read it.
- **Shelve new behind old,** so the oldest is what your hand reaches.
- **Cook from the deep stock** on a regular rotation, and replace what you use.
- **Run a "pantry week"** every few months: cook only from stores. It finds what has expired,
  what nobody will eat, and what you do not know how to cook (→ 04-10).

## What actually keeps

| Food | Realistic shelf life | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Salt, sugar, honey | **Indefinite** | Buy heavily (→ 04-13) |
| White rice, dried beans, wheat berries | Decades, properly stored | → 04-03 |
| Rolled oats, pasta | Years, properly stored | → 04-03 |
| Flour | Months; whole-grain flour least | Its oil goes rancid; store grain instead (→ 04-03) |
| Canned goods | Years, if the can is sound | Safe as long as the seal is unbroken and the can was never frozen; best quality to the code date, usually 2 to 5 years from manufacture; high-acid food (tomatoes, fruit) keeps its quality for less time (Utah State University Extension, Storing Canned Goods) |
| Cooking oil | About 1 to 2 years unopened | Rancidity is the limit (NDSU Extension; → 04-12) |
| Powdered milk | Years | → 04-14 |
| Freeze-dried | Long, if stored cool | Manufacturer claims assume cool storage (→ 04-19) |
| Spices | Years (flavour fades) | Morale value (→ 21-02) |

Dented, rusted, bulging or leaking cans: → 04-10 and → 04-17.

## Money and pacing

- **A little every trip** beats one large purchase. It spreads the cost and builds the habit.
- **Buy sales in bulk** on things that keep: salt, rice, canned goods.
- **Prioritise by what fails first:** water (→ 03-02), then calories, then variety.
- **Do not buy anything you have not cooked.** Test it first.

## Gear and supplies

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sturdy shelving in a cool, dark place | What you can see gets rotated | A basement or interior closet is usually best |
| Permanent markers | Date everything | Buy a pack |
| Airtight containers, food-grade buckets | Bulk storage (→ 04-03) | Screw-top ("gamma") lids for working buckets |
| **Two manual can openers** | The most forgotten item | One always fails |
| Written inventory sheet | You cannot plan what you have not counted | → 22-04 |
| Saved labels for repackaged food | Allergen checking | Tape inside the lid |
| A separate, labelled shelf for any allergy-safe food | Physical separation | → 02-04 |

**If you don't have it:**

- Any shelf, any cool dark corner, any sealed container.
- **Single-ingredient staples in original sealed packaging** are the best thing to acquire:
  cheap, easy to check, and the safest for anyone with an allergy.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** see → 04-01. Track the household total and any restricted diet's separate number.
- **Ration:** eat perishables first, then the working pantry, then bulk staples. Where one
  person eats an allergy-safe diet, whether their safe stock is shared by everyone or held as
  their reserve is a decision card: sharing shortens their runway, reserving means everyone
  else eats other food first. {{choice DC-26}} → 30-27. Write it on the allergy plan (→ 02-04).
- **Resupply:** preserving (→ 04-04 to → 04-08) and production (Section 05).
- **The rotation failure:** the most common way a stockpile fails is quietly expiring while
  unused. A pantry you cook from never has that problem.

## Phase notes

- **A (sheltering at home):** build it now: this is the cheapest, easiest phase.
- **B (moving):** you take days of food, not months: dense, no-cook, safe for everyone
  (→ 01-03).
- **C (rural or at a destination):** the pantry seeds the new site; bring staples and seeds,
  leave the shelving.
