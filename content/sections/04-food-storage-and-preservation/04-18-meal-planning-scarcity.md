# Meal planning and cooking from stores

**What this page is for.** How to plan a small set of meals that your household can cook from
stored food every day, using little fuel, that everyone in the house can safely eat, and that
people will keep eating for months.

> **CAUTION.** In most households several limits meet at every meal: **fuel** (→ 06-04),
> **anyone's food allergy or restricted diet** (→ 02-04), and, if anyone has diabetes,
> **steady carbohydrate** (→ 02-01, → 02-05). A meal plan built around only one of them fails
> on the others. Plan them all at once, now, while it is a planning exercise and not a nightly
> crisis.

## Do this now

1. **Build a rotation of 7 to 10 meals** you can cook from stores, that everyone eats and
   everyone can safely eat.
2. **If anyone has diabetes, write the carbohydrate per serving on each recipe.** For them it
   is dosing information (→ 02-01, → 02-05).
3. **Cook once a day, in a batch.** Every fire lit is fuel spent (→ 06-04).
4. **Test the rotation now** with a pantry week (→ 04-10), cooking outdoors on your backup
   stove or fire if you can.
5. **Keep one "everyone's favourite" meal** in reserve for a bad day. Morale is a system
   (→ 21-02).

## The limits, and why they point the same way

| Limit | What it asks for |
|---|---|
| **Fuel** | Batch cooking, long-simmer foods finished in a haybox, few separate cooking sessions (→ 07-02) |
| **Allergies and restricted diets** | Every ingredient checked; the restricted food cooked first, in its own cookware (→ 02-04, → 02-19) |
| **Diabetes** | A known, repeatable amount of carbohydrate per serving so insulin or medicine works predictably (→ 02-01, → 02-05) |

**The good news:** these point the same way, toward **a small rotation of simple, repeated,
one-pot meals made from single-ingredient staples.** Repetition is efficient, easy to check and
predictable. It is also monotonous, which is what the morale ideas below are for.

## A workable rotation

Built from staples (→ 04-03). Check each one against every allergy in your house and swap
ingredients where needed.

| Meal | Base | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Beans and rice** | Beans, rice, fat, salt, spice | The backbone. Haybox-friendly (kidney-bean rule below) |
| **Lentil soup** | Lentils, dried vegetables, fat | Fast, forgiving, good for children |
| **Porridge** | Oats, dry milk, sugar | Warm breakfast; morale (→ 04-14) |
| **Flatbread** | Flour, water, salt, fat | No yeast needed (→ 07-04) |
| **Stew with canned or preserved meat** | Meat, roots, grain | Uses home-canned meat (→ 04-05) |
| **Rice pudding** | Rice, dry milk, sugar | Dessert from staples; worth its weight in morale |
| **Potato or root hash** | Stored roots, fat | Seasonal (→ 04-08) |
| **Sprouts, steamed or stirred into the soup pot** | Sprouts (→ 04-16) | The winter fresh food. Cooked for children, older adults, anyone pregnant and anyone with a weakened immune system (FDA) |
| **Soup from dried vegetables** | Dried garden produce (→ 04-06) | Softens while it cooks |

**If anyone has diabetes, write the carbohydrate per serving next to each one.** Do it now, with
a scale and a printed reference, not later by candlelight.

## Cooking with less fuel

- **One fire, many jobs** (→ 06-02): the meal, tomorrow's beans, boiled water, hot-water bottles.
- **Soak beans and grains first.** It cuts cooking time a great deal.
- **Haybox finish** (an insulated box that keeps a hot pot cooking, → 07-02): bring the pot to
  a hard boil, then insulate for hours. Nearly free.
  - **Dry kidney beans and other dry beans of the same family** (red, white, pinto, black):
    **soak at least 5 hours, throw the soak water away, and boil in fresh water for at least
    30 minutes BEFORE the box.** Never from raw in a haybox alone; a low, slow cook does not get
    hot enough to destroy the bean toxin (FDA, Bad Bug Book; → 04-03).
  - **Hold rule:** check with a thermometer before serving. Food still above 140 °F is fine;
    food that has just dropped below it is **reheated to 165 °F**; food that has sat below
    140 °F for **more than 2 hours** is thrown out (CDC, as on → 07-02). The box is a cooker,
    not a warmer for ever.
- **Cook once a day, eat twice;** reheat small portions on little fuel.
- **Lid on, windbreak up:** the two cheapest savings you have (→ 07-01).

## If anyone has diabetes

- **Steady beats varied** for dosing, especially without a glucose monitor (→ 02-01, → 02-02).
- **Write the carbohydrate on the pot,** or keep a card with the rotation's counts.
- **Keep fast sugar for treating lows separate and protected,** never dependent on what is
  cooking.
- **Sick days change everything.** When the person cannot eat normally, CDC's sick-day guidance
  is **about 50 grams of carbohydrate every 4 hours** in a form they can keep down, such as
  1½ cups of unsweetened applesauce or 1½ cups of fruit juice (CDC, Managing Sick Days). The rest of the sick-day plan is
  on → 02-01 or → 02-05. **Stock those foods specifically** and do not let them be eaten as treats.

## If anyone has a food allergy or restricted diet

- **Cook the allergic person's portion first,** in their own cookware, before other foods come
  into the kitchen (→ 02-04).
- **Simplify:** if the whole household eats the same safe meal, most of the problem goes away.
  That is a strong reason to build the rotation around what the most restricted person can eat.
- **Label leftovers** clearly; a mislabelled container is an exposure.
- Celiac disease (gluten): → 02-19.

## Morale: not optional over months

- **Keep spices, sugar and something sweet.** They cost little to store and transform dull food
  (→ 04-13, → 21-02).
- **Mark occasions.** Birthdays, holidays, a set day of the week: a different meal on a known day
  gives the week a shape (→ 21-04).
- **Let children help.** A child stirring, kneading flatbread or rinsing sprouts is busy rather
  than anxious (→ 20-07).
- **Hot drinks** in a cold house are worth far more than their calories.

## Gear and supplies

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Written meal rotation, with carbohydrate counts if needed | Fuel, allergies and dosing solved in advance | Laminate it |
| Spices, salt, sugar, cocoa | Morale and flavour | Cheap; big effect (→ 04-13) |
| Haybox (or a cooler and blankets) | Fuel-free finishing | → 07-02 |
| Large pot and Dutch oven | Batch cooking | → 07-04 |
| **Separate cookware for any allergic person** | Avoids cross-contact | → 02-04 |
| Sick-day carbohydrate (juice, applesauce, regular soda), if anyone has diabetes | Sick days | A protected stock |
| Kitchen scale and a printed carbohydrate reference | Counting without a phone | Keep with the rotation |
| Insulated containers | Keep food hot for staggered meals | Also → 07-02 |

**If you don't have it:**

- Almost any one-pot mix of grain, legume, fat and salt is nutritionally sound.
- Dried vegetables and sprouts add nutrition to anything.
- Wild game and foraged food extend the rotation (Section 05), with your allergy rules applied.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** this page does not use anything up; it decides **how fast everything else goes.**
  Good meal planning stretches both food (→ 04-01) and fuel (→ 06-04).
- **Ration:** many households shrink adult portions before children's. Keep a diabetic's
  carbohydrate steady rather than cutting it. Where one person eats an allergy-safe diet,
  whether their safe stock is shared or held as their reserve is a decision card: sharing
  shortens their runway, reserving means everyone else eats other food first.
  {{choice DC-26}} → 30-27 (→ 02-04).
- **Resupply:** garden, game and trade (Section 05, → 18-02).
- **The combined effect:** batch cooking plus a haybox plus lids on pots can cut cooking fuel by
  more than half. Over a winter, that is a lot of wood (→ 06-04).

## Phase notes

- **A (sheltering at home):** set the rotation and the daily cooking rhythm early; it gives the
  day a structure, which helps morale (→ 21-02).
- **B (moving):** no-cook and one-pot only; restricted-diet food carried separately (→ 01-03).
- **C (rural or at a destination):** the same rotation, plus whatever the site produces.
