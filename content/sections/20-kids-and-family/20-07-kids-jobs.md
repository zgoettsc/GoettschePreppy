# Real jobs for children

**What this page is for.** A child with a real job is happier, calmer and safer, and over time a
genuinely useful member of the household. Contribution protects against fear and helplessness
(→ 20-08, → 21-02), builds skill, and keeps a child near an adult instead of bored and wandering
(→ 20-02). This page lists jobs that fit each age and how to hand them out.

> **SAFETY. Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products**
> (floor, → 30-01). That holds however busy the adults are. **Which dangerous jobs a child may do
> (fire, fuel, blades, power tools, large animals, water, chemicals), at what age, with what
> training and supervision, is the household's own written rule** on the DC-24 worksheet
> (→ 30-25). Nothing on this page overrides it. {{choice DC-24}} → 30-25.
>
> **Infants and toddlers have no jobs. They have supervision.**

## Do this now

1. **Give each child old enough two real jobs this week**: things that need doing, that they can
   do, and that visibly matter. Not busywork; children can tell the difference.
2. **Praise the contribution specifically.** "You filled the kindling basket, so we have a fire
   tonight" beats "good job."
3. **Put the jobs on the daily routine** (→ 20-03) so they are expected, not requested.
4. **Give them tools that are theirs:** a small basket, a headlamp (→ 10-01), a whistle (→ 15-07).
   Ownership makes it real.
5. **Fill in the DC-24 worksheet** (→ 30-25) with every adult, before the first dangerous job.

## Why this matters more than it sounds

In a crisis, children are usually on the receiving end of adult worry. That is frightening and
boring, and it produces the behaviour that makes everything harder. **A child with a job is:**
- **Less frightened**: competence protects against fear (→ 20-08, → 21-01).
- **Less bored**: less friction, fewer bad days.
- **Near an adult**: the safety control that matters (→ 20-02).
- **Learning real skills** at the age when skills stick (→ 20-03, → 24-01).
- **Genuinely helpful**, more so every year.
- **Part of the household**, which matters over a long event.

## Jobs by age

CDC's tips for parents of preschoolers include letting the child help with simple chores (CDC,
Positive Parenting Tips: Preschoolers). Match each job to the child's age, size, strength
and judgement, with training and supervision. The tables below are safe-by-default examples; the
household's own worksheet decides anything with a hazard.

### Infant and toddler (under 3)

- **No jobs, only supervision** (→ 20-02).
- **They can be present** while work happens: carried, in a carrier, or in a safe gated area.
  Being near working adults is good for them.
- **Around 2, they want to "help".** Let them, safely and in trivial ways: carrying a diaper or
  a spoon, putting twigs in a basket. That is how the next stage starts.

### Preschool (3 to 5)

| Job | Why it works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Fill the kindling basket** | Real, visible, needed daily | Kindling only, never near the fire |
| **Feed and water small animals** with an adult | Useful; children love it | Handwashing after (→ 13-02, CDC); eggs and allergies per → 02-04 |
| **Water plants** with a small can | Real garden work | Never at a pool, pond or barrel |
| **Sort and put away**: cutlery, socks, seeds by size | Real, and a sorting lesson | → 20-03 |
| **Set the table; simple food prep**: washing vegetables, stirring cold food, tearing greens | Real kitchen work | Allergy rules apply (→ 02-04) |
| **Fetch named things** for an adult | Small errands; learns the house | |
| **Hold the flashlight** | Useful, and taken seriously | → 10-01 |
| **Help with a baby**: fetch a diaper, entertain | Real help | **Never unsupervised care** |
| **Sweep; wipe surfaces** with a damp cloth | Real work | Cloths, not chemicals |
| **Read the thermometer and report** | A role in the plan | → 17-04 |
| **Carry their own small pack** | Ownership; matters on the move | → 22-03 |
| **Cross off the day on the calendar** | Marks time | → 21-06 |

### School-age (6 to 12)

Everything above, more reliably, plus jobs that build toward adult skills:
- **Water carrying with small jugs** filled for them (never at an open source); then their own
  run, once the habit is shown.
- **A garden row of their own**, planted to harvest (→ 05-01).
- **Dish duty**, with an adult handling knives (→ 13-10).
- **Animal chores as their own routine** (→ 05-04, → 05-05).
- **Laundry by hand**, wringing and hanging (→ 13-04).
- **Watching the fire with an adult and reporting**; building and lighting only as the worksheet
  allows (→ 30-25, → 06-01).
- **Knife work for food or carving**, taught with an adult every time, under the worksheet
  (→ 16-01).
- **Simple repairs and sewing** (→ 16-04, → 16-05).
- **Keeping a log**: water level, weather, rain gauge (→ 25-05).

### Teen (13 to 17)

Teens can do most adult work once trained, and in a long event their work matters.
- **Wood: splitting and sawing**, after training and under the worksheet (→ 16-06; OSHA, Hand and
  Power Tools). Chainsaws and power tools are a separate line on the worksheet.
- **Cooking whole meals** for the household (→ 07-01).
- **Water treatment** with an adult checking (→ 03-04).
- **Radio watch and the comms log** (→ 15-02, → 15-04).
- **First aid** once trained (→ 12-01).
- **Caring for younger children** for set periods, with a named adult nearby.
- **Teaching younger children** a skill they have (→ 18-07).
- **Fuel, firearms, butchering and large animals** only by the worksheet's rule (→ 30-25), firearms
  also by → 14-14 and → 29-11.

**Unlock by demonstrated habit, not by birthday.** A birthday is when an adult **starts teaching**
the next thing, with the child, every time. Doing it alone comes only after the habit has been
shown over weeks. A careful 7-year-old earns things a careless 10-year-old does not. Review the
worksheet on each child's birthday and at the yearly review (→ 25-10).

**Two lines that never move on a birthday alone:** open water (pools, ponds, wells, barrels) and
firearms. Those follow their own pages (→ 20-02, → 14-14) and the worksheet.

## What young children must not do

These hold however busy the adults are (→ 20-02), unless the household's worksheet says otherwise
for an older child, with training and an adult beside them:
- **Nothing at a pool, pond, rain barrel or open water container.**
- **Nothing involving fire, heaters, the stove, hot liquids or lamps.**
- **No tools with edges:** knives, axes, saws (→ 16-01, → 16-06).
- **No access to fuel, chemicals, lye, bleach or medicines:** they stay locked away whatever
  the worksheet says (floor, → 30-01; → 06-06, → 13-09, → 12-07). A supervised fuel job for an
  older child exists only if the worksheet names it (→ 30-25).
- **No access to firearms** (floor, → 30-01; → 14-14).
- **No unsupervised care of a baby or toddler.**
- **No going outside alone** in cold, dark or unfamiliar ground (→ 11-02).
- **No handling human waste or the toilet system** (→ 13-01).
- **No preparing food for an allergic person** (→ 02-04).

## How to give a job so it works

- **Make it real.** Children spot busywork at once and it does none of the good.
- **Make it visible.** A full basket, watered plants, fed animals.
- **Make it theirs.** "The kindling basket is your job" beats "can you help with the wood."
- **Same job, same time, most days** (→ 20-03).
- **Accept imperfect work.** A job done badly by a child is worth more than one done perfectly by
  an adult, because the point is the child. Redoing it in front of them undoes the benefit.
- **Praise the effort specifically**, and connect it to the household.
- **Grow the jobs as they grow.**

## The adult side

- **It is slower.** Everything takes longer with a child helping. Budget for it; the alternative
  is a bored child interrupting anyway.
- **It is supervision**: a helping child is a located child.
- **It is morale for adults too** (→ 21-02). Working beside your children is one of the good
  parts of a hard time.
- **Do not overload.** The jobs are small; play is most of a young child's day. A child made
  responsible for adult-sized worry is being harmed, not helped (→ 20-08). Teens need time off
  and time with friends too.

**Scavenge or substitute:** every item can be improvised: a small basket, a cut jug as a watering
can, a cloth bag as a pack. **The point is that it is theirs.** The jobs cost nothing. Praise and
attention are the real inputs.

## When it runs out

- **This does not run out; it grows.** Every month a child can do more. A year into a long event,
  they are really contributing.
- **What wears out is adult patience with the slowness.** Months in, letting a child "help" feels
  like an obstacle. That is exactly when it matters most (→ 21-02).
- **When a job stops working, change it.** Interest fades; rotate jobs and add new ones.
- **The long view:** a child who spends a hard year learning to work and be useful comes out of it
  more capable and more confident.

## By phase

- **A (shelter in place):** start now, while it is easy and stakes are low. Build the jobs into the
  routine (→ 20-03) so they survive into harder times.
- **B (moving):** **a young child's job is carrying their own small pack, holding a hand and
  staying close.** That is a real job. Teens carry real loads and watch younger children at stops
  (→ 01-03, → 15-03).
- **C (establish):** the richest place for this: animals, garden, building, real work everywhere.
  **Re-teach the hazards at the new site first** (→ 20-02, → 20-04), then hand out the jobs.
