# Keeping children safe in a house without the grid

**What this page is for.** Going without power brings back every household hazard that modern
life engineered away: a heater in the room where everyone lives, open fire for cooking, flame
for light, fuel on the property, hand tools, and buckets of standing water everywhere. This
page lists those hazards in order of how fast they kill, and the controls that work when the
adults are tired and busy.

> **SAFETY. Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine
> products.** This is a floor, not a choice (→ 30-01): every source agrees, small children
> explore by mouth and by hand, and in a crisis the adults are busier and supervision is thinner,
> which is exactly when storage has to do the work. Lock firearms unloaded, with ammunition
> locked separately (AAP; → 14-14). Store fuel, medicine and chlorine locked, out of sight and
> reach, in their original labelled containers, never in drink bottles.
>
> **Two hazards dominate, and both kill fast and silently:**
> **(1) Drowning.** A small child can drown in as little as 2 inches of water, quickly and
> silently (CPSC, In-Home Drowning); CDC says drowning happens in seconds and is often silent
> (CDC, Preventing Drowning): a bucket, a tub, a rain barrel, a cooler, a pool.
> **(2) Fire and burns:** heaters, cooking fires, lamps and hot liquids (→ 12-03).
>
> **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open
> window** (floor, → 30-01). Carbon monoxide kills in minutes with no warning.

## Do this now

1. **Water first.** Lids on every water container; dump what is not in use. {{#if has.pool}}**Your
   pool:** a four-sided isolation fence at least 4 feet high, fully separating the pool from the
   house and yard, with a self-closing, self-latching gate (CDC; CPSC). Do it now, not next
   summer.{{else}}If you have or reach a pool, hot tub or pond, the barrier rules below come
   first.{{/if}}
2. **Carbon monoxide alarms** on every level and where people sleep, and in any room where a
   fuel heater runs; test them monthly (CPSC; → 08-02). No fuel heater is lit until the alarm in
   its room is up and tested.
3. **An anchored barrier around every heater or stove** before it is first lit (block below).
4. **Lock up** fuel, lamp oil, chemicals, lye, pool shock, bleach, medicines and spare button
   batteries (→ 06-06, → 13-09, → 03-04, → 12-07).
5. **Learn CPR, every adult.** CDC lists it among its drowning-prevention steps, and no ambulance
   may come. Infant and child choking and CPR: → 12-18.
6. **Walk every room on your knees** with the checklist below. Button batteries first. Put the
   walk on the drill calendar so it repeats (→ 24-02, → 25-10).

## Drowning: the one to take most seriously

**The layers of protection** (CDC, Preventing Drowning; CPSC, In-Home Drowning):

1. **Barriers.** For a pool: a four-sided isolation fence, at least 4 feet (CPSC: 5 feet or
   higher is better), with a self-closing, self-latching gate, separating the pool from both the
   house and the yard. CDC says a four-sided isolation fence cuts a child's risk of drowning by
   83 percent compared with three-sided property-line fencing (CDC, Risk Factors for Drowning).
2. **Alarms** on doors to the pool area, and a power safety cover (CPSC).
3. **Close, constant, undistracted supervision** whenever children are near water. **Name one
   adult as the water watcher**, with no phone, no chores, no conversation. "Everyone's
   watching" means nobody is.
4. **Swimming lessons** reduce drowning risk (CDC), but children who have had lessons still need
   close supervision. Worth doing while they exist.
5. **Life jackets** for any activity in or around natural water (CDC).
6. **CPR for every adult**, and rescue equipment (a reach pole, a float) at the water.
7. **Grid-down rule:** if a pool or pond becomes a water source (→ 03-03), **the gate closes and
   latches every single time**, and **no child goes to fetch water.**

**In-home drowning** (CPSC, In-Home Drowning): bathtubs, buckets, toilets and other standing
water; CPSC says nearly 90 children drown inside the home each year, two-thirds of them in the
bathtub. Coolers and rain barrels are the same hazard. In a house where water is stored in open containers everywhere, this risk
goes up a great deal. **Lids. Every container.**

**Pool covers and ice in winter.** A covered or frozen pool that is opened repeatedly for water
is a different drowning, not a safer one.
- **Only a power safety cover built to the ASTM F1346 standard counts as a barrier** in CPSC's
  guidelines (CPSC, Safety Barrier Guidelines for Residential Pools). A tarp or an ordinary
  winter cover is not on that list, and water pooled on top of any cover can be deep enough to
  drown a toddler (as little as 2 inches; CPSC).
- **Pump or bail standing water off any non-safety cover** after every thaw or rain.
- **The water-draw opening is re-covered and the gate re-latched after every draw**, by the
  adult who drew the water.
- **Nobody on the ice, and no child in the pool yard**, all year.

## Fire and burns: the daily hazard

You may be running open fire every day (→ 07-01, → 08-01, → 10-02).
- **A physical barrier around every heat source**, anchored so a climbing toddler cannot shift
  it.
- **The hot-zone line:** a line on the floor (tape works) that children do not cross when fire
  is burning. Teach it and hold it (→ 20-04).
- **Hot liquids never travel through where children are.**
- **No flame in children's rooms.** USFA says do not use lit candles in bedrooms or sleeping
  areas, and use flashlights, not candles, in a power outage (USFA, Candle Fire Safety).
- **Lamps and candles out before sleep.** Open flame indoors is a trade-off on its own card
  (→ 30-21). {{choice DC-20}} → 30-21.
- **A young child never carries a flame.** Give them a headlamp instead: a light they are
  allowed to use makes the rule easy to keep (→ 10-01).
- **Smoke alarms on every level; carbon monoxide alarms** as → 08-02.

### Portable heaters and stoves: the barrier block

The heater pages (→ 08-09, → 08-10, → 08-08) point here. The heater and the children's play
area are often in the same room.
- **A fixed, anchored barrier** (metal play-yard panels or a hearth gate, screwed or weighted)
  around the heater **at the clearances printed on its label and manual**: above, in front and
  at the sides. Write yours here: above ______ in, front ______ in, sides ______ in. Mark the
  floor inside the barrier as the hot zone. {{figure fig-heater-barrier-clearances}}
- **Never lit with a child unsupervised in the room.** An adult in the room and awake, or the
  heater is off.
- **Running a fuel heater while people sleep** is a trade-off with its own card; the options
  include running it only with a tested alarm at pillow height, vents, the barrier and wake
  checks. {{choice DC-01}} → 30-02.
- **Never moved or refuelled while lit or hot.** Refuel outdoors, with the heater cold (→ 08-10).
- **The fuel can and funnel live outside the room**, capped and locked (→ 06-06). A mouthful of
  lamp oil swallowed by a child can be breathed into the lungs and cause a chemical pneumonia
  (CDC MMWR, Near Fatal Ingestion of Household Lamp Oil, 1997; → 12-17).
- **Nothing drying on or over the heater; nothing stored inside the barrier.**
- **The play area or crib on the far side of the room** from the heater and from the vent.
- **The heater's own safety switches** (tip-over, low-oxygen) stop a fire, not a burn. They are
  not child-proofing.
- **A wood stove:** the hearth-gate rules on → 08-08 apply; same logic.

## Poisoning: the grid-down house is full of it

New things in the house that were not there before:
- **Lamp oil, kerosene, gasoline, propane** (→ 06-06, → 10-02; what each is: → 29-01).
- **Pool shock (calcium hypochlorite).** An oxidiser, not "bleach": a mouthful of granules burns
  the mouth and gullet, and it releases chlorine gas if it gets wet or meets acid, ammonia or
  fuel (→ 29-05). Store it sealed, dry, labelled, off the floor, away from fuel and heat, behind
  a latched door (→ 03-04).
- **Lye and soap-making chemicals** (→ 13-09): caustic.
- **Bleach and disinfectants** in larger amounts (→ 13-11). **Never mix bleach or pool shock with
  ammonia, acids or other cleaners** (floor, → 30-01).
- **Medicines**, including any insulin, epinephrine and the stockpile (→ 12-07).
- **Wood ash** (caustic when wet), **antifreeze**, **battery acid** (→ 09-03).
- **Rodent bait** (→ 04-11): a real hazard with children and pets; consider traps instead.

**All of it locked, high and out of reach.** "Out of reach" for a toddler is a moving target:
they climb. Poison Control: **1-800-222-1222** while phones work (→ 12-17).

## Supervision, honestly

Supervision degrades when adults are exhausted and busy, which is the normal state of a
grid-down household. **The answers are structural, not attentional:**
- **Barriers do the work supervision cannot.** Fences, gates, locks and latches do not get tired.
- **Name who has the children** during any hazardous job. "We're both sort of watching" is how
  accidents happen.
- **A safe zone:** a gated area or play yard where the youngest can be while adults work.
- **Older children can help** (→ 20-07), and a helping child is a located child. Which dangerous
  jobs a child may do, at what age, is a worksheet the household fills in (→ 30-25).
  {{choice DC-24}} → 30-25.
- **Do the dangerous work while young children sleep** or are watched elsewhere: chainsaws, fire
  building, chemicals.

**Household-specific hazards:**
{{#if has.anaphylaxis}}- **Severe allergy in the house.** An accidental exposure may be the most
  likely serious emergency you face. Everyone who feeds that person knows the list, and the safe
  food chain holds even when everything else is improvised (→ 02-04, → 02-03).
{{/if}}{{#if has.refrigerated_meds}}- **Refrigerated medicines and injection supplies** (insulin,
  needles, pens) are secured from children while staying instantly reachable for the adult who
  needs them.
{{/if}}{{#if has.pets}}- **Pets** around a toddler: food bowls, litter and the animals
  themselves (→ 20-09).
{{/if}}{{#if has.firearms}}- **Firearms in the house:** locked, unloaded, ammunition separate,
  every time (→ 14-14, → 29-11; AAP, Guns in the Home; NSSF, Project ChildSafe).
{{/if}}- Any of these that apply later: the same rules.

## The room-by-room walk, at a child's eye level

A house changes under crisis load: new gear, new fuel, new cords, staged bags. **Walk every room
on your knees** (that is a toddler's sight line) every few months and whenever the house
changes, and put it on the drill calendar (→ 24-02, → 25-10).

- [ ] **Button and coin batteries, first, every time** (AAP, Poison Prevention). Every headlamp,
      radio, clock, thermometer, remote and key fob: **is the battery door screwed shut?** A
      battery stuck in the gullet can cause serious burns in as little as 2 hours (National
      Capital Poison Center, Button Battery Ingestion guideline). If one is swallowed, the
      steps are on → 12-17 (National Capital Poison Center guideline); read it before you need
      it.
- [ ] **Spare coin batteries stored like medicine:** locked or high, in the original pack,
      never loose in a drawer or kit lid.
- [ ] **Fuel, lamp oil, chemicals, lye, bleach, pool shock:** up high and locked. Anything moved
      down "just for now" goes back up now.
- [ ] **Charging cables and power stations:** cords out of pull-reach (a pulled cord brings down
      the device, the battery or hot liquid with it); stations against a wall and off the floor;
      nothing charging on fabric (→ 09-03).
- [ ] **Staged go-bags hold medicine.** Staged means hung high or behind a latched door, not by
      the front door at toddler height (→ 22-03, → 12-07).
- [ ] **Water containers:** lids on; dump what is not in use (→ 03-02, → 13-01).
- [ ] **Tools and knives** back in locked storage, not where the last job ended (→ 16-01).
- [ ] **Heater season:** barrier anchored at the label clearances; nothing drying on it or stored
      inside it; play area on the far side; carbon monoxide alarm in the room tested this month.
- [ ] **Generator:** runs outside only, at least 20 feet from the house (CPSC) and more than 20
      feet from any window, door or vent (CDC), exhaust away from the house (→ 09-04); its hot muffler is a
      burn; cords routed where a child cannot pull or trip on them.
- [ ] **Car seats:** installed and tight, harness at each child's current size, no bulky coat
      under the harness (AAP; → 11-02, → 22-05).
- [ ] **Matches and lighters:** counted, high and locked. They migrate.
- [ ] **Firearms:** locked, unloaded, ammunition separate (→ 14-14).
- [ ] **What can the youngest reach this time that they could not last time?**

## Arriving at a new place: farms, ponds and woods

A destination or a new site has hazards this house does not. **Walk the new place on the first
day, before anything is unpacked and before children roam.** A farm holds hazards a house does
not: machinery, open water, large animals and stored chemicals.

**The first-day walk (ask the hosts to walk it with you):**
- [ ] **Augers and power take-off (PTO) shafts are absolute no-go zones.** Name them, mark them
      with flagging tape at a child's height. There is no safe "quick look" distance.
- [ ] **Stock tanks, ponds, creeks, cisterns and pools:** the same drowning layers as at home; no
      child near water without a named watcher; covers or barriers where possible.
- [ ] **Large animals: no child alone with them, ever.** A cow with a calf is the specific danger.
- [ ] **Equipment cabs locked, keys out of ignitions.**
- [ ] **Chemical storage found and confirmed locked.** A working farm holds drums and jugs this
      house does not (→ 12-17).
- [ ] **Wells and outbuildings:** covers on wells, doors latched.
- [ ] **Kid zones agreed out loud** with the hosts: where children may be without an adult, and
      who is watching when.
- [ ] **Woods and open land:** walk the boundary with the children and pick landmarks ("never
      past the big rock without a grown-up"); **the buddy rule**: nobody in the woods alone;
      "**we don't eat anything from the ground**" (→ 05-19, → 05-06); "**step on logs, never
      blindly over them**" where there are snakes.

## When it runs out

- **Safety equipment does not wear out; vigilance does.** The risk on this page rises with time,
  as the household tires and as a 1-year-old becomes a 2-year-old who climbs, opens latches and
  reaches higher. Re-walk the house as children grow.
- **The failure to guard against is convenience erosion:** the pool gate propped open because
  you are hauling water twenty times a day; the heater barrier moved aside because it is in the
  way; the lamp left burning because everyone is tired. Knowing these in advance is most of the
  defence.
- **Structure beats attention, always.** A latch that closes itself is worth more than a promise
  to be careful.
- **Teaching never runs out.** A child who understands the hot zone and the water rule is safer
  than one who is merely watched (→ 20-04).

## By phase

- **A (shelter in place):** the highest-hazard phase: a normal house plus a heater in the living
  room, open flame, stored fuel, open water containers and any pool. Alarms and the heater
  barrier first, then water barriers, then locked storage.
- **B (moving):** traffic, unfamiliar ground, water crossings, cold (→ 11-02) and separation
  (→ 15-03, → 15-07). Every child in a car seat, harness snug, no puffy coat under it, blanket
  over the top. **Nobody out of arm's reach;** a whistle and an ID card on each child. Carrying
  fuel inside a passenger vehicle is a trade-off on its own card (→ 30-07); wherever the cans
  ride, children cannot reach them. {{choice DC-06}} → 30-07.
- **C (establish):** ponds and creeks, livestock, machinery, wells, outbuildings, and much longer
  help times (→ 12-16). The first-day walk above, then the room-by-room walk restarts at the new
  house, and the heater barrier comes with the heater.
