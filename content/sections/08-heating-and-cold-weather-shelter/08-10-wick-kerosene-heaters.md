# Wick kerosene heaters: setup, safe use and troubleshooting

**What this page is for.** How to set up, fuel, light, run, shut off, clean and troubleshoot a
portable **wick kerosene heater** of the round "convection" type sold for indoor use. The figures
here come from the owner's manual of one common 10,500 BTU/h model, the Dyna-Glo WK11C8 (GHP
Group/Dyna-Glo, Portable Kerosene Heater owner's manual). **Other models differ:** spec tables are
on → 28-04, and **your own heater's manual and data plate govern.** Kerosene and the other fuels it
must never meet are explained on → 29-01; carbon monoxide (CO) on → 29-03.

> **SAFETY.** This is an **unvented** heater: it burns room air and its exhaust stays in the room.
> Its manual warns of indoor air pollution and calls for more air in a small room (GHP
> Group/Dyna-Glo manual). **1-K kerosene only. Gasoline in this heater can cause an explosion or
> uncontrolled flames** (manual). A tested battery CO alarm is up **before**
> it is lit (→ 08-02). **Never leave it unattended** (manual). **The common model's manual lists no
> oxygen depletion sensor,** so the alarm is the only thing watching. **Fuel is never stored or
> transferred near an open flame or a running appliance:** refuel outdoors, heater off and cold
> (→ 30-01). **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products**
> (→ 30-01): drinking or breathing in kerosene can cause hydrocarbon pneumonia, a lung injury
> (MedlinePlus, Hydrocarbon pneumonia; → 12-17). Never dry clothes on or over it; never put anything on its top.

## The can rule

**Kerosene goes in a can that is not red, clearly marked KEROSENE.** The manual says store it only
in a blue container marked "KEROSENE", never a red one, because red containers are associated with
gasoline (manual; → 29-01). **Read the label aloud, sniff, then pour. An unlabelled can,
or any doubt, and you do not pour.** Never dyed (red) kerosene, 2-K kerosene, "lamp oil", camp fuel,
diesel, alcohol or anything else in this heater.

{{photo kerosene-cans}}

## Do this now: the sequence, every time

1. **CO alarm first.** Battery in, hold TEST until it sounds. In the warm room at pillow height, on
   the wall opposite the heater, not above it, not in a still-air corner, not by the cracked window
   (→ 08-02).
2. **Fuel outdoors, heater cold,** from the KEROSENE can, by siphon pump, watching the gauge. **If
   the tank had run dry, wait 60 minutes before lighting** so the wick soaks (manual). Set a timer.
3. **Placement:** **36 in clear above and in front** (manual; give it 36 in all round if you can),
   level floor, out of traffic and draughts, behind a barrier (→ 20-02). **Nothing on the top plate;
   nothing drying on, over or near it** (manual).
4. **Air:** the window cracked **at least 1 inch** in a small room (below).
5. **Light, then check the flame at 5 to 7 minutes,** and every 30 minutes after. An adult stays in
   the room while it burns.
6. **Off and confirmed out** before leaving the room.
7. **One fuel-burning heater at a time.** Never moved or carried while lit.
8. **Never where fuel vapour may be present** (manual), which means never run it in a garage or room
   where fuel is stored.

**Runtime from your stock:** gallons on hand ÷ the manual's gallons per hour = hours of burn. With
the figures above, 1 gallon is about 12½ hours at full output. The manual says the heater is meant
to run at its correct flame, not turned down: a low wick makes smell, tar and CO instead of savings.

## Ventilation (manual)

In an ordinary, not unusually tight house, air leaks supply what the heater needs. **But if the room
has less than 200 cubic feet of air per 1,000 BTU/h** of heater, keep the doors to the next rooms
open or **open a window at least 1 inch**. For a 10,500 BTU/h heater that is 2,100 cubic feet, about
a 13 × 20 ft room with an 8 ft ceiling. **Never in a bathroom or any small room with the door
closed.** A warm room is usually small and sealed on purpose (→ 08-01), so **plan on the window
being cracked at least 1 inch whenever it burns.** That does not replace the CO alarm.

## Heat while people sleep

The manual says turn it off and make sure it is out before going to bed, and CPSC says never run
an unvented fuel-burning appliance in any room where people are sleeping (CPSC, Carbon Monoxide
Questions and Answers). Keeping it burning keeps the room warm, but this heater has no oxygen
sensor listed, so CO and fire would have only the
alarm and whoever wakes to check. The household decides on the card: {{choice DC-01}} (→ 30-02).
If the household's answer allows overnight use, on a wick heater **"lowest" does not mean a
turned-down wick**, which smokes and makes CO: it means the wick exactly at the manual's ½-inch
flame, checked before bed and at every check; the tank filled before bed (a tank running dry
overnight gives a dying, smoky flame); the window cracked at least 1 inch; the heater behind its
barrier on the far side of the room; the alarm on the opposite wall at pillow height.

## First-time setup (in daylight, before you need it)

1. Unbox (keep the box for summer storage). Assemble by the manual. **Never run it without its
   guard or grille.**
2. Batteries into the igniter holder.
3. **Fill outdoors** (below), then **wait at least 60 minutes before lighting** so the wick soaks.
   Lighting early traps air in the wick and can ruin it.
4. **First burn outdoors** on a calm day: the factory oils burn off with a smell.

## Refuelling: outdoors, cold

- Heater **off and cool.** Carry heater, KEROSENE can and siphon pump outside.
- Siphon: tighten the cap on top of the pump, straight tube in the can, flexible tube in the heater's
  fill opening, squeeze the bulb.
- **Watch the gauge.** Near full, loosen the pump cap: the flow stops. **Do not overfill.** Let the
  pump drain before pulling it out.
- Wipe spills before the heater comes back inside. Fill cap on tight.
- **Tank was dry? Wait 60 minutes** before lighting. A top-up of a tank that was not empty needs no
  wait.
- Where the cans are stored matters: away from the living space and any flame (→ 06-06). Storage in
  an attached garage is a decision card: {{choice DC-05}} (→ 30-06).

## Lighting

1. Turn the **wick knob** to raise the wick all the way.
2. **Push the ignition lever:** the glowing igniter touches the wick. When it catches, release; the
   burner drops back over the wick.
3. **Turn the burner knob side to side** a few times so the burner sits evenly.
4. **Match backup** (dead batteries): wick fully up, lift the burner by its knob, touch a match to the
   top edge of the wick, lower the burner, turn it to seat. **Leave no match debris** in the burner.

## Flame height

{{figure fig-kerosene-flame-height}}

- **Check at 5 to 7 minutes** (the flame builds as the burner warms), then **every 30 minutes**: as
  the room and tank warm, the flame climbs.
- **Correct: a ½-inch flame above the flame spreader, even all round,** seen through the glass.
- **Too high: smoke and soot.** Lower the wick.
- **Too low: smell and carbon monoxide.** Never run below the wick stop.
- **Smell:** slight at start-up and shut-down is normal; after 5 to 10 minutes it should be very
  slight. Strong or continuing smell is not normal (table below).
- Do not touch the burner knob once the flame has settled; it is very hot.

{{photo kerosene-flame}}

## Shutting off

Push **down on the shut-off knob** with one hand while holding the **wick knob** with the other; ease
your grip and let the spring lower the wick. Turn the wick knob down as far as it goes. After 10 to 15
seconds, lift the burner by its knob and **look: no flame.** Wait **at least 10 minutes before
relighting**; a hot relight gives a strong smell and can flare up.

## The tip and jar shut-off

A pendulum drops the wick if the heater is knocked or tipped. **Test it weekly in season:** heater
off, wick fully up, grab the grille and give a firm shake. You should hear a snap and the wick drops.
If the wick knob then turns further down, the wick is gummed: dry-burn it. **Wick will not rise?**
Push the safety reset lever, then turn the wick up.

## Wick care: the job that keeps it safe

Tar and carbon build up on the wick. A tarred wick smokes, smells, will not lower fully, and **can
fail to go out.** The manual makes this the owner's job.

- **Dry burn (clean burn):** within 7 days of first use, then **every 2 tankfuls or weekly** in
  season, and whenever the wick feels hard. **Outdoors only,** calm day. Burn the tank nearly empty;
  when the flame starts dying, raise the wick fully and let it burn out. Wait 60 minutes, relight, let
  it burn out again. Cool, then brush the wick top with a stiff old toothbrush.
- **Monthly:** heater off and cool, batteries out, grille and burner off, wick raised level with the
  guide; scrape tar off the wick guide with a flat screwdriver.
- **Replace the wick** when cleaning does not fix slow lighting, a stiff knob, smell while burning,
  low heat or damage. **Water-contaminated fuel cannot be dry-burned out:** drain, clean the tank,
  new wick, soak it 60 minutes in fresh 1-K. **Use the maker's wick for your model** (part number on
  the plate or in the manual). After any wick change: set the height by the manual, even all round,
  and **test the shut-off.**

## Fuel quality

- **Water-clear 1-K only.** Water or dirt in the fuel ruins the wick and makes the heater smoke,
  smell and flicker out (manual).
- **Old kerosene:** the manual says never use kerosene stored from one season to the next, because
  old kerosene does not burn properly. Whether old fuel is used after a jar test is a decision card:
  {{choice DC-17}} (→ 30-18). Either way, an unlabelled or cloudy can is never poured (→ 06-06).

## Summer storage

Rinse the tank with a little kerosene and pour it **all** out (never water). Burn the heater dry with
the wick fully up, **outdoors**. Batteries out. Clean tar off the wick knob, guide and burner. Store
with the **wick fully lowered**, in the original box with the manual and siphon pump, somewhere
ventilated.

## Troubleshooting (manual)

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Will not light** | Tank empty | Fill outdoors with clear 1-K; 60-minute soak if it ran dry |
| | Igniter does not glow | New batteries; then a new igniter plug; or a match |
| | Igniter touching the side of the wick | Lower the wick slightly |
| | Water in the kerosene | Drain; new wick; refill with clear 1-K |
| **Smokes** | Flame too high | Lower to ½ in |
| | Draught; burner not level | Move it; turn the burner side to side to seat it |
| | Tar on the wick; bad fuel | Dry burn or new wick; drain and refill |
| **Strong smell** after the first 5 to 10 minutes | Flame too **low**; tar; bad fuel; hot relight | Set ½ in; dry burn; fresh 1-K; wait 10 min before relighting. **Persistent smell or any CO alarm: off, ventilate, → 08-02** |
| **Flame low, uneven, flickers out** | Water in fuel; tar; burner not seated | Drain, new wick, refill; dry burn; reseat |
| **Wick burns down fast** | **A volatile fuel mixed in: gasoline, camp fuel, alcohol, thinner** | **Off, cool, outdoors:** drain and clean the tank, new wick, clear 1-K. Find out how it happened (labels) |
| **Wick will not rise** | Safety shut-off tripped | Push the reset lever, then turn up |
| **Wick sticks, will not lower fully** | Tar; water in fuel | Dry burn; new wick. **If it will not go out:** keep pushing the shut-off; if flame continues, treat as below |
| **Tall flames, flare-up, flames outside the burner** | Runaway flame, hot relight, wrong fuel | **Push the shut-off knob if you can reach it safely. Get the children out. Do not carry it** |
| **Heater surrounded by flames** | — | **Everyone out.** Extinguisher if safe (→ 29-12); otherwise smother with blankets and wet the blankets. **Never water straight onto the heater** |
| **CO alarm sounds, or headache, dizziness, nausea** | CO | **Off, everyone out to fresh air**, → 08-02 |

## When it runs out

- **Runtime:** gallons ÷ the manual's gallons per hour. Log cans on → 23-02.
- **Ration:** burst-heat the warm room while awake; bedding and bodies carry the night (→ 08-06).
  **Never turn the wick down to stretch fuel:** it makes CO, not savings.
- **Substitute:** a propane heater (→ 08-09), then insulation and layers (→ 08-06). Kerosene is hard
  to find in a long outage.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** a steady warm-room heater for long burns. Fuel stays outside the living space;
  filling happens outdoors.
- **B (moving):** never in a vehicle or tent.
- **C (established somewhere else):** the same rules; buy fresh 1-K when you can.
