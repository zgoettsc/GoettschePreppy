# Trapping heat: tents indoors and insulation tricks

**What this page is for.** What works when the fuel is gone or low: stop heating air and start
trapping body heat. A tent or blanket fort inside the warm room, insulation under you, shared
warmth, pre-warmed bedding, and using a vehicle as a short warming refuge without poisoning
anyone.

> **CAUTION.** **Nothing that burns goes inside a small enclosed sleeping space:** no heater, no
> stove, no candle. In a tent or fort that means carbon monoxide or a fire around sleeping people
> (CDC; → 08-02). Warmth inside the shelter comes from **insulation and bodies only.** **Never run
> a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open window** (→ 30-01).

## Do this now

1. **Pitch a tent inside the warm room** (→ 08-01). It is the cheapest, most effective cold-weather
   move in this section: a small space warmed by the people in it.
2. **Insulate from the floor first.** A cold floor draws heat out of anyone lying on it. Foam
   pads, cushions, cardboard, rugs: a thick layer of anything.
3. **Sleep together** in one shelter. Body heat is shared heat.
4. **Hot-water bottles into the bedding before you get in, and out before a child gets in.**
5. **Nothing burning inside the shelter. Ever.**

## Why small is warm

A resting person gives off heat all the time. That heat is lost in a whole house and noticeable
in a small tent. Shrinking the space is how body heat starts to matter. As fuel gets short, step
down:

**House → one room (→ 08-01) → tent inside the room → sleeping bags inside the tent → layers
inside the bags.** Each step is a smaller space with the same heat source: you.

## Building the indoor shelter

- **A camping tent indoors** is ideal: it is made to trap warmth and it stands on its own. Drape
  blankets over it for more.
- **No tent?** Build a blanket fort with real structure: a table with blankets over it, or cord
  strung across the room. Children can help, which is good for morale (→ 21-02).
- **Floor first, then walls, then roof.** Most people insulate over before under and stay cold.
- **Keep it small.** Snug is warm; a big fort is a cold fort.
- **Leave a small opening.** Breath is water vapour, and a sealed shelter gets damp; damp bedding
  is a cold-injury risk (→ 08-05).

### Insulation, by what you probably have

| Material | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Foam sleeping pads, cushions | Under bodies | Best floor insulation |
| Cardboard | Under everything | Free, effective, plentiful |
| Rugs, blankets, comforters | Floor and walls | Layer generously |
| Newspaper | Inside clothing, under bedding | Really does insulate |
| Bubble wrap | Windows, walls | Traps air |
| Dry leaves, straw | Under a pad or inside a liner | Rural option |
| Curtains, towels, clothing | Everywhere | Nothing is too small |

## Bedding that works

- **Loft is warmth.** Squashed insulation does not insulate; do not cram a bag so tight that it
  flattens.
- **Wool over cotton.** Wool insulates when damp; cotton is dangerous when wet (→ 11-05).
- **Hat in bed.** Cover the head and neck as well as the body (CDC wraps a chilled person's head
  and neck too).
- **Dry sleep clothes.** The day's sweat will chill you all night (→ 11-03).
- **A wrapped hot-water bottle at the feet**, put in before you get in.
- **Babies and toddlers:** warmth is worn, not piled on. Layers and a sleep sack, no loose
  bedding in a baby's sleep space (AAP); keep the room and shelter warm instead (→ 11-02).
  Whether a small child shares an adult bed for warmth is a decision card: {{choice DC-09}}
  (→ 30-10).

## A vehicle as a warming refuge at home

{{#if has.vehicle}}You have a vehicle. {{/if}}A car's cabin is a small insulated space with a real
heater. Used at home it has its own rules:

- **In the open, never in the garage,** not with the door open, not "for a few minutes". Park it
  clear of the house's windows and vents (→ 08-02).
- **Clear the tailpipe of snow before every warming session.** A drifted-over exhaust fills the
  cabin with CO (NWS, What To Do If You're Caught in a Winter Storm).
- **Run it about 10 minutes each hour with a window open a little,** the same rule NWS gives for a
  stranded car (NWS; → 22-05). The cabin holds the heat between runs.
{{#if has.hybrid}}- **A hybrid** in its ready mode starts the engine only as needed to hold the
  battery and cabin temperature, which uses far less fuel than idling a conventional engine and
  can charge devices too (→ 09-11). The tailpipe and garage rules still apply.
{{/if}}- **Children are never alone in it,** awake or asleep.
- **If it becomes a daily refuge, put a battery CO alarm in the cabin.** Repetition is when the
  tailpipe check gets skipped.
- **A refuge, not a bedroom.** Nobody sleeps overnight in a running vehicle. A parked, unheated car
  is just a small cold room: insulate it like one.

## Children

Small children in a tent for weeks is either miserable or an adventure, and that depends mostly on
how the adults frame it. Make it a fort. Let them help build it. Give it a name. It costs nothing
and pays back in behaviour and morale (→ 21-02, → 20-03).

| Closed-cell foam pads, one per person | The floor layer | Also for travel |
| Cold-rated sleeping bags, all sizes | Where the warmth lives | Child sizes too |
| Wool blankets | Insulate when damp | |
| Hot-water bottles | Pre-warm bedding | |
| Emergency reflective blankets | Reflect body heat | Cheap; stock several |
| Cord, hooks, nails | Hang blankets, build structure | |
| Wool hats and socks for sleeping | Head and feet | A dry sleep set |

**No gear?** Cardboard is the most underrated insulation there is. Sofa cushions make a floor and a
wall. Newspaper between two blankets makes a warm quilt. A parked car, insulated with blankets and
**without** the engine running, is a usable small shelter.

## When it runs out

- **Insulation is found, not used up.** This is the heat plan with no depletion clock, which is why
  it is the end of the fuel ladder.
- **Nothing to ration.** Shrink the space further, add layers, share heat.
- **Fuel buys comfort; insulation and body heat buy survival.** Practise one cold weekend now; it
  teaches more than reading (→ 24-02).

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** tent inside the warm room. Practise one weekend while you still have heat, so
  children find it fun.
- **B (moving):** the same skills in a vehicle or rough shelter: insulate from the ground, keep it
  small, nothing burning inside.
- **C (established somewhere else):** the same, plus natural insulation (straw, leaves, snow) and a
  proper vented stove if the building has one.
