# Layered defence: locks, lights, dogs and neighbours

**What this page is for.** Security that works while you sleep, while you are out, without
ammunition and without hurting anyone. It is built in layers, from the street inward, and each
layer buys time and warning.

> **CAUTION.** Every layer here works while you sleep, works when you are not home, works without
> ammunition, and never requires you to hurt anyone. **That is why they matter more than the last
> layer.** A household{{#if has.child}} with children{{/if}} cannot keep a 24-hour guard, so the
> system has to work without one.

## Do this now

1. **Build from the outside in:** street, yard, structure, inside, response.
2. **Put warning before strength.** Knowing early beats resisting hard.
3. **Think seriously about a dog.** For most households it is the best security investment
   there is.
4. **Know your neighbours.** In a residential area they are the strongest layer (→ 18-01).
5. **Test the alarms you rely on**, including smoke and CO alarms, which cover more likely threats
   (→ 08-02).

## The layers

{{figure fig-security-layers}}

| Layer | Tools | What it buys you |
|---|---|---|
| **Deterrence** | Lighting, a normal-looking house, a dog, a tidy yard | Being skipped for an easier target |
| **Detection** | Motion lights, gravel, bells, a dog, neighbours | **Time**, the most valuable thing |
| **Delay** | Locks, reinforced frames, fence, doors (→ 14-02) | Time to wake, gather and decide |
| **Response** | Family plan, radios, neighbours, and last, force | Options |

**Detection is the layer people skimp on.** A reinforced door you learn about after it is kicked
is worth far less than a bell that wakes you before.

## Dogs: an honest assessment

A dog is not a weapon. It is the **best detection system a household can have**.

- **It barks at things** you will never notice, day and night, awake or asleep.
- **It deters.** A barking dog takes a house off most opportunists' list.
- **It costs** food (a real depletion item), water, care, and noise (→ 14-03).
- **Any size works** for warning. A small loud dog warns as well as a large one.
- {{#if has.dogs}}**Your household has a dog.** Count its food and water in your stores
  (→ 20-09, → 23-02).{{else}}{{#if has.cats}}**Your household has cats.** They help keep rodents
  away from stored food (→ 04-11) but give no warning.{{/if}} If you ever consider a dog, do it
  **now**: a dog settled into the family before a crisis is an asset; one taken on during a crisis
  is another mouth and a training problem.{{/if}}

## Free warning that never runs out

- **Gravel** on paths and under windows: easy to lay, impossible to cross quietly.
- **Bells or cans** on gates and doors.
- **Motion-activated solar lights**: no grid, no wiring, and they work when the power is out.
- **Thorny plants** under ground-floor windows.
- **Clear views:** trim shrubs so approaches can be seen from inside.

## Powered warning

{{#if has.power_station}}Your household has a power station (→ 09-03), which can run a small
warning device.{{else}}If you have a power station or other battery bank (→ 09-03), it can run a
small warning device.{{/if}} A low-draw driveway alarm or a single camera uses a few watts; a
5-watt device left on all day uses 120 watt-hours a day (5 watts × 24 hours), small enough to come
out of a modest daily solar surplus (→ 29-02).

- **Solar motion lights stay first.** They need no power budget and fail independently of
  everything else. A powered device is an *addition* for the approaches you most want watched,
  not a replacement.
- **Keep the draw small:** a driveway sensor with an indoor receiver, or one camera, not a whole
  recording system. If security devices start competing with medical or food-keeping loads, they
  lose (→ 09-01).
- **Lit-up or dark?** Being visibly normal (porch light, motion lights on) and being dark and
  disciplined are both valid. In a short, local outage, staying normal deters. In a long event
  with a dark neighbourhood, darkness usually wins, and an indoor chime from a driveway alarm fits
  that better than a floodlight. Decide it on purpose (→ 10-05).

## Neighbours: the strongest layer

In a residential area this is not sentiment; it is practical:

- **More eyes, around the clock**, than any one family has.
- **Shared response:** several households reacting deters what one cannot.
- **You may need them.** {{#if has.refrigerated_meds}}A household that depends on refrigerated
  medicine{{else}}Any household with a medical need, small children or elders{{/if}} has a lot
  to gain from a working block (→ 18-01).
- **The alternative is worse:** separate suspicious, fortified houses are collectively weaker than
  the same houses cooperating.

**Do the work now:** learn names; know who is elderly or alone; know who has medical training,
tools or a truck. Offer help before you need it.

## The inside layer

- **A gathering room** (often the heated room, → 08-01) with a solid door and the family in it.
- **Lights and a radio** there.
- **The children know where to go** without being told (→ 20-04).
- **A way to call or signal neighbours** (→ 15-03).

## Where weapons sit

**Last.** They apply only after every other layer has failed, they carry consequences that cannot
be undone (→ 14-11), and they do nothing while you sleep. The household's written rule:
{{choice DC-12}} → 30-13. Everything above prevents more harm, so it deserves more of your
attention.

## Gear

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Motion-activated solar lights | Warning and deterrence without the grid | Several; cover approaches |
| A low-draw driveway alarm or single camera | Warning on the approach you care about most | Indoor chime suits a dark household (→ 10-05) |
| Gravel | Free, silent, permanent alarm | Under windows and along paths |
| Door and gate bells or alarms | Lets you sleep | Simple mechanical is fine |
| Reinforcement hardware | Delay (→ 14-02) | 3-inch screws, strike plates |
| A dog (a real decision, made now) | Best detection there is | Settle it in before a crisis |
| Two-way radios | Coordinate with neighbours | → 15-02 |
| Block map and neighbour contact list | The strongest layer | → 18-01 |
| Smoke and CO alarms, extinguishers | More likely threats | → 08-02 |
| A flashlight or headlamp at every bed | Identify and move safely | → 10-01 |

**If you do not have it:** cans on a string, gravel and a bell do most of what commercial
detection does, for nothing. Furniture and lumber give delay; a wedge under a door helps.
**Neighbours stand in for nearly everything.**

## When it runs out

- Most layers do not get used up. The consumables are **batteries** (lights, alarms) and **dog
  food**: count both (→ 23-02).
- **If power is short, keep warning over deterrence lighting.** A bell needs no power at all.
- **Passive layers** (gravel, bells, thorns, clear views) never run out. Build those first.
- **Layers multiply.** Lighting plus gravel plus a dog plus a neighbour at the window is a far
  higher wall than any one of them.

## By phase

- **A (at home):** every layer is available, and neighbours are the strongest. Invest here.
- **B (on the move):** layers shrink to awareness, avoidance, and not looking worth stopping
  (→ 14-03).
- **C (rural or a new place):** longer approaches favour early warning at distance (dogs, gravel,
  clear views); neighbours are farther away but count for more.
