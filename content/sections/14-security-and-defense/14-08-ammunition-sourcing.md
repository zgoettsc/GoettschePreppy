# Finding, stretching and reloading ammunition

**What this page is for.** In a long emergency the ammunition you have is, for most households,
all you will get. This page covers how to make it last, where more can honestly come from, what
reloading (re-assembling used cartridges) needs, and the hazards of lead.

> **SAFETY.** **Never guess a load.** Reloading is safe and routine when you follow current
> published data from the powder or bullet maker, and it destroys firearms and hands when you
> improvise. **No load data appears on this page, by design.** **Never try to make primers or
> powder at home**: both are dangerous chemistry and are where people are badly hurt. Children do
> not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products; that includes ammunition,
> lead and reloading components (floor, → 30-01).

## Do this now

1. **Stretch first, find second.** Dry practice, .22 LR practice and careful hunting cut use far
   more than scrounging adds supply.
2. **If you intend to reload, set it up now**: press, dies, and above all **components**. It is a
   capability you build in advance, not improvise later.
3. **Primers are the bottleneck.** They cannot practically be made at home. If you stock one
   reloading component deeply, stock primers.
4. **Save your brass** (the empty cases). It is the one reusable component.
5. **Think hard before trading ammunition** (→ 18-02): you arm the person you trade with.

## Stretching what you have: the biggest lever

| Habit | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Dry practice** (unloaded, no ammunition in the room, → 14-15) | Keeps most shooting skills alive with **no** ammunition |
| **Practise with .22 LR** | The same basics for a fraction of the cost; saves centerfire |
| **Confirm the zero, do not redo it** | A few rounds to check, not a box (→ 14-09) |
| **Hunt carefully** | Closer shots, steady rests, fewer misses |
| **Traps and snares** | Take small game with **no** ammunition (→ 05-15) |
| **Stop casual shooting** | The largest avoidable drain |

**Traps deserve special mention.** In a long emergency they feed you while you do other work,
and they use nothing up. A rifle cartridge is a poor way to catch a rabbit if a snare will do it.

## Where ammunition really comes from

| Source | Realistic? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bought in advance** | **The only reliable one** | Everything else is speculation |
| Sporting goods, hardware and farm stores | Early on | Common calibers sell out first |
| Other shooters, trade | Real | See the warning below |
| Reloading your own | **Yes, if set up in advance** | Needs components you already own |
| Private and estate sales, now | Yes | Legal and often cheaper |
| "Scavenging" | Mostly fantasy | Ammunition does not lie around; assume nothing |

**The honest summary: your supply is what you buy before it matters.** Plan on that.

## Reloading: what it really needs

Reloading turns **brass + primer + powder + bullet** back into a cartridge. You need all four, plus
the tools. Brass can be reused many times; the other three are used up.

**Components, most critical first:**

1. **Primers**: **the hard bottleneck.** Cannot be safely made at home. Store cool and dry; they
   are sensitive to moisture and oil, so keep solvents away (→ 14-07).
2. **Powder**: buy in the container sizes you will use; store as SAAMI's smokeless powder
   guidance says (original containers, cool, dry, never closely confined; → 14-07). **Watch for
   breakdown** (a vinegar-like acid smell or reddish-brown fumes) and dispose of bad powder at
   once. Never salvage powder from old cartridges or mix powders (SAAMI, Smokeless Powder:
   Properties and Storage).
3. **Bullets**: buy now, and **cast later** from lead if you set up for it.
4. **Brass**: reusable; save every case. Inspect for splits, stretching and loose primer pockets.

**What you cannot do safely at home:**

- **Make primers.** The priming compound is sensitive explosive chemistry. Do not.
- **Make smokeless powder.** Do not.
- **Reload .22 LR** or any rimfire. **This is why .22 LR is stocked deep** (→ 14-04): you can
  never make more.
- **Reload shotgun shells without a press made for them**, though with one it is a real option.

## Casting bullets: the renewable part

Lead can be melted and cast into bullets, which makes **bullets** the one component you can
renew.

- **Lead sources:** older wheel weights, scrap lead, plumbing lead.
- **Needs:** a melting pot, a mould for your caliber, a sizing and lubricating setup, and an
  outdoor heat source (→ 07-01).
- **Cast bullets suit** handgun calibers and lower-speed rifle loads better than high-speed
  rifle loads. (Never .22 LR: rimfire is never reloadable.)

**Lead is a serious hazard, above all to children.** The CDC states that **no safe blood lead
level in children has been identified**, that children under 6 are at highest risk because they
put their hands in their mouths, and that **hobbies are a recognised way lead comes home**, on
clothes and hands (CDC, Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention).

- **Cast outdoors only**, well away from where children play. Never in the kitchen.
- **Never bring casting clothes, tools or residue into the living space.** Change and wash before
  touching children or food.
- **Store lead, moulds and cast bullets locked and out of reach.** A cast bullet is exactly what
  a toddler puts in their mouth.
- **Molten lead plus any moisture causes a violent steam explosion.** Everything that touches the
  pot must be bone dry.
- {{#if has.young_child}}**There is a child under 6 in your household.** Weigh honestly whether
  casting is worth it while they are this young.{{else}}With small children in the house, weigh
  honestly whether casting is worth it while they are young.{{/if}}

## Trading ammunition: the problem

Ammunition will be among the most valuable trade goods in a crisis. That cuts three ways:

1. **You arm whoever you trade with**, possibly against you.
2. **It tells them you have more** (→ 14-03).
3. **It draws coercion.** Being known as the house with ammunition is a danger, not an asset.

**If you trade at all:** only calibers you do **not** depend on, only with people you would be
comfortable seeing armed, in small amounts, and never in a way that shows how much you have.
There are better trade goods (→ 18-02).

## Setting up now: a realistic order

1. **Components first** (primers, powder, bullets): they are what vanish from shelves.
2. **A single-stage press, dies for your two most-used calibers, a scale and calipers.**
3. **Reloading manuals with current published load data, printed** (→ 19-01). Data on a phone is
   data you may not have.
4. **Practise now.** Load and shoot your own ammunition while you can check results safely.
5. **Casting** later, as the long-term extension.

## Gear

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Primers, deep stock** | The bottleneck; cannot be made at home | Cool, dry, away from solvents |
| Powder, in the containers you will use | Consumable | Store as → 14-07 |
| Bullets, or a casting setup | Renewable if you cast | Moulds in your calibers |
| Brass: **save all of it** | The reusable component | Sort by caliber, inspect |
| Single-stage press and dies | The core tool | Simple and durable |
| Scale, calipers, case trimmer, tumbler | Accuracy and safety | Digital, plus a beam scale as backup |
| **Printed load manuals** | Never guess | More than one source; keep them current |
| Shotshell press (optional) | Shotgun reloading | Wads, hulls, shot |
| Lead pot, moulds, sizer | Renewable bullets | **Outdoors only; lead is toxic** |
| Snares and trapping supplies | Game with **no** ammunition | → 05-15 |

**If you do not have it:** traps and snares are the real substitute for ammunition when the goal
is food. Lead can be scavenged if you can cast. Brass piles up wherever people shoot. **There is no
substitute for primers.**

## When it runs out

- **How long it lasts:** rounds on hand (→ 14-04), plus the cartridges you could reload. **Primers
  are the limit**: you can load only as many cartridges as you have primers.
- **Stretch it:** dry practice; .22 LR for practice; traps for small game; careful hunting.
- **After that:** accept that the supply is finite and move food-getting toward trapping, fishing
  and growing (→ 05-15, → 05-08). Plan your food so that it does **not** depend on ammunition.

## By phase

- **A (at home):** buy components, set up and learn the press, save brass.
- **B (on the move):** reloading gear is heavy and stays behind; carry loaded ammunition by job.
- **C (rural or a new place):** casting and reloading become valuable, and trapping matters more
  than shooting for daily food.
