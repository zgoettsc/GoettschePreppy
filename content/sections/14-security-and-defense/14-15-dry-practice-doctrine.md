# Dry practice: the safety routine

**What this page is for.** "Dry practice" (or dry fire) means practising with a firearm that is
unloaded, with no ammunition in the room. It keeps shooting skills alive without using
ammunition and without noise. Because it means handling firearms inside the house, it is only
safe when done with the exact routine below, every time. New to firearms? Read → 29-11 first.

> **CAUTION.** **The four rules head every session** (NSSF, Firearm safety):
> 1. **Treat every gun as if it is loaded.** Always, including the one you just checked.
> 2. **Never point a gun at anything you are not willing to destroy.**
> 3. **Keep your finger off the trigger** and outside the trigger guard unless you are about to
>    shoot.
> 4. **Know your target and what is beyond it.**
>
> Unintended shots almost always break at least two of these at once. Children do not have
> access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products (floor, → 30-01). Dry practice
> handles firearms in the house{{#if has.child}}, with children in it{{/if}}: **the routine
> below is what makes that safe, and no part of it is optional.**

## Do this now

1. **Choose one practice room and one backstop** (something that would stop a bullet; below).
   Write them here: room __________________ backstop __________________
2. **Adopt the routine word for word.** A routine is only valuable if it never changes.
3. **Put short sessions on the calendar**, for example 5 to 10 minutes twice a week (→ 24-03).
   Regular beats long.
4. **Buy snap caps** (dummy cartridges that protect the firing pin) for rimfire firearms, where
   dry firing can damage the chamber edge (check each manual), and for reload practice.

## Why it matters

- **Ammunition is finite** and may not be replaceable (→ 14-04, → 14-07, → 14-08). Practice is
  what actually uses ammunition up; dry practice keeps every basic skill except handling recoil,
  with no ammunition at all (→ 14-09).
- **Skill fades in weeks; the supply has to last years.** Trigger control, drawing, sight
  picture, reloads and opening the lockbox in the dark (→ 14-14) are all dry skills.
- **No noise**, so nothing tells the neighbours anything (→ 14-03), and no live fire at home
  (that is a separate decision: {{choice DC-11}} → 30-12).

## The routine: every session, no exceptions

{{figure fig-unload-and-verify}}

1. **The practice room, door closed.** The same room every time. {{#if has.child}}Children
   elsewhere with another adult, who{{else}}Anyone else in the house{{/if}} knows practice is
   on. Say out loud: **"Nobody opens this door until I come out."**
2. **No ammunition in the room.** All of it (loaded magazines, loose rounds, any ammunition kept
   with a ready firearm) is **carried out of the room before starting**. Not to the other side of
   the room: out of it. Most dry-practice accidents involve a live round that "couldn't have been
   there".
3. **Check it is unloaded, three times.** Magazine out and the magazine well checked; chamber
   checked **by eye and by finger** (dim light fools eyes); and checked again from the start after
   any interruption.
4. **Aim only at the backstop**: something that would really stop a bullet, such as a masonry or
   concrete basement wall or a purpose-made dry-fire backstop. **Never an interior wall with rooms
   beyond it.** Rule 4 applies even to a checked-empty firearm, because the routine assumes the
   check failed.
5. **Session: short, one skill.** Slow, perfect trigger presses on a mark; drawing from a holster;
   reloads with snap caps; the lockbox drill (→ 14-14). When attention wanders, mistakes begin,
   so stop.
6. **End on purpose: say "Practice is over" out loud** before any ammunition comes back into the
   room. From that moment the firearm is loaded again in your mind and the four rules apply in
   full. **No "one more" after reloading.** That exact habit is how trained shooters put bullets
   through bedroom walls.
7. **Storing it is part of the session.** Reload deliberately only if the household keeps it
   ready, then straight into its safe or lockbox (→ 14-14). The session is not over until the
   firearm is locked away. Write it in the practice log (→ 24-01).

## What to practise (one per session, rotating)

| Skill | Dry? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pressing the trigger without moving the sights | Yes: the core skill | A coin balanced on the front sight is the classic check |
| Drawing and first sight picture | Yes | From a holster or from the lockbox (→ 14-14) |
| Reloads and clearing stoppages | Yes, with snap caps | Snap caps only, never live rounds (step 2) |
| Opening the lockbox in the dark | Yes | Fast access depends on this |
| Handling recoil, confirming the zero | **No: live fire only** | Occasional live rounds confirm what dry work built (→ 14-09) |

## Gear

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Snap caps, for every caliber** | Protects rimfire firearms; reload drills | Brightly coloured, so they are obviously not ammunition |
| A real backstop | Rule 4, with the firearm "empty" | Or face a masonry basement wall |
| Small aiming marks (stickers, index cards) | A specific target, not a wall | Free |
| A timer | Keeps sessions short | 5 to 10 minutes |
| Practice log | Regularity is everything | → 24-01 |
| Chamber flags | Show a firearm is checked empty | Pairs with → 14-14 |

**If you do not have it:** the routine costs nothing. A full bookshelf against a masonry wall, a
full water-storage barrel or a bucket of sand behind the mark all beat drywall as a backstop.
Without snap caps, skip rimfire dry firing and practise drawing and the lockbox instead.

## When it runs out

- **It does not.** Snap caps last years; the skill lasts as long as the habit does.
- **In a long emergency, dry practice replaces live practice** except for rare confirming shots;
  .22 LR covers what dry work cannot (→ 14-04).
- **Short focused sessions twice a week** keep basic marksmanship sharp, use no ammunition and
  make no noise.

## By phase

- **A (at home):** build the habit now, while a mistake means embarrassment instead of tragedy.
- **B (on the move):** no dry practice on the road. Firearms stay unloaded and locked (→ 22-05);
  there is no safe room in a parking lot.
- **C (a new place):** set up a practice room and backstop before starting again. The routine
  travels; the room does not.
