# Suppressors: care, paperwork and practical use

**What this page is for.** A suppressor (often called a silencer) is a tube fitted to the muzzle
that traps and slows the gas from a shot, making it quieter. This page covers what one really
does, how to care for it, how it changes where a rifle hits, and the paperwork. It is in every
binder; if you own none, the hearing and noise sections still apply.

> **CAUTION.** A suppressor makes a shot **quieter, not quiet**, and it does not change where the
> bullet goes or how far. The four safety rules apply (→ 29-11). Whether the household fires at
> home at all is a decision card: {{choice DC-11}} → 30-12. A suppressor right after firing is
> hot enough to burn skin badly and to start a grass fire if set down.

> **Before the collapse:** suppressors are regulated under the federal National Firearms Act
> (NFA). Owning one requires an ATF-approved transfer or making application and registration
> before possession; the approved form is your proof. **Making a suppressor without prior
> approval, including improvising one, is a federal felony** (26 U.S.C. Chapter 53). Your state
> may ban them, and states differ on hunting with them. Rules on taking NFA items across state
> lines depend on the type of item (ATF, National Firearms Act interstate transportation). Check
> current ATF guidance and your state's law while they are published and enforced. This is not
> legal advice.

## Do this now

1. **Copy every approved form.** One copy with the documents grab-kit (→ 19-01), one stored
   separately. If you leave home with an NFA item, its paperwork travels with it.
2. **Zero with the suppressor fitted** if that is how you shoot: where the bullet hits shifts,
   sometimes a lot (→ 14-09).
3. **Clean rimfire (.22) suppressors regularly.** A neglected one can **weld itself solid** with
   lead and carbon.
4. **Check the mount before every session.** A loosening mount is how a bullet strikes the inside
   of the suppressor ("baffle strike").
5. **Expect a dirtier, hotter firearm** and oil it more (→ 14-05).

## What a suppressor really does

- **Protects hearing**, which is the real benefit. Gunshots are loud enough to damage hearing
  permanently, even after a few exposures (CDC/NIOSH, About Noise and Hearing Loss). With no
  audiologist and no hearing aids, that injury is permanent. A suppressor **plus** ear protection
  is the difference between hearing your family in ten years and not.
- **Lets people talk while shooting**: warnings and instructions can be heard.
- **Reduces how far the sound carries** (→ 14-03). Noise tells neighbours what you have.
- **Less recoil and blast**, so the shooter sees the result better.

**What it does not do:** make a firearm quiet like in films. Ammunition faster than the speed of
sound still makes a loud crack in flight. Only **subsonic** ammunition (slower than sound,
such as subsonic .22 LR) is truly discreet, and it still makes noise.

## Care by type

| Type | Cleaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Rimfire (.22 LR)** | **Required, regularly** | .22 ammunition is dirty and leaves lead. Most rimfire suppressors come apart for cleaning and **must** be taken apart, or they seize permanently |
| **Centerfire rifle** | Generally **self-cleaning** | Usually sealed; carbon largely burns off. Do not take apart a sealed suppressor |
| **Handgun** | Periodically | Often has a "booster" (a spring piston that lets the handgun cycle) that needs cleaning and oil |

**Rimfire:** take it apart as the manual says, remove lead and carbon (solvent soak, scraping, or
the maker's tools), and **never let it go too long**: lead builds up until the parts will not
separate. Do this outdoors with gloves; the sludge is mostly lead and goes in a sealed can (the
lead rules on → 14-05).

**Wear items:** mount threads, boosters and their springs, and any O-rings. Keep spares (→ 14-06).

**Heat:** let it cool, or use a heat-resistant cover, before setting it down or casing it. The
shimmer ("mirage") off a hot suppressor also blurs the view through a scope (→ 14-09).

## Where the bullet hits

Fitting a suppressor almost always shifts where the bullet hits. The shift is usually the same
each time for a given suppressor on a given firearm. Do this once, properly, and write it on the
data card (→ 14-09):

1. Zero the rifle **without** the suppressor; write it down.
2. Fit the suppressor; shoot the same load at the same distance; write down the shift.
3. Decide which way you will normally shoot, **zero for that**, and note the offset for the other.
4. **Check again after any removal and refitting.**

## Paperwork and travel

- **Keep copies of approved forms** with the firearm and in the grab-kit (→ 19-01); keep originals
  safe.
- **If you evacuate**, the paperwork travels with the item.
- **Never lend an NFA item.** Possession rules are strict, and a casual loan can be a serious
  offence while the law is enforced.

## Practical use in a long emergency

- **Subsonic .22 LR through a suppressor** is the quietest way to take small game where people
  live close. It is still a bullet that travels far beyond any yard, so every rule on → 30-12
  applies, and traps (→ 05-15) come first.
- **A suppressed centerfire rifle** is still loud, but far less punishing to hearing.
- **Expect more gas in the face** on semi-automatic rifles, more fouling, and a hotter firearm.
  An adjustable gas block helps if the rifle has one; otherwise clean and oil more (→ 14-05).

## Gear

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Copies of all approved NFA paperwork** | Proof of lawful possession; travel | → 19-01 and a separate copy |
| Rimfire suppressor tools and solvent | Prevents a seized suppressor | As the maker says |
| Spare mounts, boosters, O-rings | Wear items | → 14-06 |
| **Subsonic .22 LR** | The truly quiet option | Check it cycles and where it hits |
| Heat-resistant glove or cover | Hot suppressors burn and start fires | Also reduces mirage |
| Thread protectors | Protect muzzle threads in storage | Cheap |
| **Ear protection anyway** | Suppressed is safer, not silent | Electronic muffs and foam plugs |

**If you do not have one:** there is no substitute, and improvising one is a federal felony while
the law runs. **Ear protection** gives the hearing benefit: stock plugs and muffs deeply and use
them, for everyone nearby. **Subsonic ammunition** alone also reduces noise.

## When it runs out

- Suppressors last; the consumables are **subsonic ammunition**, **O-rings and boosters**, and
  **solvent**.
- **Do not use subsonic ammunition for practice** you could do dry (→ 14-15).
- **Protect the suppressor itself:** clean mount, no seized rimfire suppressor, stored dry with
  thread protectors.
- **Hearing again:** with no medical care, protecting hearing protects something you cannot
  replace.

## By phase

- **A (at home):** the noise benefit matters most where houses are close, within the household's
  DC-11 choice and after traps.
- **B (on the move):** paperwork travels with the item. Suppressors are heavy; decide on purpose.
- **C (rural or a new place):** hearing protection and discretion still matter; open ground may
  finally offer a real backstop (→ 30-12).
