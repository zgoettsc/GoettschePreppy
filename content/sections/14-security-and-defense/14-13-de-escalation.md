# De-escalation and avoiding conflict

**What this page is for.** "De-escalation" means calming a tense encounter so it ends without
anyone getting hurt. This page covers who you will really meet at the door, what works, scripts
to have ready, the door itself, pepper spray as a middle step, and the stress that drives bad
decisions.

> **CAUTION.** Most confrontations in a crisis are with **frightened, hungry, ordinary people**,
> not predators. Handling them badly makes enemies on your own street who will still be there
> next month. **The best outcome of nearly every encounter is that nothing happens**, and that
> is a skill, not luck.

## Do this now

1. **Decide your policies in advance**: charity, strangers at the door, who answers it (→ 14-03).
   Making them up under stress brings out the worst in anyone.
2. **Practise the scripts below.** Knowing what you will say stops you hesitating or saying
   something worse.
3. **Never open the door to negotiate.** The door is a barrier and a decision point; keep it.
4. **Give people a way to leave with dignity.** Cornered or humiliated people escalate.
5. **Assume you will meet them again.** Neighbours are permanent.

## Who you will actually meet

| Who | How often | Right response |
|---|---|---|
| **A neighbour asking for help** | Most common | An honest answer; help if you can (→ 18-01) |
| **A stranger asking** | Common | Calm, brief, no inventory, a clear answer |
| **Someone testing**: looking, walking the yard | Sometimes | Be seen, light on, "Can I help you?" |
| **An opportunist** after property or empty houses | Sometimes | Warning and delay do the work (→ 14-12) |
| **A real violent threat** | Rare, and usually late | The household's rule (→ 14-11) |

**The proportions matter.** Treat the first row like the last and you destroy the relationships
that actually keep your family safe{{#if has.child}}, in front of your children{{/if}}.

## What works

- **Distance and barriers calm things.** A closed door, a fence or twenty feet of yard takes most
  of the pressure out.
- **A calm, low, slow voice.** Loud and fast escalates both people automatically.
- **Do not insult, mock or lecture.** Nothing is gained and a grudge is made.
- **Do not show a weapon to "settle" a conversation.** It turns a social problem into a deadly
  one, it goes against the four tests on → 14-11, and it closes off every other option.
- **Give a clear answer, not a debate.** "I'm sorry, I can't help with that", repeated calmly,
  works better than a justification, which invites argument.
- **Offer something real if you can:** water, one small item, where aid is being handed out. Even
  a small yes changes the encounter.
- **Let them leave.** Do not follow, do not have the last word, do not escalate at the end.

## Scripts to have ready

Write your own versions and say them out loud once, so they are familiar.

- **A stranger asking for supplies:** *"I'm sorry, we're stretched thin ourselves. I can't give
  food. There's help at ______ if you can get there."*
- **Someone in the yard:** from a window or doorway, light on: *"Can I help you?"* Being seen
  resolves most of these.
- **Persistent or aggressive:** *"I need you to leave now."* Once, clearly. Then stop: close the
  door and do not carry on the conversation through it.
- **A neighbour you know:** a different conversation entirely. Honesty, and a real talk about what
  the block can do together (→ 18-01).

## The door

- **Do not open it** to talk. Speak through it or through a window.
- **Do not confirm what you have:** no inventory, no numbers, no "we're fine for months".
- **Do not say you are alone**, and do not say exactly who is home.
- **One person handles the door**; another stays with {{#if has.child}}the children{{else}}anyone
  vulnerable{{/if}}, out of sight.
- **Children never answer the door.** Make that an absolute rule now (→ 20-04).

## When it cannot be talked down

Keep the steps simple:

1. **Withdraw:** inside, doors locked, lights on, family together.
2. **Alert:** neighbours, radio (→ 15-03).
3. **Barrier:** let the hardening do its job (→ 14-02).
4. **Only then**, and only within the household's written rule (→ 14-11): {{choice DC-12}}
   → 30-13.

**Never chase.** Following someone off your property turns you from defender into aggressor, and
leaves your family unprotected while you do it.

## The middle step: pepper spray

The steps above jump from "talk through a barrier" to the last resort with nothing in between.
**Pepper spray (OC spray, made from hot-pepper extract) can fill that gap**, and many households
choose to have it for each adult.

- **Where it fits:** encounters that are dangerous but nowhere near deadly: the stranger who will
  not leave, an aggressive dog on a walk, a shoving match that a firearm would make catastrophic.
  Having a middle step is itself de-escalation: it removes the choice between doing nothing and
  doing everything.
- **Store it like any dangerous chemical:** out of children's reach and sight, the same discipline
  as firearms (→ 14-14). Clipped high on the adult or in an adult-only zipped pocket; not in a bag
  pocket a child can reach, not loose in a car console (→ 20-02). Cans can burst in a hot car: not
  on the dashboard.
- **The honest limits:** wind blows it back at you or away; **indoors it fills the room**, so you
  share whatever you spray in a hallway; some determined or intoxicated people fight through it
  (National Institute of Justice, The Effectiveness and Safety of Pepper Spray); and it expires
  (replace by the date on the label). It buys distance and seconds, not certainty. Use them to get
  away (→ 14-12), never to close in.
- **Practise:** inert (water-filled) trainer cans are cheap. Practise the spray pattern, range and
  getting it out, once a year, outdoors, with the wind from the side.
- **If an adult is exposed:** fresh air, blinking, cool water, time (→ 12-01). It is painful and
  usually passes in a healthy adult.
- **It is a real breathing risk for small children and people with asthma or lung disease** (in
  the NIJ review, the deaths in which pepper spray played a part involved people with asthma).
  **Never use it indoors with children in the house unless the alternative is worse.**
- **If a child is exposed:** carry them out into fresh air; **take off the contaminated clothing**
  (it keeps exposing them); flush each eye with a steady stream of room-temperature water into
  the inner corner for 15 minutes, holding the lid open, no rubbing, and rinse the skin with
  lukewarm water (AAP, Poison Prevention); wash with soap or baby shampoo; **keep watching their
  breathing** until they are fully settled.
  Wheezing, a high-pitched noise when breathing in, or anything like a severe allergic reaction is
  an emergency{{#if has.anaphylaxis}}; for a member with a known severe allergy, follow their plan
  on → 02-03{{/if}}.

> **Before the collapse:** pepper spray laws are set by states and cities. Some limit the
> container size or strength, who may buy or carry it, or how it is sold. Check your state, and
> any state you travel through.

## Inside your own head

Fear and adrenaline damage judgement. You will feel a strong urge to act, to escalate, to have
the last word. That urge is a body state, not information (→ 21-01). Slowing down by even a few
seconds is the most reliable way to avoid a mistake you cannot undo.

**Talk it through afterwards**, as adults, and with children at their level (→ 20-04). Frightening
encounters left unspoken build up into constant alarm and worse decisions later (→ 21-03).

**If you do not have it:** a closed door and a calm voice are the core tools and cost nothing. A
window, a light and being seen handle most yard encounters. **A second household appearing**
changes almost any situation (→ 18-01).

## When it runs out

- **Patience is what runs out**, and stress drains it (→ 21-01). Sleep, food and sharing the load
  restore it.
- **Take turns at the door.** Do not let one exhausted person handle every encounter.
- **Community.** A block that handles its own problems together has far fewer confrontations at
  any one door.
- **Every conflict avoided** costs nothing, uses no ammunition, and leaves a neighbour rather than
  an enemy. Nothing else in this section pays back like that.

## By phase

- **A (at home):** you will have these conversations. Prepare the scripts and the policy.
- **B (on the move):** avoidance is nearly everything: do not stop, do not engage, do not look
  worth approaching (→ 14-03).
- **C (rural or a new place):** fewer encounters, but each one is with someone you may depend on
  later. Treat it as the start of a long relationship.
