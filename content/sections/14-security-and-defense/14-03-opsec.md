# Not becoming a target

**What this page is for.** "Operational security", or OPSEC, means not giving away what you have.
This page covers the four ways a house gives itself away (light, smoke and smell, sound,
traffic), what to say and not say before anything happens, how to decide in advance what you
will give to people who ask, and how to keep a loaded vehicle from being a target.

> **CAUTION.** In a neighbourhood where everyone is cold and hungry, **the house that obviously
> has food, heat and light is the house people come to**: first to ask, later to demand. The
> opposite extreme is also a mistake: in a long crisis, **isolation is more dangerous than being
> seen**. The goal is not to be invisible. It is to **not stand out**, while being a known and
> valued part of the neighbourhood (→ 18-01).

## Do this now

1. **Say nothing now about what you store.** The most common failure happens years before the
   emergency, in casual conversation.
2. **Manage the four signatures:** light, smoke and smell, sound, and traffic.
3. **Look like everyone else** from the street: same darkness, same quiet, no visible stockpile.
4. **Do not post about preparedness** on social media, ever. It is a searchable inventory.
5. **Decide what you will give, and to whom, in advance** (below), so you are not making it up
   at the door{{#if has.child}} with your children watching{{/if}}.

## The four signatures

| Signature | What gives you away | How to control it |
|---|---|---|
| **Light** | Windows glowing on a dark street; flashlights outside | Blackout curtains, lights away from windows, low or red light (→ 10-05) |
| **Smoke and smell** | Woodsmoke, and especially **cooking smells** | Cook at midday when others do; a rocket stove makes far less smoke than an open fire (→ 07-02) |
| **Sound** | Generator, power tools, gunshots, loud voices, a barking dog | Site the generator away from the street and run it only for specific jobs; no shooting at home unless the household has chosen to (below) |
| **Traffic and movement** | Repeated trips, hauling, visitors, a loaded vehicle | Move supplies at quiet times; unload {{#if has.garage}}inside the garage{{else}}out of sight{{/if}} |

**Cooking smell is the underrated one.** On a cold, still evening the smell of food carries much
farther than you think, and nobody mistakes it. That is a real argument for **cooking once a
day, in daylight**, in a contained stove, rather than over a fragrant open fire at dusk
(→ 07-01).

**Generator noise is the loudest advertisement you can make.** It says *this house has fuel and
power*. Run it briefly, for specific jobs such as charging medical devices (→ 09-06), and place
it as far from the street as you can while keeping it **at least 20 feet from any door, window
or vent** (CDC, Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics). **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any
engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open window.** Carbon monoxide kills in minutes with no
warning; alarms fail; there is no mitigation that works every time (→ 08-02).

**Gunshots.** A shot fired at home, for pests, practice or sighting in a rifle, is heard all
around and tells everyone the house is armed and occupied; a missed shot also keeps going toward
whatever lies beyond the yard. Traps (→ 05-15) are quieter, work while you sleep and have no
backstop problem. Whether this household fires at home at all, and under what rules, is a
decision card: {{choice DC-11}} → 30-12.

## The long game: what you say before anything happens

- **Do not tell people what you have.** Not how much food, not ammunition, not fuel, not "we
  could last a year".
- **Do not post it.** Photos of a full pantry, a range trip or a new purchase are permanent,
  searchable and shareable.
- **Watch your delivery patterns.** Pallets of buckets and cases arriving again and again are
  noticed.
- **Teach the children at their level** (→ 20-04). A four-year-old repeats everything. The rule is
  not "keep secrets"; it is simply **"we don't talk about what's in our cupboards."**

## The balance: privacy and community

Hard-line secrecy is a poor long-term strategy where people live close together:

- **You cannot hide** a household of {{ household.size }}, its smoke and its daily activity from
  the houses next door. Trying looks strange, and strange draws attention.
- **Neighbours are your best early warning and your best defence** (→ 18-01). A block that
  cooperates is far safer than separate fortified houses that distrust each other.
- **You have needs too.** {{#if has.refrigerated_meds}}Your household depends on refrigerated
  medicine; {{/if}}in a long crisis you may need the community more than it needs you.

**The workable posture: be a normal, helpful, unremarkable neighbour who does not discuss
supplies.** Contribute skills and labour openly; keep quantities private.

## Charity: decide it now

Someone will knock. Decide together, **before** it happens:

- **What will you give?** Food? Water? Nothing?
- **To whom?** Known neighbours? Anyone?
- **From where?** Keep a small, visible "give" supply apart from your main stores, so helping
  someone never shows how much you have.
- **Where is the line?** And what do you do if someone will not leave? (→ 14-13)
- **What do the children see and hear?** They will remember how it was handled.

## On the move (phase B)

- **A visibly loaded vehicle is a target.** Cover loads; do not strap gear to the outside.
- **Do not display firearms** while travelling. It draws exactly the attention you want to avoid
  (→ 14-11).
- **Avoid choke points and crowds; travel in daylight** (→ 01-03).
- **Do not tell strangers where you are going.**

> **Before the collapse:** openly carrying or displaying a firearm in a vehicle or in public is
> unlawful in some states and some places. Your state's law, and the laws of every state on your
> route, apply while they are enforced.

### A trailer at stops

{{#if has.trailer}}Your household has a trailer.{{else}}If you tow a trailer:{{/if}} it solves
the space problem and creates a security one. It is a box of your supplies sitting in a parking
lot.

- **Coupler lock and hitch-pin lock at every stop.** A hitched trailer can be stolen by backing
  another vehicle under it and driving off in under a minute.
- **Tongue lock whenever it is unhitched**, including while it is staged at home.
- **Park where you can see it** from wherever you eat or refuel. At fuel stops, if there are two
  adults, one pumps and one stays with the children and the load (→ 01-03).
- **The cargo split:** **firearms, medicines, documents and cash ride in the cabin, never in the
  trailer** (→ 22-05, → 06-08). The trailer carries bulk: water, tools, totes. If it is stolen,
  you have lost weight, not the plan.
- **Overnight:** keep it where you can see it, backed against a wall or another vehicle so the
  doors cannot open, **or** move anything you cannot replace into the cabin before you sleep.
  Deciding "it's probably fine" without looking is the only wrong answer.
- **Keep it plain.** No stickers for guns, gear brands or "prepping", no visible racks of fuel
  cans. Plain and boring reads as "moving house", not "supplies".

## What OPSEC is not

- It is **not** suspicion of your friends and neighbours.
- It is **not** refusing all help. Giving and accepting help builds the network that keeps you
  safe.
- It is **not** a replacement for hardening (→ 14-02) or awareness (→ 14-01).

## Gear

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Blackout curtains or heavy drapes | Hides light; also insulates (→ 08-03) | Doubles as winterizing |
| Red-filter headlamps and lanterns | Low-visibility light inside and out | → 10-05 |
| Rocket stove or contained cooker | Far less smoke and smell than an open fire | → 07-02 |
| Long outdoor extension cord | Lets the generator sit farther away and quieter | Still at least 20 ft from doors and windows |
| Traps for pests and small game | Quiet, no backstop problem | → 05-15 |
| A small, separate "give" supply | Help without showing your depth | Cheap staples |
| A written charity policy | Decide once, calmly | This page |
| Opaque storage tubs or covers | Nothing recognisable through a window or open door | Cheap totes |

**If you do not have it:**

- Blankets and cardboard over windows do what curtains do, and insulate.
- A light kept behind a wall rather than in a window; light bounced off a ceiling.
- Cooking inside on the house's own range when it works removes the outdoor smell. **Never** use
  an unvented fuel-burner indoors because of carbon monoxide (→ 08-02); open flame indoors is a
  household decision: {{choice DC-20}} → 30-21.

## When it runs out

- OPSEC uses almost nothing. It is discipline, and discipline does not run out, but it **fades**.
  After a few weeks people get careless with lights and noise. Make it a nightly routine, not an
  intention.
- Community goodwill is the renewable resource here (→ 18-01).
- **Every signature adds up to a reputation.** By the second month the neighbourhood has an
  opinion about which houses have supplies. Manage it from the first day, because you cannot undo
  it later.

## By phase

- **A (at home):** the phase where this page matters most{{#if is.urban}}, with houses close
  together and every signature carrying{{/if}}.
- **B (on the move):** look unremarkable; do not show gear or weapons; do not broadcast plans.
- **C (rural or a new place):** you cannot be invisible in a small community and should not try.
  Be known, be useful, keep quantities private.
