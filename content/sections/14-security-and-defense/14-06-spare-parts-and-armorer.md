# Spare parts and the armorer's kit

**What this page is for.** A firearm is a machine with parts that wear out, and in a long
emergency there is no gunsmith. This page lists the small parts worth buying now for the common
types of firearm, the tools to fit them, and the few jobs to learn before you need them. An
"armorer" is the person who maintains and repairs firearms.

profile lists no firearms. Keep this page for reference: if firearms come into the household,

> **CAUTION.** Unload and check every firearm before any work, and carry all ammunition out of the
> room (→ 14-05). **Springs and magazines fail long before barrels wear out**, and a rifle with a
> broken extractor spring and no spare does not work. Some jobs here change how safely a firearm
> fires: if you have not been shown how by someone qualified, leave barrels and bolts alone.

## Do this now

1. **List your platforms** (types of firearm that share parts, such as "AR-pattern rifle" or a
   particular pistol model) on → 14-04.
2. **Buy spring kits** for each platform. Springs are the main long-term wear item across every
   kind of firearm.
3. **Treat magazines as consumables** and stock deeply. They cause more stoppages than everything
   else combined.
4. **Learn to replace these parts now**, with the manual on a bench in daylight, not later by
   lamplight.
5. **Print the manual and the parts diagram** for every firearm and keep them with this binder's
   paper documents (→ 19-01).

## Springs and magazines first

**Springs deserve their own category.** Recoil and buffer springs, magazine springs, extractor
springs and trigger springs all tire. A full spring kit for each platform is cheap and is the most
cost-effective purchase in this section.

**Magazines: count them like ammunition.** In any semi-automatic they are the most frequent cause
of stoppages, and they cannot be repaired forever: followers (the platform the cartridges sit on)
crack, feed lips spread, springs weaken.

- **Stock heavily.** Cheap now, impossible later.
- **Mark and retire** a magazine that causes a stoppage. Do not keep mixing a bad one back in.
- **Loaded or empty in storage?** Good modern magazine springs tolerate long storage loaded, but
  rotating a few is sensible. Store them clean and dry (→ 14-05), and loaded magazines are
  ammunition: locked away from children (→ 14-14).
- **Keep spare followers and magazine springs.** They rebuild an otherwise good magazine.

## AR-pattern rifles

If you own more than one AR-pattern rifle, parts are cheap, standardised and mostly
interchangeable between them.

| Part | Why | Stock |
|---|---|---|
| **Complete bolt** (or bolt carrier group) | Highest-stress part; lugs and extractor live here | **2 or more** if you run several rifles |
| **Extractor, spring and insert or O-ring** | Classic failure; cheap | Several sets |
| **Ejector, spring and roll pin** | Small, easily lost, stops the rifle | Several |
| **Gas rings** | Wear item, cheap, easy | Several sets |
| **Firing pin and retaining pin** | Breakage happens | 2 or more |
| **Cam pin** | Rare failure but stops the rifle completely | 2 or more |
| **Gas tube and roll pin** | Can crack or clog | 1 to 2 |
| **Buffer spring** | Tires over time | 2 or more |
| **Lower parts kit** (springs and detents) | Tiny springs fly across rooms during repairs | 1 to 2 kits |
| **Castle nut and end plate** | Loosens with use | A spare |
| **Magazines** | **The most common failure point** | **As many as you can** |

## Bolt-action rifles

Simpler and more durable, but stock a **firing pin spring**, an **extractor**, an **ejector
plunger and spring**, and **action screws**. Write down the **torque value** (how tight, from the
maker) for the action screws: it affects where the rifle shoots (→ 14-09).

## The armorer's tool kit

| Tool | For |
|---|---|
| **Armorer's wrench** for your platform | Castle nut, barrel nut, muzzle device on AR-pattern rifles |
| **Vise with receiver blocks** | Holding parts without crushing them |
| **Roll pin punches, starter punches, brass hammer** | Almost every take-down |
| **Torque wrench reading in inch-pounds** | Optic mounts, action screws, barrel nuts (→ 14-09) |
| **Headspace gauges** (go and no-go, for each centerfire caliber) | **Required after any barrel or bolt change** |
| Bench block, hex and Torx sets | General work |
| Digital calipers | Measuring, and reloading (→ 14-08) |
| Magnifier and a good light | Looking for cracks, worn lugs, tired springs |
| **Printed manuals and parts diagrams** | Doing it right under pressure |

**Headspace, in plain words:** the gap between the face of the bolt and the part of the chamber
that stops the cartridge. Too much and the case can rupture when fired. If a barrel or bolt is
ever swapped, checking headspace with gauges is not optional; it is the difference between a safe
firearm and a dangerous one. If nobody in the household knows how, do not swap barrels or bolts.

## Learn it now

Buying parts you cannot fit is a hobby, not preparedness. Before you need to, do each of these at
least once, with the manual and someone who knows how:

- Take apart and reassemble a bolt carrier group; replace the extractor and its spring.
- Replace a handgun recoil spring.
- Rebuild a magazine (spring and follower).
- Tighten an optic mount to the maker's torque and confirm the zero afterwards (→ 14-09).

**If you do not have it:**

- **Other firearms of the same pattern are parts sources.** A second rifle of the same type is,
  in effect, a spare-parts bin. This is the practical argument for owning common platforms
  (→ 14-04).
- Springs can sometimes be made from music wire, but the right strength and heat treatment matter.
  Treat a home-made spring as temporary.
- Ordinary hand tools (→ 16-01) cover much of the kit; the platform wrenches and headspace gauges
  have to be bought in advance.

## When it runs out

- **Count spares per platform, not overall.** Two bolts for four rifles is thinner than it sounds.
- **Keep the main firearms fully supported** and let secondary ones of the same pattern become
  parts donors.
- **Small parts are cheap insurance.** Springs, bolts and magazines bought today are worth more in
  the second year than another case of ammunition.

## By phase

- **A (at home):** buy the parts, learn the jobs, print the diagrams.
- **B (on the move):** a small repair kit: spare bolt or extractor kit, a punch, oil.
- **C (rural or a new place):** the full kit travels if the vehicle does; otherwise springs and
  small parts, which are light.
