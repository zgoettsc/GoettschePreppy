# Safety disclaimer — read this first

**What this page is for.** It tells you, in plain words, what this binder is, who checked it (a
computer, not a person), what it can and cannot do for you, and which choices in it are yours to
make. Read it once, properly, before you rely on anything else in the binder.

> **This binder was assembled by an AI and reviewed by an AI. No doctor, nurse, engineer,
> electrician, lawyer or firearms instructor has checked your copy.** Every page carries the
> stamp "AI-reviewed, not human-verified", and the stamp means exactly that.
>
> **It is a reference for a time when no better reference exists:** when the phones, the
> internet, the pharmacy and the professionals are not available. While they are available, use
> them. **Where this binder and a professional disagree, the professional is right.**
>
> **You decide the risks.** Some pages offer choices that carry real danger. The binder explains
> them; the choice, and the responsibility for it, are yours. **You accept all risk from acting
> on this material.**

## Do this now

1. **Read the private-copy notice** (→ 00-09). It explains what the file contains and why you
   must not share it.
2. **Install the binder on more than one device and print the key pages** (→ 00-10). A binder
   on one dead phone is no binder.
3. **Read the floors** (→ 30-01). They are the few rules this binder never offers as a choice.
4. **Answer the decision cards you left open** (→ 30-02 to → 30-32). Every open card prints
   "Not decided yet" on the pages that depend on it.
5. **Book the real training** while classes exist: first aid and CPR, bleeding control, and
   anything your household's medical conditions need. No book replaces hands-on practice,
   including this one.
6. **Ask each prescriber now, while you can,** what to do if a medicine or device runs out or
   fails, and write the answer on that condition's page in Section 02.

## How this binder was made

Four kinds of material go into it (the full story is → 29-15):

| Part | Where it came from | How it was checked |
|---|---|---|
| **Core pages** (water, food, heat, first aid, and so on) | Re-written by an AI from one household's binder, whose facts were checked against their cited sources in November 2026 | AI review passes; never by a person for your copy |
| **Region pages** (climate, hazards, plants, radio frequencies) | Public datasets, by state and county | AI review passes against the datasets |
| **Household pages** (your family plan, roles, destinations) | Written by an AI from the answers you gave in settings | AI review passes; only as good as your answers |
| **Libraries** (medical conditions, gear, explainers, decision cards) | Written by an AI from cited public sources | AI review passes |

The review passes check that numbers on dangerous pages trace back to a cited source or to your
own answers, that one rule is stated the same way everywhere, that no blank was filled with a
guess, that nothing personal from another household survived, and that the writing is plain.
No person checks any of this, for the kit or for your copy.

**The known limit.** An AI review can catch a page that disagrees with its source. It cannot
catch a source that is wrong, or several sources that are all wrong in the same way. Only
experience and a professional can catch that.

## The pages that can kill someone if they are wrong

Treat these as **plan it with a professional now** topics, not **read it and do it** topics.
They are marked `critical` at the top of the page.

## Two kinds of safety rule: floors and decision cards

**Floors are not choices.** A floor is a rule where there is no safe way around it and the
failure is death or permanent injury without warning. The binder states each floor as a rule,
with the reason, on every page where it applies, and never prints a choice box for it. The full
list, with the reasons, is → 30-01. In short:

- Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open window.
- Never connect a generator to house wiring except through a transfer switch, or an interlock
  kit with the main breaker off. Never backfeed through an outlet with a double-male ("suicide")
  cord.
- Never loosen a tourniquet once placed while help can still come; mark the time. If no help
  will ever come, the conversion decision is DC-25 (→ 30-26).
- Low-acid foods are pressure-canned to the published time and pressure, or not canned.
- No wild plant or mushroom is eaten on a "test"; identification or nothing.
- Fuel is never stored or transferred near an open flame or a running appliance.
- A frozen-then-thawed insulin vial or pen is discarded.
- Bleach or pool shock is never mixed with ammonia, acids or other cleaners.
- Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products.

**Decision cards are choices.** Many survival questions are real trade-offs: an unvented heater
while people sleep, fuel in the car, medicine past its date. Each one has a card (→ 30-02 to
→ 30-32) that explains the hazard, what the manufacturer or agency says and why, how people get
hurt, what makes it safer, and the options. **Your household picks.** The binder prints your
answer wherever the trade-off comes up, and "Not decided yet" where you have not chosen. It never
presents another family's choice as the answer.

## What this binder deliberately does not contain

These are choices, not oversights:

- **No dose worked out for anyone.** The binder never calculates, adjusts or suggests a dose,
  ratio or schedule for a person's medicine. Where an agency or manufacturer publishes a table,
  it is printed as published, with the publisher named. Your own medicines appear exactly as you
  typed them from the bottle.
- **No canning time that a tested source did not publish.** Times depend on the food, the jar,
  the method and your altitude.
- **No ammunition reloading data.** That comes from current published manuals only.
- **No legal advice.** Where law matters, a short "Before the collapse" note says what the law
  was while government worked, and stops. A law never decides a survival procedure in this
  binder.
- **No made-up pictures for identification.** Plant and injury pictures come from published,
  attributed sources.
- **No web links.** There is no internet to look things up. Sources are named by title,
  publisher and date on every page. Which source documents are inside your file depends on the
  size you downloaded (below).

## Using it safely: five rules

1. **Prefer the source over the summary.** Every page names its sources. If a page and its source
   ever disagree, the source wins and the page is wrong.
2. **Get the training now**, while classes, clinicians and hardware stores exist.
3. **When in doubt, do not.** In a real emergency the cautious option is usually right.
4. **Check your own answers.** Household pages repeat what you typed. A wrong phone number or a
   wrong medicine strength will be printed faithfully.
5. **Keep it current.** Write corrections on your printed copy, then change your answers in
   settings and regenerate (→ 00-01). A binder nobody corrects becomes fiction (→ 25-10).

## The honest limits

- **It cannot make insulin, epinephrine, infant formula or any other medicine**, and no skill in
  it can (→ 23-04). It can help you understand how long you have and use that time well.
- **It cannot replace surgery, imaging, blood, oxygen supply or a skilled birth attendant**
  (→ 12-16).
- **Region data is only as good as the public datasets.** Check it against local knowledge,
  especially hazards and plants.
- **It was written in 2026.** Products, laws and guidance change; the date on each page tells you
  how old it is.
