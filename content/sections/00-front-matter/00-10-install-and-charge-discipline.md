# Keeping the binder alive: install it, print it, keep it charged

**What this page is for.** This binder is one file. It only helps if it is on a device that is
switched on, or on paper, at the moment you need it. This page shows how to keep the file on more
than one device, how to open it with no internet, why you still want key pages on paper, and how
to ration phone and laptop batteries so the binder outlasts the outage.

> **CAUTION.** Lithium batteries (phones, laptops, power banks, power stations) can bulge, leak
> or catch fire if damaged or faulty. Charge them on a solid, flat surface: charging on a soft
> surface such as a bed, pillow or couch, or blocking the vents, raises the fire risk. **If a
> battery is smoking, hissing, punctured or swollen, stop using it at once** (US Fire
> Administration, Lithium-Ion Batteries). If you can do it safely, move it away from anything that
> can burn.

## Do this now

1. **Copy the file onto at least two devices**: a phone for each adult and one laptop or tablet.
   {{#if has.teen}}A teenager's phone is a good third copy.{{/if}}
2. **Turn on airplane mode and open the file on each device.** If it opens, searches and turns
   pages with no connection, that copy is real. If not, fix it now (below).
3. **Press the PDF export button and print the key pages** listed below. Put them in a binder
   with sheet protectors.
4. **Choose the binder device** (below) and keep its battery for the binder and medical apps.
5. **Start the power-bank rotation** (below) today, while the grid works.

## Where the file lives

The binder is a single web-page file. It opens in the phone's or computer's web browser, but it
does not need the internet: everything (pages, pictures, search{{#if size.large}}, and the source
documents bound in your download{{/if}}) is inside the file.

- **Keep the file on the device itself, not only in a cloud folder.** A file that lives only in an
  online storage service is not on your phone when the network is down. In your phone's file app,
  check that the file is stored on the phone, or marked as downloaded for offline use.
- **Check the free space.** Roughly: Small 30 to 60 MB, Large 200 to 450 MB, Everything 0.4 to
  0.9 GB (meant for a laptop; → 00-09). Phones that are nearly full delete "offloaded" files
  without asking.{{#if size.everything}} Your Everything file may be too big for some phones: keep
  it on the laptop and put a Small or Large copy on each phone.{{/if}}
- **One copy on a memory stick or SD card** in the go-bag or the document kit (→ 19-01) is a cheap
  extra copy for a borrowed laptop.
- **An old phone or tablet in a drawer**, charged, with the binder on it and nothing else, is a
  spare that nobody drains. For protecting a spare device from a solar storm or EMP, see → 27-03.

## Opening it offline

- Open the file from the file app. If the phone offers several apps, choose a web browser.
- The pages are readable even where the browser does not run scripts. **Search, the region
  picker, the condition picker, photo slots and PDF export need the browser to run scripts**; a
  full web browser does.
- **Test in airplane mode on every device, every time you replace the file.** Untested copies fail
  at the worst moment.
- The binder opens in a dark theme to save battery. Turn the screen brightness down as far as you
  can still read.

## Updating, and which copy wins

- When you change your answers in settings and generate again, **replace every copy**: every
  phone, the laptop, the memory stick. Delete the old file so nobody opens it by mistake.
- The date on the cover and the `revised` date on each page tell you which copy is newer. **If two
  copies disagree, the newer one wins.**
- **Photos you attach** stay inside the copy where you attached them only after you press "Save a
  new copy". Save, then put that new copy on the other devices.
- **Small, Large and Everything.** Small has no source documents inside; Large binds those cited
  by the life-safety sections; Everything binds all of them. You can upgrade later from your saved
  answers file and this binder file without the pages being written again (→ 00-09).

## Why a printed copy still matters

Phones run out of charge, break, get wet, get lost, and slow down in hard cold. Paper does none of
that. It can be read by headlamp, by a child, by a neighbour who has never seen your phone, and by
two people at once.

The **PDF export button** turns the binder into printable pages (a file you print while the
printer and power still work). You do not need to print all of it. Print these first, in this
order:

1. → 00-02 emergency quick reference and → 00-06 the first 60 minutes
2. → 00-04 your family plan, one copy for every go-bag and vehicle
3. → 25-08 the quick-reference cards (cut out, for the fridge, bags and vehicles)
4. → 25-09 the family medical summary, and the Section 02 page for every condition your household
   has
5. → 30-01 the floors and your own decision cards
6. Section 01 (the phase playbooks) and → 09-16 outage day one

Then, if you can, the rest. Write on the paper copy as you learn things, and carry the corrections
into settings the next time you regenerate.

## Which device holds the binder

Decide this now, out loud, so nobody drains the binder phone scrolling news.

| Device | Job in an outage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **The binder device** (one phone or tablet) | The binder, and medical apps that must keep running | Kept in airplane mode except at agreed check-in times; charged first after medical devices |
| **Each other phone** | Messages and calls at the agreed check-in times (→ 15-03) | Airplane mode between check-ins |
| **Laptop** | Reading the binder at home when a power station or generator is running; printing while there is power | Big battery, but it uses far more power than a phone; do not run it for comfort |
| **A spare device in a drawer** | Backup binder | Charge it with the rest; do not use it |

If a household member's medical device or app runs on a phone (for example a glucose monitor or
pump app), **that phone is a medical device first** and does not double as the binder device
(→ 09-06).

## Charge discipline

**Power priority in an outage:** medical devices first, then light, then communications, then
everything else (→ 09-01, → 09-06).

### While the grid still works
- Keep phones, laptops and power banks topped up as a habit. When an outage starts, **charge
  everything to full while the power flickers** (→ 09-16).
- **Recharge stored power banks every few months**, because lithium batteries slowly lose charge
  in a drawer. Follow each manufacturer's storage instructions.

### Power-bank rotation
{{figure fig-power-bank-rotation}}

Number your power banks. At any moment:

- **one is in use** (in a pocket, charging a phone),
- **one is charging** (from a power station, a solar panel, or a vehicle that is running anyway),
- **one is full and set aside** as the reserve.

When the one in use is empty, it goes to charging; the charging one, when full, becomes the
reserve; the reserve goes into use. Write the number and the date on a strip of tape when each one
is filled. If you own only one power bank, keep it full and use it only for the binder device and
medical devices.

{{#if has.power_station}}
**Your power stations** (about {{ gear.station_wh_total }} watt-hours in total) can refill power
banks and phones many times over. Charge small batteries from them in daytime, when solar can
refill the station (→ 09-03, → 29-02).
{{else}}
**Without a power station,** refill power banks from a vehicle only when the vehicle is running
for another reason, outdoors, and never in a garage (carbon monoxide; → 08-02). Solar chargers
and hand-crank radios with a charging port are the other options (→ 09-11).
{{/if}}

### Make each charge last
- **Airplane mode is the biggest single saving.** A phone with weak or no signal burns battery
  searching for a tower. Turn airplane mode on, and turn it off only at agreed check-in times to
  send and receive messages (→ 15-03).
- **Turn off Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and location** unless a medical device needs them.
- **Turn on the phone's low-power or battery-saver mode.** It slows background activity.
- **Dim the screen** and keep the binder's dark theme. Close other apps. No video, no games.
- **Text, do not call.** Texts get through on a busy network and use less battery.
- **Keep batteries warm in cold weather.** Lithium batteries lose usable charge in the cold and
  recover when warmed. Carry the phone in an inside pocket, and warm a cold battery before
  charging it (→ 09-07).
- **Keep them out of heat.** Do not charge them in direct sunlight (US Fire Administration,
  Lithium-Ion Batteries), and do not leave them in a parked car in the sun.

### Charging safely
- Use only the original charger and cable, or a certified replacement; avoid cheap knockoffs
  (US Fire Administration, Lithium-Ion Batteries).
- Charge on a solid, flat surface, never under a pillow or on a bed or couch.
- A battery that has been crushed, punctured, soaked or overheated is not trusted again.
