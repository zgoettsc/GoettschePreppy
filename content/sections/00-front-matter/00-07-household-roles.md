# Who does what: household roles and assignments

> **Roles are not about who is in charge. They are about speed and redundancy.** Two adults who
> each assume the other grabbed the medical kit is a real failure, and it happens in the first
> sixty seconds. **The rule that matters most on this page: every role has a primary and a
> backup, and the backup has actually done it.**

**What this page is for.** It turns "we'll all pitch in" into named jobs, each with a backup, so
nothing falls between people when everyone is tired, frightened or missing.

## Do this now

1. **Fill in the assignments below together**, out loud. Disagree now rather than later.
2. **Circle every role where the backup has never actually done the job.** That list is this
   year's cross-training plan (→ 24-01).
3. **Assign the 60-second grab exactly**: who takes which person, who takes the medical kit, who
   takes the documents{{#if has.pets}}, who takes the pets{{/if}} (→ 22-03).
{{#if has.child}}4. **Give each child old enough a real job** and let them own it (→ 20-07).{{/if}}

## Why / how it works

### Three rules
1. **Primary and backup for everything.** If only one person can do a thing, that thing fails the
   day that person is ill, away, injured or asleep.
2. **The backup must have done it, alone, at least once.** Watching does not count (→ 24-01).
3. **Unpleasant jobs rotate.** Waste duty, night watch and hand laundry are shared, or they breed
   resentment, and resentment is a real threat to a household under strain (→ 21-05).

### The one that is not optional

**Every adult must be able to run every life-sustaining routine in this household**: not "know
about it", **run it**, in the dark, at speed. Where the supplies are, what the backup plan is when
a device fails, what an emergency looks like and what to do, where the rescue medicine is, how the
cold chain works, and how many days of supply remain. A household where only one person can do
this has a single point of failure walking around in it, and that person is often the one the
emergency hits hardest.

### Roles that quietly matter

- **The one who writes things down.** Logs (→ 25-05) are how a household hands over cleanly.
- **The one who notices the mood.** Long events are lost to despair and conflict far more often
  than to running out of something (→ 21-02, → 21-05).
- **The one who makes the deliberate good thing happen**: the fire, the treat, the birthday
  (→ 21-06). Not frivolous; it is why people keep going.
- **The one who talks to the neighbours** (→ 18-01). Community is a survival system (CDC, About
  Social Connection).

## Gear / items

**Have now (buy toward this):** a printed copy of this page inside a cabinet door; a wall calendar
for rotations and watch shifts; and **the training that makes a backup real**: a first-aid class
for every adult, trainer devices for any rescue medicine (epinephrine, glucagon), and a run-through
of each device's failure plan with the prescriber (→ 24-01).

**Scavenge / substitute:** a list on the fridge in marker does the whole job. **The substitute for
assigned roles is arguing at the door**, and it costs more than any gear on this page.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** assignments hold for about a year; they break sooner as children grow and as one
  adult gets more practised than another.
- **Ration:** never concentrate roles to save effort. That is exactly how a single point of
  failure forms.
- **Resupply:** review at the annual review and after any drill that exposed a gap (→ 24-02).
- **Substitute:** **cross-training.** It is the only real redundancy a household has.

## Phase notes

- **A:** roles are mostly about efficiency, and about making sure nothing falls between people.
- **B:** the first-sixty-seconds table **is** the plan. Rehearse it until it needs no words.
- **C:** roles become jobs (wood, water, garden, animals, repair, care) and the schedule matters
  more than the list. **Rotate the miserable ones.**
