# Hurricanes and tropical storms

**What this page is for.** A hurricane gives days of warning, which is its one gift. Most of the
deaths come from **water**: storm surge at the coast and flooding from rain far inland, and then
from carbon monoxide and accidents in the days after. This page covers the alert words, the
evacuation decision, sheltering, and the long, hot, powerless aftermath.
{{#if is.hurricane_region}}**Your home county is rated 2 or 3 for hurricanes in the region data.**
Plan before each season.{{else}}Your home county is rated lower for hurricanes, but their rain and
tornadoes can reach hundreds of miles inland.{{/if}}
Ratings in this binder are FEMA's National Risk Index for your county, on a 1-to-3 scale: they measure expected harm to people and property there, not how likely the hazard is (→ 29-10).
{{#if is.coastal}}**Your home county is on the coast: find your evacuation zone this week.**{{/if}}

> **SAFETY.**
> - **Run from the water; hide from the wind.** Storm surge is historically the leading cause of
>   hurricane deaths in the United States (Ready.gov, Hurricanes; NHC, Storm Surge Overview). **If
>   you live in an evacuation zone and officials tell you to leave, leave.**
> - **A mobile or manufactured home is not a shelter in a hurricane. Leave it for a sturdy
>   building or a public shelter before the storm arrives, whatever the category.**
> - **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open
>   window.** Carbon monoxide kills many people after hurricanes (CPSC; → 30-01).
> - **Never connect a generator to house wiring except through a transfer switch, or an interlock
>   kit with the main breaker off. Never backfeed through an outlet with a double-male ("suicide")
>   cord.**
> - **Turn Around, Don't Drown:** 6 inches of moving water knocks an adult down; 12 inches carries
>   away most cars (NWS).

## The words and the scale

**Watches and warnings** (NWS, Hurricane and Tropical Storm Watches and Warnings):

| Alert | Means | Issued about |
|---|---|---|
| **Tropical Storm Watch / Warning** | Sustained winds of 39 to 73 mph possible / expected | 48 / 36 hours ahead |
| **Hurricane Watch / Warning** | Sustained winds of 74 mph or more possible / expected | 48 / 36 hours before tropical-storm-force winds |
| **Storm Surge Watch / Warning** | Life-threatening flooding from sea water pushed inland possible / expected | 48 / 36 hours |
| **Extreme Wind Warning** | The extreme winds of a major hurricane (115 mph or more), usually the eyewall, are about to arrive | Within about an hour |

**A hurricane warning is the last good time to finish preparing.** Once tropical-storm winds
arrive, outside work becomes dangerous. On an Extreme Wind Warning, go at once to the interior
shelter spot as for a tornado (→ 17-06).

**The Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale** (NHC) rates **wind only**:

| Category | Sustained wind |
|---|---|
| 1 | 74 to 95 mph |
| 2 | 96 to 110 mph |
| 3 (major) | 111 to 129 mph |
| 4 (major) | 130 to 156 mph |
| 5 (major) | 157 mph or more |

**The category says nothing about surge or rain.** A "weak" storm that moves slowly can flood
worse than a strong fast one.

## The hazards

- **Storm surge:** sea water pushed ashore by the storm, rising over hours and moving inland with
  waves on top. It drowns people in their homes. Your evacuation zone is drawn for it.
  {{figure fig-storm-surge}}
- **Inland flooding from rain:** can reach far from the coast, for days after landfall (→ 17-03).
- **Wind:** breaks windows, peels roofs, throws debris, drops trees and power lines.
- **Tornadoes:** spun off in the storm's bands, often with little warning (→ 17-02).
- **Rip currents and high surf**, even far from the storm.
- **The aftermath:** heat with no power, carbon monoxide, chainsaw and ladder injuries, downed
  lines, contaminated water.

## Leave or stay

- **Leave if you are told to, if you live in a surge zone, if you live in a mobile home, or if
  anyone in the house depends on power for medical equipment** (→ 01-07).
- **Leave early.** Evacuation routes clog. Fill the tank and go while it is light and roads move.
- **The goal is out of the water's reach and into a sturdy building.** That is often tens of miles
  inland, not hundreds; a shorter trip means less time stuck on jammed roads.
{{#if has.pets}}- **Pets go with you.** Find pet-friendly shelters or lodging now (→ 20-09).{{/if}}
- **If you stay**, you are deciding to be without help until the storm passes; emergency services
  stop answering in high winds.

## Sheltering at home (outside the surge zone)

- **An interior room on the lowest floor that will not flood**, away from windows: a closet,
  bathroom or hallway (Ready.gov).
{{#if has.shelter_room}}- **Your named room:** {{ dwelling.shelter_room }}.{{/if}}
{{#if has.basement}}- **Your basement** ({{ dwelling.basement }}) is good for wind but **can flood
  fast** in heavy rain or surge. Do not shelter below ground if flooding is possible; have a way
  up.{{/if}}
{{#if has.apartment}}- **Apartment:** wind is stronger on high floors. Shelter in an interior hallway
  or room on a lower floor that is above any flood or surge level; ask the building manager now
  where that is.{{/if}}
{{#if has.mobile_home}}- **Mobile or manufactured home: do not stay. Leave for a sturdy building or
  public shelter before the storm.** Write where: ______________.{{/if}}
- **Do not go outside in the calm eye of the storm.** The other side of the eyewall arrives fast,
  with wind from the opposite direction.
- **Do not climb into a closed attic** to escape rising water; you can be trapped. Go to the
  highest floor and signal; go onto the roof only if you must (Ready.gov, Floods).

## Before the storm (at the watch)

- Shutters closed or plywood up; loose outdoor items inside or tied down.
- **Vehicles fuelled**; cash on hand (→ 19-02).
- Water stored; bathtub filled for washing and flushing (→ 03-02).
- Everything charged; refrigerator and freezer set colder; freeze containers of water.
- Medicines refilled; documents in waterproof bags (→ 19-01).
- Generator, if any, tested outdoors and fuel stored safely (→ 09-04).

## After the storm

- **Wait for the all-clear** before going out; flooding can still be rising.
- **Floodwater:** never drive or walk into it (→ 17-03).
- **Downed lines:** assume live.
- **Generators outdoors only, far from windows and doors** (→ 08-02, → 09-04).
- **Heat:** the days after a hurricane are often hot and humid with no power. Cool the body; check
  on elders (→ 17-04).
- **Food:** a closed refrigerator keeps food safe about 4 hours; a full freezer about 48 hours
  (FoodSafety.gov; → 04-17).
- **Water:** follow boil notices; disinfect a flooded well before use (→ 03-07).
- **Chainsaws, ladders and roofs** injure many people after storms. Work in pairs, rested, in
  daylight (→ 16-06).
- **Photograph damage** before cleanup (→ 19-04).

**If you do not have it:** an interior closet with a mattress over you is a wind shelter; trash
bags and duct tape keep papers dry; a full bathtub is washing and flushing water.

## When it runs out

- **Power can be out for weeks after a major hurricane** (→ 17-08). Water, cooling and medicine are
  the clocks.
- **Fuel stations stop pumping** without power; fill before the storm.
- **Help comes from outside the area, slowly.** Plan to be on your own for at least the first days.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** outside the surge zone and in a sturdy building, sheltering can be right; inside
  it, leaving is right.
- **B (moving):** leave early, on the official route, toward the nearest safe inland place rather
  than the farthest (→ 01-03, → 17-11).
- **C (rural or new site):** do not choose a site in a surge zone or a flood plain; look at where
  water went in past storms.
