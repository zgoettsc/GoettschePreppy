# Floods and flash floods

**What this page is for.** Floods kill more people than most weather, and most of those deaths
are in vehicles. This page covers the kinds of flooding, the alert words, the rules for water on
roads, getting out fast, and the long tail: contaminated water, food and mold.
{{#if is.flood_region}}**Your home county is rated 2 or 3 for flooding in the region data.** Do
the "Do this now" list this month.{{else}}Your home county is rated lower for flooding, but
flash floods and basement floods happen almost anywhere it rains.{{/if}}

> **SAFETY. Turn Around, Don't Drown.** Never drive or walk into moving water. The NWS numbers,
> printed so they work offline:
>
> | Moving water depth | What it does (NWS, Turn Around Don't Drown) |
> |---|---|
> | **6 inches** | Knocks an adult off their feet |
> | **12 inches** | Carries away most cars |
> | **2 feet** | Carries away SUVs and trucks |
>
> There is no depth of flowing water across a road that is safe to drive. You cannot judge depth
> or current from the driver's seat, and **the road under the water may be gone**. Floodwater
> also hides open manholes, washed-out culverts and live power lines.
>
> **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open window.**
> Generators and pumps run indoors during flood cleanup kill people every year (→ 30-01).

## Do this now

1. **Find out whether your home is in a mapped flood zone.** FEMA's Flood Map Service Center
   holds the maps; your county keeps them too. Remember that flooding does not stop at a map
   line (Ready.gov, Floods). Mark the result on your map (→ 25-04).
2. **Find two routes to high ground** and mark the low spots on them. The road you would take may
   be the one that floods.
3. **Pack the document grab kit and go-bags and keep them upstairs**, never in a basement
   (→ 19-01, → 22-03).
4. **Flood insurance, while that system exists:** standard homeowner's policies do not cover
   floods, and a new flood policy typically takes up to 30 days to start (Ready.gov, Floods). Buy
   it long before the rain (→ 19-04).
5. **Program the weather radio for flood alerts** (→ 15-01).

## The kinds of flooding

| Type | What it looks like | Warning time |
|---|---|---|
| **River flooding** | A river or its tributaries rising over days, often from snowmelt or long rain | **Days**: the most predictable |
| **Ice jam or rain on snow** | Ice dams a river, then lets go; rain melts deep snow fast | **Hours**: fast and dangerous |
| **Flash flood** | Heavy storm rain overwhelms creeks, gullies and drains | **Minutes to an hour** |
| **Coastal and storm surge** | Sea water pushed ashore by a storm (→ 17-13) | Hours to days |
| **Burn-scar flash flood and debris flow** | Rain on ground bared by wildfire runs off like pavement (→ 17-17) | Minutes |
| **Street and storm-drain flooding** | Streets and basements when drains are overwhelmed | Minutes |
| **Basement flood from a failed sump pump** | **Power out means sump pump out** | Immediate |

**The last one is easy to miss.** In a power outage (→ 17-08), a basement that depends on a sump
pump floods. If your shelter spot, food, medicine or batteries live in the basement, put them on
shelves, and plan a backup pump or a battery-powered water alarm.

floor.{{/if}}

## Alert words

- **Flood Watch:** flooding, including flash flooding, is possible. Get ready. (Older radios and
  some offices still say "flash flood watch.")
- **Flood Warning** or **Flash Flood Warning:** flooding is happening or about to. **Act now:
  move to higher ground.**
- **Flash Flood Emergency:** a rare, severe threat to life in a specific place. Get to high ground
  immediately.
- **Flood Advisory:** minor flooding; be careful on roads.

More on alert words: → 29-10.

## The rules when water is rising

- **Never drive through flooded roads**, and never drive around barricades.
- **Never walk through moving water.** If you must cross still water, probe ahead with a pole.
- **If your car is caught in rushing water, stay inside unless water is rising inside the car;
  then get onto the roof** (Ready.gov, Floods).
- **If you are trapped in a building, go to the highest floor.** Go onto the roof only if you
  must, and signal for help. **Do not climb into a closed attic**: rising water can trap you there
  (Ready.gov, Floods).
- **Stay off bridges over fast water.**
- **Keep children and pets away from floodwater.** It moves fast, it is dirty and it hides
  hazards.
- **Downed power lines in water are live.**
- **Leave early.** Leaving at night, in water, with children is far worse than leaving in daylight
  when it only feels inconvenient. When you would leave is a household decision: {{choice DC-19}}
  → 30-20

## Getting out fast

Flooding is the hazard most likely to force a sudden departure at night (→ 01-03).
- **Go-bags and documents upstairs and reachable** (→ 19-01, → 22-03).
- **Medicines and devices first** (→ 01-07).
- **Vehicle fuelled and parked where water will not trap it**, facing the way out.
- **Children into car seats is a real, minutes-long step: practice it.** Pets into carriers is
  another; start it first, not last.
- **Know two routes out**, because one will flood.

{{#if has.mobile_home}}**Mobile and manufactured homes** can float or shift off their foundations
in moving water. Leave early for a sturdy building on high ground.{{/if}}
{{#if has.apartment}}**In an apartment,** an upper floor may keep you dry, but the building may
lose power, water and elevators, and the streets may be cut off for days. Decide early whether
to stay up high with supplies or leave.{{/if}}

## What the water leaves behind

**Even if your house stays dry, a regional flood can foul the water supply.**
- **Town water** may go under a boil notice, or worse. Follow it.
- **Wells** that floodwater reached are not safe because the water looks clear. They must be
  disinfected and tested before use (CDC, How to Disinfect Wells After an Emergency; → 03-07,
  → 03-10).
- **Surface water** carries sewage, fuel, farm chemicals and dead animals.
- **Your stored water becomes the supply** (→ 03-02), and your treatment ability matters
  (→ 03-04). **Boiling and most filters do not remove chemicals**; fuel or chemical contamination
  needs a different source.
- **Toilets and septic systems back up.** Do not use a toilet on a flooded system (→ 13-01).
- **Illness follows floods.** Hand washing and clean water matter more in the weeks after than on
  the day (→ 13-11, → 12-05).

**Food, garden and belongings** (CDC, Preparing for Floods):
- **Throw out food that touched floodwater**, including anything in paper, plastic, cardboard or
  screw-top containers. Undamaged, sealed commercial metal cans can be kept only after the
  cleaning and disinfecting steps in the CDC guidance. Food from a freezer that lost power is a
  separate question: {{choice DC-14}} → 30-15
- **Do not eat garden produce that floodwater touched.**
- **Porous things that got wet** (mattresses, upholstery, carpet, drywall) grow mold and usually
  have to go.
- **Mold** is a health hazard for weeks. Dry fast; ventilate. Keep people with asthma or other
  lung disease, people with weak immune systems, and children out of moldy buildings and out of
  cleanup work (Ready.gov, Floods).

## Cleanup safety

- **Gloves, boots, eye protection**, and wash hands well afterward (→ 13-02).
- **Keep open wounds out of floodwater**; cover them and watch for infection (→ 12-02, → 12-04).
  Check that everyone's tetanus shot is up to date.
- **Do not touch anything electrical while standing in water.** Turn off power at the panel only
  if you can reach it while dry.
- **Disinfecting with bleach:** follow the label and the CDC dilution. **Bleach or pool shock is
  never mixed with ammonia, acids or other cleaners.** The mix makes poison gas (→ 29-05, → 30-01).
- **Generators and pumps outdoors only**, far from windows (→ 08-02, → 09-04).

**If you do not have it:** height is the substitute for almost everything: stores up, bags up,
papers up. Sandbags can be feed sacks, pillowcases or heavy trash bags filled with sand or soil,
with plastic sheeting under them. Drying is open windows, fans and time.

## When it runs out

- **Clean water is the consumable that matters.** A flood can take out the town supply, the well
  and every surface source at once. Your runway is what you stored plus what you can treat
  (→ 03-01). Cleanup and hand washing raise the need.
- **Ration** drinking and cooking first, hand washing next (never cut it), cleaning last. Use
  untreated floodwater for nothing.
- **The long tail is illness and mold**, and hygiene is what prevents it.
- **For a new site:** do not choose a site in a flood plain, and put the well uphill and away
  from the latrine (→ 01-05, → 13-07).

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** know your flood zone, keep bags and documents high, and know two routes out.
- **B (moving):** flooded roads are the classic fatal error. Turn around. Never cross water you
  cannot see the bottom of, and avoid driving at night when flash floods are possible (→ 01-03).
- **C (rural or new site):** flood plain status should rule a site out. Learn where the water goes
  in a wet spring by seeing the land then, not only in a dry month.
