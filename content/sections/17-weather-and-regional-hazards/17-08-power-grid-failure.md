# Power grid failure: hours, days and weeks

**What this page is for.** Section 09 is how to make power. This page is what happens when the
grid goes down, over three very different lengths of time, and the moment when one becomes the
next is where households get caught. Start from the safety box; it is what kills people.

> **SAFETY.**
> - **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open
>   window.** Carbon monoxide (CO) is the main killer after a power outage. CPSC says one portable
>   generator can make as much CO as hundreds of cars, and opening doors or windows does not make
>   it safe (CPSC, Portable Generator Safety; → 29-03).
> - **Never connect a generator to house wiring except through a transfer switch, or an interlock
>   kit with the main breaker off. Never backfeed through an outlet with a double-male ("suicide")
>   cord.** Backfed power can kill line workers and neighbours (→ 29-04, → 30-01).
> - **Battery CO alarms on every level and where people sleep, tested, before any heater or
>   generator runs.** You have {{ gear.co_alarms }} CO alarms.
> - **Flashlights, not candles** (USFA).

## Do this now

1. **Install and test the CO alarms**, with spare batteries. This is the single most valuable step
   on this page (→ 08-02).
2. **Walk through each duration below on paper:** hours, days, weeks. The answers differ, and the
   gaps show up at once.
3. **Check the medical answer at every duration:** refrigerated medicine, device charging, the
   backup plan for each powered device (→ 09-06, → 09-10).
4. **Learn the manual release on the garage door** and anything else that needs power to open.
5. **Do a blackout weekend** (→ 24-04). Nothing on this page is real until you have.

## The three durations

| | **Hours (0 to 12)** | **Days (1 to 7)** | **Weeks or longer** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typical cause** | Storm, equipment fault | Ice storm, hurricane, derecho, major storm | Cascading failure, long crisis |
| **Food** | Keep the fridge and freezer **closed** (→ 04-17) | Eat down in order; outdoor cold in winter (→ 04-09) | Stored food is the plan (Section 04) |
| **Water** | Town water probably fine | **Pressure may fail: fill containers now; treat anything doubtful** (→ 03-02, → 03-04) | Water is the daily job (→ 03-01) |
| **Heat or cooling** | The house holds its temperature | **One room** (→ 08-01); in summer, the lowest, shadiest room (→ 17-04) | Fuel and wood clocks (→ 23-02) |
| **Light** | Headlamps (→ 10-01) | Batteries, rationed | Solar and LED, then flame (→ 10-02) |
| **Medical** | Little changes | **Refrigerated medicine and device charging matter now** (→ 09-10, → 09-06) | **The medicine supply is the clock** (→ 01-02) |
| **Sanitation** | Fine | Sewer may still work: pour-flush (→ 13-01) | Bucket system; disposal |
| **News** | Phones probably work | Phones degrade; **NOAA and AM radio** (→ 15-01) | Radio only (→ 15-04) |
| **The decision** | Wait | **Prepare for longer** | **Go/no-go** (→ 01-02) |

**The mistake is treating each stage as the last one.** Households that spend day two as if it
were an inconvenience find themselves unprepared on day five. **Prepare for the next-longer stage
from the start.**

## Hours

- **Keep the refrigerator and freezer shut.** A closed refrigerator keeps food safe for about **4
  hours**; a full freezer holds about **48 hours**, a half-full one about **24 hours**
  (FoodSafety.gov, Food Safety During a Power Outage). → 04-17
- **Headlamps, not candles.**
- **If the outage was forecast, everything was charged at the warning** (below).
- **Check on neighbours**, especially anyone elderly or on powered medical equipment (→ 18-01).
- **Unplug sensitive electronics** so the return surge does not damage them (Ready.gov, Power
  Outages).

## Days

1. **Heat or cooling first.** One room (→ 08-01, → 09-16). CO alarms up before any heater is lit.
   Run one fuel-burning heater at a time, under its own page's rules (→ 08-04).
   - **Whether an unvented heater runs while people sleep** is a household decision:
     {{choice DC-01}} → 30-02
   - **Whether a propane cylinder larger than 1 lb comes indoors** is another:
     {{choice DC-02}} → 30-03
   {{#if has.gas_any}}- **Gas often keeps flowing** when electricity stops, but most furnaces need
     electricity for the blower and igniter. Powering a furnace from a generator or power station
     needs a safe connection (→ 09-04). Converting the furnace to a plug yourself is a decision
     card: {{choice DC-07}} → 30-08{{/if}}
2. **Food.** Fridge first, then freezer, then pantry. In winter, outdoor cold is free storage
   (→ 04-09). Food from a freezer that thawed is a decision card: {{choice DC-14}} → 30-15
3. **Water.** Town pressure often survives on backup power, but not always. **Fill containers when
   the outage starts**, and treat anything doubtful (→ 03-04). In winter, watch for freezing pipes
   (→ 03-06).
   {{#if has.well}}**Your well pump needs electricity.** Without it you have only what is in the
   pressure tank and what you stored (→ 03-10).{{/if}}
4. **Medicine and devices.**
   {{#if has.refrigerated_meds}}Refrigerated medicine: keep it in its label range (→ 29-07, → 09-10). In
   winter this is easy; in summer it is the hard problem.{{/if}}
   {{#if has.cpap}}CPAP: battery or power station plan (→ 09-10).{{/if}}
   {{#if has.oxygen}}Oxygen: a concentrator stops without power. Know how many hours of cylinder
   backup you have: ______ (→ 02-08).{{/if}}
   Phones that run medical devices or apps get the power banks first (→ 09-06).
5. **Power.**
   {{#if has.power_station}}Your power stations hold about **{{ gear.station_wh_total }} Wh** in total,
   roughly **{{ derived.station_fridge_days }} days** of a typical full-size fridge if nothing else
   ran (an estimate; → 29-02). Give each station a job before the outage, by priority (→ 09-01).{{/if}}
   {{#if has.generator}}Your generator ({{ gear.generator_watts }} running watts, {{ gear.generator_fuel }})
   is a **bridge**: run it outdoors, at least 20 feet from windows and doors, in bursts to charge
   batteries, not all day (→ 09-04). House connection: {{ gear.house_connection }}.{{/if}}
6. **Sump pump.** Power out means sump out (→ 17-03). A pump draws a large surge when it starts;
   read the nameplate and test it on your backup power before you need it. If both the sump and
   heat need the same battery, the sump usually wins: water damage is permanent.
7. **Sanitation.** If the sewer works, pour-flush with non-drinking water (→ 13-01).
8. **News.** Battery or crank NOAA and AM/FM radio (→ 15-01).
9. **Sleep and morale.** Long dark evenings in one room are harder than people expect after two
   days (→ 21-02, → 21-07).

**The charge-at-the-warning rule.** Ice storms, hurricanes and big windstorms come with hours of
notice. **When the warning (not the outage) is issued:** every power station and power bank to
full, every phone charged, the vehicle above half a tank, water containers filled. Grid power in
the warning window is free storage.

## Weeks and longer

The outage stops being an event and becomes **the situation**, and the rest of this binder is the
plan.
- **Solar and batteries become the power system**; generator fuel is finite (→ 09-02, → 09-04).
- **The medicine supply becomes the household clock** and drives the go/no-go decision (→ 01-02).
- **Water, food, heat and sanitation become daily work** instead of utilities.
- **Town water may fail** once treatment and pumping run out of backup fuel. Pressure usually sags
  before it stops.
- **Natural gas may outlast electricity**, but if gas pressure is lost, restoring it takes a long
  time because each house must be checked and relit.
- **Community effects begin** (→ 14-01, → 18-01).

## The cascade: what fails because power failed

- **Water treatment and pumping**: boil notices, then low or no pressure (→ 03-04).
- **Sewage lift stations**: backups (→ 13-01).
- **Cell towers**: batteries for hours, generators for days if fuelled, then quiet.
- **Fuel stations**: pumps need electricity. Fuel becomes hard to get quickly.
- **Refrigeration everywhere**: food supply chains falter within days.
- **Pharmacies**: medicine access narrows. For many households this is the one that matters most.
- **Card payments**: stop. **Cash matters early** (→ 19-02).
- **Heat in an unprepared house**: in a cold winter, a danger to life within a day or two; in a
  heat wave, the same for elders and the sick.

## The blackout weekend

Turn off the main breaker for a weekend, once in winter and once in summer. Live on your plan:
cook, light, heat or cool, wash, run the medical routine. Write down what failed. Everyone finds
three or four things. Fix them, then do it again next year (→ 24-04).

**If you do not have it:** a vehicle can charge phones and devices, **outdoors, never in an
attached garage** (→ 09-11); winter cold is a refrigerator (→ 04-09); neighbours have generators,
wood stoves and wells, and mutual aid works best if you already know each other (→ 18-01). **What
you cannot substitute: the CO alarm, and life-sustaining medicine.**

## When it runs out

- **The outage does not run out; your buffers do**, in a fairly predictable order: phone batteries,
  then refrigerated food, then generator fuel, then water pressure, then heating fuel, then
  medicine. Know the order and watch the shortest one.
- **Ration by priority** (→ 09-01): life-sustaining medical loads first, everything else shed in
  order.
- **Substitute:** solar for grid, wood for furnace, outdoor cold for refrigeration, radio for
  internet, cash for cards, neighbours for services.
- **Decide in advance** the point at which an outage that looks indefinite becomes a reason to
  leave (→ 01-02), and decide it early, while fuel, roads and daylight still exist.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** Section 09's system plus the fallbacks here. Most long outages come with weather
  warnings (→ 15-01).
- **B (moving):** an indefinite outage plus a short medicine supply is the classic trigger to move
  (→ 01-02). Keep the vehicle above half a tank; fuel goes when pumps lose power.
- **C (rural or new site):** no grid to fail, but no restoration either. The household's own power
  is the system from day one (→ 09-09).
