# Tsunamis

**What this page is for.** A tsunami is a series of ocean waves, usually caused by an undersea
earthquake, that floods the coast far beyond any normal tide. Near the earthquake that caused it,
the first wave can arrive in minutes, before any official warning. This page is about recognising
the natural warnings and getting to high ground fast.
{{#if is.tsunami_region}}**Your home county is rated 2 or 3 for tsunamis in the region data. Learn
your route this week.**{{else}}{{#if is.coastal}}Your home county is on the coast; keep this page
even though the rating is lower.{{else}}Your home is not on the coast, but if you visit or move to
one, this page applies.{{/if}}{{/if}}
Ratings in this binder are FEMA's National Risk Index for your county, on a 1-to-3 scale: they measure expected harm to people and property there, not how likely the hazard is (→ 29-10).

> **SAFETY.**
> - **Natural warnings: a strong or long earthquake; the ocean suddenly rising like a fast flood
>   or a wall of water, or draining away; a loud roar from the ocean.** Any one of these means go
>   now (NWS, During a Tsunami).
> - **Do not wait for an official warning.** As soon as you can move safely, **go to high ground or
>   as far inland as you can.**
> - **The first wave may not be the last or the largest.** Stay away until officials say it is
>   safe.

## Do this now

1. **Find out whether home, work and school are in a tsunami hazard zone.** Coastal communities
   post maps and blue-and-white route signs. Write your zone status: ______.
2. **Learn the route to high ground on foot**, and walk it with everyone, timed (→ 24-02). Write it
   here: ______________. Have a second route.
3. **Agree a meeting place on high ground** and an out-of-area contact (→ 15-03).
4. **Keep a go-bag where you can grab it on the way out** (→ 22-03).
5. **Make sure your NOAA Weather Radio and phone alerts are on** (→ 15-01).

## The alert words

| Alert | Meaning (NWS) | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Tsunami Warning** | A dangerous tsunami is likely; widespread flooding possible | **Go to high ground or inland now** |
| **Tsunami Advisory** | Strong currents and dangerous waves are expected or happening | Stay out of the water and away from beaches and harbours |
| **Tsunami Watch** | A distant event may cause a tsunami | Stay informed and be ready to act |
| **Tsunami Information Statement** | An earthquake happened; no threat or a very distant one | No action unless that changes |

A **natural warning is as serious as an official one** (NWS). Near the source there may be no time
for an official one.

## During

- **If you feel an earthquake near the coast:** protect yourself first with **Drop, Cover, and Hold
  On** until the shaking stops (→ 17-12). **Then go**, without waiting.
- **Go to high ground or inland** by the route you practised. **On foot is often faster** than
  driving when everyone leaves at once and roads are cracked or jammed.
- **If you cannot reach high ground in time,** some communities name tall, strong buildings for
  vertical evacuation. Know whether yours does: ______________.
- **Stay away from rivers and streams** that lead to the ocean; tsunamis travel up them.
- **Get news** from the NOAA Weather Radio, local radio or phone alerts once you are safe.

**By dwelling:**
{{#if has.mobile_home}}- **Mobile or manufactured home:** offers no protection from a tsunami. Leave
  for high ground on any natural or official warning.{{/if}}
{{#if has.apartment}}- **Apartment:** if you are in a hazard zone, plan to leave for high ground, not
  to go upstairs, unless your building is an officially named vertical evacuation building.{{/if}}
{{#if has.basement}}- **Basement:** never shelter below ground from a tsunami; it floods first.{{/if}}
- **Any building in the zone:** leave it for high ground.

## After

- **Stay out of the hazard zone until officials say it is safe.** Waves can keep arriving for hours.
- **Avoid damaged buildings, bridges and piers**, and keep away from downed power lines (NWS).
- **Floodwater rules apply:** it is dirty and it hides hazards (→ 17-03).
- **Expect aftershocks** if an earthquake caused it (→ 17-12).
- **Water and sewer:** treat water as unsafe until told otherwise (→ 03-04).

**If you do not have it:** nothing replaces knowing the route. Walk it.

## When it runs out

- **The practice fades.** Walk the route each year and when anyone new joins the household.
- **After a major tsunami,** the coast may be without water, power and roads for a long time; plan
  for it as for a hurricane (→ 17-13, → 17-08).

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** route known and walked; go-bag ready.
- **B (moving):** on any coast road, a strong earthquake means turn inland at once.
- **C (rural or new site):** do not choose a site in a mapped tsunami zone; if you must, know the
  high ground within a walk (→ 01-05).
