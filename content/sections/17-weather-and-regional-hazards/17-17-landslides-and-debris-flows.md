# Landslides and debris flows

**What this page is for.** A **landslide** is a mass of earth or rock moving down a slope. A
**debris flow** (often called a mudslide) is a fast, wet landslide: mud, rocks and trees moving like
wet concrete, fast enough to outrun people and to destroy houses. Most are set off by heavy rain,
snowmelt or earthquakes, and **ground burned by wildfire** produces them in ordinary rain. This
page covers the warning signs and what to do before, during and after.
{{#if is.landslide_region}}**Your home county is rated 2 or 3 for landslides in the region
data.**{{else}}Your home county is rated lower for landslides, but any steep slope, road cut or
burned hillside can slide.{{/if}}

> **SAFETY.**
> - **Stay alert and awake during a storm that could cause a landslide** (Ready.gov). Many debris
>   flow deaths happen to people asleep at night.
> - **Leave if you are told to, or if you feel unsafe staying** (Ready.gov).
> - **If you are caught in the path, move uphill and out of the path as fast as you can.**
> - **Never cross a flowing road or bridge when a flow is coming** (Ready.gov).

## Do this now

1. **Look at the slopes above and below your home** and where water runs (USGS, Landslide
   Preparedness). Do gutters, the driveway or a neighbour's drain send water onto a slope? That
   soaks the ground.
2. **Check your state geological survey or county for landslide maps.** Remember that maps of
   where slides start often do not show how far they run out onto flat ground below (USGS).
3. **Keep plants on slopes** (roots hold soil) and **never dump yard waste or fill on a slope**
   (USGS).
4. **Sign up for local warnings**, and keep a go-bag ready (→ 22-03).
5. **If you see warning signs on your property,** a geologist or engineer with landslide experience
   can assess it while that is possible (USGS).

## Warning signs

**Fast-moving flows** (Ready.gov):
- **Rushing water, mud, or unusual sounds**; trees cracking or boulders knocking together upslope.
- A creek suddenly turning muddy, or its flow dropping while rain keeps falling (debris may be
  damming it upstream).

**Slow-moving slides** (Ready.gov; USGS):
- **Doors or windows sticking or jamming for the first time.**
- **New cracks in plaster, tile, brick or foundations.**
- **Bulging ground at the base of a slope.**
- Fences, poles, trees or walls tilting; outside stairs or decks pulling away from the house;
  widening cracks in the ground or pavement; broken water or sewer lines.

## When they happen

- **During and after intense rain**, especially after a wet period, and during fast snowmelt.
- **On ground burned by wildfire:** the burned soil sheds water like pavement, and even short,
  intense rain can send debris flows down the channels below. This danger lasts for years after a
  fire (→ 17-14). The NWS issues flash flood warnings for burn scars; treat them as debris flow
  warnings.
- **After earthquakes**, which loosen slopes (→ 17-12).
- **Along road cuts, stream banks and coastal bluffs.**

Alert words: **Flash Flood Watch/Warning** and local debris flow warnings cover most of these
(→ 29-10).

## During

- **Stay awake and listening** when heavy rain falls on steep or burned ground.
- **Follow evacuation instructions.** When you would leave on your own is a household decision:
  {{choice DC-19}} → 30-20
- **If you hear or see a flow coming, move uphill and to the side, out of its channel.** Do not run
  downhill or down the channel.
- **Do not cross a flowing road, bridge or creek** in its path.
- **If you cannot get out of the way,** get to the upper floor of a sturdy building on the side away
  from the slope, or curl into a tight ball and protect your head.

**By dwelling:**
{{#if has.basement}}- **Basement:** do not shelter below ground on the uphill side; debris can push
  through walls and fill it.{{/if}}
{{#if has.mobile_home}}- **Mobile or manufactured home:** offers little resistance to a flow; leave
  early when warnings are out.{{/if}}
{{#if has.apartment}}- **Apartment:** an upper floor on the side away from the slope is safer than the
  ground floor facing it.{{/if}}
- **Any home at the foot of a slope or in a canyon mouth:** these are the debris flow paths. Leave
  before the storm, not during it.

## After

- **Stay away from the slide area.** More slides can follow (Ready.gov).
- **Watch for flooding,** which often comes with or after a slide.
- **Report broken utility lines and damaged roads** where anyone can still act on it (Ready.gov).
- **Have the building checked** before going back in. Look for cracks, tilt and broken lines.
- **Replant bare ground** as soon as you can (Ready.gov).

## When it runs out

- **Nothing here is consumed;** what matters is noticing slow changes. Walk the property after big
  storms and once a year, and note new cracks and sticking doors.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** know your slope and your route uphill; stay awake in big storms on steep or
  burned ground.
- **B (moving):** mountain and canyon roads close with slides; never drive into a flowing channel
  (→ 01-03).
- **C (rural or new site):** do not choose a site at a canyon mouth, at the foot of a steep slope,
  or below a recent burn (→ 01-05).
