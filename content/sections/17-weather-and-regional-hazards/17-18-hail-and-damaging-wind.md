# Hail and damaging wind

**What this page is for.** Thunderstorm wind damages more than tornadoes do: severe thunderstorm
winds make up about half of all severe weather reports in the lower 48 states (NSSL, Damaging
Winds). Hail breaks windows, panels, roofs, gardens and sometimes people. This page covers both,
plus strong wind without a storm (high wind and dust). Tornadoes are on → 17-02.
{{#if is.tornado_region}}Your home county is rated 2 or 3 for tornadoes, which usually means
frequent severe thunderstorms with hail and wind as well.{{/if}}

> **CAUTION.**
> - **Treat a severe thunderstorm warning tagged "destructive" like a tornado warning**: wind of
>   80 mph or more or hail of 2.75 inches or more (NWS, 2021). Go to the shelter spot (→ 17-06).
> - **Mobile and manufactured homes are the most dangerous place to be in damaging wind; even
>   anchored ones can be badly damaged when gusts pass 80 mph** (NSSL). **Leave for a sturdy
>   building before the storm arrives.**
> - **Get inside when hail starts and stay away from windows.** Large hail can injure and kill.

## Do this now

1. **Make sure your weather radio sounds for severe thunderstorm warnings** (→ 15-01).
2. **Decide what comes inside or under cover when a warning is issued:** portable solar panels,
   vehicles, outdoor gear, animals. Write the list: ______________.
3. **Trim dead branches** over the house, vehicles and power lines.
4. **Know where to put the vehicles** when hail threatens (garage, carport, a covered public
   place).

## The words and the numbers

- **Severe thunderstorm:** hail of **1 inch (quarter size)** or more, or wind of **58 mph** or more.
- The NWS adds a **damage threat** tag to the warning: **"considerable"** for hail of 1.75 inches
  (golf ball) or more or wind of 70 mph or more; **"destructive"** for hail of 2.75 inches (baseball)
  or more or wind of 80 mph or more. **Only "destructive" sets off a Wireless Emergency Alert on
  phones** (NWS, 2021).
- **High Wind Warning** or **Wind Advisory:** strong wind without a thunderstorm.
- **Dust Storm Warning:** blowing dust cutting visibility to near zero.
More alert words: → 29-10.

## Hail

{{figure fig-hail-sizes}}

- **How fast it falls** (NSSL, rough estimates): hail under 1 inch about 9 to 25 mph; 1 to 1.75
  inches about 25 to 40 mph; 2 to 4 inches about 44 to 72 mph; over 4 inches possibly more than
  100 mph.
- **What it breaks:** windows, skylights, siding, roofs, vehicles, solar panels, greenhouses,
  gardens, and it can kill livestock and people caught outside (NSSL).
- **Wind-driven hail** comes in sideways, through windows and into houses.
- **On the road:** hail deep enough on the pavement drives like ice (NSSL).

**What to do:**
- **Get inside a sturdy building** and away from windows and skylights. Close curtains or blinds to
  catch glass.
- **In a vehicle:** pull off the road if you can do it safely, but **not under an overpass**, where
  stopped cars cause crashes and block the road (SPC). Stay inside, away from the windows, and cover
  your head; lie down with your back to the windows if the glass may break.
- **Outdoors with no shelter:** crouch low and protect your head with your arms or anything you
  have.
- **Solar panels:** portable panels come inside or under hard cover at the warning; it takes
  minutes and a panel may be irreplaceable (→ 09-02). A fixed ground array can sometimes be tilted
  steep or covered.
- **Garden:** row cover and shade cloth give partial protection; a hailstorm can wreck a season's
  crop in minutes (→ 05-01).
- **Animals:** livestock and poultry need shelter (→ 05-04).

## Damaging wind

- **Damaging winds are those above about 50 to 60 mph** and can reach about 100 mph; one storm's
  damage path can run for hundreds of miles (NSSL).
- Most thunderstorm wind damage is **straight-line wind** from the storm's downdraft, not tornadoes.
  A small, intense burst of it is a **downburst**; a long, wide windstorm is a **derecho** (→ 17-02).
- **What it does:** brings down trees and power lines across whole regions at once, rolls mobile
  homes, tears roofs, flips vehicles and trailers, and turns loose objects into missiles.

**What to do when a warning is issued:**
- **Go inside a sturdy building, to an interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows** (the
  tornado spot, → 17-06).
{{#if has.mobile_home}}- **Your mobile or manufactured home: leave for a sturdy building before the
  storm arrives.** Where: ______________.{{/if}}
{{#if has.apartment}}- **Apartment:** a lower-floor interior hallway or bathroom, away from windows
  and glass doors.{{/if}}
{{#if has.basement}}- **Basement:** the best place in the house for damaging wind.{{/if}}
- **Bring in or tie down** anything loose outside.
- **Do not drive** into the storm; high-sided vehicles and trailers blow over (→ 17-11).

**Afterward:**
- **Treat every downed line as live.** Keep everyone, children and pets far away.
- **Tree work after storms injures many people.** Bent trees and trapped limbs spring back. Work in
  daylight, rested, with a partner, and leave anything near power lines alone (→ 16-06).
- **Expect a long, wide outage** after a derecho (→ 17-08).
- **Photograph damage** for insurance while that works (→ 19-04).

## High wind and dust without a storm

- **High wind:** secure loose items; avoid wooded areas; take care with high-sided vehicles and
  trailers.
- **Dust storm while driving** (NWS, Dust Storms and Haboobs): if visibility drops, **pull off the road as
  far as you safely can, turn off all lights, set the parking brake, and keep your foot off the
  brake pedal**, so drivers behind do not follow your lights and hit you. Stay buckled and wait it
  out.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** warning list of what comes inside; dead branches trimmed.
- **B (moving):** do not drive into a severe thunderstorm; a trailer or tall vehicle is at
  particular risk in high wind (→ 17-11).
- **C (rural or new site):** windbreaks of trees, and a sturdy building for the animals.
