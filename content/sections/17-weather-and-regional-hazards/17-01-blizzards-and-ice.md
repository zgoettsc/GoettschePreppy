# Blizzards, ice storms and winter storms

**What this page is for.** Winter storms are the hazard most likely to cut a household off: roads
close, the power fails, and help cannot reach you for days. This page covers the four winter
hazards, the alert words that come before them, what to do before, during and after, and the one
scenario that kills people every winter: being stranded in a vehicle.
{{#if is.snow_region}}Your home county gets about **{{ region.home.snow_in }} inches of snow a
year**, so plan on this page every winter.{{/if}}
{{#if is.cold_winters}}January nights average about **{{ region.home.jan_low_f }} °F** where you
live, and the record low is **{{ region.home.record_low_f }} °F**.{{/if}}

> **SAFETY.** The three killers in a winter storm, in order:
> 1. **Carbon monoxide** (CO, a poison gas you cannot see or smell, → 29-03) from generators,
>    grills and heaters brought indoors during a cold outage. It kills prepared and unprepared
>    people alike. **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near
>    an open window.** (→ 30-01, → 08-02)
> 2. **Travel.** People die in vehicles in blizzards. Do not travel under a blizzard warning. If
>    you are stranded, **stay with the vehicle** (below).
> 3. **The cold itself:** hypothermia (the body's core getting dangerously cold) and frostbite
>    (frozen skin), → 08-05.
>
> **A frozen-then-thawed insulin vial or pen is discarded.** In a winter outage, refrigerated
> medicine must be kept from freezing (→ 29-07).

## Do this now

1. **Get a NOAA Weather Radio with SAME programmed** (→ 15-01, → 29-09). Winter storms come with
   hours of warning, and those hours are everything.
2. **CO alarms on every level and where people sleep, tested, before the first heater is lit**
   (→ 08-02). Replace each by the date printed on it.
3. **Do the "three days with no power in deep cold" walk-through on paper:** heat, water, food,
   light, medicine, sanitation (→ 13-05). Find the gaps now.
4. **Winterize the house** (→ 08-03) and **stock the vehicle kit in autumn** (→ 22-05). In a cold
   region the vehicle kit is a survival kit, not a first-aid kit.
5. **Bring firewood and fuel in reach before the storm**, not during (→ 06-04).

## The four winter hazards

| Hazard | What it does | The main danger |
|---|---|---|
| **Blizzard** | Wind plus snow makes a **whiteout**. Travel becomes impossible; drifts bury things | Stranding and getting lost. People have died a few yards from shelter |
| **Ice storm** | Freezing rain coats everything in ice | **Power lines and trees come down**: outages of days to weeks |
| **Extreme cold** | Very low air temperature, often with wind | Frostbite in minutes, hypothermia, frozen pipes, failing equipment (→ 17-04) |
| **Heavy snow** | Deep snow, roof loads, blocked doors and roads | Being cut off; roof damage; heart strain from shoveling |

## The alert words, and what each one means for you

A **watch** means get ready; a **warning** means it is happening or about to; an **advisory**
means take care (→ 29-10). Each NWS office sets its own trigger amounts for snow, ice and cold, so
copy your office's numbers into the blanks. Your office: **{{ region.home.nws_office }}**.

| NWS alert | What it means | What the household does |
|---|---|---|
| **Blizzard Warning** | Sustained wind or frequent gusts of 35 mph or more with falling or blowing snow, cutting visibility to a quarter mile or less for 3 hours or more (NSSL, Winter Weather Types) | **Nobody travels.** Lifeline rules in force. Full storm preparation should already be done at the watch |
| **Winter Storm Warning** | Heavy snow or sleet, or a mix with big impact. Your office's amount: snow over ______ in | Run the "before" list below; no travel plans for 48 hours |
| **Ice Storm Warning** | Damaging ice on lines and trees. Your office's amount: ______ in of ice | **Treat it as a power-outage warning** (→ 17-08): charge everything, fill water, stay off roads |
| **Extreme Cold Warning** | Dangerously cold air temperature or wind chill. Your office's line: ______ °F | Outside only for essential minutes, no bare skin, children stay in, no unnecessary travel |
| **Cold Weather Advisory** | Cold enough to be dangerous without care. Your office's line: ______ °F | Short, supervised time outside; full layers (→ 11-01) |
| **Winter Weather Advisory** | Snow, sleet or light ice that makes travel hazardous | Errands early; vehicle kit in the car (→ 22-05) |

> **Old names.** In October 2024 the NWS replaced the Wind Chill Watch, Wind Chill Warning and Wind
> Chill Advisory with the **Extreme Cold Watch**, **Extreme Cold Warning** and **Cold Weather
> Advisory**, because cold is dangerous with or without wind (NWS, cold product changes, 2024).
> An old card or radio that says "wind chill warning" means the same thing.

**Wind chill** is how cold it feels on bare skin when wind is added (→ 29-10). The NWS wind chill
chart shows whether bare skin freezes in 30, 10 or 5 minutes at each temperature and wind speed
(NWS, Wind Chill Chart). Use the chart, not a feeling.

## The blizzard: the whiteout is the danger

**A blizzard is defined by wind and visibility, not by how much snow falls.** Wind lifting snow
already on the ground can make a whiteout under a clear sky; this is a **ground blizzard** (NSSL).

- **Visibility can drop to nothing.** People have died walking between a house and a barn. **If
  you must go out in a whiteout, tie a rope from the door to where you are going** and keep a hand
  on it. This is an old, proven practice.
- **Drifts bury doors, vehicles, woodpiles and solar panels** (→ 09-05). Bring in what you will
  need before it starts.
- **Do not travel.** When the NWS or the road authority says do not travel, believe it.
- **Plan on three days or more of complete self-reliance**, with little or no solar power and no
  way to leave.

## The ice storm: the outage maker

- **Ice brings down power lines and trees.** Outages can last a week or more over a wide area,
  with repair crews working through terrible conditions.
- **Treat every downed line as live**, and keep everyone, children and pets far away.
- **Falling ice and limbs** injure people under trees.
- **Walking is dangerous**: traction devices, sand, wood ash or cat litter on paths (→ 11-06).
- **This is when CO poisoning happens.** People bring generators and grills inside or run heaters
  in closed rooms. Heaters run indoors only under their own pages' rules (→ 08-04, → 08-09,
  → 08-10).

## Stranded in a vehicle

This kills people in cold regions every winter, and the guidance is specific (NWS, Winter Weather
Safety; Red Cross, Winter Storm Safety):

- **Stay with the vehicle.** It is shelter, it is visible, and searchers look for vehicles.
  Walking for help in a whiteout is how people die.
- **Run the engine about 10 minutes each hour** for heat, and **first make sure the exhaust pipe
  is clear of snow.** A drifted-in tailpipe pushes CO into the cabin; this is a recurring cause of
  death.
- **Open a downwind window slightly** while the engine runs.
- **Move arms and legs** to keep blood flowing (→ 11-04).
- **Be visible:** a bright cloth on the antenna; **raise the hood once the snow stops**; dome
  light on at night if the battery allows (→ 15-07).
- **The vehicle kit is why this works** (→ 22-05): blankets, hats, mittens, hand warmers, food,
  water, a light, a shovel. Stock it before the first storm.

Some households carry a candle in a metal can for heat in a stranded car, with a window cracked.
That is an open flame in a closed space, which is a trade-off: {{choice DC-20}} → 30-21

## The household during a winter storm

**Before it hits (at the watch):**
- Firewood and fuel in reach (→ 06-04). Water containers filled (→ 03-02). Everything charged.
- CO alarms tested. Refrigerated medicine plan checked (→ 29-07).
- Warm clothes sorted for every person (→ 11-01, → 11-02).
- Vehicle fuel above half (→ 22-05).

**During:**
- **Heat one room** (→ 08-01). Layers on indoors. Light without flame where you can (→ 10-01).
- **Nothing fuel-burning without ventilation and a working CO alarm** (→ 08-02).
- **Whether an unvented heater runs while people sleep is a household decision with a real risk
  on each side**: cold on one side, carbon monoxide on the other. Read the card before the
  storm: {{choice DC-01}} → 30-02
- **Check children and elders by touch**, often: belly and back of the neck (→ 11-02).
- **Water:** let taps on outside walls drip and open cabinet doors under sinks; know where the
  main shutoff is (→ 03-06). Snow is water if you can spend the fuel to melt it (→ 03-05).
- **Medicine:** insulin and other refrigerated medicine must not freeze (→ 29-07). Keep it in the
  heated room with you, off cold floors and outside walls.

**After:**
- **Dig out carefully.** Shoveling heavy snow causes heart attacks and back injuries (CDC, Winter
  Weather). Take breaks; share the work.
- **Clear the tailpipe before starting any vehicle**, and clear snow from furnace and dryer vents
  outside the house before running them.
- Sweep snow off solar panels (→ 09-05). Check on neighbours, especially older people living
  alone (→ 18-01). Check the roof, pipes and chimney for damage.

## Sheltering by dwelling type

{{#if has.mobile_home}}**You live in a mobile or manufactured home.** These lose heat fast and
their water lines often run under the floor, where they freeze first. Skirting closed, heat tape
checked, and an agreed sturdier place to go (family, a neighbour, a warming centre) if the power
will be out for long in deep cold.{{/if}}
{{#if has.apartment}}**You live in an apartment.** Shared walls help you hold heat. Your
building's heat, water pumps and elevators may stop with the power, so stairs, stored water and
the building's exits matter. Never run a grill or generator on a balcony.{{/if}}
{{#if has.basement}}**You have a basement** ({{ dwelling.basement }}). Below-ground rooms hold
their temperature better than the floors above, but a basement on an outside wall with windows
loses heat. See → 08-01 before choosing the warm room.{{/if}}
Whatever the building: one heated room, the CO rules, stored water, and nobody on the roads.

## The worst realistic case

A blizzard or ice storm **during a longer grid failure** is the case to plan against:
- Little or no solar power for days (→ 09-05).
- No travel, no resupply, no help coming.
- Heating fuel burning faster than on any other day (→ 06-04).
- Everyone in one room for days, which strains tempers and spreads illness (→ 21-02, → 13-11).

If your plan survives that on paper, it is a real plan. Then test it (→ 24-04).

**If you do not have it:** snow is water (→ 03-05); snow banked against a wall cuts wind and
heat loss; outdoor cold is a refrigerator (→ 04-09), and a freezing risk for medicine at the same
time; any rope, cord or garden hose makes a lifeline; blankets over windows hold heat (→ 08-03).

## When it runs out

- **Heat, water and battery tighten at once.** That is what makes winter storms hard: the
  firewood burns faster, the pipes freeze, and the solar panels are buried. Work out the storm
  case, not the average day (→ 06-04, → 23-02).
- **Stretch it:** one room, layers indoors, long sleep, cook once a day, and use the free cold for
  food storage instead of burning anything to make it.
- **The habit that matters: prepare when the watch is issued, not when the warning is.**

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** the most likely emergency in a cold region and the most survivable if you have
  prepared: fuel in, water filled, CO alarms tested, everything charged, one room warm.
- **B (moving):** **do not move in a winter storm** (→ 01-02, → 01-03). If you are caught out:
  stay with the vehicle, keep the tailpipe clear, stay visible (→ 17-11).
- **C (rural or new site):** longer isolation, longer outages, no plow coming. Provision a rural
  site for a week or more of complete isolation as a normal winter condition. A lifeline between
  buildings is standard practice there.
