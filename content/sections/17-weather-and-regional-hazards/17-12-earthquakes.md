# Earthquakes

**What this page is for.** An earthquake gives no warning you can count on and is over in seconds
to a minute. What protects you is what you did beforehand (heavy things fastened down, a practiced
reflex) and what you do in the first hour after. This page covers both.
{{#if is.earthquake_region}}**Your home county is rated 2 or 3 for earthquakes in the region
data.** Do the "Do this now" list this month.{{else}}Your home county is rated lower for
earthquakes, but you may travel or move to a place that is not; the reflex below is worth knowing
everywhere.{{/if}}
Ratings in this binder are FEMA's National Risk Index for your county, on a 1-to-3 scale: they measure expected harm to people and property there, not how likely the hazard is. A big city can rate 3 for a rare hazard because so much is exposed (Cook County, Illinois, rates 3 for earthquakes), and a thinly settled county can rate 1 for a hazard that does strike it (→ 29-10).

> **SAFETY.**
> - **Drop, Cover, and Hold On.** Drop to your hands and knees, cover your head and neck (under a
>   sturdy table if one is close), and hold on until the shaking stops (Ready.gov, Earthquakes).
> - **If you are inside, stay inside.** Do not run outside, and do not stand in a doorway.
> - **After the shaking:** smell gas, leave and shut it off; expect aftershocks; near the coast,
>   **go to high ground at once** without waiting for an official warning (NWS, During a Tsunami;
>   → 17-15).
> - **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open
>   window.** After an earthquake people lose power and cook and heat improvised; carbon monoxide
>   follows (→ 30-01).

## Do this now

1. **Practice Drop, Cover, and Hold On** with everyone, including children, until it is a reflex
   (→ 24-02).
2. **Fasten heavy things:** bookcases, tall dressers, refrigerators and **the water heater** to the
   wall studs. **Move heavy and breakable things to low shelves** (Ready.gov).
3. **Know where the gas, water and electric shutoffs are** and keep the gas wrench at the meter
   (→ 16-12).
4. **Keep shoes and a flashlight by every bed.** After a night quake the floor is broken glass.
5. **Pick an out-of-area contact** everyone calls or texts; local lines jam after a quake (→ 15-03).

## Warning and alerts

- **Earthquakes cannot be predicted.** There is no watch.
- **On the West Coast, the USGS ShakeAlert system** can send a phone alert seconds before shaking
  reaches you (USGS, Early Warning). A few seconds is enough to Drop, Cover, and Hold On. If an
  alert sounds, do it at once; do not look outside first.
- **Aftershocks** follow the main shock, sometimes for days or longer. Each one is a reason to Drop,
  Cover, and Hold On again.

## During the shaking

{{figure fig-drop-cover-hold-on}}

| Where you are | What to do (Ready.gov, Earthquakes) |
|---|---|
| **Indoors** | Drop, Cover, and Hold On. Under a sturdy table or desk if one is near; otherwise against an inside wall, away from windows. Stay inside |
| **In bed** | Turn face down and cover your head and neck with a pillow. Stay there |
| **In a wheelchair or with a walker** | Lock the wheels. Bend over, cover your head and neck with your arms, a book or a pillow, and hold on. Get as low as you safely can |
| **Outdoors** | Move to an open area away from buildings, trees, streetlights and power lines. Drop, Cover, and Hold On |
| **Driving** | Pull over, stop and set the parking brake. Stay in the vehicle, away from bridges, overpasses and power lines if you can |
| **Near the coast** | Protect yourself from the shaking first. When it stops, go inland or to high ground at once (→ 17-15) |

**By dwelling:**
{{#if has.apartment}}- **Apartment:** stay inside and Drop, Cover, and Hold On. Do not use elevators
  or crowd into stairwells during shaking. Upper floors sway more; it is frightening but usually
  not the danger. Falling objects and glass are.{{/if}}
{{#if has.mobile_home}}- **Mobile or manufactured home:** it can be shaken off its supports. Drop,
  Cover, and Hold On inside; after the shaking, check whether it has shifted before using gas or
  electricity, and leave if it has. Ask a professional about earthquake bracing.{{/if}}
{{#if has.basement}}- **Basement:** no safer than the floor above during shaking; the stairs can
  fail. Drop, Cover, and Hold On where you are.{{/if}}
- **Any building:** the danger is falling objects, breaking glass and heavy furniture. That is why
  fastening things down beforehand matters more than where you stand.

## The first hour after

1. **Check yourself, then others, for injuries** (→ 12-02, → 12-09). Shoes on before you walk.
2. **Gas:** if you smell gas or hear hissing, **get everyone out and shut it off at the meter**
   (→ 16-12). Do not use matches, lighters or switches. Do not turn the gas back on yourself while
   a utility exists; if none will come, relighting yourself is a decision card:
   {{choice DC-28}} → 30-29.
3. **Fire:** small fires are common after earthquakes. Put them out if you safely can (→ 29-12).
4. **Damaged buildings:** leave if there are large cracks, a shifted foundation, or a leaning
   chimney, and do not go back in until it has been checked.
5. **Downed power lines:** keep everyone far away.
6. **Near the coast:** go to high ground now (→ 17-15).
7. **Text, do not call,** your out-of-area contact (→ 15-03).
8. **Check on neighbours** (→ 18-01). After a large earthquake, neighbours are the first rescue.

## The days after

- **Water:** pipes break. Shut the main valve if lines are broken so clean water in the house does
  not drain away. **The water heater tank is a water reserve** (→ 03-02). Treat town water as
  unsafe until told otherwise.
- **Toilets:** do not flush until you know the sewer line is intact; use a bucket toilet (→ 13-01).
- **Chimneys and stoves:** earthquakes crack chimneys and flue liners, which can set the house on
  fire or leak carbon monoxide. Using one before it is checked is a decision card:
  {{choice DC-21}} → 30-22
- **Power:** expect outages (→ 17-08).
- **Landslides:** earthquakes loosen slopes; stay clear of steep ground and cuts (→ 17-17).
- **Aftershocks:** sleep with shoes and a light by the bed; keep heavy things off shelves above
  beds.

**If you do not have it:** a sturdy table is your cover; a pillow covers your head in bed; rope or
strapping and screws into studs hold a bookcase; a bucket and bags make a toilet.

## When it runs out

- **Nothing here runs out quickly.** What expires is the practice. Drill once a year and whenever a
  child is old enough to learn it.
- **After a big earthquake,** services can be out for days to weeks across a whole region. Plan
  water, food and sanitation for that (→ 17-08).

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** things fastened down, the reflex practiced, shutoffs known.
- **B (moving):** stop the vehicle away from bridges and overpasses; after a large quake expect
  roads cut by slides and broken bridges (→ 01-03).
- **C (rural or new site):** check the building for heavy unreinforced chimneys and walls, and
  where the slopes above and below it would go (→ 17-17).
