# Skills checklist and practice schedule

**What this page is for.** A list of the skills this binder relies on, a way to score each
adult honestly, and a schedule for practising. Practise **while failure is free**: with the
shops open, emergency care a phone call away and a hardware store to fix your mistake.

> **Caution.** Two practice rules have no exceptions. **Anything that burns fuel is practised
> with a working carbon monoxide alarm and fresh air** (→ 08-02): **Never run a generator,
> charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open window.** And **medical
> fallbacks** (a pump failing, a missed dose) **are practised with your clinician's
> instructions, not from a checklist** (→ 12-11).

## Do this now

1. **Score each adult honestly** on the checklist below, 0 to 3. Most people score 1 on things
   they believe they can do, because reading is not doing.
2. **Pick the three lowest scores marked ★** and schedule them this quarter. Three real skills a
   quarter is twelve a year, which is most of this list.
3. **Score every adult separately.** A skill only one person has fails when that person is
   away, hurt or asleep (→ 18-07).
4. **Write the date on every line you practise.** An undated tick is a memory, not a record.

## The scoring scale

| Score | Meaning | The honest test |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Never done it | You have read about it |
| **1** | Done once, with help and everything to hand | You would need to look it up |
| **2** | Done alone, in good conditions | You could do it today, slowly |
| **3** | Done alone **in bad conditions**: dark, cold, tired, no internet | You could teach it |

**Only a 3 counts in a plan.** A 2 is a skill you have; a 3 is a skill you can rely on when it
is cold, dark and a child is crying. Making conditions worse on purpose is the whole practice
method.

### The five-step method (from → 23-05)

1. Read the page. 2. Do it with everything available. 3. **Do it with one thing taken away**:
no power, no running water, in the cold, in the dark, one-handed. 4. **Have someone else do it
while you say nothing.** 5. **Write what surprised you on the page.** Your notes about your own
stove, your own water heater and your own pump are worth more than any source this binder
cites.

### How fast skills fade, and how often to practise

| Fades | Skills | Practise again |
|---|---|---|
| **Fast** | Anything done under stress: bleeding control, epinephrine, the grab-and-go, medical device fallbacks | **Every 3 to 6 months** |
| **Medium** | Fire in bad weather, cooking on coals, map and compass, radio, sharpening | **Every year** |
| **Slow** | Gardening, canning, sewing, soap, butchering | **Each season it applies** |
| **Hardly** | Knots, knowing the plan | At the yearly review (→ 25-10) |

**The fast list is also the life-safety list.** That is why → 24-02 exists.

## What you need

{{#if has.epinephrine}}An **epinephrine trainer device** (no needle, no drug), {{/if}}a Stop the
Bleed kit and course, practice supplies (extra jars and lids, seed, fabric scraps, a cheap knife
to learn sharpening on{{#if has.firearms}}, inexpensive practice ammunition{{/if}}), a wall
calendar and a notebook for each skill. **Practice supplies are the cheapest line on → 22-07
and the one most often skipped.**

No money? Scrap wood, thrift-store clothing to practise repairs on, a borrowed tool, and best of
all **a neighbour who already knows how** (→ 18-03). An hour watching someone skilled beats a
week of reading.

## When it runs out

Skills do not run out; **chances to practise do.** Once the grid is down, every practice
session costs real supplies and real risk, and mistakes stop being free.
- **Runway:** exactly as long as normal life lasts.
- **Ration:** do not. Practice is the cheapest thing in this binder.
- **Resupply:** teach someone. Teaching is the form of practice that sticks best (→ 18-07).
- **Substitute:** none. A skill you have only read about is a plan, not an ability.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** all of it. This is when practice is free.
- **B, moving:** only the ★ rows matter: medical, fire, water, warmth, finding your way, the
  grab-and-go.
- **C, establishing:** the slow-fade production skills come to the front; keep drilling the ★
  rows anyway.
