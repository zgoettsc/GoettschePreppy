# Family drills: fire, grab-and-go, shelter, medical, blackout

**What this page is for.** Under stress, people do what they have practised, not what they
have read. These drills take ten minutes each, four times a year, and they find the problems
(a blocked exit, a missing battery, a bag nobody can lift) while finding them costs nothing.

> **Caution: how you run a drill matters as much as running it.** {{#if has.child}}With
> children in the house, **a frightening drill is a failed drill**: a child scared of the
> practice may freeze in the real thing. Keep it short, calm, matter-of-fact and praised.
> {{/if}}**Never use a live epinephrine auto-injector in a drill**: trainer devices only.
> **Never run a fuel-burning drill without a working carbon monoxide alarm** (→ 08-02).
> **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open
> window.**

## Do this now

1. **Run the fire-and-exit drill this month.** A house fire is the life-threatening emergency
   your household is most likely to actually face. Ready.gov says a fire can become
   life-threatening in two minutes (Ready.gov, Home Fires).
2. **Run the grab-and-go drill** (→ 22-03), timed, and write the time down. The first time is
   always embarrassing. That is the point.
3. **Put the four quarterly drills on the calendar**: one a quarter, ten minutes each.
4. {{#if has.child}}**Teach each child who can talk three things by heart**: their full name
   and their grown-ups' names; what the smoke alarm sounds like and where to meet; and **"if
   you feel funny or hurt, tell a grown-up right away."**{{else}}**Agree who checks on whom**
   (neighbours, relatives nearby) and add them to the comms plan (→ 15-03).{{/if}}

## Drill rules: read before running any of them

1. **Announce the first run.** Surprise drills are for people who have done the announced
   version. Ambushing small children teaches fear, not what to do.
2. **Keep it under ten minutes.** Long drills get resisted, and resisted drills stop happening.
3. **Time it, write it down, try to beat it.** A stopwatch works better than a lecture.
4. **Praise taking part; never criticise the performance.** Name the specific thing someone
   did well (CDC, Positive Parenting Tips).
5. **Debrief for five minutes**: what worked, what did not, what to change (below).
6. **Rotate who leads.** Every adult must be able to run the whole thing.{{#if has.child}}
   Older children can be "in charge" of one step.{{/if}}
7. **Make one thing harder each repeat**: in the dark, a phone "dead", one adult missing, in
   winter clothing.
8. **Stop if a child is truly frightened**, finish gently, and try a smaller version next time
   (→ 20-08).

## Drill 1: fire and exit (quarterly; the most important one)

**Scenario:** the smoke alarm sounds at night.

| Step | Who | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Everyone | **Sound the real alarm** (its test button) so everyone knows the noise |
| 2 | Adults | One adult to each person who needs help{{#if has.infant}}; **babies are carried**{{/if}} |
| 3 | Everyone | Out **by the main route**; then run it again with that route blocked |
| 4 | Everyone | **Meet at one fixed outside spot**, touch it and stay. Same spot every time: ______________ |
| 5 | Adults | Head count{{#if has.pets}}, pets only if it is safe; **never go back in for a pet**{{/if}} |
| 6 | Everyone | Debrief: what was in the way, what was dark, what was hard |

{{photo meeting-point}}

**Teach it and mean it: get out, stay out.** Practise crawling low under smoke, feeling a door
with the back of your hand before opening it, and the meeting point. Ready.gov advises practising
your escape plan at least twice a year and having two ways out of every room (Ready.gov, Home
Fires). **Upstairs window exits and escape ladders** get their own, separate practice: walk it
slowly, do not rush it (→ 20-02).

## Drill 2: the grab-and-go (quarterly)

**Scenario:** you have one minute to leave.
- **Timed.** People{{#if has.pets}}, pets{{/if}}, **medical kit**{{#if has.refrigerated_meds}} and the medicine cooler{{/if}}, go-bags, phones, keys, wallets,
  out the door (→ 22-03).
- **Run it in the dark** the second time. Then with one adult "unavailable".
- **What it always finds:** bags moved, a broken strap, carriers buried in storage, and nobody
  sure who takes which child.
- **Then run the 10-minute version**: documents, water, food box, coats and boots for
  {{ household.size }}. Is the pile really ready where it should be (→ 22-05)?

## Drill 3: storm shelter (before your storm season; quarterly in season)

**Scenario:** a tornado warning for your area, or a severe storm warning.

NWS advice: get to the **lowest floor, the most interior room with no windows**, and **cover
your head** (NWS, Tornado Safety; → 17-02, → 17-06). Your spot:
{{#if has.mobile_home}}- **You live in a mobile or manufactured home. It is not a safe shelter in a tornado,
  even tied down.** NOAA's Storm Prediction Center says leave it for a sturdy building or a
  storm shelter **before** the storm arrives (NOAA SPC, Tornado Safety FAQ). Your drill is
  the drive or walk to that building: where it is ______________, how long it takes ______.
{{/if}}{{#if has.basement}}- **Basement**, under something sturdy, away from windows{{#if has.walkout}} and away from the walkout's glass wall{{/if}}.
{{/if}}{{#if has.shelter_room}}- **Your named interior room**: {{ dwelling.shelter_room }}.
{{/if}}{{#if has.apartment}}- **Apartment**: an interior hallway or bathroom on the lowest floor you can reach
  safely; not the top floor. Know the stairs; never the elevator.
{{/if}}- Shelter spot in writing: ______________. Second choice if the first smells of gas or is
  blocked: ______________.

{{photo shelter-spot}}

- **Practise getting everyone there**{{#if has.child}}, carrying the small children{{/if}}{{#if has.mobility_aid}} and moving the person who uses a wheelchair or walker{{/if}}, shoes on, the weather radio going (→ 15-01).
  Helmets (bike helmets are fine) protect heads.
- **Keep at the shelter spot:** shoes for everyone, a light, a whistle, water, a weather radio.
- **Medicines that must stay in a temperature range are grabbed on the way, not stored in the
  shelter.** An unheated basement or storm room can freeze or overheat them (→ 29-07).
  {{#if has.epinephrine}}Epinephrine travels with the person or the medical kit.{{/if}}
- **The debrief question:** how long did it take, and would that have been fast enough at 2 a.m.?

## Drill 4: medical drills (quarterly; rotate them)

These are fast-fade skills (→ 24-01). Run one each quarter.

**4a. Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis).**{{#if has.anaphylaxis}} Someone in your household has an anaphylaxis allergy (→ 02-03).{{/if}}
- **Read the person's written emergency plan aloud**, then have another adult do it.
- **Practise with a trainer auto-injector** on the correct spot (the outer thigh), saying it
  aloud: *epinephrine first, then call for help, then a second dose if the plan says so*
  (CDC, Recognizing and Responding to Anaphylaxis).
- **Practise finding it in the dark** in every place it is kept: the person's bag, the other
  adults' bags, the vehicles, the house kit.
- Rehearse the babysitter or grandparent version: **someone who has never done this must be
  able to follow the plan.**

**4b. Diabetes emergency.**{{#if has.diabetes}} Someone in your household has diabetes (→ 02-01, → 02-05).{{/if}}
- **Severe low blood sugar:** the other adults find the emergency glucagon and **go through the
  steps on a trainer or the package diagram**, not just point at the box; say the signs aloud
  and where the fast sugar is kept.
- **Pump or sensor failure:** walk through the backup plan your clinician wrote, out loud
  (→ 02-02).
- **Power loss:** run the devices and phone on a power bank and check the runtime you believe
  (→ 09-10).
- **Heat and cold:** practise where the insulin goes on a very cold car trip and a very hot one
  (→ 29-07). **A frozen-then-thawed insulin vial or pen is discarded.**
{{#if has.child}}- **Children's version:** if a grown-up collapses, the child's job is noise and feet (shout,
  whistle, run for help), never medicine (→ 20-04).
{{/if}}

**4c. Bleeding.**
- Tourniquet on a limb (a practice device), wound packing on a trainer, and **saying the time
  aloud and writing it down**. **Never loosen a tourniquet once placed while help can still
  come; mark the time. If no help will ever come, the conversion decision is DC-25 (→ 30-26).**
  (→ 12-02)

**4d. Your household's own condition.**
{{#if has.oxygen}}- Oxygen: switch from concentrator to cylinder in the dark; read the cylinder gauge (→ 02-08).
{{/if}}{{#if has.cpap}}- CPAP: run it for a night on battery and note the percentage left in the morning (→ 02-24, → 09-10).
{{/if}}{{#if has.condition.CL-11}}- Seizure: every adult says the seizure first-aid steps aloud and times a pretend seizure (→ 02-11).
{{/if}}- Other: ______________ (from the condition page's warning signs, → 12-11).

## Drill 5: communications (twice a year)

- **Everyone finds the printed contact card** and reads out the out-of-area contact
  ({{ contacts.out_of_area.name }}) and the meeting points (→ 15-03).
{{#if has.gmrs}}- **Handheld radios:** everyone can switch one on, find the family channel and pass a
  message (→ 15-02).
{{/if}}{{#if has.noaa_radio}}- **NOAA Weather Radio:** anyone can switch it on and get the local forecast (→ 15-01).
{{/if}}- **The "phones are dead" version:** how do you find each other with no phones and no power?
  If there is no answer, that is the finding. Fix it on → 15-03.

## Drill 6: blackout (twice a year; see → 24-04)

The full version is a whole weekend with the main breaker off (→ 24-04). **The half version** is
one evening with the power off: cook a meal, light the house, do bedtime, and write down every
single thing you reached for that did not work.

## Drill 7: the sky call (once each season)

**Scenario:** there is no forecast; you are the forecast.
- **Write tomorrow's weather call down**: sky, wind, pressure trend if you have a barometer,
  your prediction. **Before** checking the weather radio (→ 17-10, → 15-01).
- Check it, and **score it right or wrong on the page.** No partial credit.
- One call a season keeps the skill alive while a real forecast still exists to grade you.

## Drill 8: heat and generator (every year, before {{#if is.cold_winters}}the first hard freeze{{else}}the season you would need them{{/if}})

**Scenario:** the power is out in bad weather and nobody in the house has ever lit the heater
or started the generator. **This drill exists so the first time is not that night.**
**Every adult at the gear**, in daylight and calm weather.{{#if has.child}} **Children with
another adult or away: never within reach of a lit heater, open fuel or a running
generator.**{{/if}} A fire extinguisher within reach (→ 29-12). The manuals and the full pages
govern every step.

**8a. Carbon monoxide alarms (do this first; 10 minutes).**
- Find the alarms ({{ gear.co_alarms }} CO and {{ gear.smoke_alarms }} smoke in settings).
  **Confirm they run on batteries**: an alarm that only plugs in does nothing in an outage.
- Put batteries in and hold **TEST** until each sounds. Mark the mounting spots: one where
  people sleep (in the warm room, → 08-01), one on every level, not in a dead-air corner and not
  right next to a heater (→ 08-02). Buy to that number.
- Mount them now, or store them **with batteries** beside the heater so they can be up in two
  minutes.

{{#if has.kerosene_heater}}**8b. Wick kerosene heater practice light** (first time about 2 hours, mostly waiting; → 08-10).
- Unbox, assemble, batteries in. **Fill outdoors** from a **labelled kerosene can**; read the
  label aloud first. **Fuel is never stored or transferred near an open flame or a running
  appliance.**
- Let the new wick soak as long as the manual says before lighting. Lighting early ruins it.
- **First burn outdoors** (factory oils burn off), never in a garage. Check the flame height
  the manual gives.
- Shut down as the manual says, confirm the flame is out, and wait before any relight. When
  cool, test the tip-over shut-off.
{{/if}}{{#if has.propane_heater}}**8c. Portable propane heater practice light** (30 minutes; → 08-09).
- Outdoors, or for the full rehearsal in the warm room with the CO alarm up and the fresh-air
  openings the manual requires.
- Connect as the manual says; **soapy-water leak test** at every joint. Light, hold, and
  confirm the flame.
- Shut down: **cylinder valve closed first**, let the line burn empty, then the control to off.
- Where the cylinder sits: {{choice DC-02}} → 30-03
{{/if}}{{#if has.generator}}**8d. Generator first start** (about 1 hour; → 09-04, → 09-15).
- **Outdoors only, at least 20 feet from the house, exhaust pointed away** (CDC, Carbon Monoxide
  Poisoning: Basics). Never in a garage, even with the door open. A CO alarm on inside, on the
  generator side of the house.
- Follow the manual's first-start steps in order: **oil first**, fuel, start, a light load.
  Prove one real job: charge a power station or run one appliance through an outdoor-rated cord.
- **Never connect a generator to house wiring except through a transfer switch, or an interlock
  kit with the main breaker off. Never backfeed through an outlet with a double-male ("suicide")
  cord.**
- Shut down, let it cool before refuelling, log the date and hours run.
{{/if}}
Running an unvented heater while people sleep is a household choice: {{choice DC-01}} → 30-02

**What drill 8 always finds:** a missing battery, no soapy-water bottle, a hose or cord that
does not reach, a fuel can nobody labelled. Fix it before you need it.

## The debrief: where the value is

Five minutes, every time, three questions:
1. **What took longer than we expected?**
2. **What did we reach for that was not there or did not work?**
3. **What one thing do we change: in the house, in a kit, or in this binder?**

Write the change on the page it affects. **A drill that changes nothing was a performance.**

## Targets: write the result against a number

Timing is half the record; the other half is a target. **Set your own targets in pencil** and
tighten them as real times come in. Never tighten one by shaming a slow run.

## What you need

{{#if has.epinephrine}}An **epinephrine trainer device** (ask the prescriber or the maker; they
are often free), {{/if}}a tourniquet and wound-packing trainer, a stopwatch or phone timer,
whistles for everyone, helmets for the shelter spot, working smoke and CO alarms with fresh
batteries, and escape ladders for upstairs bedrooms.

No money? Drills cost nothing but time. A rolled towel practises wound packing; a kitchen timer
times the grab. **The only thing with no substitute is the trainer auto-injector**: never
practise with a live one.

## When it runs out

Drills do not run out; **the willingness to run them does.**
- **Runway:** enthusiasm lasts about two drills. Then it becomes a calendar item or it stops.
- **Ration:** short and often beats long and rare. Ten minutes, four times a year.
- **Resupply:** let a child lead one; let them "catch" the adults out.
- **Substitute:** none. A plan nobody has rehearsed is a document, not a plan.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** every drill here, on a calendar, for good. Most of them cover emergencies
  that happen in normal life too: fire, storms, a severe allergic reaction, a winter outage.
- **B, moving:** the grab and comms drills **are** the phase B plan. Add a "reload the vehicle
  at a stop" drill and a "one adult alone with all the children" version.
- **C, establishing:** run all of them again at the new place in the first week. New building,
  new exits, new meeting spot, new shelter spot: the old drills are wrong there.
