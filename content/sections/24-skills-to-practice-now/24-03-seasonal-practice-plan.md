# Seasonal practice plan

**What this page is for.** Most preparedness skills have a season. You cannot practise
cold-weather cooking in July or can tomatoes in March, and **wood you cut this autumn is not
fuel until next year.** This is a year-round calendar that puts each skill in the month where
it belongs, adjusted to where you live.

**Your home base:** {{ region.home.county }}, {{ region.home.state_name }} · hardiness zone
{{ region.home.zone }} · median last spring frost {{ region.home.last_frost }} · median first
fall frost {{ region.home.first_frost }} · January mean low {{ region.home.jan_low_f }} °F ·
July mean high {{ region.home.jul_high_f }} °F. Frost dates are medians: about half of years
frost comes later in spring or earlier in autumn than the date printed.

## Do this now

1. **Find the current month below and do that month's items.** Do not wait for January.
2. **Put the four anchors (below) on the family calendar now.** One spring and one autumn
   weekend each for the kit check and inventory, plus a blackout weekend in each.
3. **Add the four quarterly sittings** (table below) to the same calendar: one entry each.
4. **Write your frost dates on → 25-01**; the garden half of this calendar hangs off them.
5. **Log everything you actually do** on → 24-01, with the date.

## The quarterly sittings: one calendar entry, not five

Several pages ask for something "every quarter". As separate reminders they all lapse; folded
onto the two anchor weekends plus two more evenings, they are four sittings a year. The method
for each lives on its own page; this table only schedules it.

| Every quarter | What gets done | Page |
|---|---|---|
| **Power station check**{{#if !has.power_station}} (if you get one){{/if}} | Run each station down and back up as its manual says; note the charge you found it at (a rough health check); store it at the charge level the manual gives. **Before a forecast storm or a trip away, charge to full** | → 09-03 |
| **Shielded-spares check** | Confirm spare electronics stored in shielded bags or a metal box still hold a charge; reseal | → 27-03 |
| **Children re-measured**{{#if !has.child}} (if children join the household){{/if}} | Height, weight, current sizes into the ledger; check the next sizes fit the season ahead | → 11-09 |
| **Room-by-room safety walk** | At a small child's eye level: button batteries, cords, fuel, chemicals, medicines. **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products.** | → 20-02 |

In the two anchor months these ride the anchor weekend; in the other two quarters they get their
own evening. The yearly review (→ 25-10) checks one thing about this table: **that all four
quarters actually happened.**

## Which check matters most where you live

{{#if is.cold_winters}}**Winter is the season that kills people where you live.** The autumn check is the one that
has to be right: boots and layers that fit, vehicle kits in, wood stacked and dry, carbon
monoxide alarms tested, the heating plan rehearsed. **Do it before the first hard freeze, not
after** (NWS, Winter Weather Safety).
{{/if}}{{#if is.hot_summers}}**Summer heat is a serious hazard where you live.** The spring check is the one that has to be
right: cooling plan, shade, water stored before the heat, medicines that spoil in heat, fans
on battery (→ 11-08, → 17-04).
{{/if}}{{#if is.hurricane_region}}**Hurricane season** runs through summer and autumn on the Atlantic and Gulf coasts. Finish
the spring check and the evacuation plan before it starts (→ 17-13).
{{/if}}{{#if is.tornado_region}}**Tornado season** peaks in spring in much of the country. Run the shelter drill before it
(→ 24-02, → 17-02).
{{/if}}{{#if is.wildfire_region}}**Wildfire season** peaks in the dry months. Clear around the house and rehearse the
evacuation before them (→ 17-14).
{{/if}}{{#if is.flood_region}}**Floods** come with snowmelt, spring storms and tropical rain. Know your route to higher
ground before the wet season (→ 17-03).
{{/if}}Your top hazards from the region data: {{#each region.home.hazards as h}}{{h.name}} ({{h.rating_text}}); {{/each}}see → 17-01 and the region appendix.

## The calendar

Months are for the continental US. If your winters are mild, move the cold-weather items to
your coldest month and spend the winter months on the garden.

### Winter: December, January, February

{{#if is.cold_winters}}The hardest season, so the best training: everything you practise now is practised in real
conditions.{{else}}Your winters are mild by national standards. Use them for indoor skills and the cool-season
garden, and still practise one cold night.{{/if}}

## The wood calendar: the one with a one-year lag

{{figure fig-wood-year-lag}}

| When | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Late winter to early spring** | Fell and cut to length | Easier hauling on frozen ground; less sap |
| **Spring** | Split | Split wood starts drying at the right time of year |
| **Summer** | Stack off the ground, covered on top, open on the sides | This is where the drying happens |
| **Autumn** | Move a winter's worth close to the house | You will not want to haul it in the coldest month |
| **Winter** | Burn **last year's** wood | **Wood cut this year is next year's fuel** (MU Extension, Wood Fuel for Heating; → 06-03, → 06-04) |

**Take the lag to heart.** It is why "we will cut wood when we need it" fails, and it is the
strongest single reason to start the wood cycle now (→ 23-04).

## The garden calendar (short version)

Full detail is on → 25-01, built from your zone ({{ region.home.zone }}) and frost dates. The
rhythm in most of the country:
- **Midwinter:** order seed; start onions and slow crops indoors.
- **Some weeks before the last frost** (the seed packet gives the number for each crop): start
  warm-season transplants indoors; sow cool crops outside as soon as the soil can be worked, **before** the last frost.
- **After the last frost** ({{ region.home.last_frost }}): warm-season transplants go out.
- **Summer:** sow in succession, water, preserve as things come in.
- **Late summer:** save seed; plant garlic and cover crops; last plantings under cover.
- **After the first frost** ({{ region.home.first_frost }}): clean up, mulch, compost, and **plan
  next year while it is fresh.**

## What you need

A **wall calendar or a set of repeating phone reminders** (the whole page fails without one), a
notebook for results, and each season's supplies bought **before** the season: seed in midwinter,
canning lids before summer, wood in spring{{#if has.child}}, children's winter gear in late
summer{{/if}}, while it is all in stock and cheap.

No money? Any calendar works. **The substitute for a calendar is a habit tied to something you
already do**: the kit check on the weekend the clocks change, seed ordering on the first snow
day.

## When it runs out

The calendar never runs out; **the season does, and it does not come back for a year.**
- **Runway:** each item here has a window of a few weeks. **Miss the preserving month and you
  have no preserved food; miss spring and the wood is not dry next winter.**
- **Ration:** if a year is too busy, keep the **four anchors** and drop the rest.
- **Resupply:** next year. That is the whole cost of a missed window: a year.
- **Substitute:** none for the seasonal items. That is why this is a calendar and not a to-do
  list: a list can slip a month; a season cannot.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** run the full calendar. Every year you do, the binder gets more accurate and
  the household gets faster.
- **B, moving:** the calendar pauses, but **the season you move in decides almost everything**
  about whether it works (→ 01-02). Moving in deep winter{{#if has.child}} with small
  children{{/if}} is a very different thing from moving in late spring.
- **C, establishing:** the calendar becomes the work of life: wood, garden, preserving, repair,
  repeat. It stops being practice and becomes the year.
