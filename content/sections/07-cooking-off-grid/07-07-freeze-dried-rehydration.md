# Freeze-dried and dehydrated meals: rehydrating them safely

**What this page is for.** How to turn emergency food pouches into safe meals: telling the two
kinds of "just add water" food apart, how much water, how long to wait, cold-water rehydration
when there is no heat, feeding small children, and preparing an allergic person's meal with no
chance of cross-contact.

> **SAFETY.** **Two lines that must never blur.** **Allergy:** if anyone in the household has a
> food allergy, their meal is a **checked pouch prepared in its own pouch with their own marked
> tools**; it never touches the family pot, spoon or counter (→ 02-04; FDA, Food Allergies).
> **Scald:** a just-filled pouch is boiling water in a floppy bag. Stand it in a bowl or pot on a
> hard surface, never on a lap or a sleeping bag, and test the temperature before any child eats
> (→ 12-03). And **only true freeze-dried food may be rehydrated with cold water** (below).

## Do this now

1. **Read the pouch first: two kinds of food wear the same label.**
   - **"Add boiling water, wait" = true freeze-dried:** already cooked before drying; the method
     on this page applies, and cold water works.
   - **"Boil" or "simmer X minutes" = a dehydrated blend** (many bucket kits and "just add water"
     pasta or rice meals): its pasta, beans and textured soy protein are **not pre-cooked**. It
     needs a **real boil on a stove** (→ 07-05, → 07-01). **It is not a cold-water meal**, and a
     kettle pour alone will not make it safe or edible. Note the type in your inventory (→ 04-19).
2. **Water per pouch = the figure on the label.** Products differ; measure it. Extra water is
   wasted energy and soup where you wanted stew.
3. **Boil in batches:** one litre of water is about two pouches (→ 07-06).
4. **Seal the pouch and wait** the time on the label (15 minutes or less for one common maker's
   meals; Mountain House FAQ). Stir once half-way,
   reseal, keep waiting. Opening early gives crunchy centres and loses heat.
5. **The allergic person's pouch first**, with their own marked tools, before any other pouch or
   pot is opened (procedure below).
6. **No way to boil? Cold water works, for true freeze-dried pouches only:** same pouch, cold or
   room-temperature water, **at least twice the label's hot-water time** (one common maker's
   advice; Mountain House FAQ), longer in a cold room; check that the centre is soft. It is safe
   because the food was fully cooked before it was dried; it is just cold and less cheering. **A "boil or simmer" pouch in cold water is
   raw pasta and raw beans: wait for a stove.**

## Why / how it works

### Why true freeze-dried food is safe cold

True freeze-dried meals are cooked, then frozen and dried under vacuum, and sealed; makers of
true freeze-dried meals state that they are pre-cooked (for example, Mountain House FAQ). Kept
intact they keep on the shelf for years. Adding water **reconstitutes** them;
it does not cook them. That is why cold water is safe for them and not for dehydrated blends that
were never cooked.

### In the pouch or in a pot

| | **In the pouch** | **In a pot** |
|---|---|---|
| Dishes and wash water | **None**: the pouch is the bowl | Pot, bowls, spoons (→ 13-02) |
| Cross-contact risk | **No shared surfaces** | The one-pot problem (→ 07-01) |
| Texture | Fair; corners rehydrate unevenly | **Better**: stir, simmer, adjust |
| Portioning | One pouch, fixed | **Easy**: split, thin, stretch |
| Heat in a cold room | Poor; wrap it in a towel | Better under a lid; best in a haybox (→ 07-02) |

**For an allergic person the pouch is a real safety gain.** Prepared in its own bag with its own
tools, a checked meal has no route to the family's food: no shared pot, no shared spoon, no shared
serving surface. That is more control than a shared pot can give.

### The allergic person's pouch: the fixed sequence

{{#if has.condition.CL-04}}This household has a food allergy on record (→ 02-04).{{else}}Use this
sequence for anyone with a food allergy, including a guest.{{/if}}

1. **Checked pouch only.** The label has been read against the person's allergen list and the pouch
   is marked as checked, with the date (→ 04-19). No mark, no meal. Labels can change between
   batches of the same product.
2. **Clean hands**, washed after touching any other food.
3. **Open it with their own marked scissors**, fill with measured boiling water **before** any other
   pouch is opened at that station, stir with **their own spoon only**, seal.
4. **Rest, cool, serve** in the pouch or their own marked bowl. Their tools are washed first, in
   clean water, and kept apart.

### Feeding small children

{{#if has.young_child}}This household has a child under 6.{{/if}}
- **Cooling takes time.** Food made with boiling water is still scalding **after** the rest
  time, and longer if wrapped for warmth. Stir to let steam out, taste a test
  spoonful, and check it on your wrist like a bottle.
- **Thin and mash for toddlers:** a splash of warm (not boiling) water and a mash with the spoon
  makes most pouch meals soft. Cut or mash anything chunky; choking rules do not relax in an
  emergency (→ 20-01).
- **Serve from their own bowl**, not from spoons going in and out of the family pouch.
  Half-eaten rehydrated food spoils like any cooked food (→ 04-17).
- Rice and potato meals rehydrate softest and are usually the reliable children's meals; note
  the favourites in the inventory (→ 04-19).

### Respect the rest time

The wait is not a suggestion: water is still soaking in and heat is doing the work. Opening early
loses heat and leaves hard centres. In a cold room, wrap the resting pouch in a towel or put it in
the haybox (→ 07-02).

### Adding calories

Freeze-dried meals are often lean for people working hard in a cold house. The household's daily
need is about **{{ derived.calories.day }} calories** (→ 04-01). The densest food in the pantry is
**fat** (→ 04-12): a spoonful of oil, ghee or lard stirred in after rehydrating adds calories and
fullness for no extra water or fuel. For an allergic person, the fat passes the same label check
as everything else (some oils contain soy or other allergens).

### Every pouch drinks water

This food uses water; that is the price of its shelf life. Plan stored water **above** the
drinking floor when freeze-dried food is a large part of the diet (→ 03-01: your planning figure
is {{ derived.water.plan_gal_day }} gallons a day for the household). When water is scarcer than
energy, eat freeze-dried food **last**: canned food carries its own water.

| A bowl or pot to stand hot pouches in | Scald prevention | |

**Scavenge or substitute:**

- Any zip-top bag or lidded jar rehydrates loose freeze-dried food; allow a longer rest.
- No marked scissors? A knife **washed and dried first, used on the allergic person's food only**,
  then straight back to their wash rack.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** the pouch count and calorie arithmetic are on → 04-19. If anyone has a food allergy,
  count their safe pouches separately: that number is usually the one that runs out first.
- **Ration:** cold-water rehydration (true freeze-dried only) uses no energy; half-pouch meals
  stretch the count; add fat before opening a second pouch.
- **Resupply:** none; this food is bought. Stored staples (→ 04-03), home-dried food (→ 04-06) and
  food production (Section 05) come next.
- **Spend it on the right days:** travel days and days with little power, not days when the kettle
  is running comfortably on sun.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** the everyday supper of a long outage: kettle (→ 07-06), pouches, flask.
- **B (moving):** the travelling food. Cold-rehydrate at stops if you cannot boil. The allergy kit
  and safe pouches are packed first and kept within reach.
- **C (establishing):** the bridge stock while gardens and animals come in. The in-pouch procedure
  for an allergic person matters **more** in a shared or borrowed kitchen, not less.
