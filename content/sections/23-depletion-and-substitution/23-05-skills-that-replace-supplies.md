# Skills that replace supplies

**What this page is for.** Everything else in this section is arithmetic on a shrinking pile.
A skill is different: it **does not run out, cannot be stolen, weighs nothing, travels with
you, and is worth more the longer things go on.** This page ranks the skills that actually end
a supply problem and says where in this binder to learn each one.

## Do this now

1. **Pick one skill from tier 1 below and do it this month**, badly, while a failure is funny
   rather than costly. Reading is not learning; the first try is always the worst.
2. **Every adult learns the water chain** (→ 03-04){{#if has.diabetes}} **and the diabetes
   routine** (→ 02-01, → 02-02, → 02-05, → 02-06){{/if}}{{#if has.anaphylaxis}} **and how to
   give epinephrine** (→ 02-03){{/if}}. If only one person knows a life-safety routine, the
   household depends on that one person being there.
3. **Put practice on the calendar** (→ 24-03). Skills fade. Unscheduled practice does not
   happen.
4. {{#if has.child}}**Teach the children the version that fits their age** (→ 20-04,
   → 20-07). Young children can fetch, sort and water plants: that is a real contribution, and
   it is how the skill outlives you. Which dangerous jobs children may do is a household
   worksheet: {{choice DC-24}} → 30-25{{else}}**Teach someone outside the household** one
   skill you know (→ 18-07). That is how a skill outlives you.{{/if}}

## What makes a skill worth learning

Rank a skill by four questions:
1. **Does it end a supply problem, or only stretch one?** Making soap ends one. Using less
   soap stretches one.
2. **How long until it is useful?** Water treatment: an afternoon. Gardening well: years.
3. **What does it still need?** A skill that depends on something irreplaceable is not a stage
   4 answer (→ 23-04). It is a substitute with extra steps.
4. **Is it worth something to other people?** Skills are the longest-lasting trade good there
   is (→ 18-03): you trade what you make and keep the ability.

## Skills nobody counts, and everybody needs

They are not on gear lists, and they carry more weight than half the tools in the garage.
- **Keeping a routine when nothing is normal** (→ 21-04): it keeps children steady and adults
  sane.
- **Calming a conflict** (→ 18-06, → 14-13): the cheapest security measure in this binder.
- **Teaching** (→ 18-07, → 20-03): how one person's skill becomes two people's.
- **Keeping records and an inventory** (→ 22-04): you cannot ration what you have not counted.
- **Saying no calmly** (→ 18-05, → 14-13): a real survival skill.
- **Sleeping properly** (→ 21-07): the multiplier under every other skill on this page.

## How to actually learn one

1. **Read the binder page once.** That is orientation, not ability.
2. **Do it with everything available**: shops, spare parts, someone to call.
3. **Do it again with one thing taken away**: no power, no running water, in the cold.
4. **Have someone else in the household do it while you watch and say nothing.**
5. **Write down what surprised you** on the page itself (→ 24-01).
6. **Repeat every year.** Everything here fades.

**Do not stockpile a tool for a skill you have never used.** An unused pressure canner, an
untested water filter and a gun that has never been sighted in are decorations. Every
purchase in this binder implies a practice session.

No money?
- **People.** A neighbour who cans, hunts, welds or gardens is worth more than any book, and
  the relationship is itself preparation (→ 18-01, → 18-03).
- **Libraries and university Extension offices**: free, local, reliable, and staffed by people
  whose job is to teach these skills (→ 25-03).
- **Old manuals and printed books** from thrift stores: the older ones already assume no
  internet.

## When it runs out

**It does not.** That is the point of this page.
- **Runway:** unlimited, but skills **fade** without practice. Budget one practice session a
  month; that is the real upkeep cost.
- **Ration:** never ration teaching. A skill known by one person is a supply line with one
  truck.
- **Resupply:** teach it. Two people knowing it costs nothing and doubles it.
- **Substitute:** a skill **is** the substitute. Its only replacement is someone else's skill,
  which costs you trade.
- **The exception, once more:** no skill produces insulin, epinephrine, infant formula or most
  daily prescriptions. Skills change how far the stock goes and how well you use the time it
  buys. They do not change how those rows end (→ 23-04).

## By phase

- **A, at home:** the learning phase. Every skill here is cheaper, safer and faster to learn
  now, with the hardware store open and a failed batch costing nothing but groceries.
- **B, moving:** you carry only what you know. Fire, water, finding your way, first aid, and
  keeping everyone fed and calm are the skills that travel.
- **C, establishing:** wood, food production, livestock and building come to the front; the
  tier 3 skills finally have the room and the reason to pay off.
