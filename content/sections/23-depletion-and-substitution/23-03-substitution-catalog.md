# Substitution catalog: what replaces what

**What this page is for.** A one-page index of "when this runs out, use that", each line
pointing to the page that explains it properly. **Read the linked page before acting on any
line.** Most substitutes carry safety limits that do not fit in a table cell. The catalog opens
with the most important rows: the ones where the answer is **nothing**.

> **Caution.** Several substitutes involve fuel, flame or chlorine. The floors apply to every
> line (→ 30-01): **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or
> near an open window.** **Fuel is never stored or transferred near an open flame or a
> running appliance.** **Bleach or pool shock is never mixed with ammonia, acids or other
> cleaners.**

## The rows where there is no substitute

Knowing these matters more than any clever improvisation.

| Need | Substitute | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Insulin** | **None** | Supply and runway (→ 02-01, → 12-07). Syringes can replace a pump or pen as a way of *giving* insulin (→ 02-02), but nothing replaces the drug. The leave-or-stay decision is the plan (→ 01-02) |
| **Epinephrine for anaphylaxis** | **None.** Antihistamines are not a substitute | Supply and expiry (→ 02-03); avoiding the trigger is the strategy (→ 02-04) |
| **Other daily prescription medicines** (seizure, heart, thyroid, transplant, steroid replacement and others) | **None you can make or improvise** | Build the supply while pharmacies work (→ 12-07); the condition pages (→ 12-11) |
| **Infant formula** | **None. Never dilute it, never make it at home** | Clinician, community, public health (→ 20-05, → 02-13) |
| **A skilled attendant at a difficult birth** | None | Be near care (→ 02-12, → 01-05) |
| **Surgery, imaging, blood transfusion** | None | Know the limit (→ 12-16) |
| **A working smoke or carbon monoxide alarm** | None | Keep spares and batteries; test before any fuel-burning heater is lit (→ 08-02, → 29-12) |
| **Safe water for an infant** | None | Treat it properly (→ 03-04, → 20-05) |

## How to read the catalog

- **Every line points to a page.** Go there before acting; the limits are on the page.
- **Most substitutes are worse than the original**: slower, harder, less output. That is
  expected. The point is that the job still gets done.
- **Some substitutes are better.** Outdoor winter cold beats a refrigerator and costs nothing;
  a wire antenna can beat a shop one; a mechanical repair outlasts a glued one.
- **The top block matters most.** Knowing what has no substitute keeps the rest of the plan
  honest.

### The pattern across the catalog

- **Water, heat, food, light and sanitation have deep chains.** These are class A problems
  (→ 23-01): solvable with work, materials and knowledge.
- **Tools, repair and clothing substitute easily**, because a town is full of materials
  (→ 22-02).
- **Communications and transport step down gracefully** to low-tech options that work.
- **Medical substitutes exist for supplies but not for skill or equipment**: cloth for gauze,
  yes; surgery, no (→ 12-16).
- **The top block substitutes for nothing.**

## When it runs out

- **When a substitute runs out, go one step further down its chain.** Most have three or four
  steps.
- **When a chain ends, the answer is either a skill (→ 23-05) or a decision (→ 01-02).**
- **Push upward over time**: from "substituted" to "produced" (→ 23-04). Soap made, wood grown,
  food raised.
- **The honest close.** For the items at the top of this page there is no chain. Everything
  else in the catalog buys time and ability, so the household can act on that one problem early,
  deliberately, and with resources still in hand.
