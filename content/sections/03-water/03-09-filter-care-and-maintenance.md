# Filter care: straws, bottles, squeeze and gravity filters

**What this page is for.** How to choose, use, clean, store and retire the portable water
filters most households buy. Specifications by filter class are on → 28-06; what "micron" and
"virus" mean is on → 29-06.

> **SAFETY. A filter fails silently.** A damaged filter looks and works exactly like a good
> one: water still comes out. **You will not know.**
>
> **Freezing is the failure that matters.** Water left inside a used hollow-fiber filter (a
> bundle of tiny porous tubes) expands when it freezes and splits the fibers. The makers of
> these filters warn against it, and **the damage cannot be seen**: the filter still flows,
> but it no longer filters.{{#if is.cold_winters}} Where you live, a used filter left in a
> vehicle or an unheated garage overnight is the most likely way to lose your water
> treatment (→ 03-06, → 22-05).{{/if}}
>
> **Most filters do not remove viruses** (CDC). Filtering is not the end of the chain
> (→ 03-04).

## Do this now

1. **Find every filter you own and write on it, in marker, whether it has ever been used and
   whether it has ever been below freezing.** A filter with an unknown history cannot be
   trusted.
2. **Move used filters out of garages and vehicles** for the winter. They live in the heated
   part of the house (→ 08-01).
3. **Buy the backflush syringe or cleaning tool for your filter now,** and spare elements
   (→ 26-04). A filter without its cleaning tool has a much shorter life.
4. **Practice the full chain once:** pre-filter → filter → disinfect with chemical or boiling
   → store covered (→ 03-04). Doing it for the first time in an emergency is how steps get
   skipped.

## What each type is for

| Type | How it works | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Straw** | You suck water through it at the source | Emergency use by one person; go-bag weight | **You cannot store or carry treated water.** Useless for cooking, or for a child who cannot suck hard |
| **Bottle filter** | Filter built into a bottle | One person on the move | Small; the filter limits the flow |
| **Squeeze** | Squeeze a pouch through a filter in the line | Light, fast, fills containers | Pouches split; carry spares |
| **Gravity** | Hang a bag and let it drip through | **Households.** Volume with no effort | Bulky; slow when cold or clogged |
| **Pump** | Hand pump through a cartridge | Shallow or awkward sources | Moving parts and seals that break |
| **Ultrafilter (0.01 micron)** | Hollow fiber with pores small enough for viruses | Removes viruses too, says the CDC | Costs more, slower, needs more pressure |

**For a household of {{ household.size }}, a gravity filter is the right main filter**, with a
squeeze filter to carry and a straw only as a go-bag backup. **Do not build the household
plan on straws.**

## What filters do and do not remove

The CDC is direct: **pore size is the main thing that decides how well a filter works**, and
**many filters do not reliably remove viruses.** Its advice for anyone using a microfilter is to
treat the water with chlorine as well, to deal with viruses (CDC, Water Disinfection for
Travelers).

So the chain is:

1. **Pre-filter:** cloth, coffee filter, or let it settle (→ 03-04).
2. **Filter:** removes parasites and bacteria, and clears the water.
3. **Disinfect:** chlorine or boiling. **This is the virus step.**
4. **Store:** in a covered, clean container (→ 03-02).

The CDC also notes that filtering **does not prevent the water getting dirty again in
storage.** A perfectly filtered gallon poured into a dirty jug is dirty water.

**Ultrafilters at 0.01 micron are the exception:** the CDC says they should remove viruses,
bacteria and parasites. If you own one, know it. If you do not, **assume you need step 3.**

## Freezing: read this twice

Once a hollow-fiber filter has been used, water stays inside the fibers. **When that water
freezes it expands and splits them.** After that the filter passes water freely, and passes
everything in the water with it.

- **There is no reliable field test.** You cannot see, taste or measure it at home.
- **Makers say to protect these filters from freezing.** Your filter's own instructions
  govern.
- **A brand-new filter that has never been wet is not at risk.** The danger starts the first
  time it gets wet.
- **The rule: if a used filter has been below freezing, or you cannot be sure, retire it.**
  Use it only to pre-filter dirty water, or throw it away. **Do not gamble a child's health on
  it** (→ 12-05).

**What that means in practice:**

- Used filters do not live in a garage, a shed or a vehicle in winter.
- The vehicle kit gets **chemical treatment and a metal cup for boiling**, not the good filter
  (→ 22-05, → 03-04).
- After winter use, **shake it as dry as you can and bring it inside** (→ 03-06). In very cold
  weather, carry it inside your coat.

## Clogging and backflushing

The CDC notes that filters **eventually clog** with particles and then need replacing,
maintenance or repair. Slower flow is normal ageing, not necessarily failure.

**Pre-filtering is the biggest single thing that extends filter life.** Cloth, a coffee filter,
or letting sediment settle and pouring off the clear water costs nothing and makes the element
last many times longer.

**Backflushing** means pushing clean water backwards through the element to knock trapped dirt
loose. It restores flow on filters designed for it:

- [ ] Backflush with **clean, already-treated water**, never dirty source water.
- [ ] Use the syringe or tool made for **your** filter, by its instructions.
- [ ] Do it when flow slows noticeably, and before storage, not after.
- [ ] Some filters are shaken, tapped or scrubbed instead (ceramic elements are scrubbed and
  slowly wear thin). Know which you have.
- [ ] Never poke anything into the element to clear it.

## Storing a filter between uses

- **Dry it as far as its instructions allow** before long storage, then keep it **inside,
  above freezing**.
- **Some filters are stored with a preservative solution and some must not be.** Your
  instructions govern; there is no universal rule.
- **Keep new spare elements sealed and dry.** They keep well, and they are the part that runs
  out (→ 26-08).
- **Label each filter with the date of first use** and roughly how much water has gone
  through it.

## When to retire a filter

Retire it (not "keep an eye on it") if any of these is true:

- [ ] It has been below freezing after being used, or you cannot be sure it has not.
- [ ] It was dropped hard, or the housing is cracked or damaged.
- [ ] Flow does not come back after a proper backflush.
- [ ] It has passed its rated volume, or you have lost track of the volume.
- [ ] It was stored wet and smells, or shows mold or growth.
- [ ] Its history is unknown: scavenged, borrowed or found (→ 22-02).

**A retired filter still has a job:** pre-filtering muddy water before boiling. Mark it clearly
so it is never used as a main filter again.

## Why spare elements are the real purchase

The housing lasts. **The element is what wears out**, and a filter with no spare has a hard
deadline. Buy at least two spares with each filter and count them in → 23-02. That count is
your real filtering runway, not the filter itself (→ 23-04).

## Gear and supplies

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Gravity filter and 2 spare elements** | Household volume with no effort | The main filter; elements are the consumable |
| **Squeeze filter and spare pouches** | Portable; fills containers | Pouches split before elements fail |
| **The backflush tool for your model** | Restores flow, extends life | Buy it with the filter |
| Straw filters, two | Go-bag backup only | Not a household plan |
| **Chemical disinfectant** | **The virus step** filters miss | → 03-04 |
| Cloth or coffee filters | The cheapest life-extender there is | Stock many |
| A clean container for backflush water | Never backflush with source water | |
| Permanent marker | First-use date, volume, freeze history | The habit that makes this page work |

**If you don't have it:** cloth and settling clear water. **Boiling replaces filtering
entirely** and needs nothing but fuel (→ 03-04, → 06-04). Sand-and-charcoal home-made filters
clear water but **do not make it safe**: they are a pre-filter, and the disinfecting step still
applies.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** the rated volume per element, and **far less on dirty water** without
  pre-filtering. Write the number in → 23-02.
- **Ration:** pre-filter everything. Use the filter for drinking and cooking water, not
  washing (→ 13-04).
- **Resupply:** spare elements while shops exist. **After that there is none**, which is why the
  boiling path matters.
- **Substitute:** **boiling**, at a fuel cost (→ 06-04). Chemicals deal with germs but not
  cloudiness or chemicals (→ 03-04). **Filters are a stock that runs down; boiling is the
  answer that lasts** (→ 23-04).

## Phase notes

- **A (sheltering at home):** gravity filter at the sink, chemicals as backup, spares stocked,
  everything above freezing.
- **B (moving):** the squeeze filter and chemical treatment travel. **The good filter does not
  sit in a cold vehicle** (→ 22-05).
- **C (rural or at a destination):** protect the elements you have and move the daily load onto
  boiling, because a filter you cannot replace is a filter on a countdown.
