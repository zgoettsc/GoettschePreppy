# Water when everything is frozen

**What this page is for.** Winter brings two separate water problems: **your plumbing freezing
and bursting** (which can flood the house and destroy the water you have), and **every
outdoor source turning solid**. This page covers both.
{{#if is.cold_winters}}Your January nights average about {{ region.home.jan_low_f }} °F, so
this page applies to you every winter.{{/if}}

> **CAUTION.** Never thaw a pipe with an open flame: that is how houses burn down. Never eat
> snow to stay hydrated: that is how people become hypothermic (dangerously cold inside).

## Do this now

1. **Before the cold:** drain and store garden hoses. Close the inside valve to each outdoor
   tap and leave the outdoor tap **open** so trapped water can expand without bursting the
   pipe.
2. **During deep cold:** let taps on outside walls **drip**, and **open the cabinet doors**
   under sinks so house heat reaches the pipes.
3. **If the power fails in winter:** fill every container with water **at once**, before pipes
   freeze or pressure is lost: tub, pots, jugs, bottles.
4. **If a pipe freezes** (a tap gives only a trickle):
   - **keep the tap on the frozen line open**: as the ice melts, running water helps melt the
     rest (American Red Cross);
   - **warm the pipe starting from the tap end** and working back toward the frozen part, with
     a hair dryer, an electric heating pad or towels soaked in hot water. **Never a blowtorch,
     a kerosene or propane heater, a charcoal stove or any open flame** (American Red Cross);
   - **if the pipe has split or starts leaking, close the main water valve at once.** A frozen
     pipe often cracks and only leaks once it thaws, so check the whole run before you trust
     it, and check the other taps: if one pipe froze, others may have too.
5. **Melt snow; never eat it.** Put a little water in the pot first (→ 03-05).

## Why pipes burst

Water expands as it freezes, with enough force to split metal or plastic pipe. The break is
often not found until the ice thaws and the pipe floods. The pipes at risk are in unheated
space: along outside walls, in crawlspaces, in the garage, in any unheated corner of a
basement.

**In a grid-down winter this gets worse.** With no furnace, the house cools toward the outside
temperature, and pipes *inside* start to freeze too. If you are heating only one room
(→ 08-01), the rest of the plumbing is exposed. Often the right move is to **drain the system
on purpose** rather than fight it:

1. **Fill every container first.** Draining means no running water at all. Fill first, drain
   second.
2. Shut off the main water valve.
3. Open the lowest tap and any drain valves; open the taps upstairs to let air in.
4. Flush the toilets to empty the tanks.
5. **Drain the water heater** using the six-step procedure on → 03-02. Do not do it from
   memory: the heat must go off first and the water can be scalding. Until you drain it, that
   tank is your safe **hot washing water** (a gas heater keeps it hot while gas flows), so use
   it for the first days' washing before you empty it.
6. Pour plumbing (RV) antifreeze, the non-toxic kind, into sink and tub drain traps and toilet
   bowls if you have it.

A drained system cannot burst. Toilets without water: → 13-05.

### Drawing from a pool in winter: the cover comes first

{{#if has.pool}}This applies to your pool.{{else}}This applies if you have, or reach, a pool
with a safety cover.{{/if}} **A safety cover is a drowning barrier for small children, and it
matters as much in January as in July.** Never cut the cover, never fold it back in a way that
defeats it, never leave it open or half open. Cold adds another reason: a cover peeled back
in freezing weather can stiffen and ice into its tracks, and then it cannot be closed again.

1. **First choice: draw without touching the cover.** Use a skimmer opening at deck level, if
   it sits outside the cover and is open to the pool, or the cover's own pump or drain
   access. A bottle on a pole through the skimmer fills buckets slowly but touches nothing.
   **Check this before the first freeze:** many skimmers sit under the cover's lid box, are
   plugged for winter, or sit above a lowered water level. Skimmer reachable with the cover
   closed: ☐ yes ☐ no. Checked on: ________. If no, step 2 is your first choice and snow is the
   better answer.
2. **If you must open the cover: the smallest corner only, an adult there the whole time.**
   Open only enough at the shallow end for a bucket, draw fast, and **close and secure it at once**,
   before anything else. In freezing weather, check that ropes and tracks are free of ice and
   that you *can* close it **before** you open it. If you cannot be sure of closing it, do not
   open it: melt the snow lying on top of it instead.
3. **If there is ice under the cover:** break through **at the access point only**: one hole
   the size of the bucket, chipped with a bar (never an axe swung over water). Draw, then
   cover the hole again and **mark the spot** with a flag or stake at the edge so the next
   person knows where the ice is thin. Never walk on the cover or the ice.
4. **Watch the water level.** Read your cover's manual now for the water level it needs:
   some covers are designed to sit on, or be supported by, the normal water level (→ 03-08). Winter draws count. Draw sparingly; snow and ice around the yard are the bulk
   source.

### Protect what you have

{{#if has.garage}}Water stored in an unheated garage will freeze and can split its containers.{{else}}Water stored in any unheated space will freeze and can split its containers.{{/if}}
Keep the working supply inside the heated part of the house. Leave air space at the top of
any container that might freeze. Do not store water in glass where it can freeze.

**Winter is also a refrigerator.** The cold that threatens your pipes also keeps food
(→ 04-09). But some medicines must never freeze.

{{#if has.refrigerated_meds}}> **Floor:** A frozen-then-thawed insulin vial or pen is discarded. It loses strength with no
> way to tell. Keep insulin and other refrigerated medicines in the heated room, never on a
> windowsill or in an unheated space (→ 29-07).
{{else}}> **Floor:** A frozen-then-thawed insulin vial or pen is discarded. It loses strength with no
> way to tell. If anyone in your group uses refrigerated medicine, keep it from freezing
> (→ 29-07).
{{/if}}

## Gear and supplies

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pipe insulation sleeves, heat tape | Prevention beats repair | UL-listed heat tape only (American Red Cross) |
| Hair dryer, heating pads, towels | Thawing pipes safely | Never an open flame |
| The main shutoff, its tool, and knowing where the drain valves are | Draining before a burst | Practice in the dark now |
| Plumbing or RV antifreeze (non-toxic) | Protects drain traps and toilets once drained | |
| Snow shovel, sled, large pot | Collecting and hauling snow | The sled saves the most work |
| Insulated containers or a cooler | Keeps stored water from freezing | Also keeps it cool in summer |
| Headlamps | All of this happens in the dark at 5 p.m. | → 10-01 |

**If you don't have it:**

- Towels soaked in hot water thaw a pipe with no equipment.
- Foam pool noodles, rags or blankets make rough pipe insulation.
- Any tarp collects clean snow; any dark container melts it faster in the sun.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** in winter the limit is **fuel to melt**, not water. Track wood or propane
  against the melting load (→ 23-02).
- **Ration:** melt in big batches while a fire is already going for cooking or heat. Never
  light a fire just to melt snow (→ 06-04).
- **Resupply:** ice before snow; on bright days, set dark containers of snow in the sun for
  free melting.
- **The real winter failure** is a burst pipe flooding the house while everyone keeps warm in
  one room. Drain the system early rather than gambling.

## Phase notes

- **A (sheltering at home):** fill everything, then drain the plumbing if the house is going
  cold. Snow from a fenced yard is clean and private.
- **B (moving):** winter travel with young children is among the riskiest moves in this
  binder. Carry water inside your coat against your body or it freezes; melt snow at stops
  (→ 11-02).
- **C (rural or at a destination):** protect barrels, cisterns and tanks against freezing.
  A well pump needs power; hand pumps, bailers, generators and keeping the wellhead from
  freezing are on → 03-10.
