# Purifying water: boil, chemical, filter

**What this page is for.** How to make fresh water (rain, snow, rivers, ponds, wells, doubtful
tap water) safe to drink, with or without fuel. Salt water is different: it needs → 03-03.
New to the words? "Disinfect", "micron", "virus" and "turbidity" are explained in → 29-06;
bleach and pool shock in → 29-05.

> **SAFETY.**
> - Bad water causes diarrhoea. In an infant, a young child, an elder or a person with
>   diabetes that is not an inconvenience: it is dehydration and a medical emergency with no
>   clinic to go to (→ 12-05).
> - **You cannot boil or disinfect away fuel, toxic chemicals or radioactive material**
>   (CDC). If water may hold those, do not use it; distil it (→ 03-03) or find another source.
> - **Floor:** bleach or pool shock is never mixed with ammonia, acids or other cleaners. The
>   mix gives off chlorine gas. (→ 30-01)
> - **Floor:** children do not have access to chlorine products. (→ 30-01)

## Do this now

1. **Cloudy water: clear it first.** Pour it through clean cloth, paper towel or a coffee
   filter, or let it settle and pour off the clear water. Then disinfect.
2. **Boiling is best.** Bring the water to a **rolling boil for 1 minute**. Above about 5,000
   feet, boil for **3 minutes** (EPA says above 5,000 ft; CDC says above 6,500 ft; use the
   lower, safer figure). {{#if is.high_altitude}}**Your home is at about {{ region.home.elevation_ft }} ft: boil for 3 minutes.**{{else}}Your home is at about {{ region.home.elevation_ft }} ft.{{/if}}
   Let it cool, then store it covered.
3. **No fuel? Use plain unscented household bleach (5 to 9 percent): 8 drops per gallon.**
   Stir, wait **30 minutes**, then drink. **Double the bleach** if the water is cloudy,
   coloured or very cold.
4. **Filters do not remove viruses.** After filtering, still disinfect with chemicals or
   boiling.
5. **Salt water needs distilling, not this page** (→ 03-03).

## Three different jobs

People mix these up all the time.

- **Clarifying** removes dirt and particles (settling, cloth, filters).
- **Disinfecting** kills germs (boiling, chlorine, UV, sunlight).
- **Distilling** removes salts and chemicals (→ 03-03).

Boiling and bleach disinfect. They do **not** remove salt or chemicals.

## 1. Boiling: the most reliable

The CDC says boiling is the best way to kill germs. **One minute at a rolling boil**: a true
tumbling boil, not the first small bubbles. Let it cool on its own; do not add cold untreated
water to speed it up. A flat taste is normal: pour it between two clean containers to put air
back in, or add a pinch of salt per quart.

The catch is **fuel**. Boiling a household's water every day burns a lot of wood or gas
(→ 06-04). That is why chemical treatment is the everyday method when the water is already
clear.

## 2. Chlorine bleach: cheap, small, no fuel

Use **unscented liquid household bleach**. Check the label for "sodium hypochlorite" and its
percentage. Do not use scented, colour-safe, splash-less or "with cleaners" bleach. **If the
label gives directions for disinfecting drinking water, follow the label.**

Published table (US EPA, Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water; CDC, How to Make Water Safe
in an Emergency), printed as published:

**5 to 9 percent sodium hypochlorite (typical US household bleach):**

| Water | Drops | Millilitres | Spoon |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 quart / litre | 2 drops | 0.1 mL | too small to measure |
| 1 gallon | 8 drops | 0.5 mL | slightly less than 1/8 tsp |
| 5 gallons | 40 drops | 2.5 mL | 1/2 tsp |

**1 percent sodium hypochlorite:**

| Water | Drops | Millilitres | Spoon |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 quart / litre | 10 drops | 0.5 mL | 1/8 tsp |
| 1 gallon | 40 drops | 2.5 mL | 1/2 tsp |
| 5 gallons | 200 drops | 12.5 mL | 2.5 tsp |

Stir well and let stand **at least 30 minutes**. **Double the dose** for water that is
cloudy, murky, coloured or very cold. A faint chlorine smell afterwards is the point. No smell
at all can mean the dirt in the water used up the chlorine: treat again and wait again.

**Very dirty water that will not clear** after settling and filtering is a decision. The
published method is to clear the water first and then use the label or table dose (doubled for
cloudy water). Some households go further, adding more chlorine and waiting longer, which
raises the chlorine they drink and still may not reach germs hidden in dirt. Your household's
choice: {{choice DC-23}} (→ 30-24).

**Limits that matter:**

- Chemicals **do not work as well as boiling against parasites** (tiny organisms with tough
  shells).
- Iodine will **not** kill *Cryptosporidium*, a common parasite. **Chlorine dioxide tablets
  will**, if you follow the label exactly.
- **Do not drink iodine-treated water** if pregnant, with thyroid problems, or with iodine
  sensitivity, and nobody should drink it for more than a few weeks.

## 2b. Pool shock (calcium hypochlorite): the long-storage chemical

Bleach loses strength within months. Dry, sealed **granular calcium hypochlorite** ("pool
shock") keeps for years, and a small amount treats a very large volume of water. It is used
in **two steps**. This is the US EPA's published emergency method:

**Step 1. Make a stock solution** (somewhere with fresh air; wear eye protection):
**One heaping teaspoon (about 1/4 ounce) of granular calcium hypochlorite into 2 gallons of
water.** Stir until it dissolves. This makes roughly a 500 mg/L chlorine solution.

**The stock is itself a poison**, far stronger than drinking water. Keep it in a container
nobody could mistake for drinking water, **labelled "CHLORINE STOCK: DO NOT DRINK"** in
marker, lid on, up high and out of children's reach (→ 20-02). If anyone swallows it, call
Poison Control and see → 12-17.

**Step 2. Treat the water:** add **1 part stock solution to each 100 parts of water**: about
**1 pint (16 oz) of stock per 12.5 gallons** of water. That is about 1.3 fl oz (2½
tablespoons) of stock per gallon, or 6.4 oz per 5 gallons. Stir, wait **30 minutes**, and
expect a slight chlorine smell. No smell means add more and wait again. If the taste is
strong, pour it between clean containers and let it stand a few hours.

**Rules that keep this safe:**

- **Read the label:** it must be 65 to 73 percent calcium hypochlorite with **no algaecides,
  clarifiers or stabilisers**. "Multi-action" shock products cannot be made drinkable.
- Add granules **to water**, never water to granules. Use plastic or glass, never metal.
- **Do not store mixed stock solution**: it weakens within days to weeks. Mix it fresh.
- Never use pool shock as dry granules in drinking water, and never dose it like bleach
  drops.

**Storing pool shock.** Calcium hypochlorite is a strong oxidiser: it **feeds a fire**,
**gives off chlorine when it gets wet or hot**, and its dust burns skin, eyes and lungs (CDC
and ATSDR, Medical Management Guidelines for Calcium Hypochlorite). Store it:

- in its sealed bag inside a lidded plastic bucket, labelled "POOL SHOCK: OXIDISER";
- **off the floor** (a flood or wet floor reaching it releases chlorine), dry, away from
  furnaces, water heaters and other heat;
- **never near fuel, acids, ammonia or ammunition** (→ 06-06), and **never on the same shelf
  or in the same bucket as trichlor or dichlor pool tablets**: calcium hypochlorite and
  trichlor can make chlorine gas or fire on contact;
- away from electronics and power stations (the fumes corrode them);
- where children cannot reach it.

If your only good dry spot is also your shelter room, a sealed, lidded bucket on a shelf
removes the two triggers that matter most: water and heat.

## 3. Filters: clear water and remove parasites, not viruses

Portable filters do **not** remove all germs. The CDC says a filter needs an **absolute pore
size of 0.3 micron or smaller** to remove bacteria, and **1 micron or smaller** for parasites
such as *Giardia* and *Cryptosporidium* (a micron is a thousandth of a millimetre, → 29-06).
**No common portable filter removes viruses.** So filter first, then disinfect. Filter care:
→ 03-09; filter specifications: → 28-06.

## 4. UV and sunlight: the backups

- A portable **UV pen or bottle** works on small amounts of **clear** water.
- **Solar disinfection (SODIS):** fill **clear PET plastic bottles of 2 litres or less** (not
  glass, not coloured or scratched plastic, not bigger: the sunlight has to reach the middle)
  with clear water. Lay them on their side in the sun for **6 hours** if sunny or **2 days**
  if cloudy. A dark surface underneath helps.
- Neither works on cloudy water.

**Never** drink water from car radiators or home heating boilers.

**If you don't have it:**

- **Pool shock** is concentrated chlorine; additive-free pool shock is usable for drinking
  water by the two-step method above.
- Any tightly woven cloth (a T-shirt, a bandana) pre-filters; fold it in layers.
- Clear PET bottles of 2 litres or less plus sunshine is disinfection with no equipment.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** count bottles of bleach and strips of tablets. A year-old jug of bleach is
  weaker than the tables assume. Pool shock stored dry lasts years.
- **Ration:** use chemicals for clear water and save fuel. Boil when the water is doubtful,
  someone is sick, or you cannot measure a dose with confidence.
- **Resupply:** boiling always works if you have fuel. Sunlight works with nothing but
  bottles. A good filter reduces how much chemical you need.
- **The real failure:** running out of *clean container space*, not treatment. Keep clean,
  labelled containers ready so treated water does not get dirty again.

## Phase notes

- **A (sheltering at home):** clear tap or stored water usually needs nothing. Treat rain and
  snow (→ 03-05){{#if has.pool}}, and distil pool water (→ 03-03){{/if}}. Keep both bleach and
  tablets.
- **B (moving):** tablets and a small filter: light, no fuel, fast. Treat everything you pick
  up on the way, no exceptions.
- **C (rural or at a destination):** a well or spring is usually fresh and needs only
  disinfecting. Test it when you can (→ 03-07); until then, treat it as unsafe.
