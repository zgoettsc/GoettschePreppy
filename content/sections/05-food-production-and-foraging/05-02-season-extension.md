# Season extension: row cover, low tunnels and cold frames

**What this page is for.** Cheap covers that let you plant earlier in spring and keep
harvesting later in fall, beyond the frost-free window on → 05-01. At home that window runs
from about **{{ region.home.last_frost }}** to **{{ region.home.first_frost }}**
({{ region.home.growing_days }} days).

> **CAUTION.** Season extension is one of the highest-value garden techniques there is, but
> covered structures **overheat very fast** on a sunny day, even in early spring when the air
> outside is near freezing, and can cook everything inside. Plastic covers must be opened every
> day to let the heat out (UGA Extension, Low Hoop Houses). **Vent it, and put a thermometer in
> it.**

## Do this now

1. **Build or buy one cold frame this season.** It is the cheapest way to add real weeks.
2. **Put a thermometer inside** every covered structure, and open it on sunny days.
3. **Plant cool-season crops early**: before {{ region.home.last_frost }}, once the soil is 40 to
   50 °F (University of Minnesota Extension, Planting the Vegetable Garden).
4. **Plan a fall planting**, not just a spring one. Fall is usually the easier win.
5. **Use row cover**: cheap, light, reusable, and it buys several degrees.

## Why / how it works

### The words

- **Row cover** (floating row cover): a thin, white, see-through fabric laid loosely over
  plants. Light and rain pass through; frost and insects mostly do not.
- **Low tunnel**: wire or plastic hoops over a bed, covered with plastic or row cover.
- **Cold frame**: a bottomless box with a clear lid (an old window works), set on the soil.
- **Hoop house**: a walk-in tunnel greenhouse. A real building project.

### What each method buys

| Method | Frost protection | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Row cover** | A few degrees; heavier fabric gives more (read the label) | Very low | Lay directly on crops; also keeps out pests (→ 05-12) |
| **Low tunnel** | Typically 2 to 8 °F warmer inside than out (UGA Extension) | Low | The best value per dollar |
| **Cold frame** | More than a tunnel, with a solid frame and a clear lid | Low to moderate | Durable; reusable for decades |
| **Cold frame inside a low tunnel** | Substantial | Low | Layering multiplies protection |
| **Hoop house / greenhouse** | Large | High | A project, not a weekend |
| **Deep mulch** | Protects roots | Free | Lets carrots and parsnips sit in the ground into winter |

**Fabric must reach the ground on all sides** to hold in the heat that rises from the soil
overnight (UGA Extension). **Layering is the trick.** Row cover inside a low tunnel is much
warmer than either alone.
That is how gardeners in cold places pick greens in midwinter.

### The seasons you gain

**Spring:** sow cool-season crops (spinach, lettuce, radish, kale, peas) weeks before the
ground would otherwise be ready. Warm the soil first with clear plastic for a week or two.

**Fall: the easier and more reliable win.** Sow in late summer so crops mature as the days
shorten, then cover them. Hardy greens (spinach, kale, corn salad) and roots (carrots,
parsnips) hold under cover well past the first frosts, and **carrots get sweeter after
frost**.

**Parsnip foliage burns skin.** Garden parsnip is the same species as wild parsnip, with the
same sap: wear gloves and long sleeves when weeding or harvesting parsnip tops in sun, or you
get the blistering burns described on → 05-06.

**Winter:** in the northern half of the country, growth nearly stops in midwinter whatever
the temperature, because the days are too short. Covered beds become **storage in place**: you
are picking what already grew. That is still fresh food from the yard in January. In the
mildest climates ({{ region.home.zone }} is your zone; see the bands on → 05-01), covered
greens keep growing slowly all winter.

### Building a cold frame

- **Frame:** scrap lumber, straw bales, concrete blocks, or an old window frame.
- **Lid:** an old storm window, a salvaged glass door, rigid clear plastic, or plastic sheeting.
  Never use treated wood that touches food-crop soil (→ 05-10).
- **Slope the lid toward the south** so it catches low winter sun and sheds snow and rain.
- **Insulate the north wall** and bank the sides with leaves, straw or snow.
- **Add thermal mass**: jugs of water inside soak up daytime heat and give it back at night
  (the same idea as → 08-04).
- **Hinge or prop the lid** so venting is easy, because you will vent often.

**Venting is where people fail.** More cold-frame crops are killed by heat than by cold. On
any sunny day near freezing, prop the lid. Make it a habit, or use an automatic vent opener (a
wax-filled piston that opens the lid as it warms, with no power).

### Snow is insulation

Snow on and around a covered bed protects it: the soil beneath stays warmer than bare frozen
ground. Leave light snow on a low tunnel; **clear heavy, wet snow** before it collapses the
hoops.

### No fuel needed

Nothing here burns fuel (→ 06-04), uses power, or needs supplies beyond the covers themselves.
That makes season extension one of the few food methods in this binder that costs nothing to
run. Build it now, while materials are easy to get.

## Gear / items

**Have it now (buy toward this):**

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Floating row cover** (rolls) | Cheapest degrees per dollar | Reusable for years |
| Hoops (wire, plastic pipe, metal conduit) | Low tunnels | Cheap to make |
| Greenhouse plastic or clear sheeting | Tunnels and frames | UV-stabilised lasts longer |
| **Old windows or glass doors** | Cold-frame lids | Often free from remodels |
| **Min/max thermometer** for each structure | Tells you when to vent | Essential |
| Automatic vent opener (wax piston) | Vents with no power and no attention | Worth buying |
| Straw bales | Walls, insulation, mulch | Cheap where farms are near |
| Clips, clamps, sandbags | Holding covers down in wind | Wind is the usual enemy |
| Deep mulch (leaves, straw) | Overwintering roots in the ground | Free |

**Scavenge or substitute:**

- **Old windows, storm doors, shower doors**: free cold-frame lids.
- **Milk jugs or clear tubs** with the bottom cut out as covers over single plants.
- **Straw bales in a rectangle with a window on top** make a complete cold frame.
- Bedsheets and blankets as emergency frost covers for one night. Take them off in the morning.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** frames last for years. **Plastic sheeting breaks down in sunlight** within one to
  three seasons and is the main consumable. Keep spare sheeting and clips.
- **Ration:** cover the highest-value crops (winter greens for vitamins) rather than trying to
  cover everything.
- **Resupply:** glass and rigid panels outlast plastic; salvage them now. Straw and leaves are
  free insulation.
- **Why it matters:** every extra week of fresh greens at either end of the year is a week less
  on stored food and sprouts alone (→ 04-16).

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** build a cold frame from salvaged windows this season.
- **B (moving):** row cover is light and packs small. Take some.
- **C (establishing):** scale up to low tunnels or a hoop house. Wind protection matters more
  in open country.
