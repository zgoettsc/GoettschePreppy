# Container and vertical growing in a small space

**What this page is for.** How to grow real food in pots, bags and buckets and up fences and
trellises, on a patio, a balcony, a driveway or a small yard.

## Do this now

1. **Count your real sun.** Check the space at about 9 a.m., noon and 4 p.m. and mark where you
   get **6 or more hours of direct sun**. That map, not the size of the space, sets how much food
   you can grow. A fenced town lot usually has less full sun than it looks.
2. **Buy containers big.** The most common failure is a pot too small: it dries out by noon and
   the plant stalls. Use the size table below and err large.
3. **Stock the growing mix now.** Potting mix, compost and fertilizer are the inputs you cannot
   scavenge quickly. → 05-11 for making your own.
4. **Put containers where you can water them from stored water** without carrying buckets across
   the yard. In an outage, water is measured by the gallon (→ 03-02).

## Why / how it works

**Containers trade soil volume for control.** You can grow food on a driveway, a deck, a
balcony, a south-facing wall, or the hot strip along a fence. You control the soil completely,
which matters in town where ground soil may hold lead, old fill or weedkiller. But you accept
three permanent costs:

1. **Water.** A container has no deep soil to draw from. In summer a 5-gallon pot in full sun
   can need water **every day**, sometimes twice. Push a finger an inch down; if it is dry,
   water until it runs out of the bottom. **Every container needs drainage holes**; without them
   the roots drown.
2. **Nutrients.** Every watering washes nutrients out of the bottom. Container plants must be
   **fed through the season**: a slow-release fertilizer mixed in at planting plus a weak
   liquid feed while watering is the Extension baseline (University of Minnesota Extension).
   Without shops, that becomes compost and compost tea (→ 05-11).
3. **Cold.** A container freezes from all sides; garden soil does not. Where winters freeze hard
   ({{#if is.cold_winters}}yours do{{else}}check your zone, {{ region.home.zone }}{{/if}}),
   containers are a **one-season** setup unless you sink them in the ground, mulch them deeply,
   or move them under cover. Perennials in pots often die over a hard winter.

**Growing up buys area, not sunlight.** Training cucumbers, pole beans, peas and small squash up
a fence or a livestock-panel arch gives far more yield per square foot of ground: more leaf in
the light, fruit off the dirt, and less disease because leaves dry faster. It does **not** make
sun. Put trellises on the **north** side of a bed so they shade nothing you are growing.

### What pays in a container

Choose crops with high value per square foot and a long harvest, not bulk calories.

| Tier | Crops | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Best** | Bush or cherry tomatoes, peppers, bush beans, chard, kale, leaf lettuce, spinach, herbs | Long harvest, compact, tolerate small roots |
| **Good with a trellis** | Pole beans, peas, cucumbers, small winter squash | Vertical space is free space |
| **Works but greedy** | Potatoes in grow bags, bush zucchini, paste tomatoes | Need the biggest containers you have |
| **Don't bother** | Sweet corn, field pumpkins, melons, sprawling vines | Terrible calories per square foot in a pot |

### Container size: the rule that matters most

| Crop | Minimum volume | Practical |
|---|---|---|
| Lettuce, spinach, radish, herbs | 1 to 2 gal | Wide and shallow beats deep |
| Bush beans, chard, kale | 3 to 5 gal | |
| Peppers, bush tomatoes | 5 gal absolute floor | **7 to 10 gal** is the difference between a plant and a crop |
| Potatoes | 10 to 15 gal grow bag | Yield grows with volume |

## Gear / items

**Have it now (buy toward this):**

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fabric grow bags (5 to 15 gal) | Cheap, fold flat, store for years | Best storage-space-to-growing-space ratio |
| Bagged potting mix and compost, several seasons' worth | The input you cannot improvise fast | Stores dry; rotate |
| Slow-release and water-soluble fertilizer | Containers leach; unfed pots stall | Fertilizer is an early shortage item |
| Livestock panel(s) and metal fence posts | An arch trellis that lasts decades | A 16-ft panel bent into an arch is the classic |
| Jute or sisal twine | Trellising and tying; it composts | |
| Watering can with a fine head | Precise watering from stored water | |
| Drip line or soaker hose | Cuts water use a lot | Keep a manual backup |
| Shade cloth (30 to 40 %) | Saves greens in a heat wave | Doubles as a frost layer (→ 05-02) |

**Scavenge or substitute:**

- **5-gallon buckets:** food-grade if you can get them. Drill 6 to 8 half-inch drainage holes
  near the bottom edge.
- **Totes, coolers, tubs, feed sacks, woven bags, laundry baskets lined with landscape fabric.**
  Anything that holds soil and drains.
- **Trellis:** the existing fence, branches lashed into an A-frame, old bed springs, chain-link
  panels, concrete mesh, string grids.
- **Growing mix:** finished compost cut with garden soil and something for structure: shredded
  leaf mould, sand, or crumbled charcoal. **Wood ash is not a substitute** (it raises soil pH
  hard).
- **Do not reuse:** containers that held fuel, solvent, pesticide or unknown chemicals; treated
  lumber as bed sides for food; tyres.

## When it runs out

- **Potting mix and compost** are the real limit in year two. Start a compost system **now**
  (→ 05-11). Reuse container soil: empty it, mix in fresh compost, replant. Growing the same crop
  in the same tired mix year after year builds up disease.
- **Fertilizer:** substitute compost, compost tea, well-rotted manure (→ 05-11), **urine heavily
  diluted with water, applied to soil, never to leaves, and not in the last month before you
  harvest anything eaten raw**, wood ash **sparingly** for potassium, and crushed eggshell or bone
  for calcium. All are slower and weaker than bagged fertilizer: plan for lower yields.
- **Water:** a container garden is a standing daily demand. Budget it against Section 03 before
  you plant, and mulch every container. Rinse water without soap stretches the supply; never put
  greywater on leaves you will eat raw (→ 13-03).
- **Seed:** → 05-03. Save from open-pollinated varieties only.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** in an apartment or small lot this **is** the garden: containers plus fence
  trellises on the sunny side, placed so a frost warning means carrying pots under cover rather
  than losing the crop.
- **B (moving):** rigid containers full of soil do not travel. **Fabric grow bags fold flat**
  and go with the seed (→ 05-03); dump the soil and keep the bags. Harvest everything edible
  before you leave.
- **C (establishing):** containers become the starter garden while you break ground, and a
  permanent home for anything that must come indoors.
