# Growing indoors: microgreens and seed starting in winter

**What this page is for.** Fresh greens in the dead of winter from a windowsill, and starting
garden transplants indoors so the outdoor season produces more.

> **CAUTION: food safety.** **Sprouts and microgreens are not the same, and the risk is not the
> same.** **Sprouts** are germinated seeds eaten whole, grown warm and wet: the same conditions
> that grow *Salmonella* and *E. coli*, and sprouts have caused many documented outbreaks.
> **Microgreens** are seedlings grown in a growing mix and cut above the seed, which is much
> safer. For young children, the elderly, pregnant women and anyone sick or with weak immunity,
> **default to microgreens, or cook sprouts.**{{#if has.young_child}} This household has a child
> under 6.{{/if}}{{#if has.elder}} This household has an elder.{{/if}}{{#if has.pregnant}} This
> household has a pregnant member.{{/if}}

## Do this now

1. **Buy microgreen seed by the pound, not the packet.** Sunflower, pea, radish and broccoli
   seed keep for years cool and dry, and a pound goes a long way.
2. **Grow one tray this week and eat it.** It takes 7 to 14 days from sowing to harvest: cheap
   practice.
3. **Find your best south-facing window.** Winter sun through it is weak but free.
4. **Check seed against your household's food allergies** before you buy (→ 02-04). Soybean and
   sesame are common sprouting seeds and major allergens.

## Why / how it works

**What indoor growing is for.** It is **not** calories. A windowsill will never feed a family.
It delivers what stored food is worst at: **fresh vitamins and minerals, plus flavour and
morale, in midwinter** (→ 04-18).

### 1. Microgreens: the best return; master this one

Seedlings cut at their first true leaves, 7 to 14 days after sowing. Grown in a shallow tray of
potting mix, watered gently, cut above the soil with clean scissors (SDSU Extension, Growing
Microgreens at Home).
- **Light:** a bright south window is enough for most; more light means less stretching. They
  need far less light than a full-grown plant, which is why this works with the grid down.
- **Yield:** one 10 × 20-inch tray gives several servings every 10 days or so, from a few
  tablespoons of seed.
- **Best crops:** sunflower (most bulk), pea shoots (sweet; children eat them), radish (fast,
  spicy), broccoli, kale, mustard, arugula, beet, cabbage.
- **Food safety:** wash hands before sowing and harvesting; clean trays and scissors; **cut above
  the seed and roots**.
- **Mould or roots?** Fine white root hairs are normal and disappear when sprayed with water.
  Real mould is grey or green, smells musty and spreads: that tray goes out. Prevent it with
  thinner sowing, air movement and less standing water.

### 2. Seed starting: the highest leverage

Every transplant you start indoors in late winter is a plant producing outdoors in summer. In
short-season climates (→ 05-01, your frost-free days: {{ region.home.growing_days }}), tomatoes,
peppers, cabbage-family crops and onions often **only** make a crop if started indoors.
- **Light is where people fail.** A window alone usually gives pale, stretched, weak seedlings.
  If you have any power (→ 09-02), a cheap LED shop light a few inches above the plants for
  14 to 16 hours a day is one of the best uses of stored power in late winter (University of
  Minnesota Extension, Starting Seeds Indoors).
- **Warm to sprout, cool to grow.** Most seeds want warm soil to sprout, then cooler, brighter
  conditions to grow stocky.
- **Damping off** (seedlings collapsing at the soil line) is a fungus of overwatered, still,
  dirty conditions. Prevent it with clean containers, fresh sterile mix, watering from below and
  **moving air**.
- **Harden off** over 7 to 10 days before transplanting: a little more outdoor time each day.
  Skip it and the first wind or sun kills them.

### 3. Full-grown food plants indoors: the weakest option

Herbs (basil, chives, parsley, mint), leaf lettuce and dwarf peppers can be kept alive indoors
all winter, with **slow growth, small yields and heavy light needs**. Herbs are worth it; they
transform stored food. A tomato crop in the living room in midwinter is not.

### Sprouts: read this before you do it

Sprouts need no soil and no light, which makes them tempting. If you sprout (→ 04-16): use seed
**sold for sprouting**, rinse at least twice a day, keep them well drained, chill them when done
if you can, and **cook them** for young children, elders, pregnant women and anyone sick or with
weak immunity. Cooking removes most of the risk.

**Never sprout or eat treated garden seed** (dyed pink, blue or green). It is coated with
pesticide and is not food (→ 05-03).

## Gear / items

**Have it now (buy toward this):**

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bulk microgreen seed (sunflower, pea, radish, broccoli) | Fresh greens all winter | Store cool, dry, dark (→ 05-03) |
| 10 × 20-inch trays, some with holes, some without | Grow tray plus a bottom-watering tray | Get more than you think; they crack |
| Clear domes | Sprouting | Or a second tray turned upside down |
| Potting mix or coir, several seasons | You cannot scavenge sterile mix | Same stock as → 05-10 |
| LED shop light and timer | What makes seed starting work | Runs from a modest solar setup (→ 09-02) |
| Seedling heat mat | Sprouting in a cold house | Low watts, high payoff |
| Small fan | Prevents damping off; stronger stems | Any USB fan |
| Spray bottle, fine-headed watering can | Seedlings drown easily | |
| Clean scissors | Harvest | Keep them for this job |
| Wire shelf unit | Three or four growing levels in a corner | |

**Scavenge or substitute:**

- **Trays:** foil roasting pans, clear produce boxes, cut-down milk jugs, baking sheets lined with
  plastic.
- **Domes:** plastic wrap, a clear lid, a cut soda bottle.
- **Light:** the south window; a mirror or white board behind the tray nearly doubles the light;
  any 12-volt LED strip off a battery (→ 10-01).
- **Medium:** sifted compost, coir, or for microgreens a damp paper towel, burlap or felt mat
  (they live mostly on the seed's own reserves).
- **Heat:** the warmest room (→ 08-01), a jug of hot water under the tray.

## When it runs out

- **Seed** is the true consumable. Buy bulk now and rotate. Later, grow some plants out to seed
  to feed the tray (→ 05-03). Sunflowers are the easy one: they set huge amounts of seed.
- **Potting mix:** spent microgreen mix goes to compost (→ 05-11) and comes back as growing mix.
  Do not reuse a tray of mix without refreshing it; mould problems build up.
- **Light and power:** if power shrinks (→ 09-01), drop the full-grown plants first, keep **seed
  starting** (highest leverage) and **microgreens** (least light). Windowsill microgreens are
  the last fresh food you will have.
- **Warmth:** if the house is heated to one room (→ 08-01), the growing shelf lives in that room.
  Warm-season seeds stall in a cold room; plan the calendar around the room you can actually keep
  warm.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** the winter fresh-food system: a wire shelf, a few trays on rotation, the south
  window. It also gives children something growing in winter (→ 21-02).
- **B (moving):** seed travels; trays do not. Carry microgreen seed.
- **C (establishing):** the same system, plus a real seed-starting setup for spring transplants.
