# Hunting for food

**What this page is for.** What hunting can realistically add to a household's food, how to
take game cleanly and keep the meat, and the lead problem that matters for children and
pregnancy. Laws appear only as notes for while government works.

> **CAUTION.** Hunting gives the **largest single return** of any food-gathering method: one
> deer is a lot of protein. But it is **seasonal, and useless without a way to keep the meat.**
> A deer taken with no plan for keeping the meat is mostly waste. And **a lead-core bullet puts
> lead in the meat** (below), which matters most for children under 6 and anyone pregnant. If
> you are new to firearms, read → 29-11 first.

> **Before the collapse:** seasons, licences, tags, legal methods and calibres, hunter-education
> cards, blaze-orange rules and non-toxic-shot rules are set by your state's fish and wildlife
> agency and change every year. Hunt legally now; it is how you learn the ground and the people.
> Keep the agency's printed hunting guide with your documents (→ 19-01). This never decides a
> survival procedure.

## Do this now

1. **Get licensed and hunt now**, in normal times. Skill, access and local knowledge cannot be
   learned during a crisis.
2. **Solve preservation before you shoot** (→ 05-09, → 04-05): jars, lids, salt and fuel ready.
3. **Decide the bullet now:** copper (solid, "monolithic") bullets for deer keep lead out of the
   meat. Steel or bismuth shot for birds the children will eat.
4. **Arrange access now:** public land, and permission from landowners. That takes time.
5. **Practise with the gun you will actually use** (→ 14-09).

## Why / how it works

### What you can hunt

Game differs by region; your state's hunting guide lists what lives near you. Across most of the
US the useful groups are:

| Game | Typical firearm | Yield |
|---|---|---|
| **Deer** (whitetail almost everywhere; mule deer in the West) | Centrefire rifle, or shotgun with slugs. **Copper bullets** (below) | **The most meat of any common game**: the big prize |
| **Elk, moose, pronghorn** (where present) | Centrefire rifle | Very large, regional |
| **Wild turkey** | Shotgun | Substantial |
| **Upland birds** (pheasant, grouse, quail, dove) | Shotgun | Small |
| **Waterfowl** (ducks, geese) | Shotgun with **steel or bismuth shot** | Good, and fatty, which matters (→ 04-12) |
| **Rabbit, hare, squirrel** | **.22 LR** rifle or shotgun | Small, steady, near towns |
| **Feral hogs** (mainly the South) | Centrefire rifle | Large and fatty. Wear gloves: they carry diseases (→ 05-09) |

"Centrefire" and "rimfire" (.22 LR) are explained on → 29-11 and → 14-04.

### The calorie reality

- **One deer is weeks of protein for a family**, if you can keep it (→ 05-09).
- **A day of small-game hunting** might give a rabbit or two: real food, not comparable.
- **Ammunition matters** (→ 14-08). A deer costs very little ammunition. Shooting squirrels with
  centrefire is waste. **Use .22 LR for small game**; that is its job.
- **Traps and snares** (→ 05-15) take small game while you do other work, with **no ammunition
  at all**. In a long emergency they beat hunting for small game.

### The yearly rhythm

- **Fall: hunt.** Animals are fat and the cold helps you keep meat.
- **Late fall and winter: preserve.** Can (→ 04-05), cure and smoke (→ 04-07), dry (→ 04-06),
  or hold in winter cold where it is cold enough (→ 04-09).
- **Winter: live on it**, plus stored staples.
- **Spring and summer: garden** (→ 05-01), fish (→ 05-08), prepare for fall.

**Missing the fall window can cost you the year.** That is why hunting in normal times matters:
you learn the timing while it is not urgent.

### Field to table, in brief

1. **Shoot cleanly.** A wounded animal that runs is meat lost and needless suffering.
2. **Field dress immediately** (→ 05-09). Body heat spoils meat fast.
3. **Cool it fast.** Easy in cold weather; in a warm early season it is the whole problem.
4. **Butcher and preserve promptly**; cold weather buys time, warm weather does not. Pressure canning (→ 04-05) is the most
   reliable answer without a freezer. **Low-acid foods are pressure-canned to the published
   time and pressure, or not canned.** Meat is a low-acid food (→ 30-01).
5. **Cook it through.** FoodSafety.gov gives **160 °F for venison and rabbit, wild or
   farm-raised**, whole cuts and ground alike. Cooking does not remove the chronic wasting disease
   agent in deer (Minnesota DNR; → 05-09): test where CWD is known, and never eat an animal that
   tested positive or looked sick (CDC).

### Lead in the meat

Lead-core bullets break up inside the animal. In a Minnesota DNR study, lead particles turned
up **as far as 18 inches from the wound channel**, too small to see, feel or notice when chewing,
and Minnesota testing found lead fragments in **26 % of packages of ground venison** and 2 % of
whole-muscle cuts. **Pregnant women and children under 6 should never be in a position to eat
lead particles** (Minnesota DNR, Tips for Deer Hunters).

- **Copper (monolithic) bullets** for deer fragment very little. Buy them now.
- **If a deer was shot with lead:** trim liberally around the wound channel (there is no
  distance known to be safe) and throw out all bloodshot meat. Ground meat, trim, stew meat and sausage from that deer carry the most
  risk, because fragments get mixed through them. Whole-muscle cuts far from the wound carry the
  least.
- **Lead shot in birds and rabbits:** pick out the pellets; steel or bismuth shot keeps lead out
  of the pot.

### Taking game when there are no rules

When there is no warden, the limits that remain are real ones:
- **Take what feeds you, take it cleanly, and do not strip the local herd.** Deer near a town
  can be shot out in one hungry season and take years to recover.
- **Land access:** a landowner who knows and trusts you is worth more than gear (→ 18-01).
- **Shooting at home:** whether anyone fires a gun on your own property (for small game, pests
  or practice) is a decision card, because of what lies beyond the target (→ 30-12). The
  household's choice: {{choice DC-11}}. A suppressor (→ 14-10) changes the noise, not the
  backstop.

### The long view

In a long emergency, **game near towns will be hunted hard** and populations will drop fast.
Expect diminishing returns after the first season. Small game and trapping (→ 05-15) last
longer than deer near population centres. Treat a good fall as a windfall to preserve, not an
income.

## Gear / items

**Have it now (buy toward this):**

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Your state's current hunting guide, printed** | Pre-collapse rules; the maps and species notes inside outlast the law | → 19-01 |
| Rifle zeroed for hunting, **copper bullets** | One clean shot; no lead in the meat | → 14-04, → 14-09 |
| Shotgun with **steel or bismuth shot** | Birds and waterfowl | Lead-free for what children eat |
| **.22 LR and a deep stock of ammunition** | Small game, the sustainable kind | → 14-04 |
| Sharp knives and a sharpener | Field dressing | → 16-02 |
| Game bags, rope, gloves | Handling and cooling meat | Cheap |
| Sled or cart | Dragging a deer out | |
| **Preservation ready before the season** | Jars, lids, salt, fuel | → 04-05, → 04-07 |
| Blaze orange | Not being mistaken for game | Pre-collapse: often required |
| Instant-read thermometer | Cooking temperatures | → 07-01 |

**Scavenge or substitute:**

- **Traps and snares** (→ 05-15): no ammunition, work while you sleep.
- **Fishing** (→ 05-08): often a better return per hour than small-game hunting.
- **Local knowledge** from experienced hunters is the most valuable thing to gather now
  (→ 18-03).

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** ammunition (→ 14-08) and seasons. The binding limit is usually **ammunition and
  preservation capacity**, not game.
- **Ration:** one clean shot, close range, no hopeful shooting. .22 LR for small game; save
  centrefire.
- **Resupply:** trapping and fishing scale better and cost nothing to run.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** in town, hunting means leaving town: fuel, time and access all matter. Small
  game and birds within reach are the realistic option.
- **B (moving):** a hunting rifle is a weight decision (→ 01-03); a .22 LR is the light,
  high-use choice.
- **C (establishing):** hunting becomes central; land access and neighbours matter more than
  gear. Learn the destination's game and habitat on ordinary visits.
