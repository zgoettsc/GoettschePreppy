# Fishing and ice fishing

**What this page is for.** Why fishing is one of the best food-gathering methods there is, how
to fish passively, how to keep the catch safe to eat, and how not to die on the ice.

> **SAFETY.** **Ice kills people every winter.** No ice is ever guaranteed safe, and a fall into
> cold water brings on hypothermia fast, even on a day above 40 °F (CDC, Preventing Hypothermia;
> → 08-05). **Under
> 4 inches of new, clear ice, nobody goes out** (Minnesota DNR). **Never eat local freshwater
> fish raw, lightly pickled or cold-smoked** unless it was first frozen solid at −4 °F for
> 7 days: it can carry the broad fish tapeworm, and salting, smoking or pickling does not kill it
> (CDC). **Cook fish to 145 °F**, or until the
> flesh is opaque and flakes apart (FoodSafety.gov).

## Do this now

1. **Get licensed and fish now.** Fishing is one of the best calorie-per-effort methods there
   is, and it uses no ammunition (→ 14-08).
2. **If anyone in the household reacts to fish, set the separation rule before the first fish
   comes home** (below, → 02-04).
3. **Never go on ice alone, never onto moving water in winter, and never without a spud bar and
   the table below.**
4. **Learn passive methods**: set lines and limb lines fish while you do other work.
5. **Find your state's fish-consumption advisories** for the waters you fish, and write the ones
   that matter on this page.

> **Before the collapse:** licences, limits, seasons and which waters allow set lines, trotlines,
> nets or traps are set by your state's fish and wildlife agency; fish-consumption advisories
> come from the state health or environment agency. Keep the printed fishing guide with your
> documents (→ 19-01). This never decides a survival procedure.

## Why / how it works

### Why fishing beats most food gathering

- **No ammunition:** it costs line, hooks and time.
- **Passive methods work while you sleep:** set lines, limb lines, traps and weirs.
- **Renewable** in a way that local deer are not (→ 05-07).
- **Year-round** in many places: open water spring to fall, **ice fishing in winter** where
  lakes freeze, which is exactly when nothing else produces.
- **Fish is a useful fat source** in a lean season (→ 04-12).

**Panfish** (perch, crappie, bluegill, sunfish) and **catfish** are the practical targets:
abundant, catchable in numbers and reliable. Trophy fish are not a food plan.

### Passive methods

- **Set line:** a baited hook on a line tied to a stake or the bank, left in place.
- **Limb line:** the same, tied to an overhanging branch so the branch flexes.
- **Trotline:** a long main line across the water with short baited lines hanging from it,
  spaced so the hooks cannot tangle.
- **Fish trap or weir:** a funnel or fence that lets fish in and makes them hard to leave.

In an emergency, set what feeds you, **check every line daily** (fish spoil and suffer on an
unchecked line), and **pull any line you are not fishing**. These are the rules that matter
when there is no warden.

### Water quality and advisories

- **After a flood, assume contamination** (→ 17-03). Prefer moving, cleaner water, cook
  thoroughly, and avoid fishing just downstream of towns, sewage outfalls or feedlots (→ 03-07).
- **Mercury:** FDA and EPA advise **anyone who is or might become pregnant, anyone
  breastfeeding, and children aged 1 to 11** to choose fish lower in mercury (FDA/EPA, Advice
  about Eating Fish). **For fish caught by family or friends: check the local advisory, and if
  there is none, eat only one serving that week and no other fish.** Larger carp, catfish, trout
  and perch are more likely to carry advisories. A child's serving is about 1 ounce at age 1 to
  3, 2 ounces at 4 to 7, 3 ounces at 8 to 10 and 4 ounces at 11 (FDA/EPA). Cooking does not
  remove mercury.

### Ice fishing: the winter advantage and the winter danger

Where lakes freeze, ice fishing is one of the few things that produces food in midwinter
({{#if is.cold_winters}}your January lows average {{ region.home.jan_low_f }} °F{{else}}your
lakes may not freeze reliably; January lows at home average {{ region.home.jan_low_f }} °F{{/if}}).

**But ice is genuinely dangerous:**
- **No ice is ever "safe" ice.** Thickness varies a great deal across one lake: currents,
  springs, inlets and pressure ridges all make weak spots.
- **Check the thickness yourself, again and again**, as you go out. Do not trust someone else's
  tracks.
- **Clear, solid ice is stronger** than white, slushy or refrozen ice.
- **Never go alone, and never onto moving water in winter.** River ice over current can be thick
  at the bank and gone in the middle. Tell someone where you are and when you will be back.
- **Early and late season, and any warm spell, are the most dangerous times.**
- **Wear ice picks around your neck:** two spiked handles that let a person drag themselves out.
  Carry rope and a throwable float too.
- **If someone goes in: do not go in after them.** Reach, throw or extend something. Get them
  out, then treat for hypothermia at once (→ 08-05), including CPR for a person with severe
  hypothermia (→ 12-18): **cold and apparently dead is not the same as dead** (CDC).

**The decision table (Minnesota DNR, for new, clear ice):**

| Clear ice | Load |
|---|---|
| **Under 4 in** | **Stay off** |
| **4 in** | Walking, ice fishing on foot |
| 5 to 7 in | Snowmobile or small ATV |
| 7 to 8 in | Side-by-side ATV |
| 9 to 10 in | Small car or SUV |
| 11 to 12 in | Medium SUV or small truck |
| 13 in | Medium truck |

**White ice (snow that melted and refroze on top) is only about half as strong as new, clear
ice: double every figure** (Minnesota DNR). Ice can be two feet thick in one place and one inch
thick a few yards away, so check with a spud bar (a heavy steel chisel on a pole) or an auger
**again and again** as you go out, and again on the way back if the day warmed. Many families never drive onto ice at all. Write

### If anyone reacts to fish

Fish is a major food allergen. If anyone in the household reacts to fish (→ 02-04):
- **A dedicated cooler, board, knife and pan** for fish, stored apart.
- **Clean fish outdoors**, away from the kitchen.
- **Cook the allergic person's food first**, before fish enters the kitchen.
- **Wash hands and surfaces** thoroughly afterwards.
- **Label any preserved fish clearly.** Smoked or canned fish in an unlabeled jar is exactly the
  failure to avoid.

### Keeping the catch

- **Eat fresh** where possible. Fish spoils fast.
- **No raw, pickled or cold-smoked local fish** unless first **frozen solid at −4 °F (−20 °C)
  for 7 days**: pike, walleye, perch, salmon and other freshwater fish can carry the broad fish
  tapeworm (CDC). An unheated building in winter does not reliably hold −4 °F. Pickled fish
  starts from hard-frozen fish or it is not made.
- **Winter cold** (→ 04-09) where it is reliably below freezing.
- **Smoking** (→ 04-07): traditional and excellent for fish.
- **Pressure canning** (→ 04-05): fish has its own published procedures; follow them exactly.
  **Low-acid foods are pressure-canned to the published time and pressure, or not canned.**
- **Salting and drying** need a lot of salt (→ 04-13).

**Scavenge or substitute:**

- **Hooks, line and a stick** catch fish. The reel is a convenience.
- Hooks from wire, safety pins or bone.
- Fish traps and weirs are ancient, passive and effective.
- Any hand auger or a chisel opens a hole in ice, slowly.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** **hooks and line** are the real consumables. Stock them deeply: they weigh nothing
  and cost little.
- **Ration:** passive set lines catch more per unit of effort and tackle.
- **Resupply:** improvised hooks and cordage; traps and weirs; trade (→ 18-02).
- **Why it matters:** fishing may be the most **sustainable** protein you have: no ammunition,
  works in winter where lakes freeze, and runs passively.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** nearby rivers and lakes. Watch water quality (→ 03-07).
- **B (moving):** a hand line and hooks weigh nothing. Carry them.
- **C (establishing):** often better water and less competition; set lines become genuinely
  productive. In warmer regions, watch for venomous snakes at the water's edge (→ 05-19).
