# Soil, compost and fertility without bought inputs

**What this page is for.** How to keep garden soil fertile year after year without buying
fertilizer: compost, manure, mulch and cover crops, and how to handle manure and human waste
without making anyone sick.

> **CAUTION.** Two real hazards. **Raw manure and human waste carry germs** that cause serious
> diarrhoeal illness; they must be composted properly and kept away from food crops and water
> (→ 13-01). And **cat and dog waste never go into food-garden compost**: cat faeces can carry
> *Toxoplasma*, a parasite that is a particular danger in pregnancy and to small children
> (→ 20-09).

## Do this now

1. **Start composting now.** Fertility takes years to build and cannot be improvised in a crisis.
2. **Compost everything organic you have:** kitchen scraps, leaves, grass, cardboard, paper.
3. **Sheet-mulch new beds:** cardboard, then leaves and compost on top. It builds soil while you
   wait.
4. **Get a soil test** through your state Extension service while it operates.
5. **Never compost cat litter or pet faeces** for a food garden.

## Why / how it works

### Fertility is the limit nobody plans for

You can store seed, tools and water. **You cannot store soil fertility.** Every harvest takes
nutrients out, and in a long emergency there is no bag of fertilizer to put them back. The
households still producing in year three are the ones that built a **closed loop**: everything
organic goes back into the soil.

### Compost basics

| Ingredient | Examples | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Greens** (nitrogen-rich) | Kitchen scraps, grass clippings, fresh manure, green weeds | Feed the microbes |
| **Browns** (carbon-rich) | Dry leaves, straw, cardboard, paper, sawdust, wood chips | Structure and air |
| **Water** | | Damp as a wrung-out sponge |
| **Air** | Turning the pile | Stops it going sour and smelly |

**Mix browns and greens**, with more browns than greens by volume. Too much green goes slimy
and smells; too much brown just sits there. A pile about **3 to 5 feet each way** works best:
smaller does not heat, larger does not let air and water in, and an active pile heats to
**130 to 160 °F** (University of Minnesota Extension, Composting in Home Gardens).

**Hot or cold:** a hot pile (turned, balanced, big enough) heats up and finishes in months, and
the heat helps kill germs and weed seeds. A cold pile works too; it takes a year or more and
does not kill germs or seeds.

**Where winters freeze, the pile freezes and stops.** That is fine: it starts again in spring.
Keep adding to it.

### Free inputs, especially in town

- **Leaves:** the best free soil input. Neighbours bag them up every fall. Collect them.
- **Grass clippings:** high nitrogen; use in thin layers.
- **Cardboard and paper:** carbon, and the base of sheet mulching. Remove tape and glossy print.
- **Wood ash** (→ 06-02): potassium and lime. **Use sparingly**; it raises soil pH and too much
  harms soil. Test before adding it, especially where soils are already alkaline.
- **Kitchen scraps:** the daily input.
- **Coffee grounds** from cafés while they exist.

### Manure: useful and hazardous

- **Plant-eater manure** (rabbit, chicken, horse, cow, goat) is excellent fertility.
- **Rabbit manure is the standout** because it is "cold": low in ammonia, so it will not burn
  plants (→ 05-05).
- **Chicken manure is "hot"**: it must be composted before use or it burns plants.
- **"Will not burn plants" is not "free of germs."** Rabbit droppings carry *E. coli*,
  *Salmonella* and other germs like any raw manure. **Compost all manure before it touches food
  crops, or spread it raw only on beds that will not be harvested for 120 days; never on crops
  eaten raw, never as a side-dressing on growing food plants.** The US organic rule sets
  **120 days** between raw manure and harvest for crops whose edible part touches the soil, and
  **90 days** for others (7 CFR 205.203). With children eating from the garden, the longer gap
  is the one to use.
- **Never use dog or cat waste** on food gardens.

### Human waste: the honest version

In a long outage, human waste is a sanitation problem (→ 13-01) and a possible fertility
resource.
- **It carries serious germs.** Composting it safely needs sustained heat, long curing and
  discipline. It is not a casual project (→ 13-06).
- **The safe compromise** for most households: compost it separately, cure it for a long time,
  and use the result on **non-food** plantings (fruit-tree drip lines at most, ornamentals,
  windbreaks), not vegetables.
- **Keep it far from any water source:** well, rain barrel or pool (→ 03-08). CDC's well
  guidance treats nearby human and animal waste as a contamination risk to wells (CDC,
  Guidelines for Testing Well Water).
- **With small children in the house**, err strongly toward caution: getting this wrong means
  diarrhoeal illness in the most vulnerable people (→ 12-05).

### Building beds fast: sheet mulching

The way to turn lawn into garden with no digging:
1. **Lay cardboard directly on the grass**, overlapping, and wet it.
2. **Pile leaves, compost, grass clippings and straw** on top, in a thick layer.
3. **Wait a season**, or plant straight into pockets of compost.

It smothers grass, feeds the soil, holds water, and needs no tools and no fuel. Fall is the
ideal time to start it, ready for spring planting.

### Keeping fertility in the loop

- **Compost everything:** kitchen, garden, paper, leaves.
- **Cover crops** where you have space: clover and vetch take nitrogen from the air into the
  soil; cereal rye holds soil over winter.
- **Rotate crops** so the same family does not drain the same bed year after year.
- **Return what you harvest:** stalks, husks and trimmings go back.
- **Mulch permanently.** Bare soil loses water, structure and nutrients.

### Know your soil

A soil test tells you the **pH** (how acid or alkaline the soil is) and which nutrients are
short. Soils differ a lot across the country: many wet, wooded regions have acid soils that
need lime; many dry western soils are alkaline and should never get wood ash. Test before you
add lime or ash; overdoing either locks up nutrients.

**Scavenge or substitute:**

- **Leaves, cardboard, grass and kitchen scraps** are free and plentiful in town: one of the few
  things town does better than country.
- Wood ash from the fireplace, sparingly, after a soil test.
- Manure from neighbours' animals, composted before use.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** fertility declines over seasons if you take out more than you put back. It is
  invisible until yields drop.
- **Ration:** put the compost on the beds growing calorie crops rather than spreading it thin.
- **Resupply:** leaves and cardboard are close to unlimited in a residential area; manure if you
  or neighbours keep animals; cover crops fix nitrogen for free.
- **Why it matters:** of everything in Section 05, **soil is the slowest to build and the
  fastest to neglect.** A compost pile started this month is the best thing you can do for the
  garden three years from now.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** free leaves and cardboard everywhere. Start now; build beds by sheet mulching.
- **B (moving):** you cannot take soil. Take seed (→ 05-03) and knowledge.
