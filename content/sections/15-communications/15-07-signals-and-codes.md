# Signals without radio, and family codes

**What this page is for.** Signals that need no batteries, no licence and no network: whistles,
mirrors, lights, bright cloth, marks on the ground, and a handful of family codes agreed in
advance. They are the layer still working when everything else has failed.

> **CAUTION.** **If someone needs rescue, signal.** The discretion trade-offs on this page are for
> ordinary coordination. **They are never a reason to stay quiet when a person is injured, lost or
> freezing** (→ 12-16). **Give every person in the household a whistle**{{#if has.child}},
> children included{{/if}}: it carries far farther than a voice, especially a child's.

## Do this now

1. **Buy pealess whistles** (no ball inside to freeze) for everyone, and fix them to jackets and
   go-bags. Teach every child: **"If you're lost, stay where you are and blow it three times, over
   and over"** (→ 20-04).
2. **Agree the family signals** in the table below and write them here. Keep it to a handful: a
   code nobody remembers is worse than no code.
3. **Put a signal mirror and a flashlight in every go-bag and the vehicle** (→ 22-03, → 22-05).
4. **Teach children the "stay put" rule.** A lost child who stays in one place and makes noise is
   found; one who wanders is much harder to find.

## Distress signals everyone uses

Widely recognised, but **never assume they will be understood**:

- **Three of anything** (three whistle blasts, three shouts, three flashes) **means distress.** Two
  of anything is a common answer. **Gunshots as a signal: only in open wilderness, never in
  town**: where people live close together, three shots read as an attack, not a call for help,
  and every shot still needs a safe backstop (→ 14-11).
- **SOS**: three short, three long, three short, in any medium (light, sound, tapping).
- **Anything bright, moving and out of place**: a mirror flash, a signal fire, a coloured panel on
  snow, an X marked on the ground.
- **Patterns read as deliberate**; random noise reads as noise. **Signal in patterns.**

## Methods that work

| Method | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Whistle** | **Far beyond a voice, with far less effort** | **The best single item here.** Works when you are exhausted or hoarse; carries through wind and snow |
| **Signal mirror** | **Miles in sunlight**, one of the longest-range signals there is | Needs sun; needs practice to aim |
| **Flashlight or headlamp** | Miles at night | Flash it; a steady light is easily missed |
| **Bright fabric or panel** | Visible far away on snow or from the air | **Orange or red on snow stands out** |
| **Fire and smoke** | Miles | Smoke by day (green leaves make white smoke), flame by night. **Fire risk** (→ 17-05) |
| **Marks on the ground** | From the air and the ground | An X, an arrow, tracks stamped in snow |
| **Banging on metal** | Several hundred yards | Carries better than shouting |
| **An object in a particular window** | The neighbourhood | Pre-agreed meaning; discreet (→ 10-05) |
| **Voice** | **The shortest range, and it tires you fastest** | Use the whistle instead |

**Weather changes everything.** Wind carries sound away, and blowing snow kills visibility
completely (→ 17-01). **In a whiteout, assume nobody can see or hear you beyond a very short
distance**, which is exactly why you do not travel in one. Cold also kills people who are lost and
waiting, so a lost person stays put **and** gets out of the wind (CDC, Preventing Hypothermia).

## The family signal set

Agree these, write them down, **practise them once.** Fewer is better.

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **3 whistle blasts, repeated** | **"I need help / I'm lost."** Everyone stops and responds |
| **2 whistle blasts** | "I hear you" |
| **1 whistle blast** | "Where are you?" or check-in |
| **Flashlight, 3 flashes** | Distress, at night |
| **Flashlight, slow sweep** | "Here I am / come to me" |
| **An object in a window** (ours: ______________) | ______________ ("all clear", "come in" or "do not approach") |
| **A word in ordinary conversation** | **The duress word** (below) |
| **The hidden note spot** | The rally-point notes (→ 15-03) |

## Family codes: a small number, agreed in advance

Two are really worth having:

1. **A duress word.** A word or phrase meaning *"I am saying this because someone is making me,
   or because something is wrong that I cannot say out loud."* Agreed between the adults (→ 14-01),
   never used casually, easy to slip naturally into a sentence. **It exists so that "come on in,
   everything's fine" can be a warning.** Do not write it anywhere a stranger could read it.
2. **A "come" and a "do not come" signal**: an object in a window, a mark on a gatepost, or a
   phrase on the radio (→ 15-02). Simple, unmistakable, and known to everyone.

**Keep the set small.** Elaborate codes fail under stress and give false confidence. Three or four
meanings everyone really remembers beat twenty clever ones.

**Be realistic about codes on open radio** (→ 15-02, → 14-03). FRS, GMRS, CB and amateur radio are
unencrypted, and anyone can listen. Simple codes hide meaning from casual listeners, not from
anyone paying attention; their real value is brevity and not broadcasting details.

> **Before the collapse:** amateur radio rules forbid messages coded to obscure their meaning
> (47 CFR Part 97; → 15-05). Keep codes off the amateur bands while the rule is enforced.

## Teaching the children

This is the part of the page with the most real-world value.

- **A young child:** whistle on their jacket, always. **"If you can't find a grown-up: stay where
  you are, blow your whistle three times, keep blowing it."** Practise it as a game, outdoors,
  until it is automatic (→ 20-04).
- **"Stay put" is the most important thing a lost child can do**, and it goes against their
  instinct to go looking. Practise it on purpose.
- **Teach them to answer** when they hear their name or a whistle. Some children hide when called,
  especially if they think they are in trouble. **Make it clear they are never in trouble for being
  lost.**
- **Babies and toddlers cannot signal**, so their identification is on them (→ 15-03) and they are
  never out of an adult's sight.
- **A bright outer layer** for every child in unfamiliar places makes them far easier to find
  (→ 11-02).

## Discretion versus being found

Signalling is broadcasting (→ 14-03, → 10-05): a signal fire, a mirror flash or a whistle tells
anyone in range that people are here. **In most situations (a blizzard, a lost child, an injury)
that is exactly what you want.** In a long breakdown where you are avoiding attention, it is a
decision.

**How to hold both:** **quiet signals for routine coordination** (an object in a window, a mark on
a post, a single whistle note) and **loud, unmistakable, universal signals when someone needs
help.** Do not let habitual discretion stop a call for rescue: reaching help is usually what
matters most (→ 12-16).

## Gear

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Pealess whistles, one per person and spares** | Carries far, no power, works when exhausted | **Pealess**: pea whistles freeze and fail in cold |
| **Signal mirrors, one per adult** | One of the longest-range signals | Learn to aim it; practise |
| A flashlight or headlamp per person | Night signalling and everything else | → 10-01 |
| **A bright orange panel, cloth or vest** | Very visible on snow and from the air | Also for children |
| Bright outer layers for children | Easier to find | → 11-02 |
| Chemical light sticks | Marking; for children; no heat or flame | → 10-01 |
| Chalk, marker, flagging tape | Marks, trail marking, notes | → 15-03 |
| Air horn (optional) | Very loud, very far | Consumable |
| **The family signal list, written** | Short; everyone knows it | This page; → 25-08 |
| An agreed window object | Discreet neighbourhood signal | Costs nothing |

**If you do not have it:**

- **Whistle:** any whistle. Real ones cost almost nothing; buy them.
- **Mirror:** any mirror, a polished metal lid, a CD, a phone screen, foil on cardboard.
- **Bright panel:** an orange tarp, a bright jacket, a bedsheet, a safety vest.
- **Marking:** tracks stamped in snow, branches laid in an arrow, rocks, a scratched X, torn cloth
  tied to branches.
- **Noise:** metal on metal, a car horn, a pot and spoon.
- **Light:** a headlamp, a vehicle's headlights or hazard lights, a fire.
- **What you cannot improvise is the agreement.** Signals only work if everyone knows what they
  mean **in advance.** That part is free, and it is the part people skip.

## When it runs out

- **Almost nothing here runs out.** A whistle, a mirror, a bright cloth and an agreement last
  indefinitely with no power. **The most durable communications in the binder.**
- **The consumables are batteries for lights** (→ 09-07) and light sticks; both minor.
- **What fades is memory.** Review the signals every year (→ 25-10), and **re-teach children as
  they grow**, because what they can understand and do changes every year.
- **When the last battery is gone, this page still works.** The most valuable thing on it costs
  almost nothing: a whistle on a child's jacket, and ten minutes teaching them to use it.

## By phase

- **A (at home):** whistles for everyone, a window signal agreed with neighbours (→ 18-01), and
  the note spots at the rally points (→ 15-03). Teach and practise.
- **B (on the move):** **where signalling matters most.** Whistles on everyone, bright layers on
  children, a mirror and a light per adult, and **signals agreed before you set off.** In a
  whiteout nobody sees or hears anything (→ 17-01); staying physically together beats any signal.
- **C (rural or a new place):** long distances and few people. **A loud signal at the house (an
  air horn, a bell, a whistle) that everyone knows** is a permanent, low-tech capability that works
  in all weather with no power.
