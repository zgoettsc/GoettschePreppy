# Group organization and decisions

**What this page is for.** What to do when cooperation goes beyond neighbours helping neighbours:
shared work, shared resources, or households moving in together. Most cooperation in a disaster is
informal and works fine that way (→ 18-01). **What breaks groups is almost never the outside threat.
It is unclear expectations, unequal contribution and unresolved disputes** (→ 18-06, → 21-05): the
same failures as inside one household, at a larger scale.

> **CAUTION. Decide what your household will not take part in, before you join anything.**
> Detaining, punishing or using force on people are the decisions that most often turn a group into
> something its members never agreed to. Your household's own rule on the use of force is a
> worksheet you write yourselves: {{choice DC-12}} (→ 30-13). A group's agreement should not quietly
> override it.
>
> **Other people's property, labour and children remain theirs.** A group that forgets this breaks
> from the inside.
>
> **Before the collapse:** private citizens have no legal power to detain or punish anyone, and
> group arrangements over property, work or children carry legal obligations (→ 14-11).

## Do this now

1. **You do not need to organize anything yet. You need relationships** (→ 18-01). Structure, if it
   is ever needed, is built on top of them.
2. **Get your own household's roles and decisions right first** (→ 00-07, → 21-04). A household that
   cannot divide its own work will not do better in a group.
3. **Read the agreement checklist below once**, so the conversation is easier if it ever happens.
{{#if has.destinations}}4. **For each place you might go** (→ 01-05), run the arrival agreement below with them now, by
   phone, in ordinary time, and write the answers down.{{else}}4. **If you ever name a place to go**
   (→ 01-05), run the arrival agreement below with its people in ordinary time.{{/if}}

## Levels of organization

**Go no further up this ladder than the situation needs.** Each step adds capability and friction.

| Level | What it is | When |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Informal mutual aid | Neighbours helping neighbours | Almost always; usually enough (→ 18-01) |
| 2. Coordinated tasks | Shared watch, shared wood cutting, a check-in list | Days to weeks; low cost |
| 3. Shared resources | A common generator, well or tool pool | Weeks to months; needs some agreement |
| 4. Combined households | Living together, shared stores and labour | Months or more; **needs real agreement** |
| 5. Formal community structure | Roles, rules, decision procedures | Long term; mostly emerges rather than being designed |

**Most households never need to go past level 2.** Over-organizing creates conflict where none was
needed.

## What to agree before combining anything

From level 3 up, **agree these out loud and write them down.** Vagueness here is what destroys
groups.

1. **Who is in**, and how someone new joins.
2. **What each person contributes:** supplies, labour, skills, space, equipment. Be specific.
3. **What each person receives:** food, shelter, protection, a share of what is produced.
4. **How work is divided and how the unpleasant work rotates.** Rotation matters more than a fair
   first split (→ 21-04).
5. **Who decides what.** Most things should have an owner, not a vote (below).
6. **How disputes are settled** (→ 18-06). Agree it before there are disputes.
7. **How someone leaves, and what they take.** The clause people skip, and the one that causes the
   worst trouble.
8. **What is shared and what stays private.** Not everything has to be pooled.
9. **How the children are looked after, and by whom**, including every member's allergy and medical
   rules, understood by every adult (→ 02-04, → 12-11).
10. **How the agreement is revisited.** Circumstances change.

**Write it down.** Memories differ under stress. A written agreement is not distrust; it is what lets
people trust each other.

## The arrival agreement (moving in with family or friends)

The most likely level-4 case is a household moving in with relatives or friends at a destination.
The hard version is arriving at a working farm or home whose people were not expecting you, with the
terms settled at the kitchen table on day one. **Settle them warmly, once, and in writing.**

**Only count on a place whose people have agreed.** At a place not asked, arrive self-sufficient,
with nothing assumed and nothing left there ahead of time (→ 01-05).

```
ARRIVAL AGREEMENT                      Date talked through: __________
Place: ______________________   Their household: __________________

1. WHO ARRIVES (names, count): _____________________________________
   Total people at the place: _______

2. WHAT WE CONTRIBUTE
   Labour (daily, each adult; what the children can do): __________
   Skills (18-03), offered plainly: ________________ Accepted? ____
   Supplies we bring or left there ahead of time: _________________
   Equipment (vehicle, trailer, tools, radios): ___________________

3. WHO DECIDES (say it plainly; it is true anyway)
   THEIR PLACE = THEIR FINAL CALL on land, animals, equipment and
   who stays.                                         Agreed: _____
   OUR final call: our own medical care, our children, our own
   medicines and medical devices (never pooled).      Agreed: _____
   Who owns each area (farm work / food / medical / watch): _______

4. POOLED OR SEPARATE
   Pooled: _______________________________ (food? fuel? work?)
   Separate: every medicine and medical device, this binder: ______
   Cold storage for refrigerated medicine there, if needed: _______

5. LEAVING
   Either side can say "this isn't working." What leaves with us:
   what we brought + ______________ Notice / handover: ___________

6. MEDICAL DISCLOSURE: what they need to know about our members'
   conditions and allergies, told to whom, and when:
   ________________________________________________________________
   (Where medicine is stored: need-to-know.)

7. SPACE AND PRIVACY: where we sleep, what is "ours", where a door
   can close: _____________________________________________________

8. CHILDREN: the place's hazards walked with our children (20-02:
   water, machinery, animals, grain, dogs); allergy rules known by
   EVERY adult there: __________ Supervision plan: _______________

9. REVISIT: checked at each visit and yearly (25-10): ____________

Signed and dated (both households): _______________________________
```

**At a place that has agreed:** run the nine questions by phone on the next call (→ 15-08),
especially 3 (whose final call), 4 (what is pooled), 6 (what they should know before you arrive) and
8 (hazards for children).
**At a place that has not:** question 6 happens in the first hour, because there is no "before";
question 2 is whatever you brought (→ 18-02); question 5 matters more, not less, when nobody invited
you.

## Decisions that work

- **Most decisions have an owner, not a vote.** Whoever's area it is decides: water, food, medical,
  watch, children. Voting on everything is slow and exhausting.
- **Group decisions are for what affects everyone:** moving, admitting someone, large commitments.
- **Agree a tie-breaker in advance**, and on safety, prefer the more cautious option.
- **Emergencies have a named decision-maker.** There is no time to deliberate (→ 21-01).
- **Decide when to decide.** "We'll decide Sunday" is often the best answer.
- **Write decisions down.**

## Fairness: what keeps a group together

- **Openness.** People tolerate scarcity far better than secrecy about scarcity.
- **Visible contribution.** Invisible work breeds resentment. Name it and thank people.
- **Rotate the bad jobs.** The single most effective fairness measure.
- **Different capacities, said openly.** Someone with a baby (→ 20-01), an injury (→ 12-09) or a
  chronic condition (→ 12-11) contributes differently. Say so rather than letting it become a
  grievance.
- **Children and the sick are cared for, not weighed.** Say that explicitly.
- **A member's medical needs** (medicine, devices, special food) are stated up front as not pooled
  and not negotiable, rather than discovered as a disagreement later.

## Roles worth naming

Not a hierarchy, just clarity (→ 00-07): water, food and stores, fuel and heat, medical (ideally the
most trained person, → 18-03), watch, repair, communications, children, and coordination (someone
has to hold the whole picture).

## What breaks groups

- **Unclear expectations**, by a wide margin.
- **Unequal contribution**, real or perceived.
- **Resentment left unspoken** (→ 21-05).
- **Disputes left unsettled** (→ 18-06).
- **One person gathering authority** nobody agreed to.
- **Scarcity plus secrecy.**
- **No way to leave**, so people who want out feel trapped and behave badly.
- **Exhaustion** (→ 21-02, → 21-07). Much group conflict in month five is a sleep problem dressed as
  a governance problem.

## The honest scale

**One household combining with one or two others is realistic** and can work well: more hands,
medical backup (→ 12-16), childcare relief (→ 21-05), better morale (→ 21-02). Anything larger is a
real organizational problem and much harder than people imagine. Start small, go slowly.

**Everything here is a conversation and a piece of paper.** Use structures that already exist: a
congregation, a block association, extended family. What you cannot substitute is trust, and it
takes time (→ 18-05).

## When it runs out

- **What runs out is goodwill**, on the same schedule as everything else in a long event (→ 21-02).
  Upkeep is openness, rotation, thanks, and settling disputes early (→ 18-06).
- **Watch for the drift:** decisions stop being discussed, work stops being shared, someone stops
  showing up, resentment stops being spoken (SAMHSA, Disaster Distress Helpline warning signs).
- **Add structure only when needed, and remove it when not.**
- **When a group is not working, leaving well beats staying badly.** That is why the exit clause
  matters.
- **Most of this page will probably never be needed.** Level 1 and 2 cooperation almost certainly
  will, and needs only relationships built in advance.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** informal mutual aid is the realistic level. Do not try to organize a neighbourhood
  that does not want organizing.
- **B, on the move:** travelling with another household is a real decision: more capability, more
  friction, slower. Agree the terms before leaving, including how you would separate (→ 01-03).
- **C, settled:** where combining households makes most sense, and where getting the agreement right
  matters most, because you are in it for a long time (→ 01-04).
