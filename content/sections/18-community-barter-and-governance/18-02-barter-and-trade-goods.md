# Barter and trade goods

**What this page is for.** What to stock for trading, how to trade without putting your household at
risk, what never to trade, and the food and medicine traps in trading. Barter is a phase, not a
destination: in the long run what lasts is relationships and reputation (→ 18-01).

> **CAUTION. Trading is a security event, not just an exchange** (→ 14-03). Every trade shows that
> you have something, hints at what else you might have, and tells someone where you live. **Trade
> skills before goods, and small things before large ones.**
>
> **Two traps:**
> - **Prescription medicine.** Medicine from a stranger may be the wrong drug, the wrong strength,
>   fake, or spoiled, and casual antibiotic use breeds resistance (CDC, Antibiotic Use; → 12-04,
>   → 12-07). Whether your household ever gives or accepts someone else's prescription is a choice:
>   {{choice DC-15}} (→ 30-16). Antibiotics without a prescription: {{choice DC-16}} (→ 30-17).
> - **Food you did not make.** Traded food has no label and unknown ingredients (FDA, Food
>   Allergies), and home-canned food from someone else's kitchen can carry botulism, which you
>   cannot see, smell or taste. Rules below.

## Do this now

1. **Over-stock the cheap, useful, shelf-stable things.** They are the best trade goods and cost
   little now: fasteners and hardware (→ 16-07), matches and lighters, soap (→ 13-09), salt
   (→ 04-13), batteries, seeds (→ 05-03), needles and thread (→ 16-04), tape and wire (→ 16-05).
2. **Build skills** (→ 18-03). They are the safest thing to trade and they do not run out.
3. **Keep some cash in small bills** (→ 19-02). Early in any disruption, cash still works and barter
   does not.
4. **Write your household's never-trade list** (below) now, calmly. Deciding under pressure is worse.

## How a broken economy goes

| Stage | What works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | Cards, cash | Prepare now |
| **Early disruption** | **Cash, and only cash** | Card machines stop when the power does (→ 19-02). Small bills |
| Deeper disruption | Cash still moves; goods start to matter | Prices rise fast |
| Extended | **Barter and reciprocity** | Trust becomes the medium (→ 18-01) |
| Settled | A local exchange system re-emerges | Skills, labor, staple goods |

**Cash matters early, and most people have none.**

## What makes a good trade good

1. **Everyone needs it**, whatever their situation.
2. **It keeps:** no expiry, no upkeep.
3. **It divides:** you can trade a little without trading a lot.
4. **It is compact.**
5. **It is cheap now**, so you can over-stock it.
6. **It is boring to be seen with.** A box of screws does not mark you as a target; a case of
   ammunition does (→ 14-03). This is the point people miss.

## The ones to be careful with

- **Prescription medicine:** see the caution above and your choices on → 30-16 and → 30-17.
- **Ammunition and firearms** (→ 14-07). High value, but trading them advertises what you have and
  arms people you may not know (→ 18-05). Be very deliberate.
- **Fuel.** Valuable, dangerous to handle, and it marks you as someone with fuel.
- **Food in quantity.** Trading visible amounts of food tells everyone you have food: the single
  most consequential thing to reveal when food is scarce.
- **Anything that reveals depth.** One of something looks like the last one you have; a case of it
  looks like a supply.
- **Water treatment and medical supplies** buy a great deal, and mark you.

## Trade freely: what builds the network without costing you

- **Skills and labor** (→ 18-03, → 18-08): repair, sewing, first aid, teaching. They regenerate.
- **Surplus garden produce.** Visibly seasonal; it does not suggest a stocked pantry.
- **Common batteries** from a deep stock.
- **The boring hardware tier** above: fasteners, matches, soap, salt, seeds, sewing supplies.
- **Information and referrals** (→ 15-04): free and welcome.

## Trading with less risk

- **Skills and labor first.** They reveal nothing about your stores.
- **Trade small.** Repeated small trades build a relationship; one large trade builds a reputation
  you may not want.
- **Meet away from your house**, especially with people you do not know well (→ 18-05).
- **Never let a trade become a tour of your supplies.** Bring only what you are trading.
- **Trade with people you know**, and build up slowly with people you do not.
- **Decline gracefully.** "We don't have any to spare" is a complete answer.
- **Do not haggle hard with neighbors you will depend on.** The relationship is worth more than the
  margin.

## Fairness and reputation

In a small community, your reputation as a trader is a real asset or a real liability.
- **Be fair and be seen to be fair.** Word travels fast.
- **Do not exploit desperation.** Beyond the ethics, it is a terrible strategy: you will need these
  people (→ 18-01).
- **Generosity with small things** buys great goodwill for little.
- **Keep your word.** In a barter economy, reliability is the currency underneath the currency.

## Traded food: the rules

- **Traded food has no label and unknown ingredients**, and anything cooked in someone else's
  kitchen may have touched an allergen (FDA, Food Allergies; → 02-04).
{{#if has.anaphylaxis}}- **Your household has a severe allergy.** {{#each allergies as a}}{{a.member}}: {{a.allergen}} ({{a.severity}}). {{/each}}
{{/if}}- **Whether an allergic member ever eats food you did not make** is a household choice: {{choice DC-18}} (→ 30-19).
- **Food that is old, dented or from someone's failed freezer** is a separate choice: {{choice DC-14}}
  (→ 30-15). **Bulging, leaking or spurting cans and jars are never opened. They are discarded**,
  away from children and animals.
- **Home-canned food from someone else's process.** Botulism toxin is invisible, and the jar's look
  and smell tell you nothing reliable. CDC's guidance: **boil all low-acid home-canned food
  (vegetables, beans, meat, fish, soup) for 10 minutes before eating or tasting it, adding 1 minute
  for each additional 1,000 feet of elevation** (CDC, Botulism Prevention; → 04-05). Low-acid foods are
  pressure-canned to the published time and pressure, or not canned (floor, → 30-01).
  Prefer factory-sealed, labeled goods when you have a choice.
- **Never honey for a child under 1 year** (infant botulism; CDC, Botulism Prevention).
{{#if has.toddler}}- **A toddler trying a genuinely new food:** one new food at a time, at home,
  with an adult watching afterward (→ 20-01).{{/if}}
- **Tell trading partners about allergies.** People are generally glad to know, and it stops a kind
  gift becoming an emergency (→ 18-01).

## When it runs out

- **Stocked trade goods run out; skills do not.** A household that can make soap, fix things,
  sharpen, sew and dress a wound has a permanent trading position.
- **Trade goods are surplus, never core supply.** Never trade into your own runway: food, water
  treatment, fuel or medicine (→ 23-02).
- **Substitute** skills for goods, made goods for stocked goods, labor for both.
- **The long view:** a household known for fair dealing, useful skills and reliable help is in a
  stronger position than one holding a large stockpile and no standing.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** cash first (→ 19-02), then small neighborly exchange (→ 18-01). Do not rush into
  visible trading.
- **B, on the move:** trading on the road is risky (→ 18-05, → 14-01). Use skills, information and
  cash while it works. If you carry trade goods, keep them to one small box of the boring tier, opened
  away from the rest of your load. **Never arrive empty-handed** at a destination (→ 01-05): bring
  goods chosen for the household that takes you in, not only for your own.
- **C, settled:** a local exchange economy is likely to re-emerge around produce, labor, skills and
  equipment. Being able to produce something tradeable (food, soap, repairs, care, teaching) is the
  durable position (→ 18-03, → 18-07).
