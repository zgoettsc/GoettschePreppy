# Skills as currency

**What this page is for.** Why skills are the best trade good there is, which skills matter most,
and how to get them while courses still exist. A stockpile is finite, visible, and defended only by
you. A skill cannot be used up, cannot be taken, improves with use, and, for a household that values
discretion (→ 14-03), **using it reveals nothing about what you have.** A person with valuable skills
is welcome almost anywhere, which is a different position from a person with valuable things.

## Do this now

1. **Pick three skills from the lists below and actually learn them this year.** Not read about:
   do. **A bleeding-control class (Stop the Bleed) and a first-aid and CPR course** are the highest
   value (→ 12-01). **An amateur ("ham") radio license** is next (→ 15-05).
2. **Practice the ones you have.** A skill last used ten years ago is a memory, not a capability
   (→ 24-01).
3. **Find out what skills your neighbors have** (→ 18-01), and let them know yours.
4. **Teach one skill to someone this year** (→ 18-07). Teaching shows whether you really know it.

## Why skills beat stockpiles

| | Stockpile | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| Runs out | Yes | No; improves with use |
| Can be taken | Yes | No |
| Marks you as a target | Yes (→ 14-03) | No |
| Portable | Poorly | Perfectly |
| Tradeable again and again | No | Yes |
| Makes you welcome | Sometimes | Almost always |
| Cost | Money | Time, which is available now |

This is not an argument against stockpiling; most of this binder is about stockpiling. **It is an
argument for spending some of your preparation on time instead of money.**

## Trading a skill safely

- **A repair for a neighbor reveals nothing about your stores** (→ 14-03).
- **You can be generous with skills indefinitely** without running anything down.
- **It builds relationship and reputation** (→ 18-01). It makes you someone people protect rather
  than someone they eye.
- **Do not undervalue it.** A day's skilled work is worth a day's food, and being known as fair
  matters (→ 18-02).
- **Teach as well as do** (→ 18-07). It multiplies your value and costs nothing.
- **Know your limits.** Working beyond your competence, in medicine especially, does real harm
  (→ 12-16). "I can clean and dress that; I can't set it" is a good answer.

## Getting skills now

- **Courses that exist now and will not later:** Stop the Bleed, first aid and CPR, wilderness first
  responder, amateur radio licensing (ARRL, Getting Licensed; → 15-05), canning classes, a welding
  class at a technical college.
- **Practice, do not just read.** Under stress you do what you have practiced (→ 21-01).
- **Do it badly first, at low stakes.** Make bad soap. Sharpen a cheap knife. Sew a crooked patch.
- **Learn from people:** neighbors, clubs, classes. Faster than books, and it builds the network
  (→ 18-01).
- **Teach the children** (→ 20-07, → 20-03).
- **Write down what you learn** (→ 24-01).

**What you cannot substitute is the time.** Skills take time to build, and the time is available
now.

## When it runs out

- **Skills do not run out. They fade without use and are lost if not passed on.**
- **Practice keeps them** (→ 24-01, → 24-03).
- **Teaching multiplies them** (→ 18-07). A skill you have taught to three people is a skill your
  community has.
- **For a skill you lack, the substitute is a person who has it** (→ 18-01).
- **Supplies get you through months; skills get you through years.** They travel at no weight, cannot
  be taken, and make you welcome.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** acquire. Courses, licenses, clubs and practice, all available now and not later.
- **B, on the move:** skills are the only thing you carry at no weight (→ 22-03).
- **C, settled:** skills become the local economy. Being the household that can fix, treat, teach or
  make is a durable and secure position.
