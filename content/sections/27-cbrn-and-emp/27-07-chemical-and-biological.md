# Chemical and biological release

**What this page is for.** Chemical and biological releases get named together, but they need
almost opposite responses. **Chemical:** fast, local, and a **seal-the-room-and-listen** problem
lasting hours. **Biological:** slow, wide, and handled by **the pandemic plan** already on → 17-09
and → 13-11. This page is mostly about the chemical half.

> **Caution.** **For fallout you go down; for chemical vapour you go up.** Getting this backwards
> is one of the few truly dangerous mistakes in this section. **The radio tells you which one you
> are in** (→ 15-01). **Bleach or pool shock is never mixed with ammonia, acids or other
> cleaners**: that mixing is itself a common household chemical release.

## Do this now

1. **Know that the likely version is an accident, not an attack.** A rail, highway or plant
   release{{#if is.rural}}, or an anhydrous ammonia release from farm storage or a field tank{{/if}}, is far more likely, and
   the response is the same.
2. **Pick the room you would seal**: interior, upper floor if you have one, few windows, water
   nearby if possible. Pre-cut plastic sheeting and tape for it and store them there (→ 08-03).
3. **Set the rule now: go inside and listen. Do not drive into or through a cloud.**
4. **For anything biological, go to → 17-09 and → 13-11.** That is the plan.

## The two are not alike

| | **Chemical release** | **Biological release** |
|---|---|---|
| **Starts** | Minutes | Days to weeks |
| **Spread** | A local cloud (a "plume"), driven by wind | Possibly everywhere |
| **You find out because** | You are told, or you see or smell it | Public health announces it, later |
| **Core response** | **Get inside, seal, listen** | Distance, hygiene, isolate the sick |
| **Lasts** | Hours | Weeks to months |
| **Where in this binder** | **This page** | → 17-09, → 13-11 |

## Chemical: shelter in place, properly

The cloud passes. Your job is to be inside and sealed while it does (FEMA/Ready.gov, Shelter;
Ready.gov, Hazardous Materials Incidents).

{{figure fig-up-for-vapour-down-for-fallout}}

```
[ ] GET INSIDE. Bring everyone and the pets in. Close all doors and windows.
[ ] SHUT OFF anything that moves outside air in: furnace and air-conditioning
    fans, bathroom and kitchen exhaust fans, fireplace dampers, the dryer.
[ ] GO UP, NOT DOWN. Ready.gov: an above-ground room with as few openings as
    possible. Some chemical gases, chlorine among them, are heavier than air
    and collect low (ATSDR), so a basement is the WRONG place here: the
    opposite of the fallout answer (27-05).
[ ] SEAL that room: plastic sheeting and tape over windows, vents and the gaps
    round the door. A wet towel along the bottom of the door helps.
[ ] TAKE IN: water, the medical kit (22-06), phones, a battery radio, and
    something to pass the time.
[ ] LISTEN. Weather radio and local broadcast. Officials will say when it is
    over and whether to leave. (15-01)
[ ] DO NOT LEAVE unless told to. Driving into a cloud is worse than sitting
    it out.
[ ] AFTERWARDS: air the house out thoroughly and follow any official cleaning
    advice (CDC, When and How to Clean and Disinfect Your Home). (13-11)
```

Your sealing room: ______________ {{#if has.apartment}}(in an apartment: an interior room of your own unit, away from the corridor
door and windows; the building's ventilation may need the building manager to turn it off){{/if}}{{#if has.mobile_home}}(a mobile home leaks air badly: seal the smallest interior room, and leave early if officials
advise it){{/if}}

### The sealed room, by the numbers

- **Room size: about 10 square feet of floor space per person.** FEMA: that gives enough air to
  prevent carbon dioxide build-up for up to 5 hours, assuming a normal breathing rate while
  resting, and many chemical releases would be diluted within a few hours (FEMA, How long to
  remain in a shelter-in-place room?). For
  {{ household.size }} people{{#if has.pets}} and the pets{{/if}}, most normal bedrooms clear that
  bar; pick the room for its position (interior, upstairs, few windows), not its size.
- **It is temporary.** A taped room with people breathing in it goes stale in **hours**. You seal,
  listen, and unseal on the all-clear. It is not a bunker.
- **The judgement line:** if hours have passed with no all-clear and the room is getting stuffy
  (headache, heavy air{{#if has.child}}, a drowsy child{{/if}}), **open the seal, refresh the air
  briefly, and reseal.** Stale air is a certainty on a clock; the cloud outside has probably
  passed. Do not let a taped window outrank the people inside.

**Pre-cut the plastic now, to the room's actual openings.** Cutting sheeting during a release
wastes the minutes that matter. Ready.gov: cut the plastic several inches wider than the openings,
label each sheet, and tape the corners first, then all the edges (Ready.gov, Shelter). Write the
sizes here:

### If you were outside and exposed

The same idea as → 27-05: most of it is on the outside of you.

```
[ ] TAKE OFF outer clothing before going inside. Cut it off rather than
    pulling it over the face if it is contaminated.
[ ] BAG it and leave it outside the living space.
[ ] WASH with soap and lukewarm water. Flush eyes with clean water.
[ ] Put on clean clothing stored inside.
[ ] GET MEDICAL ADVICE. Poison Control, 1-800-222-1222, is staffed and
    handles exposures. (25-03)
    NO PHONE: the offline poisoning page (12-17). Cleaning off as above is
    the treatment; do not make anyone vomit, do not try to "neutralise"
    a chemical on skin, watch breathing.
```

## Why this page does not recommend gas masks

A respirator helps only if **all three** are true: the **right cartridge for that exact
chemical**, a **proper seal on the face**, and **training to use it**. In a real release you will
not know the chemical in time to choose a cartridge.{{#if has.child}} And **adult respirators do not
fit young children**: there is no protective mask you can put on a small child.{{/if}}

**So the household's protection is the building, not the face.** Sealing a room protects
everyone, costs almost nothing, and does not depend on knowing what was released. **N95 masks are
still worth having** for dust, clean-up and outbreaks (→ 13-11), just not as chemical protection.

## The two you are most likely to meet

Rail lines, highways and industry carry hazardous chemicals almost everywhere, and farm country
adds its own. Two are worth knowing by sight and smell (CDC/ATSDR, Medical Management Guidelines
for Ammonia; for Chlorine):

| Chemical | Where it comes from | What you would notice | Where the cloud goes (ATSDR) | The trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Anhydrous ammonia** | Rail, and farm supply stores and field tanks in farming areas{{#if is.rural}} (likely near you){{/if}} | Sharp, choking, suffocating smell; you may see a cloud | ATSDR: the gas is **lighter than air and will rise**, **but** vapour from the liquefied gas is **at first heavier than air and may spread along the ground** | Do not read "lighter than air" as "safe low down". The early cloud, the dangerous one, behaves heavy. Treat it as ground-hugging until it visibly lifts |
| **Chlorine** | Rail (water treatment and industry) | **Yellow-green gas**; sharp, irritating smell, like strong bleach | ATSDR: **heavier than air** and **may collect in low-lying areas** | **A basement is the worst room in a chlorine release.** This is the down-for-fallout, up-for-vapour reversal at its sharpest |

**You will not identify the chemical in time, and you do not need to.** Caught outside near a
visible cloud or a choking smell: **move away at once, in a direction upwind of the source**
(Ready.gov, Hazardous Materials Incidents), never through the cloud and never downwind along it,
**then get inside, seal, listen** (→ 15-01). Both chemicals above, and everything else on a rail car, are answered by the
same sealed upstairs room.

## Biological: read → 17-09, not this page

For a household, a deliberate biological release and a natural pandemic look the same and are
handled the same way. You will learn about it from public health, not your own senses. The
response:
- **Distance and less contact** (→ 17-09).
- **Handwashing**: the measure that actually works (CDC, About Handwashing; → 13-02).
- **Isolate anyone who is sick** inside the household (→ 13-11).
- **Protect the most vulnerable**{{#if has.infant}}: babies{{/if}}{{#if has.elder}}, older members{{/if}}{{#if has.condition.CL-18}}, anyone on immune-suppressing medicine (→ 02-18){{/if}}, anyone with a chronic condition (→ 12-11).
- **Follow public health instructions** on treatment and preventive medicine, if any exist.

**Do not stockpile antibiotics against an unknown germ.** They do nothing for viruses, the right
drug depends on the organism, and misuse causes harm (→ 12-04). Antibiotics without a
prescription are a decision card: {{choice DC-16}} → 30-17

## What you need

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-cut plastic sheeting and strong tape** | The whole chemical response | Cut and label it now (table above) |
| **A battery weather radio** | How you learn what it is and when it ends (NOAA, NOAA Weather Radio All Hazards) | → 15-01 |
| Water and the medical kit in the room | You may be there for hours | → 22-06 |
| N95 masks, nitrile gloves, eye protection | Clean-up and outbreaks, **not** chemical protection | → 13-11 |
| Soap, plenty | Cleaning off, and the biological half | → 13-02 |
| Poison Control number posted | Staffed and fast while phones work | → 25-03; offline → 12-17 |

No money? Any plastic and tape seals a room: bin bags, shower curtains, painter's plastic. Wet
towels at the bottoms of doors. **The room matters more than the materials.**

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** a chemical shelter-in-place lasts **hours**. Stock the room for a day and you have
  over-prepared, which is the right way round.
- **Ration:** nothing, at these lengths of time.
- **Resupply:** afterwards, as normal.
- **Substitute:** for the biological half, the whole of → 17-09 and → 13-11.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** shelter and seal. This is an event of hours, not a change of phase.
- **B, moving:** **do not drive through a cloud.** If officials order an evacuation, take the route
  they give, not the fastest one (→ 01-02).
- **C, establishing:** distance from rail lines, highways and large chemical storage is a modest
  factor in choosing a place (→ 01-05).
