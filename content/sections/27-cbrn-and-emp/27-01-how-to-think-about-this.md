# How to think about CBRN and EMP

**What this page is for.** CBRN means chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear. EMP means
electromagnetic pulse: a burst of energy that can damage electronics. This is the part of
preparedness where published advice goes furthest wrong: much of what is written about EMP and
nuclear survival is unsourced, mistaken, or selling something. This section is small, sourced to
federal agencies, and built on one honest observation: **for almost every one of these events,
what actually happens to you is a long loss of power, water and services, which the rest of this
binder already covers.**

> **Caution.** Read this page before the rest of the section, so the other six are read in
> proportion. Nothing here replaces official instructions during a real event: listen for them
> (→ 15-01).

## Do this now

1. **Read this page first.** Then the others in order.
2. **Do not buy anything because of this section** until the basics on → 26-09 are done. A
   carbon monoxide alarm and a month of prescription medicine outrank every item any CBRN page
   could suggest.
3. **The two things in this section that really add something** are → 27-03 (protecting the
   electronics your household cannot lose) and → 27-04 with → 27-05 (the first 48 hours after a
   nuclear detonation, which are short, specific and time-critical).

## The one idea that organises this section

Every event here has **two phases**, and they need different pages:

| | **The event** | **The aftermath** |
|---|---|---|
| **How long** | Minutes to about 48 hours | Weeks to years |
| **What decides survival** | A few specific, time-critical actions | Water, heat, food, medicine, sanitation, people |
| **Where it lives** | **This section** | **The rest of the binder** |

**That is why this section is seven pages and not seventy.** The aftermath of an EMP is a grid
failure (→ 17-08). The aftermath of a pandemic is → 17-09. The aftermath of a nuclear detonation
you survived at a distance is sheltering at home (→ 01-01) with a radiation-shaped first two days
on the front.

## What is realistic, ranked

Ranked by how likely you are to live through it, not by how frightening it is:

| Event | How likely | What is really different |
|---|---|---|
| **Geomagnetic storm** (a storm from the sun hitting Earth's magnetic field; "space weather") | **Recurring and real.** NOAA's scale expects about 4 G5 "extreme" storms and about 100 G4 "severe" storms per 11-year solar cycle (NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center, NOAA Space Weather Scales) | Strain on the power grid and possible damage to large transformers. **NOAA issues warnings**: you can watch for it (→ 27-02) |
| **Pandemic or natural outbreak** | Happens | Already covered: → 17-09, → 13-11 |
| **Chemical release** from industry, rail or highway | Plausible anywhere near rail lines, highways or plants | Seal a room and shelter, or leave (→ 27-07) |
| **Deliberate biological release** | Low | Mostly the pandemic playbook (→ 27-07, → 17-09) |
| **High-altitude EMP** from a nuclear weapon | Low, and needs a nuclear attack | In practice the same aftermath as a long grid failure (→ 27-02) |
| **Nuclear detonation nearby** | Low | The first 48 hours, and only those (→ 27-04, → 27-05) |

**Notice what the table says.** The most likely item is a space-weather event that NOAA forecasts
publicly, and the least likely gets the most attention.

## Five common claims that are not true

1. **"EMP destroys all electronics everywhere."** It does not. Effects vary hugely with the
   event, the device, whether it is plugged into a long wire, and whether it is switched on.
   Plan for **losing the grid**, the reliable consequence, rather than for a pattern of device
   failure nobody can predict (→ 27-02).
2. **"Potassium iodide protects you from radiation."** **It does not.** FDA says potassium
   iodide only stops the thyroid gland taking up radioactive iodine; it is not a general
   protection against radiation, and taking more than directed can cause severe illness or death
   (FDA, Frequently Asked Questions on Potassium Iodide; → 27-06).
3. **"You need a gas mask."** A respirator works only with the right cartridge for the specific
   chemical, a proper fit and training. Without all three it is theatre{{#if has.child}}, and adult
   masks do not fit children{{/if}} (→ 27-07).
4. **"If it happens, run."** For fallout, the federal advice is the opposite: **get inside, stay
   inside, stay tuned** (Ready.gov, Nuclear Explosion; CDC, Radiation Emergencies). Driving
   through fallout is worse than sheltering from it (→ 27-04).
5. **"You can't survive a nuclear event, so why plan?"** At a distance from a detonation, what
   you do in the first 24 hours makes a very large difference, and fallout radiation drops fast
   (HHS REMM, Nuclear Explosion; → 27-05). The fatalism is as wrong as the fantasy.

## What matters most for your household

{{#if has.condition.CL-02}}- **An insulin pump and glucose sensor are electronics, and they keep someone alive.** Any event
  that damages electronics is, for your household, first a **medical continuity** problem.
  That is → 27-03, the page in this section most worth acting on. The real answer to every
  electronics scenario is the **injection backup** (→ 02-02): no power, no radio, no batteries.
  Practise it (→ 24-01).
{{/if}}{{#if has.oxygen}}- **Oxygen equipment depends on power.** Cylinders, a regulator that needs no power and a backup
  plan are the answer to any grid or electronics event (→ 02-08, → 27-03).
{{/if}}{{#if has.cpap}}- **A CPAP needs power every night.** Battery and a plan for nights without it (→ 02-24, → 27-03).
{{/if}}{{#if has.refrigerated_meds}}- **Refrigerated medicine** depends on the fridge, which depends on the grid. Every scenario
  here ends in a cold-chain problem (→ 29-07, → 12-21).
{{/if}}{{#if has.child}}- **Sheltering with children** for 24 to 48 hours in one interior space is a logistics problem
  (water, food{{#if has.infant}}, diapers{{/if}}, toilet, boredom) long before it is a radiation
  problem (→ 27-05, → 20-08).
{{/if}}{{#if has.elder}}- **Older members** may need medicines, warmth and help with stairs in a shelter space; plan the
  space around them (→ 27-05).
{{/if}}- For every household: the electronics that matter most are the ones that keep someone
  well, and the ones that tell you what is happening (a battery radio, → 15-01).

## What you need

Almost nothing that is not already on → 22-01. The honest list this section adds is short:
a battery or hand-crank radio you need anyway (→ 15-01), a shielded container for **backup**
medical and communication electronics (→ 27-03), and pre-cut plastic sheeting and tape for
sealing one room (→ 27-07). **Everything else is already in the binder for other reasons.**

No money? **Mass is mass.** Books, water containers and earth shield against fallout radiation
whatever they were bought for (→ 27-05). The best fallout shelter in most houses is the lowest,
most central space you already have.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** the event actions in this section last hours to about two days. After that, every
  clock on → 23-02 takes over.
- **Ration:** nothing here is a consumable except attention. **Do not let this section take money
  that belongs to the basics** (→ 26-09).
- **Resupply:** read it again after any change to your medical devices (→ 27-03).
- **Substitute:** the rest of the binder. That is the point of this page.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** sheltering in place is the answer for nearly every scenario here, and the home
  you have is the shelter you have.
- **B, moving:** **moving during fallout or a chemical plume is the wrong call** unless officials
  tell you to (→ 27-04). It is the one situation where staying put is most clearly right
  (→ 01-02).
- **C, establishing:** distance from likely targets and from heavy industry is a real but minor
  factor in choosing a destination (→ 01-05), well below water, timber, ground and people.
