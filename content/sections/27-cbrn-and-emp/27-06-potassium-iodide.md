# Potassium iodide: what it does and does not do

**What this page is for.** Potassium iodide (KI) is the most misunderstood item in preparedness,
and the misunderstanding is dangerous both ways. This page explains what it does, who should not
take it, and prints FDA's dose table and FDA's home recipe for children **exactly as FDA publishes
them**, because an offline household cannot look them up. Nothing here tells you **when** to take
it: only public health officials do that.

> **SAFETY.** **It is not an anti-radiation pill.** FDA: KI works only to stop the thyroid gland
> taking up radioactive iodine; it is not a general protection against radiation. **"KI should not
> be taken as a preventative before radiation exposure"**: take it **only when public health or
> emergency officials say to.** Taking more than directed does not protect more and can cause
> severe illness or death (FDA, Frequently Asked Questions on Potassium Iodide). **Sheltering
> protects far more than KI does** (→ 27-05). This binder does not work out anyone's dose: find
> each person's row in FDA's table yourself.

## Do this now

1. **Understand what it is for** before deciding whether to keep any: it protects **one gland**
   from **one kind** of contamination. Nothing else.
2. **If you decide to keep some, talk to your clinicians first.** There are real reasons some
   people should not take it (below){{#if has.condition.CL-14}}, and someone in your household has thyroid disease
   (→ 02-14){{/if}}.
3. **Never take it "just in case."** Only on official direction.
4. **Write on the family medical summary (→ 25-09)** anyone who should not take it, and anyone
   in the special groups below.

## The one-sentence version

**KI fills the thyroid with ordinary iodine so it cannot absorb radioactive iodine.** That is the
whole mechanism, and everything true about KI follows from it. The **thyroid** is a gland in the
neck; **radioactive iodine** is one of the materials a nuclear accident or explosion can release,
and the thyroid absorbs it.

## What it does not do

| Belief | What FDA says |
|---|---|
| "It protects you from radiation" | **No.** It is not a general protection against radiation |
| "It protects your whole body" | **No.** The thyroid only |
| "It protects against fallout in general" | **No.** Only against **radioactive iodine**, not other radioactive materials or radiation from outside the body |
| "Take it as soon as you hear about an event" | **No.** Not before exposure; take it when officials say |
| "More is safer" | **No.** More does not protect more and can cause severe illness or death |
| "It's harmless, so why not" | **No.** Some people should not take it (below) |
| "It replaces sheltering" | **No.** **Sheltering is the protection** (→ 27-05). KI covers one narrow route |

## Who should not take it, or should take care

From FDA's KI FAQ:
- **People with a known iodine sensitivity** should avoid it.
- **People with dermatitis herpetiformis or hypocomplementemic vasculitis** (rare conditions
  linked to iodine sensitivity) should avoid it.
- **People with nodular thyroid disease together with heart disease** should not take it.
- **People with multinodular goitre, Graves' disease or autoimmune thyroiditis** should use
  caution, especially if dosing goes on for more than a few days.
- A seafood or shellfish allergy does not necessarily mean iodine sensitivity.

**This is a conversation with your clinician, not a checklist decision**, and another reason not
to buy a bottle and assume it is good for everyone (HHS REMM, Potassium Iodide).

## Where sheltering fits, and why it matters more

| | **Sheltering (→ 27-05)** | **KI** |
|---|---|---|
| **Protects against** | Radiation from outside, breathing in fallout, contamination: broadly | The thyroid taking up radioactive iodine, only |
| **Who it helps** | Everyone | Everyone, but only their thyroid |
| **When** | At once; the first 24 hours matter most | Only when officials direct |
| **Cost** | Free: you already have the building | A purchase, with people who should not take it |
| **If you could only do one** | **This one, by a wide margin** | |

**If a page in this section changes what you do, it should be → 27-05, not this one.**

## Children, and why this page names them

Children's thyroids take up iodine more readily, and children exposed to radioactive iodine have a
greater risk of thyroid cancer later than adults, which is why public health guidance puts them
first (FDA). **That changes none of the rules above**: it is given on official direction, with a
clinician's advice where you can get it. Children's doses are fractions of an adult tablet, and
guessing fractions from memory in the dark is the error this page exists to prevent: so FDA's
table **and** FDA's measuring method are both printed below.

If you keep KI, keep the children's guidance from your paediatrician with it, and note it on
→ 25-09.

## FDA's dose table, as published

> **The when-rule comes first and does not change: KI ONLY when public health officials direct
> it.** Nothing in this table starts a dose. Officials start it; the table only answers how much,
> in FDA's own numbers.

**FDA, Frequently Asked Questions on Potassium Iodide (KI); FDA, Potassium Iodide as a Thyroid
Blocking Agent in Radiation Emergencies.** "Predicted thyroid exposure" is the radiation dose to
the thyroid at which FDA recommends KI for that group; officials work this out, not households.

| Group | Predicted thyroid exposure (cGy) | KI dose (mg) | Number of 130 mg tablets | Number of 65 mg tablets | Millilitres of oral solution, 65 mg/mL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adults over 40 years | ≥ 500 | 130 | 1 | 2 | 2 mL |
| Adults over 18 through 40 years | ≥ 10 | 130 | 1 | 2 | 2 mL |
| Pregnant or lactating women | ≥ 5 | 130 | 1 | 2 | 2 mL |
| Adolescents, over 12 through 18 years* | ≥ 5 | 65 | ½ | 1 | 1 mL |
| Children over 3 through 12 years | ≥ 5 | 65 | ½ | 1 | 1 mL |
| Over 1 month through 3 years | ≥ 5 | 32 | Use KI oral solution** | ½ | 0.5 mL |
| Birth through 1 month | ≥ 5 | 16 | Use KI oral solution** | Use KI oral solution** | 0.25 mL |

\* Adolescents approaching adult size (≥ 150 lb / 70 kg) should receive the full adult dose
(130 mg).
\*\* See FDA's home preparation procedure (below). Commercial KI oral solution comes with a
dropper marked for 1, 0.5 and 0.25 mL; each mL contains 65 mg of KI.

**The rules FDA attaches to the table:**
- **One dose a day**, only while officials say the exposure risk continues. More often is not
  more protection.
- **Pregnant women and newborns: FDA does not recommend repeat dosing** unless other protective
  measures are not available. FDA also says pregnant women and newborns should get priority for
  evacuation and sheltering.{{#if has.pregnant}} **Someone in your household is pregnant.**{{/if}}{{#if has.infant}} **There is a baby in your household**: check whether they are in the birth-to-1-month row.{{/if}}{{#if has.nursing}} **Someone in your household is breastfeeding**: they are in the "pregnant or lactating" row; ask a clinician now about repeat doses and write the answer on → 25-09.{{/if}}
- **Adults over 40:** FDA advises KI for them only at a predicted exposure high enough to destroy
  the thyroid (the ≥ 500 cGy row), because their risk of thyroid cancer from radioactive iodine is
  lowest and their risk of side effects is highest. Whether your household follows that is a
  decision card: {{choice DC-22}} → 30-23{{#if has.adult_over_40}} **Your household has at least one adult aged 40 or over.**{{/if}}
- **A dose given wrongly is not corrected with another dose.** Write down who got what and when
  (→ 25-05). Do not re-dose early.

## FDA's home recipe for children, using one 130 mg tablet

FDA, *Potassium Iodide (KI) Preparation and Dosing Instructions for Use During a Nuclear
Emergency: To Make KI Solution (Liquid Form), using one 130 mg KI Tablet* (August 22, 2012). For
anyone who cannot swallow tablets. Printed as published.

**You will need:** one 130 mg KI tablet; a teaspoon; a small bowl; four teaspoons of water; four
teaspoons of a drink. FDA recommends any one of: white milk, chocolate milk, orange juice, soda
(for example, cola), infant formula, raspberry syrup, water.

```
Step 1. Soften the KI tablet:
        Put one 130 mg KI tablet into a small bowl. Add four teaspoons of
        water. Soak the tablet for one minute.
Step 2. Crush the softened KI tablet:
        Use the back of the teaspoon to crush the tablet in the water. At the
        end of this step, there should not be any large pieces of KI. This
        makes the KI and water mixture.
Step 3. Add a drink to the KI and water mixture:
        Choose a drink from the list above. Mix four teaspoons of the desired
        drink with the KI and water mixture made in Step 2. Adding the desired
        drink makes the final KI solution.
Step 4. Give the right amount of the final KI solution, using the chart
        below.
```

**How much of the final KI solution to give each day (FDA).** Give this amount once a day until
your healthcare provider or public health official says you may stop.

| Age | Once-daily dose of KI solution |
|---|---|
| 19 years and older | 8 teaspoons |
| 13 to 18 years (150 pounds or more) | 8 teaspoons |
| 13 to 18 years (149 pounds or less) | 4 teaspoons |
| 4 to 12 years | 4 teaspoons |
| Older than 1 month to 3 years | 2 teaspoons |
| Birth to 1 month | 1 teaspoon |

**Storing extra solution (FDA):** store any extra final KI solution in a refrigerator. It can be
used another day. After 7 days, throw out any unused solution.

Use **measuring** teaspoons, not table spoons from the drawer; keep a set in the bag with the KI
(→ 22-06). Do not cut tablets into small pieces by eye for a young child: the solution is the
method FDA gives.

## If officials do direct it

1. **Listen to the actual instruction**: it will say who should take it and when (→ 15-01).
2. **Follow the product label and your clinician's advice.** The tables above are FDA's own,
   printed for when the label is not in hand, not a replacement for either.
3. **Do not take extra**, and not more often than directed (FDA).
4. **Keep sheltering.** KI adds to sheltering; it never replaces it (→ 27-05).
5. **Write down what was given, to whom, and when** (→ 25-05).

## Storage, if you keep it

- Put the **expiry date on → 26-08** like any other medicine.
- Store it **with the medical kit** (→ 22-06), with this page printed or the binder to hand, and
  the measuring spoons.
- **It is not something you grab in the first hour.** Sheltering is (→ 27-04).

## What you need

Honestly, **this is optional and ranks below almost everything on → 26-09.** If you want it: an
in-date supply suited to your household, chosen with your clinicians, stored with the medical
kit, with the children's guidance attached and the expiry tracked (→ 26-08).

**No substitute, and do not improvise one.** Iodised salt, iodine tincture and povidone-iodine
antiseptic are **not** substitutes, and some are poisonous if swallowed. **If you have no KI and
officials recommend it, shelter and follow their instructions**, which is the bigger protection
anyway.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** it has an expiry date; track it (→ 26-08).
- **Ration:** **never take more than directed**: more is not better and can be fatal (FDA).
- **Resupply:** a pharmacy now; in a real emergency, public health distribution.
- **Substitute:** none. But note what that means: unlike a daily prescription, **not having KI is
  not life-threatening on its own.** Sheltering carries the protection.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** the only phase where it applies, and only on official direction.
- **B, moving:** it does not change the rule that you shelter rather than travel (→ 27-04).
- **C, establishing:** the same rules apply anywhere; what changes is how you hear the
  instruction (→ 15-01).
