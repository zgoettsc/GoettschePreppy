# Sewing and mending gear

**What this page is for.** Repairing and making anything of fabric: packs, tarps, tents, boots,
canvas, webbing, straps, cushions. Clothing in particular is on → 11-07. The planning point:
fabric is everywhere, in every house and abandoned building, but **thread and needles are tiny,
cheap and almost impossible to make well.** Stock them in amounts that feel absurd. They are what
turn abundant material into usable goods.

> **Sewing needles and thread are not surgical tools.** Nothing here is about closing wounds. That
> is → 12-14, and most wounds in a long emergency should not be closed at all.

## Do this now

1. **Buy a real sewing kit, then far more thread and needles than it holds.** Include heavy-duty
   and upholstery thread, leather or canvas needles, and curved needles.
2. **Learn four stitches this month:** running stitch, backstitch, whipstitch and saddle stitch.
   That is the whole practical set.
3. **Buy a stitching awl and waxed thread** for boots, packs, belts and canvas (→ 11-06). An awl is
   a sharp spike on a handle for punching holes in thick material; a stitching awl also carries
   thread.
4. **Start a parts bin.** Every worn-out garment or bag gets stripped of zippers, buttons, snaps,
   elastic, webbing, buckles and usable fabric before it is thrown away.

## The four stitches

{{figure fig-four-stitches}}

**1. Running stitch.** Needle in and out along a line, evenly spaced. Fast and weak. *Use:*
temporary tacking, gathering fabric.

**2. Backstitch.** Each new stitch goes backward to meet the end of the previous one, so the thread
is continuous on the underside. **Strong.** *Use:* any seam that takes load. Make this your
default. Two passes are stronger still.

**3. Whipstitch.** The thread loops over the edge again and again. *Use:* joining two edges,
binding a raw edge so it does not fray, closing a hole, fixing the edge of a patch.

**4. Saddle stitch.** **Two needles on one thread, passing through the same hole from both
sides.** *Use:* leather, heavy canvas, webbing, boots, packs. It is the strongest hand stitch, and
unlike a machine stitch it **does not unravel when one stitch breaks.** Punch the holes first with
an awl. This is the stitch that makes heavy gear repairable.

1. Punch a line of holes with the awl, evenly spaced.
2. Thread a needle onto each end of one length of waxed thread.
3. Pass one needle through the first hole and pull until the thread is centered.
4. At each next hole, push the left needle through from the left, then the right needle through
   the same hole from the right. Pull both tight.
5. At the end, go back two or three holes the same way to lock it, and trim.

**Plus, for clothing:** darning and patching (→ 11-07).

## Techniques worth knowing

- **Backstitch anything under load.** Running stitch is only for tacking.
- **Round the corners of patches.** Square corners peel up.
- **Patch from the inside** for a flat repair; **from the outside** where it needs to resist
  rubbing.
- **Reinforce before it fails.** Add a second layer at knees, elbows, seat, pack-strap anchors and
  the bottom corners of bags while they are new.
- **Bar-tack the stress points.** A bar-tack is a short, dense block of stitches over the same spot,
  where a strap meets a bag, at a pocket corner, at the end of a zipper. This is where gear fails.
- **Wax your thread** for heavy work. Run it across a block of beeswax: it gets stronger, sheds
  water and stops tangling.
- **Match the thread to the fabric.** If the thread is much stronger than the fabric, the fabric
  tears instead of the thread breaking, which is the worse failure.
- **Seal seams on waterproof gear.** Every needle hole is a leak. Use seam sealer, wax or silicone
  after stitching (→ 11-03).

## Repairing gear

{{figure fig-box-stitch-webbing}}

| Item | How |
|---|---|
| Pack strap torn out | Sew a backing patch inside, then bar-tack heavily. The classic failure |
| Tarp or tent tear | Patch both sides, round the corners, seal the seam. Nylon repair tape for a fast field fix |
| Boot seam or sole | Awl, waxed thread, saddle stitch; glue for soles (→ 11-06, → 16-05) |
| Zipper | Squeeze a loose slider gently with pliers; replace a lost pull with a loop of cord; bar-tack a broken end stop. Spare zippers are gold |
| Webbing or strap | Overlap the ends and sew a box with an X inside it ("box stitch"), the standard strong joint |
| Canvas | Patch and saddle-stitch; wax the seam |
| Leather | Awl, waxed thread, saddle stitch, then rub in leather conditioner |
| Cushions and upholstery | Curved needle; this is what they are for |
| Window screens and netting | Patch with fine mesh. Keeps insects out, which matters for disease |

## Making, not just mending

Once you can sew, the material in every house becomes goods:

{{#if has.child}}- **Cutting adult clothes down for the children** (→ 11-02). This is often the
  most valuable use, because children outgrow clothes and nothing stops that.
{{else}}- **Cutting adult clothes down for children** in your group (→ 11-02). Children outgrow
  clothes and nothing stops that.
{{/if}}- **Wool blankets into mittens, boot liners, vests and ponchos** (→ 11-05).
- **Tarps into shelters, gaiters, over-mittens, aprons and bags.**
- **Bags and packs** from canvas and webbing.
- **Cloth diapers and menstrual pads** from flannel (→ 13-08).
- **Reusable dressings** from clean cotton (→ 12-02).
- **Insulated curtains** for windows (→ 08-03), a large heat saving.

## Machines

- **An electric sewing machine multiplies output** while there is power. It is a modest load.
- **A hand-crank or treadle (foot-pedal) machine needs no power at all.** If you see one at an
  estate sale, buy it. They were built to last a century and can be repaired.
- **Learn hand sewing anyway.** The machine is a bonus, not the plan.

## Teaching it

- **Every adult should be able to sew**, so the skill does not live in one person.
{{#if has.young_child}}- **Young children can start** with a blunt plastic needle, yarn and
  burlap. It builds dexterity and is a calm, indoor, screen-free activity in a long winter
  (→ 20-07, → 21-02).
{{else}}- **Children can start** with a blunt plastic needle, yarn and burlap. It builds dexterity
  and is a calm, indoor activity in a long winter (→ 20-07, → 21-02).
{{/if}}- **Sewing is one of the most tradeable skills there is** (→ 18-03). Everyone's gear fails;
  few can fix it.

**Substitutes:**
- **Fabric:** curtains, sheets, upholstery, tarps, tents, clothing, seat covers. Abundant
  (→ 22-02, → 11-05).
- **Thread:** paracord inner strands (excellent, → 16-03), dental floss (very strong), threads
  pulled from fabric edges, fishing line (strong, but it can cut fabric).
- **Needles:** hard to improvise. A sharpened wire with a filed eye works badly. Stock real ones.
- **Awl:** a sharpened nail, an ice pick, a punch.
- **Buttons:** cut from other clothes, or carved from wood, bone or antler.
- **Webbing and buckles:** seat belts from vehicles (very strong), backpack straps, luggage.
- **Wax:** beeswax, candle wax, paraffin.

## When it runs out

- **Thread and needles run out before fabric does.** Stock both in amounts that feel excessive.
  They weigh nothing and never expire.
- **Think in repairs, not spools.** A spool goes a long way on clothing and a short way on heavy
  canvas with doubled thread. Add them to → 23-02.
- **Make it last:** light thread for light work, heavy-duty only where load is carried. **Repair
  early**: a small tear uses a fraction of the thread of a big one.
- **Resupply:** unpicking seams from unwanted clothes yields usable thread, and every discarded
  item yields fasteners. The parts bin is a real supply line.
- **Substitute:** paracord strands, floss, cloth strips, wire (→ 16-07) for some joins, glue
  (→ 16-05) for some repairs. Nothing substitutes for a needle.
- **The long view:** if this lasts years, the chain continues into spinning and weaving, and fiber
  animals (→ 11-05). It starts with a backstitch, which starts with one evening now.

## By phase

- **A, at home:** build the kit, learn the four stitches, start the rag bag and parts bin, and
  reinforce gear before it fails.
- **B, on the move:** a small repair kit: needles, strong thread, safety pins, nylon repair tape, a
  spare zipper pull and a small awl. A blown pack strap or boot seam in winter is a real emergency
  (→ 11-06), and a ten-minute repair prevents it.
- **C, settled:** repair and remaking become routine work. A treadle machine changes everything.
  Sewing is one of the easiest things to trade (→ 18-03).
