# Summer heat and sun protection

**What this page is for.** How to dress, work, drink and cool a house when summer heat arrives and
there is no air conditioning: the heat-illness warning signs, clothing that keeps you cooler, running a
house cool without power, and protecting children.

> **CAUTION.** **Heat kills too.** A summer outage means no air conditioning in a house built to be
> air-conditioned. **Heat stroke is a medical emergency:** high body temperature, hot skin (dry or
> damp), confusion, collapse (CDC/NIOSH, Heat-Related Illnesses). Small children overheat faster than
> adults and a baby cannot tell you. **Never leave a child in a parked vehicle,** for any length of
> time (NWS, Heat Safety). Treatment for heat illness is on → 17-04 and → 12-05.

{{#if is.hot_summers}}**Your home climate:** July average high about {{ region.home.jul_high_f }} °F,
record high {{ region.home.record_high_f }} °F.{{else}}Even where summers are mild, a heat wave without
power is dangerous; this page applies when it comes.{{/if}}

## Do this now

1. **Wide-brim hats and light long-sleeved sun shirts for everyone,** children included. Covering up
   beats sunscreen over a long event: **sunscreen runs out, cloth does not.**
2. **Stock sunscreen anyway,** check its dates, and plan as if it will run out.
3. **Find the coolest part of the house** (a basement, the north side, the ground floor) and make it the
   hot-day room, the summer version of the winter warm room (→ 08-01).
4. **Work out the summer water figure** (→ 03-01). Heat and hard work raise water needs a great deal
   (CDC, How to Create and Store an Emergency Water Supply).

## Dressing for heat

The instinct is to wear less. **In strong sun, the better answer is to wear more of the right thing:**
loose, light-coloured, breathable fabric over the skin (CDC, About Heat and Your Health).

- **Loose and light-coloured.** Air moving between fabric and skin cools you; tight clothes block
  evaporation.
- **Cover up in direct sun.** A light long-sleeved shirt is cooler in sun than a bare arm, because it
  blocks the sun's heat and prevents sunburn, and **sunburned skin cools itself badly** (CDC).
- **A wide brim, not a ball cap.** Ears and the back of the neck are where people burn.
- **Cotton and linen are good in heat.** This is the one place cotton is right, the opposite of winter
  (→ 11-05): the water it holds cools you as it evaporates.
- **Light merino works in heat too,** and resists odour when washing is expensive.
- **Wet a shirt or bandana** and wear it. Evaporation cools for free, best in dry heat.

## The heat-illness ladder

Know the stages; the signs and first aid are from CDC/NIOSH (Heat-Related Illnesses) and repeated on
→ 17-04:

- **Heat cramps:** muscle cramps, heavy sweating. Rest, cool down, drink.
- **Heat exhaustion:** heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, headache, cool clammy skin. **Stop,
  move somewhere cool, loosen clothing, cool the body, sip fluids.** This is the warning stage.
- **Heat stroke: a medical emergency.** High body temperature, hot skin (dry **or** damp), confusion,
  slurred speech, loss of consciousness. **Cool the person fast and get help.** Confusion in a hot
  person is the sign that matters most.

**Who is at greater risk:** young children, older adults, people working in the sun, and people with
chronic illness (CDC, About Heat and Your Health).{{#if has.young_child}} Your household has a young
child.{{/if}}{{#if has.elder}} Your household has an older member.{{/if}} **Heat and diabetes affect
each other:** high blood sugar increases urination and dehydration, and dehydration affects blood sugar;
plan fluids and checks and follow your clinician's sick-day guidance.{{#if has.condition.CL-01}} Your
household has type 1 diabetes: → 02-01.{{/if}}

## Cooling a house with no air conditioning

The winter warm room run backwards:

- **Closed by day, open by night.** Windows and heavy curtains closed against the sun in the day; **open
  everything once it is cooler outside than inside,** usually late evening. Cross-ventilate overnight,
  close up again at dawn. **This one habit is most of what you have.**
- **Block the sun before it reaches the glass.** Outside shade (awnings, tarps, trees, plywood over
  south and west windows) works far better than inside curtains.
- **Live low.** Heat rises; the basement or ground floor is cooler.{{#if has.basement}} Your basement is
  the summer room.{{/if}}
- **A fan is worth far more than its watts** (→ 09-01). At night a fan in a window pulling cool air in
  transforms a room.
- **Do not cook indoors.** Cook outdoors (→ 07-01, → 07-02, → 07-03).
- **Wet towels and damp sheets** in a breezy window cool the incoming air, especially in dry heat.
{{#if has.pool}}- **Your pool** ({{ dwelling.pool_gallons }} gallons) is a real cooling asset for people
  and as a cool mass of water. Drinking it is a different and much harder problem (→ 03-03).
{{/if}}
## Work, rest and water

- **Move the workday:** heavy work early morning and evening; rest through the hottest hours.
- **Work-rest cycles and fluids:** take them from the NIOSH guidance (CDC/NIOSH, Heat-Related
  Illnesses). In real heat the rest is longer than people want.
- **Water is the limit** (→ 03-01). Heat plus hard work can multiply water needs; reconcile that with
  storage, purification (→ 03-04) and the garden.
- **Salt matters** when sweating heavily for days. Large amounts of plain water without salt replacement
  cause their own problems. Keep salt available (→ 04-13).
- **Urine colour** is a free rough check of hydration: dark means drink more.

## Sun protection that does not run out

- **Cloth is the sunscreen that never expires:** long sleeves, brimmed hats, a neck cover.
- **Sunglasses:** real eye protection, with no eye doctor available.
- **Shade structures:** tarps, awnings, shade cloth over work and play areas. Build once; use every
  summer.
- **Sunburn is an injury:** it impairs cooling, hurts, blisters and can get infected (→ 12-03).

## Children in summer

- **Babies and toddlers cannot cool themselves well or tell you.** Watch for hot skin, flushing, unusual
  fussiness or sleepiness, and **fewer wet nappies** (a hydration sign).
- **Hats and sun shirts on every child** outdoors.
- **Never leave a child in a parked vehicle,** in any weather that is even mildly warm. Inside
  temperatures rise fast and kill (NWS, Heat Safety).
- **Water play cools and lifts morale, under the water-watcher and barrier rules on → 20-02,** which do
  not relax in a heat wave: an exhausted household and an inviting pool or tub is how drownings happen.
- **Shade the play area.**

## Medicines in the heat

Heat damages some medicines, especially insulin and others kept in the fridge (→ 29-07). Evaporative
cooling pouches help (→ 12-21). Using a medicine after a heat excursion is a decision card:
{{choice DC-08}} (→ 30-09).{{#if has.refrigerated_meds}} Your household has refrigerated medicines:
plan their cooling with your summer power budget (→ 09-10).{{/if}}

**No gear?** Any light long-sleeved shirt; a bandana under a cap to cover the neck; a brim from cardboard
and tape. Shade from tarps, sheets, plywood, cardboard on sunny windows. Cooling from wet cloth on the
neck and wrists, a damp sheet in a breezy window, a basin of water for the feet. Netting, tulle or
cheesecloth stapled over window openings is worth doing, because open windows at night are the core of
summer cooling and insects come in through open windows (→ 03-08).

## When it runs out

- **Sunscreen runs out; cloth and shade do not.** Plan around covering up and shade from the start.
- **Water is the real summer shortage** (→ 03-01, → 23-02). Work out the summer number, not the average.
- **Ration by shifting the work, not the water.** Rest in the heat, work in the cool hours, accept a
  slower pace.
- **Substitute:** shade for sunscreen; night ventilation for air conditioning; the lowest floor for the
  whole house; outdoor cooking for indoor.
- **Refrigerated medicines need the most cooling in summer,** when solar output is also highest. Plan the
  two together (→ 09-10).

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** the coolest room by day, night ventilation, outdoor cooking, a shaded play area.
- **B (moving):** summer travel is more survivable than winter travel, but it is a **water** problem,
  and water is heavy. Move early and late, rest in shade at midday, cover up, carry more water than
  feels necessary (→ 03-01).
- **C (established somewhere else):** less shade, more sun, more outdoor work. Build shade early and plan
  the day around the heat.
