# Children's sizing ledger and next-size pipeline

**What this page is for.** Children's clothing is the one supply that runs out whatever you do: they
grow. This page is the household's tool for staying ahead: measure each child, record what fits, list
the next sizes you own and where they are stored, and check it every season. The reasons and the
clothing rules are on → 11-02. **Everything below is filled in by the household, in pencil: it
changes.**

> **CAUTION.** Children usually move up about one clothing size a year, and **babies and toddlers about
> every six months until about age 3**, then yearly (CDC growth charts show how fast children grow at
> each age). In a long emergency there is no store for the next size. **Own the next two sizes for
> older children and the next three for toddlers,** or buy for a toddler twice a year.

## Do this now

1. **Measure each child and fill in the current-size table.** Height, weight and what actually fits
   today, not the label on the coat they are wearing.
2. **Walk the closets and bins and fill in the next-size grid.** You may own more of the next size than
   you think (hand-me-downs, gifts, end-of-season buys).
3. **Circle every empty box in the next-size column** and put those items on the next thrift-store
   trip. Next-size gaps are urgent; the size after is this year's project.
4. **Add "re-measure the children" to the seasonal calendar** (→ 24-03), and "buy the toddler's next
   size" to spring and autumn. Growth does not announce itself.

## How to buy

- **Thrift stores and end-of-season sales are the whole strategy.** Buy next winter's size in late
  winter or spring, not in November. Children's winter gear is nearly free in spring and unobtainable
  in a crisis.
- **Buy by the grid, not on impulse:** fill the empty boxes first. A third fleece in the current size is
  worth less than one shell two sizes up.
- **Counts follow the count sheet on → 11-01** (sock and mitten counts apply to stored sizes too). The
  household inventory (→ 22-04) audits this page, and growth is a standing line on → 23-02.
- **Outgrown gear is trade goods** (→ 18-02): every family with children needs the next size.

## Hand-me-downs: inspect before counting

Clothing an older child outgrows can wait in a labelled bin for a younger one, which halves the buying.
**But children wear gear out:** toes worn through, knees gone, zippers dead, elastic perished by the time
it is needed. **Inspect before ticking a hand-me-down as "in hand".** Repair what can be repaired now
(→ 11-07), and treat worn-out items as raw material, not stock. A worn-out boot discovered in a crisis is
worse than a known gap.

## When it runs out

- **This ledger is the plan** for the fastest-depleting supply in the house. A full grid buys about two
  years for an older child at N+2, and about one and a half years for a toddler at N+3.
- **Beyond the grid:** cut down adult clothing (→ 11-07, → 16-04), trade outgrown gear (→ 18-02), and
  keep measuring.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** measure every season, fill gaps at thrift prices, label bins by size so anyone can
  find them.
- **B (moving):** the current-size bags and the next-size bin travel with you (→ 22-05). Bins already at
  a destination mean less to carry.
- **C (established somewhere else):** the ledger becomes the trade sheet: what your children need next
  and what they have outgrown is what other households want to swap (→ 18-02).
