# Keeping babies and children warm

**What this page is for.** Why small children get cold faster than adults and show it later, how to
dress them, the car-seat rule, warning signs by age, how to warm a cold child, and how a small child
sleeps safely in a cold house.

> **SAFETY.** **Small children get cold much faster than adults and are much worse at telling you.**
> They have a lot of skin for their size, shiver less (a baby may not shiver at all), and a carried
> or seated child makes almost no heat while the adult carrying them sweats. **A quiet, sleepy,
> unusually still child in the cold may be hypothermic** (CDC, Preventing Hypothermia; → 08-05).
> Check the child **by touch**, not by asking. **Never leave a baby or toddler unattended near any
> heat source:** a stove, a fireplace, a fuel heater or a hot-water bottle burns a small child badly
> and fast (→ 20-02).

## Do this now

1. **Get the next sizes now.** Children outgrow winter gear every year, and toddlers change size
   about every six months until about age 3 (CDC growth charts). In a long outage there is no store.
   {{#if has.toddler}}You have a toddler: plan **three sizes ahead** for them.{{/if}} For older
   children, two sizes ahead. The ledger that tracks this is → 11-09.
2. **Wool or synthetic base layers, not cotton,** for every child.
3. **Start the touch-check habit:** feel the **back of the neck and the belly**, not the hands (small
   hands run cold normally). Every 10 to 15 minutes outdoors, every few minutes when a child is
   carried or pulled on a sled, and every hour in a cold house.
4. **Teach children old enough to talk to report:** "Tell a grown-up if any part of you hurts,
   tingles, or stops feeling" (→ 20-04). Babies cannot, so they are checked, always.

## Why children are different

- **Skin area for their size.** A small body loses heat much faster for the heat it can make; the
  younger the child, the worse.
- **Poor shivering.** Babies barely shiver.
- **Passive.** A carried, sledded, stroller-bound or car-seated child makes almost no heat while the
  adults around them work hard and feel warm. **The adult is a bad thermometer for the child.**
- **They don't report.** They get quiet, clingy, sleepy or fussy, which looks like ordinary child
  behaviour.
- **They get wet:** snow down the neck, wet mittens, a soaked nappy, drool, puddles. **Wet is the main
  route to cold injury in children** (→ 11-03).
- **Thinner skin:** frostbite comes faster on cheeks, ears and fingers. The NWS wind chill chart was
  built for adults; be more cautious with children (NWS, Wind Chill Chart; → 11-01).

## Dressing children

The same three layers as → 11-01, with these differences:

- **One-piece garments win.** A bunting or one-piece snowsuit for a baby or toddler and **bib
  overalls** for an older child close the waist gap where snow gets in and warmth gets out.
- **One more layer than an adult** in the same conditions, because the child is not working.
- **Mittens, always, on a string through the sleeves.** Two spare pairs at least; they will be wet.
- **Hats that cover the ears and stay on:** a balaclava or a chin-strap hat for a toddler.
- **Boots sized for thick wool socks,** not tight (→ 11-06).
- **Neck gaiter, not a scarf.** A loose scarf on a small child is a strangling hazard on play
  equipment, sleds and machinery.

## Car seats and puffy coats

**No puffy coat under the harness.** A bulky coat squashes in a crash and leaves the harness loose
enough for the child to be thrown out of it (AAP, Winter Car Seat Safety Tips; NHTSA, Car Seats and
Booster Seats).

1. **Dress the child in thin, warm layers:** base layer plus a fleece.
2. **Buckle the harness snug over the thin layers.** Pinch test at the shoulder: if you can pinch a
   fold of webbing, tighten it.
3. **Put the warmth over the buckled harness:** a blanket tucked around, a coat put on backwards over
   the arms, or a car-seat poncho.

**In a cold car at night, the layer over the harness must be one an adult can take off from the front
seat** (a blanket or poncho, not a zipped suit): once the car warms, a bundled child overheats and
cannot tell you (AAP). Also follow the car seat maker's instructions.

## Warning signs by age

**Older children:** unusual quiet, stumbling, clumsy hands, slurred or slow speech, complaining and
then **stopping** complaining, pale or waxy skin on cheeks, ears or fingers, "my fingers don't feel
like anything".

**Babies and toddlers:** unusually sleepy or hard to wake, unusually quiet or floppy, **bright red or
pale, cold skin**, weak cry, cold belly and back of neck, not wanting to feed (CDC, Preventing
Hypothermia).

**Any of these: get warm now, and → 08-05.** In a baby, **sleepy and hard to wake in the cold is an
emergency,** not a nap.

## Warming a cold child, gently

- **Inside and out of every wet layer at once.**
- **Dry, warm layers;** cover the head.
- **Skin-to-skin under a blanket** with an adult: one of the best ways to rewarm a small child.
- **Warm drinks** for a child who is fully alert and swallowing normally.
- **No direct heat on cold skin:** not a stove, heater, hot water or heating pad. Cold, numb skin burns
  without feeling it (CDC, Preventing Frostbite).
- **No rubbing frostbitten skin.**
- **Chemical hand warmers get hot enough to burn,** and a small child cannot take one out or say it
  hurts. Keep them **outside** clothing, in an adult's or an older child's pocket, never inside a
  small child's mittens or boots, never on numb skin. The packets are also a choking and chewing
  hazard: keep them out of reach.
- **Warm the room, not the child directly** (→ 08-01), and add body heat.

## Living in a cold house with children

- **Children live in the warm room** (→ 08-01), not in their own cold bedrooms.
- **Base layers, wool socks and hats indoors.** Normal in a cold house.
- **Off the cold floor.** Children sit and play on the floor, the coldest surface in the room. A foam
  pad, rug or blanket layer under them matters more than most people expect.
- **Older children's sleep:** dry base layers changed before bed, wool socks, a hat, insulation under
  as well as over.

## Cold-room sleep for babies and toddlers

The American Academy of Pediatrics publishes **no numeric minimum room temperature** for babies. Its
guidance: dress the baby for the room, keep loose bedding out of a baby's sleep space, and avoid
overheating (AAP, How to Keep Your Sleeping Baby Safe). CDC lists **babies sleeping in cold bedrooms**
among those most at risk of hypothermia (CDC). In a cold house:

- **Warmth is worn, not piled on.** A snug base layer, a fleece footed sleeper over it, and **one
  wearable sleep sack** rated for the room; a thicker (higher-TOG) sack is sold for cold rooms. Follow
  the room-temperature chart on its label. **Do not stack two sacks:** the makers say not to, and
  overheating is its own risk. A sack cannot be kicked off at 3 a.m.; a blanket can.
- **The overheating check:** feel the **neck and chest.** Sweaty or hot means take a layer off. Cool
  hands and feet are normal.
- **The baby sleeps in the warm room**, next to the adults (→ 08-01).
- **Bed-sharing for warmth is a decision card.** The AAP says babies under 1 year sleep in the
  parents' room but on their **own firm, flat surface, never in an adult bed**. The card offers
  bed-sharing on cold nights only for children over 12 months, with its own rules:
  {{choice DC-09}} (→ 30-10).
  {{#if has.infant}}Your children under 2: {{#each infants as c}}{{c.label}} ({{c.age_text}}); {{/each}}check each one's age against the card.{{/if}}
- **Hot-water bottles and fire-warmed stones pre-warm the bed and come out before a child gets in.**
  Sleeping or numb skin burns without waking, and a small child cannot move away (→ 21-07). **Never an
  electric or heated blanket on or around a baby or toddler** (→ 08-04).
- **If a heater runs at night,** it runs only under the household's answer on the overnight-heater
  card: {{choice DC-01}} (→ 30-02).
- **Feed them often.** Staying warm burns calories, and small children have small reserves.
  {{#if has.nursing}}A nursing parent needs extra food and water in the cold house too (→ 20-05,
  → 03-01).{{/if}}{{#if has.condition.CL-04}} For a child with a food allergy, every one of those
  calories comes from safe food (→ 02-04).{{/if}}
- **Watch hydration.** Children do not feel thirsty in the cold, and dehydration makes them colder
  (→ 03-01).

## Nappies and cold

A wet nappy against skin in a cold house drains heat continuously and damages skin. **Change often and
fast, in the warm room** (→ 20-06). This competes directly with your water and washing budget.

**No gear?** Thrift stores and hand-me-downs are the whole strategy: start now. **An adult wool sweater
cut down and resewn** makes several toddler garments (→ 11-07). Wool blankets become ponchos, buntings
and wraps. Adult socks become toddler leg warmers. A down comforter section sewn into a bunting is
light and very warm. A trash bag with arm holes over a snowsuit for short supervised periods only:
**never for sleep, never over the head.** **Body heat** is the best resource you have: a child
carried inside your coat is warmer than in any garment.

## When it runs out

- **Children's clothing runs out by growth, not wear,** and no care prevents it. Next sizes stockpiled
  now is the only real answer (→ 11-09, → 23-02).
- **Socks and mittens are the fast consumables.** Stock heavily; learn to darn (→ 11-07).
- **Resupply:** cut down adult clothing; trade with other households, one of the easiest trades there
  is, since every family has outgrown gear (→ 18-02).
- **Ration outdoor time** by the NWS chart's frostbite time for today's temperature and wind (→ 11-01),
  and **not at all** under an Extreme Cold Warning, never by how much the children want to go out.
- **The substitute that always works:** an adult's body heat.

## Phase notes

- **A (at home):** children live in the warm room, in layers, off the cold floor, checked by touch.
  Outdoor play is good for morale; keep it time-limited and follow it with a full dry-out.
- **B (moving):** **the most dangerous situation for small children.** Carried children freeze while
  carrying adults sweat. Check them every few minutes, keep them inside your layers where possible,
  stop and rewarm at the first sign, and **strongly prefer not to move in winter** (→ 01-02).
- **C (established somewhere else):** more outdoor work and more chance of a child out of sight in the
  cold. Set firm boundaries and check-ins (→ 20-02).
