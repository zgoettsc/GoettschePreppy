# Annual review checklist

**What this page is for.** A binder that is never reviewed becomes fiction. Numbers drift,
children grow, medicines change, and gear expires. **One afternoon a year** keeps every other
page true, and ends with a **rebuild of this binder** from your updated settings. Pick a fixed
date (a birthday, New Year, the first cold snap) and never move it.

## Do this now

1. **Put a recurring yearly date on the calendar** and block three hours. Ours: ____________.
2. **Do this year's review now if it has been more than a year.** The most common failure of
   any binder is that it was correct once.
3. **Do it with every adult present.** Half the value is the conversation; a plan only one
   adult knows has a single point of failure.

## The rhythm this binder runs on

| How often | What | Page |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly** | Press every smoke and CO alarm's test button | → 29-12 |
| **Twice a year** | Kit checks, expiry dates, children's sizes, inventory, a blackout weekend | → 22-03, → 22-04, → 24-04 |
| **Each season** | That season's skills, garden and wood work | → 24-03 |
| **Before each device's own deadline** | Anything a medical device or app needs renewed (a sensor, an app, a battery), written on that condition's page | Section 02 |
| **Yearly (this page)** | Everything else: plans, contacts, budget, the honest assessment, and a rebuild | |

The annual review **does not repeat** the twice-a-year checks; it assumes they happened and
looks at what nobody else looks at.

## What goes stale, worst first

1. **Medical**: prescriptions, devices, weights of growing children, expiry dates, prescribers.
2. **The children**: sizes, abilities, needs, what they can be taught, what frightens them.
3. **Contacts**: people move, numbers change, offices reorganise (→ 25-03).
4. **Numbers**: water, calories, days of supply, all of which change as children grow.
5. **Gear**: expiry, wear, batteries, things that broke and were never replaced.
6. **The plan itself**: destinations, routes, the triggers for leaving.

## How to run it well

- **Three hours, every adult, no screens** except the settings page at the end. Work in order.
- **Do sections 1 and 2 first**: if the afternoon collapses, those are the ones that mattered.
- **Argue about section 9.** It is the only section that is a judgement, not a check.
- **Write everything down.** Next year's review starts by reading this year's summary.
{{#if has.child}}- **Make the children part of it** where you can: a child repacking their own go-bag is doing
  the review and learning the plan at once (→ 20-04).
{{/if}}

**What you need:** this checklist printed, a pencil, a clipboard, a luggage scale for the
go-bags, fresh batteries for every alarm, a phone for record photos, and the year's replacement
consumables (bleach, batteries, expired medicines replaced through the pharmacy), so the review
ends with things fixed rather than a list of things to fix. **If you have nothing else**, the
review needs only time and honesty; but a checklist beats a habit, because what goes stale is
exactly what you stop noticing.

## When it goes stale

- **About twelve months** before medical, contacts and children's sizes are meaningfully wrong.
- **If you only have an hour:** sections 1, 2 and 4.
- **Between reviews,** the twice-a-year checks (→ 22-03, → 22-04) carry the load.
- **An unreviewed binder is a confident document full of stale numbers**, which is more
  dangerous than none.

## Phase notes

- **A (shelter in place):** the annual review is a luxury of normal times; do it while it is easy.
- **B (moving):** no review while moving; **what you reviewed last is what you have.**
- **C (establish):** review **every three months** for the first year at a new place, and
  change the home base in settings and rebuild: water, fuel, routes, neighbours, seasons and
  numbers are all different there.
