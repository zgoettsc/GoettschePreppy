# Maps: local, regional and routes

**What this page is for.** Which maps to own, what to mark on them, and how to find your way
when the phone is dead. A phone's GPS position comes from satellites and keeps working without
cell service, but **maps, routing and search need either the internet or maps already
downloaded**, and every screen needs a charged battery. **A paper map and a compass work in a
dead-battery storm.**

Either way, **buy paper maps**: a phone or a printout of one county does not replace a road atlas.

## Do this now

1. **Buy the paper maps in the list below this month.** They cost little and are the only
   navigation that never fails.
2. **Mark them up at the kitchen table**, not on the road. The marks are the value; a blank map
   is just paper.
3. **Drive the routes out of town yourself**, and walk the first miles of one. A route you have
   not travelled is a guess (→ 01-03).
4. **Draw your vehicle's range as a circle** on the regional map: full tank plus fuel cans
   (→ 22-05, → 25-02). That circle is your real radius when moving, and it is usually smaller
   than people assume.
5. **Put a map set in each vehicle and the household bag**, in a zip bag.

## How to mark them up

{{photo annotated-map}}

{{figure fig-map-symbols}}

**On the home street map, mark:**

- **Home and the meeting places**: near home ({{ contacts.meet.near_home }}), in town
  ({{ contacts.meet.in_town }}), out of town ({{ contacts.meet.out_of_town }}) (→ 00-04, → 15-03)
{{#if has.child}}- **Each school or daycare** and the route to it
{{/if}}- **Hospitals, urgent care and pharmacies** (→ 25-03)
- **Water**: rivers, creeks, ponds, springs (→ 03-07)
- **Hazards**: floodplain, industrial sites, fuel storage, bridges (bridges are choke points and
  fail first)
- **Three walking routes out**, in different directions
- Neighbours and friendly houses (→ 18-01)

**On the regional map, mark:**

- **Primary route out** (interstate or highway), **secondary** (state roads), **tertiary**
  (county and back roads)
- **Your vehicle range circle**
- **Family and contacts** across the region
- **River crossings**: every one is a choke point (→ 17-03)
{{#if has.destinations}}- **Each destination, with a symbol, not a name** (→ 14-03), and the route to it
{{/if}}- **Fuel, water and towns** along each route, with sturdy buildings for shelter (→ 17-11)
- **Distances in days on foot**, not just miles: at 5 to 8 miles a day with small children
  (→ 25-02), the map looks very different

**On the topo map, mark:**

- Water sources and whether they are above or below you
- High ground (flood safety, → 17-03) and low ground (cold air settles there, but it is out of
  the wind)
- Woods: fuel that town does not have (→ 06-04)
- Ridges and valleys that shape where you can travel

## The three-route rule

Always have **three ways out**, known well enough to drive at night:

| Route | What it is | When it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Interstate or highway: fastest, best surface | Closed, jammed or blocked: **the most likely failure in an evacuation** |
| **Secondary** | State and county highways | {{#if is.snow_region}}Drifted or unploughed, {{/if}}flooded, or closed at a bridge |
| **Tertiary** | Gravel and back roads | Mud{{#if is.snow_region}} or deep snow{{/if}}; slowest |

**Drive each one, in each season if you can.** Write the real drive time and fuel used on the
map. **Then walk the first few miles of one**: it is the version you will use if the vehicle
stops.

## Offline maps on phones: set them up now

**GPS satellites do not depend on the power grid.** A phone's GPS chip finds your position
without cell service or an account. **What disappears is the map download**, so the whole game
is having maps already on the device.

- **Install an offline map app that routes without internet** on every phone, and download the
  map data for home{{#if has.destinations}}, each destination and every state between{{/if}} while the internet works.
- **Offline map areas in some apps expire** and silently vanish unless refreshed: put the
  refresh on the calendar with the go-bag checks (→ 22-03).
- **An offline map knows roads, not conditions.** No closures, floods or missing bridges. The
  radio (→ 15-01) and your own eyes supply conditions. **Never let an app argue you into water
  (→ 17-03).** The marked paper route is the plan; the app is the position fix.
- **Navigation with the screen on drains a phone fast.** Charge from the vehicle (→ 09-11); never
  use the phone a medical device depends on for navigation (→ 09-06).
- **Paper stays the master.** Screens break, batteries die, and a passenger cannot spread a phone
  across their lap. **Print strip maps** of each route: one page per leg, with fuel, bridges and
  turns marked.

## Navigation without GPS: the minimum skills

- **Read a map and turn it to match the ground.** Practise on a walk (→ 24-01).
- **Use a compass:** take and follow a bearing. Learn **declination** for your area: the
  difference between true north (the map) and magnetic north (the compass), printed in the
  margin of USGS topo maps.
- **Estimate distance:** count paces, use time and speed, match landmarks (→ 29-13).
- **The sun and stars:** the sun rises roughly east and sets roughly west; **Polaris (the North
  Star) marks north** and is found from the Big Dipper.
- **Match what you see to the map:** river, road, water tower, ridge, grain elevator.

**Practise all of it on an ordinary weekend walk**, before it matters.

## OPSEC: what not to write on a map

A marked map tells a stranger where you live, where your children are and where you would go
(→ 14-03).

- **Do not label a destination "our place".** Use a symbol you know.
- **Do not mark caches, stores or firearms.**
- **Do not write names next to houses.**
- **Keep the heavily marked master at home**; carry a lighter copy.
- **Do not hand your marked map to a stranger.** Give directions instead.

## Storage

- **Zip bag or dry bag**: a wet map is a lost map.
- **A set in each vehicle and in the household bag**; the master at home.
- **A compass with each set.**
- **Pencil, not pen**: pen bleeds when wet and cannot be corrected.

## When it goes stale

- Roads change slowly; **bridges, closures and conditions change fast**, and no printed map shows
  them.
- **Make copies** rather than carrying the only original.
- **Update each year** (→ 25-10): re-drive the routes and note what changed.
- **Local knowledge beats any map**: someone who drives those roads every day knows what the map
  cannot show. Ask, then check against the ground.

## Phase notes

  neighbours and walking routes.
