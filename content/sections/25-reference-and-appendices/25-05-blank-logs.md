# Blank logs and worksheets

**What this page is for.** Forms to print (or copy into a notebook) for keeping track of a long
event and of ordinary preparation. Written records turn guesses into numbers (→ 23-02), let a
second person pick up where you left off, and are the proof an insurer or the tax authority asks
for later (IRS, Reconstructing Records After a Disaster; → 19-04). **Use pencil.** Pens freeze and
run.

## Do this now

1. **Print at least five copies of each log**, or set aside a notebook, with two pencils.
2. **Start the daily log the day anything unusual begins**: an outage, an illness, a storm. The
   first entry is always "we didn't think this would last."
3. **Start the water and fuel logs during your next blackout weekend** (→ 24-04): that is where
   the real use numbers come from.

## Why logs matter more than they seem

1. **They replace estimates with facts.** Everything on → 23-02 is arithmetic on numbers you
   either measured or guessed.
2. **They hand over cleanly.** If one adult is ill or away, another can read the log and know
   exactly where things stand.
3. **They catch slow problems.** A slow drop in stored water, a slowly worsening symptom, a
   shrinking woodpile: invisible day to day, obvious in a log.
4. **They are the record afterwards**, for insurance, tax and disaster assistance (→ 19-04).
5. **They anchor morale.** Writing the day down gives shape to time when the calendar has
   stopped meaning anything (→ 21-04).

**Rule for every log: date, time, who wrote it. Always.**

## Gear

**Buy toward:** a stack of printed forms or a notebook, several pencils and a sharpener, a
clipboard, a waterproof notebook for outdoor use, a wall calendar, and a waterproof folder for
finished logs. A phone or camera for photo records of the inventory and any damage (→ 19-04).

**If you do not have it:** any notebook, the backs of old pages, or a wall you write on. **The
form matters far less than the habit**: a dated line in a spiral notebook beats a beautiful
blank form nobody fills in.

## When it runs out

- Paper and pencil last essentially forever; **the habit is what runs out**, usually in the
  second week of an event.
- **If you can keep only one log, keep the daily situation log**: it holds a little of
  everything.
- Blank paper and pencils are worth stocking (→ 22-01).
- **Written beats remembered**, especially when tired, cold and handing over to another adult.

## Phase notes

- **A (shelter in place):** logs are for practice and inventory. Start them during the blackout
  weekend (→ 24-04).
- **B (moving):** the daily log becomes a travel log: where you stopped, what you used, what you
  saw, what the route was really like.
- **C (establish):** logs become the operating record of a small homestead: garden, wood,
  animals, weather, repairs. **Years of your own data make a place work.**
