# Phase B: movement — routes, load-out, winter travel

**What this page is for.** How to move the household from home to a destination and survive the
trip: what to load first, how to work out the fuel, how to plan routes, when to turn back, and how
to travel in winter{{#if has.child}} with children{{/if}}.

> **SAFETY — CRITICAL.** Moving a household through a disaster is one of the most dangerous
> things in this binder. The American Red Cross and the National Weather Service both advise
> against unnecessary travel in winter storms. **If you move, it is because staying is worse**
> (→ 01-02), and everything below is about surviving the trip.
> - **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near an open
>   window.** In a stuck vehicle, clear snow from the exhaust pipe before running the engine:
>   a blocked tailpipe fills the cabin with carbon monoxide (CDC, Carbon Monoxide Poisoning:
>   Basics).
> - **Fuel is never stored or transferred near an open flame or a running appliance.** Vapour
>   ignites.
> - **A frozen-then-thawed insulin vial or pen is discarded.** Medicines ride in the heated
>   cabin, never in the trunk, trailer or roof box in winter.
> - **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products**, in the
>   vehicle as at home.

## Do this now

1. **Never depart into a storm, and never at night**, unless the house is actively unsafe.
2. **Medicines ride in the cabin, with a person.** Not buried in a pack, not in the cargo area, not
   in a trailer. {{#if has.refrigerated_meds}}Refrigerated medicine rides in a powered cooler or a
   cooling pouch, **checked cold before you pull away** (→ 01-07).{{else}}Keep them out of hot sun
   and freezing cold (→ 01-07).{{/if}}
3. **If the vehicle stops in a storm, STAY WITH IT.** It is shelter and it is findable. People die
   walking away from stalled cars in blizzards (NWS, Winter Weather Safety).
4. **In winter, carry water inside your clothing** or it freezes.
5. **Go-bags packed and current** (→ 22-03) so leaving takes minutes, not hours.

## Why / how it works

### Vehicle or on foot: be honest

| | Vehicle | On foot |
|---|---|---|
| Range | Tens to hundreds of miles | A few miles a day{{#if has.young_child}} with small children{{/if}} |
| Load | Everything you can fit | What you can carry{{#if has.young_child}}, minus any child who must be carried{{/if}} |
| Winter | Shelter, and heat while fuel lasts | **Extremely dangerous** |
| Risk | Blocked roads, fuel, breakdown, other people | Exposure, exhaustion, the pace of the slowest person |

**On foot in winter is a last resort, not a plan.**{{#if has.young_child}} A small child cannot walk
far in deep cold, and a child who must be carried takes an adult's hands and body heat.{{/if}}{{#if has.mobility_aid}}
Anyone who uses a wheelchair or walker needs a vehicle plan and a backup vehicle plan (→ 02-22).{{/if}}
**A sled, wagon or cart is not a nice-to-have; it is the difference between possible and not.**

### The load-out order
Pack in this order, so that if you must leave sooner, the most important things are already in:

1. **People{{#if has.pets}} and pets{{/if}}.**
2. **Medicines**: every prescription, rescue medicines (epinephrine, glucagon, inhalers),
   testing supplies, devices and their chargers, the printed action plans and "my meds" tables
   (→ 01-07, → 25-09).
3. **Documents**: the grab kit (→ 19-01), plus cash (→ 19-02).
4. **Water**: as much as weight allows, plus a way to treat more (→ 03-04).
5. **Warmth**: sleeping bags, pads, dry spare clothes for everyone{{#if has.child}}, especially
   the children{{/if}}.
6. **Food**, including **food that is safe for anyone with an allergy or special diet** (→ 02-04).
   Food from strangers on the road is the highest-risk exposure an allergic person will face.
7. **Fire, light, tools**: ignition (→ 06-01), headlamps, knife, multi-tool.
8. **Shelter**: tent, tarps, cordage.
9. **Communications**: radio, chargers, power banks (→ 00-10, → 09-06), the family plan
   (→ 00-04).{{#if has.satellite}} The satellite messenger, charged and activated (→ 15-02).{{/if}}
{{#if has.pets}}10. **Pet carriers and the pets' kit** (→ 20-09).{{/if}}
11. Everything else, if it fits.

**If it does not fit, it does not go.** Do not overload the vehicle so that it cannot handle snow,
or so that people ride without seatbelts.

### Winter vehicle discipline
- **Keep the tank well filled, always.** Practise it now; you cannot refuel in an outage.
- **The winter vehicle kit lives in the car all winter** (→ 22-05): blankets, shovel, sand or
  cat litter for traction, jumper cables, flashlight, water, food, hats and mittens.
- **If you get stuck:** stay with the vehicle. Run the engine only in short bursts for heat,
  **make sure the exhaust pipe is clear of snow**, and crack a window while it runs (NWS, Winter
  Weather Safety; → 08-02).
- **Make yourself findable**: bright cloth on the antenna, dome light on while the engine runs.
- **Move around** in the vehicle to keep blood flowing; watch everyone for hypothermia (CDC,
  Preventing Hypothermia; → 08-05).
{{#if has.young_child}}- **Car seats: no bulky coat under the harness.** Buckle the child in normal clothes, then put the
  coat or a blanket over the harness (AAP, Winter Car Seat Safety Tips).{{/if}}

- **Label every can** with its fuel, and keep the standard colours (→ 29-01). Never let two fuels
  ride unlabelled in the same load.
- **Use approved fuel containers with their flame-arrestor screens, caps tight** (CPSC, Fuel
  Container Safety). Fuel moves outside the cabin: in a trailer, on a hitch carrier or a roof
  rack (→ 06-08). Carrying cans inside a passenger vehicle is a household choice with real fire
  and vapour risk. **Our choice:** {{choice DC-06}} (→ 30-07)
- Mark two small-town fuel stops on each route anyway, for the case where pumps work and cash is
  accepted (→ 19-02). Every gallon bought on the way is a can you did not need.

### The trailer: what it carries, how it loads, when to drop it

{{#if has.trailer}}You have a trailer. Read this before the trip, not during it.{{else}}You have
not entered a trailer. If you ever tow one, this is how.{{/if}}

{{figure fig-trailer-loading}}

- **What rides in the trailer:** fuel cans, bulk water, tools, bulk totes: the heavy and the
  replaceable. **Never people, pets, medicines or documents.** Everything irreplaceable or alive
  rides in the cabin.
- **Loading:** about **10 to 12 percent of the trailer's loaded weight on the tongue** (the part
  that sits on the hitch), achieved by loading **heavy items forward of the axle**, roughly 60
  percent ahead and 40 percent behind (NHTSA, Trailer Safety). A tail-heavy trailer sways at
  highway speed; a nose-heavy one overloads the hitch. Never exceed the towing and tongue limits
  in the vehicle's owner's manual. Strap everything: a load that shifts changes the balance
  mid-trip.
- **Towing in winter:** slow down well below the limit, more on snow; leave far more following
  distance; no sudden steering or braking. **Never tow into a blizzard warning on the route.**
  Practise backing it before it matters (→ 16-11).
- **The drop-it decision, written now:** **if the trailer becomes the reason you are stuck**
  (jackknifed, stuck in snow, a failed tyre with no fix, sway you cannot control) **unhitch it,
  take the first-layer load (people, medicines, documents, water, warmth) and go.** The trailer
  carries the replaceable layer precisely so this costs stuff, not people. Nobody stays cold in a
  ditch to guard totes.

### Point of no return: turn-back rules (decide by number, not by mood)

Write these on the trip card. Check them at every stop.

- **Fuel:** turn back if the fuel left is **less than the fuel to the destination plus our margin
  of ______ percent, and less than the fuel to get home.** Past that point, forward is the only
  option: say out loud when you cross it.
- **Daylight:** **no first-time arrivals after dark in winter.** If the arrival time slips past
  dusk at an unfamiliar place, stop short at a warm public place or turn back (→ 01-02).
- **Weather:** **if conditions are getting worse toward a warning anywhere on the route, the plan
  becomes the nearest shelter, not pressing on.** A destination reached tomorrow beats a ditch
  reached tonight.

**Rivers, not roads, are the real chokepoints.** Mark every bridge and river crossing on your
routes out, and one alternate crossing for each direction. In a flood (→ 17-03) they decide which
routes exist at all. **Highways are fast, and are also the first to jam and the first to close.**
Know at least one smaller road in each direction. **Carry paper maps** (→ 25-04): GPS is a
dependency, not a plan.

### Routes to each destination

**Three rules that govern every route:**

1. **Leave early, take the highways; leave late, take the smaller roads.** In the first days of a
   disruption, highways are fast, cleared and fuelled. Once fuel is scarce or order is fraying,
   cities and highway services become the chokepoints, and two-lane roads through small towns
   become the better choice. Decide by **when** you are leaving.
2. **Plan by river crossing.** Big-city bridges draw crowds; small-town bridges often do not.
3. **Winter reverses rule 1.** Smaller roads are cleared last. A winter departure takes the main
   highway or waits for the weather (→ 17-01).

### Travelling with the household
- **Warmth before speed.** Stop and warm people before they are cold, not after.
- **Layers you can adjust**, and dry spares within reach, not at the bottom of a bag (→ 11-02).
- **Food and water often.** Small children and elders dehydrate and burn through reserves fast.
{{#if has.anaphylaxis}}- **Anyone with a food allergy eats only food that is known to be safe for them.** Roadside, charity
  and shelter food is the highest-risk exposure of the trip. How strictly to hold that line when
  safe food runs out is a household choice. **Our choice:** {{choice DC-18}} (→ 30-19){{/if}}
- **Say what is happening, calmly and simply.** Frightened adults make frightened children and
  frightened elders (→ 21-01).
- **One adult is assigned to {{#if has.child}}the children{{else}}anyone who needs help{{/if}} at
  every stop**, so nobody assumes someone else has them.

### Security while moving
- **Look unremarkable.** A visibly loaded vehicle is a target (→ 14-03).
- **Avoid crowds and chokepoints.**
- **Travel in daylight**, and with others if you trust them (→ 18-01).
- **Have a meeting point and a communications plan** in case you are separated (→ 15-03).

**Scavenge / substitute:**
- A **child's plastic sled** is the best cheap winter cargo mover there is.
- Cardboard and blankets insulate a vehicle interior for a night (engine off).
- A tote with a secured lid and air holes serves as an emergency pet carrier.

## When it runs out

- **Runway:** fuel in the tank and daylight. Both are hard limits: plan each day's travel around
  them, not around distance.
- **Ration:** heat and food for children, elders and the sick first; healthy adults absorb the
  shortfall.
- **Resupply / substitute:** water is the resupply you can actually get on the way (→ 03-05,
  → 03-04); fuel is not. Plan stops around water and shelter.
- **Turning back is allowed.** If conditions get worse and home is still viable, going back is a
  legitimate decision, not a failure.
