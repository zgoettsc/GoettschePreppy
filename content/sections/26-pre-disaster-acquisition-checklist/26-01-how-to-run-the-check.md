# How to run the check

**What this page is for.** This section is the clipboard version of the binder. → 22-01 says
**what** to own and **why**; this section finds **what you already have** and **what to buy
next**. You walk the house once with a pencil and these sheets, mark what you find, and finish
with a ranked shopping list. **About two hours, two people, one afternoon.** No experience
needed: every item says what it is for and points to the page that explains it.

## Do this now

1. **Print (or open on a second device) checklists → 26-03 to → 26-07**, plus → 26-08 (expiry
   dates) and → 26-09 (the shopping list). Put the printouts on a clipboard.
2. **Block two hours and do it with another adult** if there is one. One reads items, the other
   looks. It goes twice as fast, and you both learn where things are.
3. **Start with → 26-03 (life safety and medical).** If the afternoon collapses, that is the
   one that mattered.
4. **Finish by filling in → 26-09.** A walk that produces no shopping list was just tidying.

## What this section is, and what it is not

| | This section (26) | The other two |
|---|---|---|
| **Question** | Do we have it? What do we buy? | → 22-01: what should we own, and why |
| **Form** | Tick-box sheets you carry | → 22-04: how many, to work out days of supply |
| **Result** | A ranked shopping list (→ 26-09) | → 23-02: the shortest supply in the house |
| **How often** | Once, then yearly | → 22-04 twice a year |

**Do not do all three at once.** Run this section first: you cannot count what you have not
found. → 22-04 comes later, when you want quantities rather than presence.

## The four marks

Use these four and nothing else.

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **✔** | **Have it, it works, and I know where it is.** All three, or it is not a ✔ |
| **~** | **Partly**: some but not enough, or it needs a spare, a battery or a repair |
| **✗** | **Do not have it** |
| **n/a** | Genuinely does not apply to us |

**The "~" is where the truth lives.** Most households are mostly "~": a filter with no spare
element, a canner with no lids, one headlamp for {{ household.size }} people. **A "~" is
usually cheaper to fix than a "✗" and matters just as much.**

## Four rules for an honest walk

1. **Touch it.** If you did not lay hands on it, it is not a ✔. Memory is optimistic, and things
   get borrowed, moved and used up.
2. **Test anything with a battery or a moving part.** Turn on every flashlight. Press every
   alarm's test button. A dead device is a ✗.
3. **Write the location in the same pass.** Half the value of this walk is that every adult ends
   up knowing where things are.
4. **Read every expiry date as you go** (how: → 29-14) and copy it to → 26-08.

## The order, and why

The checklists run in the order that things hurt people, not the order that is fun to shop for:

| # | Checklist | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| **→ 26-03** | **Life safety and medical** | Minutes to hours: CO alarms, rescue medicines, the trauma kit, every daily medicine{{#if has.refrigerated_meds}}. **This household has refrigerated medicine**{{/if}}{{#if has.anaphylaxis}}. **This household has anaphylaxis risk**{{/if}} |
| **→ 26-04** | **Water and food** | Days. Water is the first shortage in almost every emergency (CDC, How to Create and Store an Emergency Water Supply) |
| **→ 26-05** | **Heat, power and light** | Days. {{#if is.cold_winters}}In your winters, heat is a life-safety item too.{{else}}{{#if is.hot_summers}}In your summers, cooling is a life-safety item too.{{else}}A long power cut changes everything (American Red Cross, Power Outage Safety).{{/if}}{{/if}}{{#if has.cpap}} Power for medical devices is here.{{/if}}{{#if has.oxygen}} Power for oxygen is here.{{/if}} |
| **→ 26-06** | **Sanitation, shelter, tools, security, comms** | Weeks |
| **→ 26-07** | **Children, pets, records, morale** | Months, and the records cost almost nothing and cannot be replaced if lost (IRS, Reconstructing Records After a Disaster) |

**Food is not first.** Most people start there because it is the easy, satisfying purchase. It is
rarely what runs out first.

## Two hours, roughly

```
0:00   Print, clipboard, pencil, phone for photos.
0:05   26-03 LIFE SAFETY & MEDICAL: do this one properly
0:35   26-04 WATER & FOOD: kitchen, pantry, basement, garage
1:05   26-05 HEAT, POWER & LIGHT: garage, shed, junk drawers, vehicles
1:25   26-06 SANITATION, SHELTER, TOOLS, SECURITY, COMMS: garage and workshop
1:45   26-07 CHILDREN, PETS, RECORDS, MORALE: closets, files, bedrooms
1:55   26-08 copy every expiry date you found
2:00   26-09 fill in the shopping list. STOP. Do not shop tonight.
```

**Do not shop the same day.** Sleep on it, then buy in → 26-09's order, which is → 22-07's
order: what hurts people soonest, then what cannot be bought later.

## What to bring

- **A pencil** (marks change), a clipboard and the sheets.
- **A phone or camera**: photograph each room and open container as you go. That record is also
  your insurance and disaster-assistance evidence (IRS; → 19-04).
- **A tape measure and a kitchen or luggage scale**, if you want quantities as well.
- **A box** for everything expired, broken or the wrong size. Deal with it at the end rather
  than interrupting the walk.
- **A cheap battery tester**: it turns "probably fine" into ✔ or ✗ in two seconds.

## What people always find

Expect these, and do not be discouraged:

1. **Dead batteries** in half the flashlights.
2. **Expired medicine**, usually including something that matters.
3. **Clothing and boots that no longer fit**{{#if has.child}}, often a child's whole current size{{/if}}.
4. **One of something everyone needs.**
5. **A tool with no consumable**: a filter with no element, a saw with no file, a canner with no
   lids.
6. **Far more food than expected, and far less water.**

## After the walk

1. **Fill in → 26-09** and buy in that order.
2. **Then do → 22-04** when you want quantities; it feeds the days-of-supply math on → 23-02.
3. **Re-run this section yearly** (→ 25-10), and re-run → 26-08 twice a year: expiry dates move
   faster than anything else.
4. **Update settings** with anything you found (gear, medicines, fuel on hand) and rebuild the
   binder so the numbers on every page are yours (→ 29-15).

**If you have nothing to print with:** any notebook and pencil. The method matters, not the
stationery. **The one thing not to do is the walk from memory.**

## When it goes stale

- A completed walk is good for about a year, less with growing children.
- **Short on time?** Do → 26-03 alone: a fraction of the sheets and most of the risk.
- **A guessed inventory produces confident, wrong decisions**, which is worse than knowing you
  do not know.

## Phase notes

- **A (shelter in place):** this is a Phase A job by definition: it only works while shops are
  open.
- **B (moving):** irrelevant while moving. What you own is what you have.
- **C (establish):** re-run the whole section in the first week at a new place, against what
  actually arrived.
