# Light discipline and blackout at night

**What this page is for.** In a long outage, a lit window in a dark street is visible a long way,
and it tells people something. This page covers how to keep light inside when you choose to, and
the things blackout must never do.

> **CAUTION. One rule beats everything on this page: nothing done for light discipline may block a
> fire exit, muffle a smoke or carbon monoxide (CO) alarm, or slow getting everyone, children
> first, out of the house.** A window cover that cannot be opened, or an escape window taped shut,
> trades a small risk for a large one. **Fire and CO kill far more people than discretion protects.**
> Design the blackout so every exit still works in the dark, by feel, in seconds. Alarms stay
> uncovered and audible from where people sleep (USFA, Smoke Alarms; → 08-02).

## Do this now

1. **Walk outside your house tonight, after dark, with the lights on inside.** Look at it from the
   street, the alley and the neighbours' side. **That is what other people see.** Most households
   have never done this and are surprised.
2. **Measure your windows and buy or make covers now**: blackout curtains, cardboard cut to fit, or
   heavy blankets on a tension rod. They double as **winter insulation** (→ 08-03): a purchase that
   pays twice.
3. **Set the household rule and teach the children:** "When it's dark, we keep the light inside."
   Give each child a red headlamp of their own, so the rule comes with a tool (→ 20-04).
4. **Switch every headlamp's default to red**, and put lanterns on their lowest setting.

## What light discipline means here

It is not paranoia and not tactics. **In a truly dark neighbourhood, a lit window is visible a very
long way, and it is information.** It says: this house has power, so it has fuel or solar; it is
occupied; these people are prepared. In a short outage, with everyone in the same position, that
is harmless.

In a **long** event, when the street has been dark for weeks and yours is the one window glowing,
it is a different signal. The point of this page is to make that a **choice** you make on purpose,
not an accident of habit (→ 14-03).

**Be proportionate.** In a two-day ice storm you do not black out; you help your neighbours and
leave the porch light on so the plough driver sees the driveway. The judgement is about how long it
has lasted and what is happening around you (→ 14-01).

## Outside light: two modes

Solar motion lights on the approaches (→ 14-12) and "no light outside" are both right, in different
situations:

| Mode | What it looks like | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Deterrence** | Solar motion lights on and covering the approaches; a house that looks occupied and cared for | Normal times, short outages, and whenever the aim is a lived-in look that makes the house a harder target than the next |
| **Blackout (low profile)** | No outside light at all; motion lights switched off or bagged; the measures on the rest of this page | A long event where a lit house is information, not deterrence |

- **Which mode you are in is a security decision** made on → 14-01 and → 14-12. This page only makes
  both modes ready.
- **Deterrence mode costs nothing from the light budget** (→ 10-06): solar motion lights draw nothing
  from stations, banks or cells. The only argument against them is visibility, and it only wins in
  blackout mode.
- **Switching must be fast and reversible.** Know how each motion light turns off (switch, bag or
  unplug), and practise the change once.

## How you are seen at night

- **Eyes adapted to the dark are very sensitive.** A single candle can be seen a long way across open
  ground on a clear night. A lit window is unmistakable.
- **Snow makes it worse.** Fresh snow reflects light everywhere, so light from a window lights up the
  yard.{{#if is.snow_region}} Your area gets real snow: winter is your most visible season, not the
  least.{{/if}}
- **Sound and smell carry too.** Wood smoke on a cold, still night carries a long way, and a
  generator (→ 09-04) is heard much further than any light is seen. **Light discipline without noise
  and smoke discipline is for show.**
- **Movement catches the eye more than steady light.** A swinging flashlight beam draws far more
  attention than a dim steady glow.
- **Cold air is clear air.** Crisp, still winter nights are the best conditions for seeing far.

## What to do, easiest first

1. **Choose the room.** Use an interior room, a basement, or a room whose windows face away from the
   street. **The most effective step, and free**, and it fits with gathering heat and light into one
   room (→ 08-01, → 10-03).
2. **Cover the windows.** Blackout curtains, heavy blankets, cardboard, foam board or a tarp. **Cover
   the whole opening, edges included**: the glow around the edges is what is actually seen. Rigid
   foam board cut to a snug fit is excellent: it blacks out, insulates, and pops out at once if you
   need the window to escape.
3. **Low, warm, indirect light.** Bounce a dim light off a wall or ceiling instead of lighting the
   room (→ 10-03). Less escapes, and it is more pleasant.
4. **Red light indoors at night.** Keeps night vision, spills less, and is much harder to see from a
   distance than white.
5. **Lights off before a door opens.** An open door with light behind it is a beacon and ruins
   everyone's night vision.
6. **No light outside, in blackout mode.** A flashlight in the yard is seen far beyond where you can
   see with it. Move by dark-adapted eyes, or a red light held low and shielded with your hand. (In
   deterrence mode the motion lights stay on; the rule about **carried** light applies in both.)
7. **Vehicles.** Dome lights, headlights and reversing lights are very visible. Know how to disable
   the dome light (pull its fuse or bulb) if you will load a vehicle at night (→ 01-03).
8. **Screens.** A phone at full brightness is a bright light in a dark room and shows through a
   covered window. Lowest brightness, facing away from windows.

## Children

- **Children need light.** Dark halls and stairs cause falls; dark bedrooms cause fear (→ 20-08).
  **Nothing about discipline overrides a child's safety.**
- The answer is **dim, warm, low and inside**: a flameless night light on the floor of an interior
  room feels safe to a child and cannot be seen from outside.
- **Teach it as a game, not a threat.** "We keep our light inside so it stays cosy." Young children
  can learn this; framing it around danger frightens them and works worse (→ 20-04, → 21-01).
- **Give each child a red headlamp of their own.** Children follow rules they have equipment for.
- **Babies and toddlers cannot be reasoned with**, so the room does the work: window covers, lights
  out of reach, a night light where they sleep.{{#if has.infant}} There is an infant in this
  household.{{/if}}{{#if has.toddler}} There is a toddler in this household.{{/if}}

## What blackout must never do

- **Never block an escape window or door.** Every cover comes off from inside, in the dark, by feel,
  in seconds. **Test it with the lights out.**
- **Never cover or muffle a smoke or CO alarm.**
- **Never seal a room so completely that air stops moving**, especially with any flame or
  fuel-burning appliance in it (→ 08-02). Winterising and blackout both push toward sealing; **CO
  risk pushes back, and it wins.**
- **Never make the stairs dark.** Falls in a dark house are among the most likely injuries in a long
  outage, and there may be no emergency room (→ 10-03).
- **Never let discipline stop you signalling for help.** If someone is hurt or you need to be found,
  **light is a tool**: signal on purpose (→ 15-07).

## Gear

**Have now:**

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Blackout curtains or panels for every window | Light and heat kept in, one purchase | Full coverage, edges included |
| Rigid foam board cut to the window openings | Blacks out **and** insulates; pops out for escape | Best two-in-one option (→ 08-03) |
| Heavy blankets and tension rods or clips | Cheap fallback, up in minutes | |
| Red-mode headlamps for everyone | Night vision, little spill, low draw | Children included |
| Red film or red tape for existing lights | Turns white lights red | Cheap |
| Dim, warm flameless night lights | Child safety without a visible signal | Interior rooms |
| Duct tape and cardboard | Seal edge leaks fast | Keep a roll with the covers |
| Dark tarps | Door curtains, porch screening, wind block | Many uses |

**If you do not have it:**
- **Window covers:** cardboard, moving blankets, comforters, garbage bags taped up, plywood, foam
  packing sheets, rugs on a rod, a mattress leaned against a window (never over an escape window).
- **Red light:** red marker on a clear lens, red cellophane, a red balloon stretched over a
  flashlight, brake-light bulbs.
- **Less spill:** a jug over a headlamp aimed at the floor (→ 10-03).
- **A door "airlock":** a blanket hung inside the door, so opening it does not flood light out. It
  also cuts heat loss (→ 08-03).

## When it runs out

- **Light discipline is a habit, not a supply.** It costs nothing. What runs out is the **discipline**
  in month three, when everyone is tired. **Make it a habit now.**
- **The habit that replaces everything:** be asleep when it is dark. Long winter nights and an early
  bedtime (→ 10-03, → 21-07) leave little light to discipline.
- **Review it on a schedule.** What made sense in week six may be unnecessary in month six, or the
  reverse. Tie the review to your situational-awareness routine (→ 14-01). **Neither permanent
  paranoia nor permanent carelessness is a plan.**
- **The failure to avoid:** blacking out so thoroughly that you cannot hear an alarm, cannot get out
  of a window, or trip on a dark stair with a child in your arms. **Discretion is worth something. It
  is not worth a broken hip or a house fire.**

## Phase notes

- **A (sheltering at home):** {{#if is.urban}}the phase where this matters most: you are surrounded by
  neighbours, some in worse shape than you.{{else}}neighbours are fewer, but a lit window still
  carries.{{/if}} Interior room, covered windows, low warm light, outside light by the declared mode.
  Pair it with noise and smoke discipline (→ 09-04, → 14-03).
- **B (on the move):** discipline is safety. Red light only, shielded and low, never standing against
  a light behind you. Children stay physically close: a small child in the dark on the move can be
  lost in seconds.
- **C (settled somewhere new):** fewer eyes, but longer sight lines. In open country a light can be
  seen for **miles**. Same measures; a lit window matters more when it is the only one for a long
  way.
