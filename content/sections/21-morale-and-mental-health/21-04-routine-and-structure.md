# Routine and structure for the household

**What this page is for.** Routine is the cheapest, most effective morale and safety tool in this
binder. It costs nothing, needs no supplies, and does four things at once:
1. **It cuts the number of decisions**, which protects judgment (→ 21-01).
2. **It marks time**, which stops months blurring together (→ 21-02).
3. **It reassures children** better than words do (→ 20-08, → 20-03).
4. **It makes the boring, critical things actually happen:** teeth (→ 12-06), handwashing (→ 13-02),
   medicines (→ 12-07), water tracking (→ 23-02).

A household without a shape to its day drifts, and drifting households make mistakes and come apart.
This page gives the day a shape and gets it written down.

## Do this now

1. **Write out the daily shape** (below) and post it where everyone sees it.
2. **Assign the roles** (→ 00-07): who does the fire, the water, the children in the morning,
   bedtime. **Written down, not assumed.** Assumed roles are the fastest route to resentment (→ 21-05).
3. **Pick the weekly markers:** a different meal, a bath day, a "Sunday". Something that tells one day
   from another.
4. **Try it for a week now**, while there is no pressure.

## The daily shape

Adapt freely. The point is that there **is** one, that it is written, and that the anchors hold.

| Block | Content | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Wake with the light** | Fire up, water on, everyone dressed | In winter that is late; fine (→ 10-03) |
| **Morning check** | **Weather (→ 15-01), water level, wood, medicines, everyone's state** | 5 minutes; catches problems early |
| **Morning work** | The heavy work: wood, water, repairs, animals | Daylight is for work |
| **Children's learning block** | Short, at the window (→ 20-03) | |
| **Main meal (midday)** | The big cooking event (→ 07-01) | Everyone together |
| **Afternoon** | Lighter work, projects, children outdoors | → 11-02 |
| **Evening together** | Warm room, lit, everyone together (→ 08-01, → 10-03) | The social anchor |
| **Children's bedtime** | Wash, teeth, story, bed: same order every night | **Defend this** (→ 12-06, → 21-07) |
| **Adult time** | After the children are down; not logistics | → 21-05 |
| **Evening check** | Fire banked or out, doors, lamps out, alarms, tomorrow's plan | Ends the day cleanly |

**The two anchors are the morning check and the bedtime routine.** If everything else falls apart in
a hard week, keep those two and the household holds.

## The weekly and seasonal shape

Daily routine prevents chaos; weekly and seasonal rhythm stops time collapsing (→ 21-02).
- **One day different.** A better meal, no heavy work, a game, a bath. Call it Sunday.
- **Laundry day, baking day, bath day.** Old-fashioned, and useful for marking the week.
- **The weekly household check** (below).
- **Seasonal markers** (→ 21-06): solstices, birthdays, holidays, first frost
  ({{ region.home.first_frost }}), last frost ({{ region.home.last_frost }}), first planting.

## The weekly household check

**Fifteen minutes, same day each week, all the adults.** This is where the binder's tracking actually
happens.

| Check | Where |
|---|---|
| **Water:** stored volume, days of supply | → 03-01, → 23-02 |
| **Food:** what is left, what is next, rotation | → 04-10, → 23-02 |
| **Fuel:** wood or fuel left, days of heat | → 06-04 |
| **Power:** battery state, generation | → 09-01 |
| **Medicines:** days left for each, expiry dates, supplies | → 12-07, your condition modules |
| **Everyone's health and state** | → 21-02 |
| **Anything broken or needed** | → 16-05, → 22-04 |
| **Decision check: has anything crossed a threshold?** | **→ 01-02** |

{{#each meds as m}}- {{m.member}}: {{m.name}}, days on hand {{m.days_on_hand}}, expires {{m.expiry}}
{{/each}}

**The last line is the point.** The go/no-go decision should be reviewed on a schedule, not made in a
panic (→ 21-01). A weekly check makes it calm and routine.

## Roles: write them down

Unassigned work becomes contested work (→ 21-05). With → 00-07:
- **Who does what, by default**, each day.
- **Who decides what**, when there is a decision.
- **Who covers if someone is sick or injured.** At least two adults should be able to run every
  member's medical routine (→ 12-07, the condition modules).
- **The children's jobs** (→ 20-07).
- **Review and rotate.** Rotating the unpleasant jobs matters more than dividing them fairly once.

## Why it works on children

CDC's parenting guidance says routines and rules help children feel safe because they know what to
expect (CDC, Tips for Relying on Routines and Rules; → 20-03, → 20-08). A child who knows what comes next is calmer than one told
everything will be fine. The routine shows competence more convincingly than reassurance.

## Why it works on adults

- **Fewer decisions.** Decision fatigue is real and it damages the important decisions (→ 21-01).
- **The critical boring things get done.** Handwashing, teeth, food rotation and the medicine check
  all lapse without structure, and each lapse has a real cost.
- **It marks progress.** A week with a shape is a week you can look back on.
- **It reduces conflict.** Most household conflict is about unclear expectations (→ 21-05).

## When to break it

**On purpose, and say so:**
- **Emergencies** (→ 12-16).
- **Illness:** the routine bends around a sick household (→ 13-11; CDC, Managing Sick Days for
  anyone with a chronic condition).
- **Weather:** a storm rearranges the day (→ 17-01).
- **Celebration** (→ 21-06). Breaking the routine on purpose is part of what makes it work.
- **But return to it.** The failure is not breaking the routine; it is not going back.

## The failure to watch for

**Routine wearing away is one of the earliest visible signs of a household in trouble** (→ 21-02;
SAMHSA warning signs): people stop getting dressed, meals stop happening at set times, bedtime
slides, washing stops. **That is not a discipline problem; it is a signal.** The answer is usually
rest, food and less work, not trying harder (→ 21-01).

**Scavenge or substitute:** the routine is free. Sunrise and sunset are a good clock; a household on
daylight time is on a natural routine (→ 10-03). Mark days with scratches on a doorframe or a pebble
in a jar. **What you cannot substitute: actually doing it.**

## When it runs out

- **Routine does not run out, but keeping to it does**, gradually and invisibly from the inside
  (→ 21-02).
- **Defences:** write it down (so it is outside your head), post it (so it is seen), assign it (so it
  is owned), and review it at the weekly check (so it is kept up).
- **When the household is depleted, shorten the routine rather than drop it.** Keep the morning check
  and bedtime; let the rest go for a while. A small routine kept beats an ambitious one abandoned.
- **The routine is itself a substitute**: for supervision, motivation, decision-making capacity and
  reassurance.
- **The return:** a household with a steady routine notices problems earlier, wastes less, decides
  better and holds together longer.

## By phase

- **A (shelter in place):** set it up in the first week. A household that drifts for a month rarely
  recovers the structure.
- **B (moving):** routine collapses on the move; that is expected. **Keep two things: food times and
  the children's bedtime** (→ 20-08). Re-set the full routine within days of stopping anywhere.
- **C (establish):** more work, more variety and a natural seasonal rhythm. A rural routine is easier
  to keep because the work has its own shape. Build the weekly check in from the first week.
