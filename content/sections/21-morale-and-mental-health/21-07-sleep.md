# Sleep in a hard time

**What this page is for.** Sleep is the biggest single lever on judgment, mood, patience and the
body's defence against infection. In a grid-down winter it is attacked from every side: cold, a
crowded room, a baby who wakes, worry, no clear day and night, and perhaps a heater that has to be
checked through the night. Lack of sleep damages exactly what this binder depends on (→ 21-01):
decisions, patience with children, safety with tools (→ 16-06), and noticing that something is
wrong. **A chronically underslept household will eventually make a serious mistake.**

> **SAFETY.**
> - **Carbon monoxide kills sleeping people without waking them** (CDC, Carbon Monoxide Poisoning
>   Basics; → 29-03). **Never run a generator, charcoal, or any engine indoors, in a garage, or near
>   an open window** (floor, → 30-01). Running a fuel heater while people sleep is a trade-off on its
>   own card: {{choice DC-01}} → 30-02.
> - **Babies sleep on their own firm, flat surface** in the parents' room (AAP). Sharing an adult bed
>   for warmth is a card offered only for children over 12 months: {{choice DC-09}} → 30-10.
> - **Hot-water bottles and warmed stones pre-warm the bed and come out before a child gets in.** A
>   hot object against small skin for an hour is a contact burn (→ 12-03).

## Do this now

1. **Sort out the winter sleep set-up:** insulation **under** everyone as well as over, dry clothes
   changed before bed, hats, wool socks (→ 11-01).
2. **Get insulating, light-blocking window coverings** (→ 08-03, → 10-05). They hold heat, block
   light and improve sleep at once.
3. **Fix the children's bedtime routine** and hold it (→ 20-03, → 21-04): the most effective sleep
   help for children.
4. **Install and test a carbon monoxide alarm at pillow height in the room where people sleep**
   before the first night any fuel heater burns (→ 08-02).
5. **Read the DC-01 card together** (→ 30-02) and record the household's choice. If it allows a
   heater overnight, agree now who does the wake checks, and put a clock by that person's head.
{{#if has.condition.CL-24}}6. **Someone uses CPAP.** Plan the power for it (→ 02-24, → 09-06).
{{/if}}

## Why it belongs in a survival binder

CDC says adults generally need **7 or more hours** a night, and that too little sleep over time harms
health (CDC, About Sleep). In a crisis the effects are immediate:
- **Judgment and decisions get worse** (→ 21-01), and the decisions are about fire, medicine, travel
  and people.
- **Patience collapses**, with children (→ 20-08) and each other (→ 21-05).
- **The body's defences weaken** just when infection risk rises (→ 13-11).
- **Accidents rise:** tools, axes, fire, ice (→ 16-06, → 11-06).
- **Blood sugar is harder to manage** for anyone with diabetes (→ 02-01).
- **Mood and resilience fall** (→ 21-02, → 12-15).

Sleep is not downtime; it is maintenance on the household's most important asset.

## Sleeping warm

**Most cold-house sleep problems are solved by insulation, not more blankets on top.**
- **Insulate underneath.** You lose a great deal of heat into a cold mattress or floor. Foam pads,
  extra blankets, cardboard, or the mattress raised off a cold floor matter more than another quilt
  on top.
- **Dry clothes, changed before bed** (→ 11-03). **Never sleep in what you sweated in**: the commonest
  cause of a cold night.
- **A wool hat** and **dry wool socks.**
- **Several thin layers of blanket** trap more air than one thick one (→ 11-01).
- **Sleep close together.** Shared body heat is real; people have always done it in cold houses
  (→ 08-01). For babies and toddlers, the separate-surface rule and the DC-09 card above apply.
- **A hot-water bottle or a wrapped warmed stone** in the bed before you get in. Cheap and effective,
  **and it comes out before a child gets in.**
- **A warm drink and something to eat before bed.** Going to bed hungry in a cold house means a cold
  night.
- **Sleep in the heated room** (→ 08-01). Heating cold bedrooms in a grid-down winter is not worth the
  fuel. CDC lists babies sleeping in cold bedrooms among those most at risk of hypothermia (CDC,
  Preventing Hypothermia).

## Sleeping through the dark half of the year

{{#if is.cold_winters}}Your winters bring long nights.{{/if}} Long winter darkness is partly an
opportunity (→ 10-03):
- **Sleep longer.** Sleeping 9 to 10 hours on December nights is not laziness; it is adaptation, and
  it saves light, fuel and food.
- **Waking for a while in the middle of a long night, then sleeping again, was common before
  electric light.** If it happens, it is not insomnia. Do not fight it or worry about it.
- **Get daylight in the morning**, even a short time outdoors or at a window. It sets the body clock.
- **Keep the same bed and wake times**, anchored to the light.

## The crowded room

Everyone in one heated room for months is warm and fuel-efficient, and hard on sleep:
- **Separate spaces where you can:** screens, blankets on a line, furniture. Privacy matters over
  months (→ 21-02, → 21-05), above all for teens and couples.
- **Stagger bedtimes:** children first, then adult time, then adults.
- **Earplugs.** Cheap and worth having.
- **A dim red light** for moving at night (→ 10-01) does not wake everyone.
- **Agree quiet hours.**
- **Accept that some nights are bad**, and do not add frustration to it.

## Fuel heaters at night: DC-01

**Portable propane and kerosene heaters sold for indoor use are "unvented":** their exhaust,
including carbon monoxide, stays in the room, and the common types' manuals say never to run them
while you sleep. Whether to run one overnight is the household's choice on the DC-01 card (→ 30-02),
because the cost of "never" is a cold night, which matters most for babies and frail elders in deep
cold. **Our choice:** {{choice DC-01}} → 30-02.

**If the household's choice is "never":** the heater is off and confirmed out before anyone sleeps,
and the night runs on one small room, shared warmth, layers, sleeping bags and pre-warmed beds
(→ 08-01, → 08-06, → 11-02).

**If the household's choice is the card's "lowest setting, alarm, vents, barrier, 3-hour wake"
option**, the card's conditions as a bedtime checklist:
- [ ] Carbon monoxide alarm in the room, **tested tonight**, at pillow height (→ 08-02).
- [ ] **The manual's vent openings open.** Write yours: ______________________. The vent does not close
      "because it's cold".
- [ ] Heater on its **lowest safe setting**, with the manual's correct flame, behind the anchored
      barrier (→ 20-02), on the far side of the room from the sleepers.
- [ ] Fuel tank filled **before** bed, outdoors with the heater cold, so it does not run dry in the
      night (→ 08-10, → 08-09).
- [ ] **One adult named for the wake checks every 3 hours:** flame colour and height, alarm working,
      vent still open, nobody flushed or complaining of headache. Alternate nights. Unassigned night
      duty becomes resentment (→ 21-05), and here it is also the safety control.
- [ ] **Headache or nausea on waking, in anyone: everyone out, heater off, doors open** (→ 08-02).
- [ ] By the bed: headlamp on a hook, the toilet bucket (→ 13-05), the clock.

**If any box is unticked, the heater goes off before sleep** and the room runs on insulation, layers
and body heat. That is the manuals' rule and the fallback, not a failure.

**A vented wood stove** is different: banking the fire (→ 06-02, → 08-08) buys an unbroken night. Keep
the carbon monoxide alarm in the room anyway.

## Children and sleep

- **The bedtime routine is the help** (→ 20-03): wash, teeth (→ 12-06), story, bed, in the same order
  every night, even when everything else is chaos.
- **Sleep problems and night waking are very common in stressed children** (→ 20-08). Expect them;
  answer with comfort, not frustration.
- **A dim flameless night light** (→ 10-02). New fear of the dark is common and normal.
- **Closeness helps enormously.** For babies, the separate-surface rule (AAP); for toddlers and older
  children sharing a bed, the DC-09 card (→ 30-10). Warmers out of the bed before a child is in it.
- **Never give a child medicine to make them sleep.** FDA warns against using diphenhydramine to
  sedate children and against its use under age 2 (FDA, Drug Safety Communication).
- **Teens need more sleep than adults and shift later.** Let them sleep where the day allows.
- **A tired child is a difficult child**, and a difficult child tires the adults. Protecting
  children's sleep protects everyone's.

## When you cannot sleep

- **Get up, do something quiet and dull in low light, and go back when sleepy.** Lying awake
  frustrated makes it worse.
- **Do not keep checking the time.**
- **Write the worry down.** A notebook by the bed moves the problem out of your head until morning
  (→ 25-05).
- **Check the body:** cold, hunger, pain (→ 12-13), a full bladder, and blood sugar in anyone with
  diabetes (→ 02-01). Much "insomnia" in a crisis has a physical cause.
- **Alcohol is not a sleep aid.** It breaks up sleep and makes cold more dangerous (→ 08-05,
  → 12-15).
- **Severe, lasting insomnia** is a warning sign worth taking seriously (→ 21-02, → 12-15).

**Scavenge or substitute:**
- **Insulation underneath:** cardboard, newspaper, foam packing, straw or leaves in a bag, a rug, a
  folded tarp. Anything that traps air between you and the cold.
- **Bed warmer:** a stone warmed by the fire and wrapped in cloth (test the heat carefully; → 12-03),
  a jug of hot water, a rice-filled sock warmed on the stove. **All pre-warm the bed and come out
  before a child gets in.**
- **Blankets:** curtains, coats, rugs, and newspaper between layers, which does insulate.
- **Privacy:** a blanket on a rope.
- **Earplugs:** wax, cloth, cotton.
- **What you cannot substitute: the carbon monoxide alarm.** Buy several.

## When it runs out

- **Sleep debt builds up and cannot be repaid in one night.** Treat chronic short sleep as a household
  emergency, and the fix is usually **less work**, not trying harder (→ 21-01, → 21-02).
- **Do not ration sleep.** It is one of the few things in this binder where the right move is to spend
  more: sleeping longer in winter saves light, fuel and food (→ 10-03) while restoring judgment.
- **Substitute:** naps, alternating night duty, and where possible another adult (→ 18-01).
- **The loop to break early:** exhausted adults, poor judgment, accidents and bad decisions, more
  stress, worse sleep. Notice it, drop non-essential work, and sleep.
- **The same loop runs the other way:** a rested household is safer, kinder, healthier and decides
  better, which is a large return on a foam pad, a wool hat and going to bed when it gets dark.

## By phase

- **A (shelter in place):** the winter sleep set-up in the heated room, the carbon monoxide alarm
  tested at pillow height, the DC-01 checklist every night a heater burns if the household chose that
  option, the bedtime routine held, and an unapologetically long night in midwinter.
- **B (moving):** sleep is the hardest thing to get on the move. **Insulation from the ground first**,
  then dry layers and shared warmth (→ 11-01, → 08-06). Exhaustion is the main danger; plan short days
  (→ 01-03). Never run a vehicle engine for heat while people sleep in it.
- **C (establish):** more space and privacy help a great deal. A household living by daylight sleeps
  better than one fighting it.
