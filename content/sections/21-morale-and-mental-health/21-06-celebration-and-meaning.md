# Celebration, marking time and meaning

**What this page is for.** This page is not decoration. It is one of the practical ways a household
gets through months of hardship (→ 21-02). Without deliberate celebration two things happen: time
collapses, as weeks become indistinguishable and the household loses any sense of moving through
something; and life becomes pure endurance, which nobody keeps up for ever. **Marking time forward
gives a hard stretch a shape.** A birthday, a solstice, a Sunday that is different, a jar of something
sweet opened on a set day: cheap, nearly weightless, and in the hardest months they carry real
weight.

## Do this now

1. **Stock the small things now**, because you will not think of them later: coffee, tea, spices,
   sugar, honey, chocolate, dried fruit, candles, a few small gifts put away.
2. **Write the year's markers on a calendar:** birthdays, holidays, the solstices, first frost, first
   planting (→ 25-01). Then actually mark them.
3. **Set the weekly marker now.** One day different from the others. Start this week.
4. {{#if has.anaphylaxis}}**Check every treat against the household's allergies** (→ 02-04). A
   celebration that leaves someone out or puts them at risk is worse than none; make the safe version
   the one everybody eats.{{else}}**Check every treat against anyone's allergies or diet** (→ 02-04,
   → 02-19), including guests'.{{/if}}

## Why marking time matters

- **Days without markers blur.** People really do lose track of how long it has been, which is
  disorienting and demoralising (→ 21-02).
- **Endurance without punctuation cannot be kept up.** People need something to look forward to on a
  scale shorter than "when this is over".
- **Children need it most** (→ 20-03, → 20-08). A young child's sense of time is short; markers give
  them something to anticipate.
- **It makes memories.** A year of undifferentiated hardship is remembered as one grey block; a year
  with markers is remembered as a story with parts.
- **It says this is life, not just survival.**

## The scales of celebration

| Scale | What | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | Coffee in the morning; the evening together (→ 10-03); a bedtime story | Almost nothing |
| **Weekly** | **One day different:** a better meal, no heavy work, a game, a bath | Small |
| **Monthly** | Something opened from the stores; a finished project; something baked | Modest |
| **Seasonal** | Solstices, first frost, first planting, first harvest (→ 25-01) | Free: they happen anyway |
| **Yearly** | Birthdays, holidays, anniversaries | Plan ahead; put things away |
| **Milestones** | A finished project, a full woodpile, a good harvest, a skill learned | Free and satisfying |

**The weekly marker is the most valuable.** It keeps time from collapsing and costs almost nothing.

## Your year's natural markers

Your local year gives you markers for free (→ 25-01):
- **The winter solstice:** the darkest day and the turning point. "The days get longer from now" means
  something in a hard winter. Mark it.
- **Last spring frost**, around {{ region.home.last_frost }} at home: planting starts.
- **First green; first foraging** (→ 05-17).
- **The summer solstice:** the peak.
- **First harvest and the big preserving push** (→ 04-04, → 04-06).
- **First fall frost**, around {{ region.home.first_frost }} at home: the year turns.
- **The woodpile full** (→ 06-04): satisfying, and a real safety milestone.
{{#if is.cold_winters}}- **First thaw; first bare ground**: a big one where winters are long.
{{/if}}{{#if is.hot_summers}}- **The first cool evening after the hot season.**
{{/if}}

**If you move,** the calendar moves with you. Rebuild the marker list from the new place's own year in
the first season ({{#each destinations as d}}{{d.name}}: last frost {{d.region.last_frost}}, first frost {{d.region.first_frost}}; {{/each}}see → 05-18) and write the new markers on the calendar.

## Doing it with almost nothing

- **Food is the classic marker.** Something different, something sweet, something saved. A jar of
  preserves opened on a set day is a celebration (→ 04-04).
- **Bake something.** Bread, a cake of some kind. The smell alone changes a house.
- **A candle**, lit on purpose, watched, and put out afterwards (→ 10-02, → 10-04). Open flame indoors
  is a trade-off with its own card: {{choice DC-20}} → 30-21.
- **Music and singing.** Free, and out of all proportion effective.
- **Stories**, including telling the children about before, and about after.
- **A made gift** (→ 16-04, → 16-08); the making is part of it.
- **A photograph, a drawing, a mark on a doorframe.**
- **No work for a few hours**, on purpose. Rest as a celebration is legitimate.
- **A wash and clean clothes**: a real treat after weeks (→ 13-02, → 13-04).

## Children

- **Birthdays matter enormously to children** and are worth real effort. Put a small gift away now
  for each child for each of the next couple of years.
- **Traditions can be kept when everything else changes:** the same song, the same story, the same
  order of things. Continuity is the point, not the scale.
- **Let them help make it happen** (→ 20-07). The preparation is most of the fun.
- **Allergy-safe for everyone at the table** (→ 02-04).
- **Then brush teeth** (→ 12-06). Frequent sugar drives tooth decay and there may be no dentist (CDC,
  Oral Health). Have the treat and brush after; do not ban treats.
- **Teens** may want their own way of marking things, with friends where possible. Let them plan one.

## Meaning, beyond celebration

Over months, people need more than punctuation. They need a sense that what they do matters:
- **Building something** rather than only holding on: the garden (→ 05-01), the woodpile (→ 06-04),
  skills (→ 24-01), the children's learning (→ 20-03).
- **Caring for others** (→ 18-01). Helping neighbours is one of the strongest sources of meaning there
  is, and it returns more than it costs.
- **Teaching:** passing something on (→ 18-07, → 20-03).
- **Faith or practice**, for those who have it: a real and lasting source of meaning for many people;
  make room for it.
- **Keeping a record** (→ 25-05). People who keep a journal through hard times often find it
  valuable, and it becomes something for the children later.
- **Doing things well** rather than just adequately. Craft is its own reward.

## The trap

**Saving everything for a better day.** A household that hoards every pleasure against some worse
moment finds the worse moment is every day, and the treats never get opened. **Spend some of it, on
purpose, on a schedule.** That is what it is for.

**Scavenge or substitute:** almost everything here can be free: singing, stories, a walk, rest, a made
gift, a fire, a good meal from ordinary food. Ordinary baking in a different shape is a celebration to
a small child. **Wild treats in season** (→ 05-17): the first berries of the year are an event, with
identification or nothing (→ 05-06). Marking: a chalk line, a notch, a stone in a jar. **What you
cannot substitute: deciding to do it.** Put it on the calendar.

## When it runs out

- **The stored treats do run out, and that is fine: they are meant to be used.** The only question is
  whether they are spent on purpose or forgotten.
- **Ration them onto the calendar, not into a hoard:** one thing a month, plus birthdays and the
  solstices. A modest stock then lasts a year and gives the year a shape.
- **As bought things run out, made and grown things replace them:** bread, preserves (→ 04-04), honey
  if you keep bees (→ 05-12), a made gift. Arguably better.
- **What never runs out:** singing, stories, rest, a walk, a good meal, marking the solstice, doing
  something for a neighbour (→ 18-01).
- **Households that mark time and make meaning come through long hardship better than those that only
  endure it:** better relationships, better memories, more morale to draw on.

## By phase

- **A (shelter in place):** the full range is available, and neighbours are part of it (→ 18-01). A
  shared meal with another household is one of the best morale investments there is.
- **B (moving):** small and portable: a sweet thing at a rest stop, a story at night, a song while
  walking. For a young child on the move, a small ritual at the end of each day steadies them (→ 20-08).
- **C (establish):** the seasonal markers become real: planting, harvest, the woodpile filling, the
  first snow. Shared celebrations with neighbours are how a community actually forms (→ 18-01).
