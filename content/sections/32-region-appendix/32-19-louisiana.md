## What this page is for

This page holds the public reference data for Louisiana: climate by area and by parish, the natural hazards FEMA rates for each parish, the National Weather Service offices, the NOAA Weather Radio transmitters with the SAME codes to program into a weather radio, the state emergency management agency, and the ecoregions (natural regions) that decide which plants and trees you meet. Your own home parish is filled into the subject pages automatically; use this page for any place you might have to go. How the numbers were made, and their limits, is on → 32-00.

## Who to call

- **Emergency:** 911.
- **State emergency management agency:** Governor's Office of Homeland Security and Emergency Preparedness (Louisiana) (FEMA, Emergency Management Agencies list, archived 2016, with later renames).
- **Poison control (anywhere in the US):** 1-800-222-1222, free, 24 hours, connects you to the local poison center (HRSA, Poison Help, 2026). → 12-17
- **Weather warnings without power or internet:** a NOAA Weather Radio set to the transmitter and SAME code below. → 15-01

## At a glance

- 64 parishes.
- Hardiness zones: 8b to 10a (USDA Agricultural Research Service and Oregon State University, 2023 USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, 2023).
- January mean low: 32 to 47 F. July mean high: 88 to 95 F (NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information, U.S. Climate Normals 1991-2020, 2021).
- Coldest and hottest on record at the stations used: -16 F and 112 F (NOAA Regional Climate Centers, Applied Climate Information System, station records through 2025).
- NWS forecast offices serving the parishes: NWS Lake Charles, LA; NWS New Orleans, LA; NWS Shreveport, LA; NWS Jackson, MS.
- Ecoregions at parish centres: Western Gulf Coastal Plain; South Central Plains; Mississippi Valley Loess Plains; Mississippi Alluvial Plain; Southeastern Plains; Southern Coastal Plain.

## Natural hazards

Ratings come from FEMA's National Risk Index (FEMA, National Risk Index, December 2025 release): 3 = High (FEMA "Very High" or "Relatively High"), 2 = Moderate ("Relatively Moderate"), 1 = Low (everything lower, including hazards FEMA does not expect here). They compare each parish with every other county in the country, and they weigh how much there is to damage as well as how often the hazard strikes, so a thinly settled parish can rate Low for a hazard that still happens there. Prepare for anything rated 2 or 3. → 17-01 → 17-02 → 17-03 → 17-04 → 17-05 → 17-07
