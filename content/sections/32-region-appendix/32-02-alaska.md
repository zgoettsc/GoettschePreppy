## What this page is for

This page holds the public reference data for Alaska: climate by area and by borough or census area, the natural hazards FEMA rates for each borough or census area, the National Weather Service offices, the NOAA Weather Radio transmitters with the SAME codes to program into a weather radio, the state emergency management agency, and the ecoregions (natural regions) that decide which plants and trees you meet. Your own home borough or census area is filled into the subject pages automatically; use this page for any place you might have to go. How the numbers were made, and their limits, is on → 32-00.

## Who to call

- **Emergency:** 911.
- **State emergency management agency:** Alaska Division of Homeland Security and Emergency Management (FEMA, Emergency Management Agencies list, archived 2016, with later renames).
- **Poison control (anywhere in the US):** 1-800-222-1222, free, 24 hours, connects you to the local poison center (HRSA, Poison Help, 2026). → 12-17
- **Weather warnings without power or internet:** a NOAA Weather Radio set to the transmitter and SAME code below. → 15-01

## At a glance

- 30 boroughs and census areas.
- Hardiness zones: 1b to 8b (USDA Agricultural Research Service and Oregon State University, 2023 USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map, 2023).
- January mean low: -23 to 31 F. July mean high: 51 to 72 F (NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information, U.S. Climate Normals 1991-2020, 2021).
- Coldest and hottest on record at the stations used: -76 F and 98 F (NOAA Regional Climate Centers, Applied Climate Information System, station records through 2025).
- NWS forecast offices serving the boroughs and census areas: NWS Anchorage, AK; NWS Fairbanks, AK; NWS Juneau, AK.
- Ecoregions at borough or census area centres: Alaska Peninsula Mountains; Aleutian Islands; Pacific Coastal Mountains; Ahklun and Kilbuck Mountains; Bristol Bay-Nushagak Lowlands; Coastal Western Hemlock-Sitka Spruce Forests; Wrangell Mountains; Alaska Range; Interior Highlands; Cook Inlet; Subarctic Coastal Plains; Seward Peninsula; Arctic Foothills; Interior Forested Lowlands and Uplands.

## Natural hazards

Ratings come from FEMA's National Risk Index (FEMA, National Risk Index, December 2025 release): 3 = High (FEMA "Very High" or "Relatively High"), 2 = Moderate ("Relatively Moderate"), 1 = Low (everything lower, including hazards FEMA does not expect here). They compare each borough or census area with every other county in the country, and they weigh how much there is to damage as well as how often the hazard strikes, so a thinly settled borough or census area can rate Low for a hazard that still happens there. Prepare for anything rated 2 or 3. → 17-01 → 17-02 → 17-03 → 17-04 → 17-05 → 17-07

## NOAA Weather Radio

A weather radio receives these transmitters with no power grid or internet. Program the SAME code of every borough or census area you care about so the radio sounds only for warnings there; reception is reliable to about 40 miles over flat ground and less in hills (NOAA National Weather Service, NOAA Weather Radio Coverage by County, 2026). → 15-01
