## What this page is for

This page holds the public reference data for Ohio: climate by area and by county, the natural hazards FEMA rates for each county, the National Weather Service offices, the NOAA Weather Radio transmitters with the SAME codes to program into a weather radio, the state emergency management agency, and the ecoregions (natural regions) that decide which plants and trees you meet. ## Who to call

- **Emergency:** 911.
- **State emergency management agency:** Ohio Emergency Management Agency (FEMA, Emergency Management Agencies list, archived 2016, with later renames).

## Natural hazards

Ratings come from FEMA's National Risk Index (FEMA, National Risk Index, December 2025 release): 3 = High (FEMA "Very High" or "Relatively High"), 2 = Moderate ("Relatively Moderate"), 1 = Low (everything lower, including hazards FEMA does not expect here). They compare each county with every other county in the country, and they weigh how much there is to damage as well as how often the hazard strikes, so a thinly settled county can rate Low for a hazard that still happens there.
