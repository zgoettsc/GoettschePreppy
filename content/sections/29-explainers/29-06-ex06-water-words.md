# Water words, explained from the beginning

**What this page is for.** Water treatment advice uses a handful of words that sound alike but
mean different jobs. Mixing them up is how people drink water they thought was safe. This page
defines them.

> **SAFETY.** **Clear is not clean.** Water can look perfect and carry enough germs to cause
> days of diarrhoea, which in a small child, an elder or someone with diabetes is a medical
> emergency. And **no boiling, filtering or chlorine makes water safe if it may contain fuel,
> toxic chemicals or radioactive material** (CDC). Find another source.

## Three different jobs

| Job | What it does | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Clarifying** | Removes dirt and particles so the water is clear | Settle and pour off; strain through cloth or a coffee filter |
| **Disinfecting** | Kills or removes germs | Boiling, chlorine (→ 29-05), a fine enough filter plus chlorine, UV |
| **Distilling** | Removes salts and many chemicals by boiling water and collecting the steam | A still (→ 03-03). Needed for salt water and pool water with salts |

Boiling and chlorine disinfect. **They do not remove salt or chemicals.** A filter removes some
germs but, on its own, not all of them.

## The words

- **Potable** — safe to drink. Tap water from a working public system is potable; after a main
  break or a boil-water notice it is not, until the utility says so.
- **Boil** — a **rolling boil**: big bubbles that keep rolling when stirred, not the first small
  bubbles. Hold it **1 minute**; above 5,000 ft (EPA) or 6,500 ft (CDC) hold it **3 minutes**.
  Boiling is the most reliable way to kill germs (CDC).
  {{#if is.high_altitude}}Your home is above 5,000 ft: boil 3 minutes.{{/if}}
- **Germs** come in three kinds that behave differently:
  - **Bacteria** — single cells, such as *E. coli*. Killed by boiling and chlorine; removed by a
    filter of 0.3 micron or finer.
  - **Parasites** (protozoa), such as *Giardia* and *Cryptosporidium*. Larger. Killed by
    boiling; removed by a filter of 1 micron or finer. **Chlorine works poorly on them, and
    iodine does not kill *Cryptosporidium*** (CDC).
  - **Viruses** — far smaller than either. Killed by boiling and chlorine. **No common portable
    filter removes viruses**, so filtered water still needs chlorine or boiling (CDC).
- **Micron** — one millionth of a metre. A filter's **absolute** pore size is the largest gap it
  lets through; a "nominal" size is an average and promises less. Read which one the package
  states (→ 28-06).
- **Turbidity** — cloudiness. Cloudy water shields germs from chlorine and UV and uses up the
  chlorine, so it is cleared first and dosed double (CDC; → 29-05).
- **Residual** — the chlorine left over after it has done its work. A faint smell after 30
  minutes means some remains; no smell means the dose was used up, so treat again.
- **UV** — ultraviolet light from a pen-style device or the sun. Works on small amounts of clear
  water only. Sunlight method: clear plastic bottles of 2 litres or less, 6 hours in full sun or
  2 days if cloudy (CDC) (→ 03-04).
- **Contaminated vs. dirty** — dirty water has mud and germs and can be treated. Contaminated
  water has fuel, chemicals, sewage overflow or floodwater from industrial areas; treat it as
  unusable unless distilled, and even then not if fuel or solvents are the problem.

## Stored water

Store water in clean, food-grade containers, sealed, cool and dark, and replace stored tap water
every 6 months (CDC, Creating and Storing an Emergency Water Supply). → 03-02

## Wells and testing

A private well is not tested by anyone but you. Test it for bacteria at least once a year and
after a flood (CDC, Guidelines for Testing Well Water).
