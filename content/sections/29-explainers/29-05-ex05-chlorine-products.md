# Chlorine products, explained from the beginning

**What this page is for.** Chlorine kills germs in water and on surfaces, and a household may
have it in three or four forms. They differ in strength by hundreds of times, and two of them
together can make a poison gas. This page says what each one is, what "ppm" means, and how to use
the published tables without guessing.

> **SAFETY — the floors that apply (→ 30-01).**
> - **Bleach or pool shock is never mixed with ammonia, acids or other cleaners.** Chlorine gas.
> - **Children do not have access to firearms, fuel, medications or chlorine products.** Fixed in
>   every source.
>
> Water contaminated with fuel or toxic chemicals **cannot be made safe** by chlorine or boiling
> (CDC). Swallowed chlorine product: **Poison Control, 1-800-222-1222**, → 12-17.

## The forms you will meet

| Product | What it is | Strength | Keeps | Drinking water? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Household bleach** | Liquid **sodium hypochlorite** in water | Regular unscented bleach is 5 to 9% (CDC); the % is on the label | Loses strength over months; a year-old bottle is weaker than the tables assume. Mixed solutions lose strength in about a day | **Yes**, unscented only, using the table below |
| **Pool shock (cal-hypo)** | Granular **calcium hypochlorite** | Far stronger than bleach; the % is on the label | Keeps well sealed, dry and cool | **Yes**, only if the label shows calcium hypochlorite with **no** algaecides, clarifiers or stabilisers, using EPA's two-step method (→ 03-04) |
| **Pool tablets (trichlor, dichlor)** | Slow-dissolving chlorine compounds for pools | Strong | Years, dry | **No** — pool use only. Never stored with cal-hypo: CDC/ATSDR says to keep cal-hypo apart from other chlorinating agents |
| **Chlorine dioxide tablets** | A different chemical sold for drinking water | Per label | Per label | **Yes**, following the label's times exactly; the one chemical that handles *Cryptosporidium* (CDC) |

Scented, "splashless", gel or "multi-action" products are **not** used for water or disinfection:
they are weaker or carry additives.

## What ppm means

**ppm** is **parts per million**. In water, 1 ppm is 1 milligram of chlorine in each litre
(mg/L). It is how strengths are compared:

- **Drinking water** gets the lowest strength: just enough to leave a faint chlorine smell after
  30 minutes.
- **Surface disinfection** uses 1,000 to 5,000 ppm for the hardest jobs (CDC, Preventing
  Norovirus).
- A **cal-hypo stock solution** made by EPA's method is about 500 ppm (500 mg/L), and is then
  diluted 1 part to 100 parts water for drinking. The stock itself is a poison: labelled, lidded
  and kept from children.

Surface strength in drinking water is poisoning. Drinking strength on a vomit spill does
nothing. **Never mix up the two tables. An unlabelled jug of mixed bleach is surface solution:
pour it out.**

## Drinking water with bleach (CDC; EPA)

Clear cloudy water first (let it settle, pour off, filter through cloth). Then:

| Water | 5 to 9% bleach | 1% bleach |
|---|---|---|
| 1 quart or litre | 2 drops | 10 drops |
| 1 gallon | 8 drops (0.5 mL) | 40 drops (2.5 mL) |
| 5 gallons | 40 drops (2.5 mL, 1/2 tsp) | 200 drops (12.5 mL) |

Stir, wait **at least 30 minutes**. **Double the dose** if the water is cloudy, coloured or very
cold. If the label gives drinking-water directions, follow the label. Full method: → 03-04.

## Surfaces and dishes (CDC)

| Job | Recipe, per gallon of room-temperature water, 5 to 9% bleach | Contact |
|---|---|---|
| Sanitising washed dishes and food surfaces | 1 tablespoon | Then air dry |
| General surface disinfection | 5 tablespoons (1/3 cup) | At least 1 minute, visibly wet |
| Vomit or diarrhoea (norovirus) | 5 to 25 tablespoons (1,000 to 5,000 ppm); the strong end for the spill itself (→ 13-11) | At least 5 minutes |

Clean first, then disinfect: bleach does not work on visible dirt. Make it fresh every day.
Rinse food surfaces with safe water afterward. Ventilate. → 13-10, → 13-11

## Storing chlorine safely (CDC/ATSDR)

Cal-hypo is an **oxidiser**: it feeds a fire, releases chlorine gas when wet or heated, and its
dust burns skin and lungs. Keep it sealed and dry in its original container, off the floor,
below 120°F, away from fuel, acids, ammonia, trichlor or dichlor, and anything that gets wet. Add granules to
water, never water to granules. Bleach goes up high, away from ammonia cleaners and acids.
