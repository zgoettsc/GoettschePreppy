# Reading a label, explained from the beginning

**What this page is for.** Almost every limit that keeps you safe is printed on the thing
itself: how much power an appliance draws, what fuel a can held, when a cylinder must be
retired, how a medicine is stored, when food stops being good. This page shows where to look
and what the abbreviations mean. Photograph your own labels into the app now: labels fade, peel
and get lost. → 28-07

## Appliance and gear data plates

A **data plate** (or rating label) is the sticker or metal plate on the back or bottom of an
appliance, generator, power station or heater.

| Mark | Means |
|---|---|
| **V** (volts) | The electrical "pressure" it needs or supplies: 120 V household, 12 V car-style |
| **A** (amps) | The current it draws or can supply |
| **W** (watts) | Power while running. Watts = volts × amps. "Rated" or "running" watts is what it does all day; "surge", "peak" or "starting" watts is a short burst for motors starting |
| **Wh / kWh** | Stored or used energy (power stations, batteries) |
| **Ah** | Battery capacity; × volts = Wh |
| **Hz** | Mains frequency; US is 60 Hz |
| **Input / PV input** | What can charge it: a voltage range and a maximum current or watts. Stay inside it |
| **BTU/h** | Heat output (or gas burned) per hour on heaters and stoves. 1 kWh of electricity = 3,412 BTU (EIA, British Thermal Units) |
| **"For indoor use" / "outdoor use only"** | Where the maker allows it to run. An outdoor-only heater, stove or generator is never brought inside → 29-03 |
| **Model / serial number** | What to quote for a manual or part |

Meaning of the electrical words: → 29-02.

## Fuel cans

- **What was in it:** the printed or written label, not the colour (→ 29-01). Write the fuel and
  the fill date on every can.
- **Safety features by date:** updated child-resistance rules for the closures of gas, diesel and
  kerosene cans took effect on December 22, 2022, and since July 2023 portable fuel containers
  must carry a **flame mitigation device**, such as a flame arrestor, which protects against
  burning vapour jetting out of the can (CPSC, Fuel Container Safety, 2023). Older cans may lack
  them; they are worth replacing.

## Propane cylinders

The collar or foot ring of a cylinder is stamped. On a refillable 1-lb cylinder you will find the
**DOT specification**, **TW (tare weight)**, the weight of the empty cylinder, and the **date of
manufacture**; a refillable cylinder must be requalified within 12 years of its manufacture date
(YSN/Flame King, Training Program 1A). The training's fill method stops when white fog shows at
the bleed vent, the 80 % liquid mark; a filled cylinder should weigh no more than its TW plus the
1 lb it is rated to hold (→ 08-09). A cylinder that hisses, is dented or rusted, or has a bent or stiff
valve is retired. Refilling 1-lb cylinders at home is a decision card: {{choice DC-03}} → 30-04

## Chlorine products

Look for the **% sodium hypochlorite** (bleach) or **% calcium hypochlorite** (pool shock), the
words unscented or regular, and any **drinking-water directions**; if the label gives them,
follow the label (CDC). Additives (scents, algaecides, clarifiers, stabilisers) rule a product
out for water. → 29-05

## Medicines

**Over-the-counter medicines** carry a **Drug Facts** box in the same order on every package
(FDA, OTC Drug Facts Label):

1. **Active ingredient** and the amount in each tablet or dose. Brands with similar names can
   have different active ingredients (FDA), so read this line every time, and do not give two
   products that list the same active ingredient.
2. **Purpose** (for example, pain reliever, antihistamine).
3. **Uses**.
4. **Warnings**: who should not take it, when to stop, when to ask a doctor.
5. **Directions**: the dose by age, as published. **Follow it as printed; never work out a
   dose of your own.**
6. **Other information**: how to store it.
7. **Inactive ingredients**: what matters for allergies.

**Prescription labels** show the patient's name, the drug and strength, directions, the pharmacy,
the prescription number and refills. The package insert gives storage (→ 29-07).

**Expiry date ("EXP")** is the date the maker guarantees full strength under the stated storage.
FDA says an OTC medicine with no expiry date on the label should be treated as expired three years
after purchase.
**Lot number ("LOT")** identifies the batch: recalls are announced by lot, so keep the box or
write the lot down. Using a medicine past its date is a decision card: {{choice DC-04}} → 30-05

## Food dates

USDA explains the phrases (USDA FSIS, Food Product Dating). **"Best if used by"** is about flavour and
quality, not safety. **"Sell by"** tells the store how long to display it. **"Use by"** is the last
date recommended for peak quality. **"Freeze by"** says when to freeze it. Except for infant
formula, these dates are not required by federal law and are not safety dates. Cans may also
carry a packing code ("closed dating", letters and numbers) instead of a readable date (FSIS). Spoilage signs and dented cans: {{choice DC-14}}
→ 30-15. **Bulging, leaking or spurting cans are thrown out, unopened.**
