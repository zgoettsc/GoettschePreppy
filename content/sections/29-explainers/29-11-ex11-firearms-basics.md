# Firearms basics, for someone who does not shoot

**What this page is for.** This page is for the person in the house who does not use guns but
may one day have to move one, store one, or keep children away from one. It covers the four
safety rules, how to tell in general terms whether a gun is loaded, how to match ammunition to a
gun, and safe storage. It does not teach shooting or tactics. The page is in every binder,
whether or not the household owns a firearm, because guns turn up in other people's homes.

> **SAFETY — the floor that applies (→ 30-01).** **Children do not have access to firearms,
> fuel, medications or chlorine products.** Fixed in every source.

## The four rules

These are the same in every safety course (NSSF, Firearm Safety). Every accident breaks at least
one of them.

1. **Treat every gun as if it is loaded.** Always, including one someone just told you is empty.
2. **Never point a gun at anything you are not willing to destroy.** Keep the muzzle (the open
   end of the barrel) pointed in a safe direction: usually at the floor, away from people, or at
   something that would stop a bullet.
3. **Keep your finger off the trigger** and outside the trigger guard, resting along the side of
   the gun, unless you are about to shoot.
4. **Know your target and what is beyond it.** Bullets go through walls, doors and people.

## Words you will hear

- **Muzzle** — the open end the bullet comes out of. **Barrel** — the tube.
- **Chamber** — where the cartridge sits, ready to fire, at the back of the barrel.
- **Action** — the moving parts that load, fire and unload. Opening the action shows the chamber.
- **Magazine** — a removable box (or a fixed tube) that holds cartridges and feeds them in.
  Removing the magazine does **not** empty the chamber.
- **Cylinder** — the turning drum on a revolver, with several chambers.
- **Safety** — a switch that blocks firing on some guns. It is a mechanical part and can fail.
  It never replaces the four rules.
- **Cartridge** (round) — one unit of ammunition. **Caliber** (or gauge, for shotguns) — the size
  and type of cartridge a gun is made for.

## Is it loaded? (general terms)

If you do not know how a particular gun opens, **do not experiment**. Leave it where it is,
pointed in a safe direction, keep others away, and get the person who knows it. If you must
check one you know how to open:

1. Muzzle in a safe direction, finger outside the trigger guard, the whole time.
2. **Remove the magazine first**, if it has a removable one. Then open the action.
3. **Look into the chamber, and feel it with a finger**: dim light fools eyes. On a revolver,
   open the cylinder and look at every chamber. On a gun with a tube magazine under the barrel,
   the tube may still hold cartridges.
4. If you closed it or put it down, it is **loaded again in your mind**: rule 1.

## Matching ammunition to the gun

Use only the ammunition the gun's maker designates. **The caliber is stamped on the barrel**, or
sometimes on the frame or receiver. The **headstamp** is the lettering on the flat base of each
cartridge. **The headstamp must match the marking on the gun** (SAAMI, Generally Accepted
Firearms and Ammunition Interchangeability). Some cartridges, such as rimfire and some military
rounds, have no caliber on the headstamp: then the original box must say, and if it does not,
the ammunition is not used until a knowledgeable person identifies it. **A cartridge that fits
is not proof it is safe**: the wrong one can burst the gun. SAAMI lists a few accepted
alternatives (for example, a gun marked 357 Magnum may fire 38 Special, not the reverse); the
gun's manual is the authority.

## Safe storage

- The safest storage is **unloaded, locked, with ammunition locked separately** (American Academy
  of Pediatrics, Guns in the Home). Locks include cable locks through the action, lock boxes and
  gun safes (CPSC; NSSF Project ChildSafe). Keys and combinations are kept from children.
- **Teach children:** if you see a gun, do not touch it, leave the area, and tell an adult
  (AAP). Ask about guns at homes where children visit.
- **Ammunition:** in its original box, cool and dry, away from fuel and pool chemicals; avoid
  temperatures over 150°F (SAAMI).
- **Lead:** handling ammunition and cleaning guns leaves lead on the hands. Wash hands before
  eating or touching children; no cleaning on the kitchen table (CDC, Childhood Lead Poisoning
  Prevention). → 14-05
